# Critique 1 (blind critic, knew only the theme "Grinding jealousy"; read crit_1.png)

**1) First three seconds:** lonely exclusion, someone left out in the cold looking in. I also feel a quiet, wistful sadness. It reads as longing or melancholy more than jealousy. Nothing in it "grinds"; it is still, not corrosive.

**2) What I see:** Dusk under a heavy blue-grey sky. A dark, hooded or cloaked figure stands at left in the foreground, half turned away. On the right is a dark house or temple with a gabled roof and one large glowing window. Inside the window two small silhouettes embrace. Warm light spills onto the misty ground toward the watcher but stops short of him.

**3) Critique:**
- Drawing: The watcher is a vague blob. Its posture shows no tension (no clenched hands, no craned neck, no fixed stare), so we can't read jealousy. The couple are stiff, peg-like mannequins with stick legs.
- Value/edges: The midground ground plane is a soft, smeared fog with no structure. The house's lower edge dissolves into mush. The window is a flat, evenly bright rectangle with hard edges, so it looks pasted on instead of glowing.
- Color/composition: The palette is all calm blue against warm gold, which is too gentle and harmonious for "grinding". The large empty sky (top 45%) drains energy, and the figure and window simply sit apart without visual tension between them.

**Biggest improvement:** Make the watcher's body carry the emotion. Bring them closer and larger, with a rigid, clenched posture and a face edge turned hard toward the window. Add an acid or sickly color note so the scene feels corrosive, not just sad.
