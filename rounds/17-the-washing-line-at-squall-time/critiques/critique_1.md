# Critique 1 (a fresh critic; knew only the commission "a public exhibition on a theme. The theme is: She (a female character the artist has a deep interest in)"; read crit_1.png)

**1) First three seconds:** Wind, release, a little wildness. It reads as joy on the edge of a cliff, a woman giving herself to a storm. It also feels a bit uneasy, because the face reads more like a mask than a person.

**2) What causes that:**
- The diagonal of the dark hill rising from lower left to right, with the figure leaning into it.
- Arms flung wide: one hand open toward the viewer, the other reaching up to the right edge.
- Hair streaming back, and a dark shawl snapping behind her like a wing.
- The cold blue-grey storm sky, with the huge pale cloud mass on the right as a counterweight.
- A bright break of light on the horizon behind her waist.
- The open mouth, which suggests laughing or shouting. The face's flat pinkish oval and the dark eye slits make it uncanny rather than joyous.

**3) Weaknesses:**
- **Composition:** The big white cloud on the right is the brightest, largest shape in the picture, and it steals focus from her. Her raised hand touches it, which creates a tangent, and the arm vanishes into the right edge. The lower third is a large dead brown slab.
- **Values:** The figure sits mid-value against mid-value sky, so she does not pop. The strongest contrast is cloud against sky, not on the head. The brightest note, the horizon light, sits at her hip and pulls the eye to the wrong place.
- **Edges:** Everything is equally soft and smeared. The lower dress dissolves into the hill without any planned lost edge. There are blurry, digital-looking smudges at the waist/horizon and along the left sea. The horizontal streak marks on the far left look mechanical. No edge is crisp enough to say "look here."
- **Drawing:** The face is the main failure: a flat profile mask with slit eyes and no structure of brow, cheek or jaw. The head is small and loosely attached to the shoulders. The torso is a featureless brown column with no waist, hip or weight. The legs are absent, so she does not stand on the hill; she grows out of it. The hands are mittens: acceptable as gesture, weak as drawing.
- **Gesture:** It is the strongest part. The open-armed lean works. But the body column is upright while the arms and hair say "lean into the wind," so the S-curve or diagonal through the spine is missing.
- **Color:** The cool sky against the warm figure is a sound idea, but the browns of dress and hill are the same muddy temperature and merge. The skin is a single uniform salmon pink with no cool shadow side.
- **Finish:** It is unevenly resolved. The sky has more textured brushwork than the figure, and the subject looks less finished than the background.

**4) The one change:** Make her the value and edge focus. Repaint the head and upper torso with real structure: a lit plane and a shadow plane on the face, a defined brow and jaw, and a believable neck into the shoulders. Then put the picture's lightest light and sharpest edges there, by lighting her face against a darkened strip of sky. At the same time, dim and break up the right cloud so it stops competing.

**Studio manager's note:**
- **The feeling lands:** "joy on the edge of a cliff, a woman giving herself to a storm". That's close to yours, and none of your nearest wrong feelings came up. This is the strongest first reading in many rounds. Two things undercut it: the mask face, and a sheet that doesn't read.
- **The critic read your sheet as a cloud.** It is the largest, brightest shape and it has no cloth logic: no edge where the cloth turns, no folds radiating from the grip, no corner, no translucency where the break shines through. So the story ("she holds a sheet torn loose by the wind") is lost, and the fist only "touches a cloud" in a tangent. Your layer 2 drapery page says it plainly: a bulging pocket, lit on the bulge and dark in the hollow; tension folds running from the fist; an edge that snaps. Make it cloth, with a darker value than the break (white cloth stays darker than the sky's light), and the competition with her face goes too.
- **The face is the main failure**, as you predicted: a flat oval with dark slits. Rebuild it from a tonal study by planes (the plane-head blockout can give you light on a three-quarter head tipped back): the lit front and side planes, the cheek lifted by the laugh, eyes as creased lit shapes rather than holes, a brow, a jaw, a neck that goes into the shoulders. The face must hold the picture's hardest edge and its lightest flesh. Fix the hands the same way (no mittens).
- **The body needs its lean.** The arms and hair say "into the wind", but the torso is an upright brown column with no waist and no legs. Give the spine its diagonal (your blockout's lean), wrap the skirt against the windward leg, and show where she stands on the crest, so she doesn't grow out of the hill.
- **Lights in the wrong place:** the horizon break sits behind her hip, so it pulls the eye to the waist. Either move the brightest part of the break toward her face's side, or keep it lower and let the face be lighter than the break's surround.
- **The digital marks:** the blurred band and pale rectangle left of the skirt, and the ruled horizontal streaks in the far sea. Paint them out with the revision tools and repaint them as broken, directional strokes.
- **Report flags:** colour strength at half the target (Romantic oils are low-chroma, so warm the flesh and lit cloth rather than the sky), too few edges overall, and the open hand masked against the sky. Keep these in mind, but the face and the sheet come first.
