# Visitor (a fresh critic; knew only the commission "a public exhibition on a theme. The theme is: She (a female character the artist has a deep interest in)"; read final.png and the contact sheet past_work.jpg)

1) First three seconds: wind, release, a shout of joy. Then unease, because the laugh reads as a grimace.

2) What makes me feel it: the long diagonal of her arms, one hand gripping a sail or rope high up and the other flung open to the sea, along with hair streaming back, a slate storm sky and a white band of breaking light on the water. Who she is: a woman of about 25 to 40 in a plain brown working dress, standing at the bow of a sailing boat with her head thrown back. She could be a fisherman's wife or a sailor, someone choosing the storm. The pose recalls the famous "flying at the bow" film image, so it reads as a quotation before it reads as her.

3) Weaknesses:
- Drawing (the face): the head is a pale pink mask with a smeared red open mouth and slit eyes. Joy turns into a clownish, almost manic leer, and that is the first thing a visitor's eye lands on.
- Drawing (body and hands): the torso is a stiff brown block with a mannequin bust. Both hands are clumps; the forward hand looks like a claw. The shadowed far arm is a flat dark cut-out that merges with the sail's shadow.
- Anchoring: she is cut off at the hips by a flat diagonal line. She seems to stand inside a bucket or sink into the hull, rather than lean on a rail. The ship never reads as a ship; it is a dark brown mound.
- Values: the dress and the hull are the same mid-dark brown, so the figure has no lower half. The bottom third of the canvas is a large dead area of muddy brown with no information.
- Light: the light is incoherent. The face and the near arm are lit warmly from the left under an overcast sky, and nothing else agrees with that source.
- Edges: they are uniformly ragged everywhere, including face, sail, sea and sky, so there is no hierarchy. There are also stippled, knitted-looking texture patches in the sky and sail.
- Color: a reasonable blue and orange-brown opposition, but muddy. The skin is a hot orange-pink that sits on the surface instead of in the light.
- Finish: the figure and the sail look worked; the sea strip and the lower hull look abandoned.

4) The one change: repaint the head as the true focal point. Give it real planes and a believable, open expression, with the eyes and mouth inside one light, and the sharpest edges and strongest value contrast in the picture. If the face reads as joy, most of the rest is forgiven. If it reads as a grimace, nothing else can save it. A close second is cropping or reworking the lower third so she stands at a readable rail.

5) Compared with the earlier work: partly new, partly the same. New: a full-figure woman in a dynamic outdoor action pose, a sea-and-sail subject, and a quieter daylight palette instead of the glowing doorway and silhouette pictures. Repeated:
- The same shorthand for joy: a pale, mask-like face with a gaping red-lipped laugh, as in 15-one-more-night and 16-cold-water-at-the-landing-stage.
- A single figure set against a large atmospheric field (05, 07, 10).
- The flung or raised arm used as the main gesture (12, 13, 16).
- The same broad, smeared, evenly ragged brushwork across the whole surface.
- Figures ending in a vague mass at the bottom of the frame (06, 07, 10).
The subject is new, but the face and the handling are the formula he has used before.
