# Critique 2 (a fresh critic; knew only the commission "a public exhibition on a theme. The theme is: She (a female character the artist has a deep interest in)"; read crit_2.png)

1) **First three seconds:** Wind and release. It feels like defiant joy at the edge of a storm, with a slightly unsettling note underneath.

2) **What causes that feeling:**
- The big X of the gesture carries it. One arm reaches up and grips a white sail or sheet, the other flings open toward the sea, and the cloak streams off to the right.
- Her hair blows back the same way, so the wind reads clearly.
- The dark rising foreground slope puts her on a cliff or prow, high above the slate sea and the bright band of surf.
- The cool storm palette against her warm ochre figure makes her the clear subject.
- The unsettling note comes from the face. It reads as a mask, a flat pale oval with a stretched grin, and that undercuts the joy.

3) **Weaknesses:**
- **Drawing:** The face is the biggest problem. It is mask-like and too pale and flat, with no eye sockets, no form turning, and a smear for a mouth. The arms are tube-like with no elbow structure. The outstretched hand is a claw of separate strokes. The torso is a block without a waist, and the lower body sinks into the slope with no legs, feet or ground contact, so she seems to grow out of the hill.
- **Values:** The figure, the sea and the sky sit too close in middle value. The lightest notes are the sail and the surf band, not her. The surf stripe is also a hard, flat horizontal that cuts behind her hips and pulls the eye away.
- **Edges:** They are almost all the same choppy, broken edge. Nothing is lost and found. The sail's lower edge is a ruled, mechanical line. The foreground is a uniform blur of brown smears, more fog than ground.
- **Composition:** The sail is a big, heavy, unresolved grey shape that crowds the upper right and competes with her. She sits a little low and central for the diagonal energy the gesture promises. The dark cloak wing ends in a stiff, knife-like point.
- **Color:** Ochre figure against blue-grey storm is a sound choice, but the skin is a single pink-orange with no cool shadows, reflected light or sky colour, so it looks pasted rather than lit by that sky.
- **Finish:** The surface treatment is the same everywhere: the same stroke size on face, cloak, sky and ground. Nothing gets the fine, deliberate handling that tells the viewer where to look, and that would naturally be the face and the reaching hand.

4) **The one change that would most improve it:** Repaint the head as a lit, turning form. Give it a clear light side and shadow side in the warm-cool of the storm light, eye sockets and a real, readable expression, and make it the place with the strongest value contrast and the finest edges in the picture. The gesture already carries the feeling; at the moment the face spends it.

**Studio manager's note:**
- **The feeling holds:** "defiant joy at the edge of a storm". The sheet now reads as cloth (a sail or sheet), so the story is back. One thing spends it: the face, for the second critic running. Your handoff said the face "now reads as a woman of about forty laughing". Two fresh readers say mask. Trust the readers over your own eye here, and give the head most of phase 3.
- **How to get a face at this size:** the head is about 90 px wide, so features need brushes of 2–6 px, not `model` (which made squiggles) and not `match` at its usual sizes.
  - Work from your tonal study (face_4), enlarged.
  - Lay the big planes first: the lit front plane, the shadow side plane in a cool reflected grey from the storm, and the eye sockets as two soft darker shapes. They are not black slits.
  - Then the laugh's structure: the cheek lifted into a lit mound under each eye, the mouth corners up, the upper lip line. A small dark for the open mouth, not a smear.
  - Last, two or three of the picture's sharpest edges: the profile against the sky, the light on the cheekbone, the brow.
  - Look at it with `look crop` at the head after every episode, and at thumbnail size: it must read as a laughing woman in both.
- **Skin colour:** the critic is right that the flesh is one pink-orange "pasted, not lit". The layer 2 face page has it: lit planes warm and thick, shadow planes thin, cool and lost into the hair. Put the storm's grey into the shadow side, and a touch of the break's silver on the lit edge.
- **The values:** the surf band and the sheet are the lightest things, not her. Lower the surf band to a broken, less ruled light, and keep the sheet's lights below the face's lightest flesh (white cloth stays darker than the face's top light only if you lower the cloth; do that).
- **"Grows out of the hill":** the crop at the crest is your choice, but it reads as sinking. Make the crest's edge a found, slightly lighter, grassy line that passes in front of the skirt, with the wind-combed grass overlapping the cloth, so the hill clearly hides her legs.
- **Mechanical edges:** the sheet's ruled lower edge and the cloak's knife point. Break them into a snapping, irregular hem.
- **Hands and arms:** elbow structure and knuckle planes on the raised fist (your ledger's break point), and the open hand as a hand rather than a claw.
- Then finishing, and the signature low on the left of the crest as planned.
