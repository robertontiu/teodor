# Journal: commission 17, "She" (Romanticism, oil on canvas)

## Phase 1, planning (no paint on the canvas)

### Research: what I take from it
- **The sublime and the beautiful were gendered.** Burke set the sublime (awe, terror, danger, "delight" as the thrill of danger met) against the beautiful (pleasure, nurture), and cast them as masculine and feminine (Anthropoetics, Views 401, on Burke and Kant). The Romantic landscape puts the woman, when she appears at all, on the "beautiful" side, small, sheltered or waiting. What I take: **put her on the sublime side.** She is not the gentle note in a terrible landscape; she meets the storm with the storm's own energy. That is my idea of her, and the reason she is big and lit, not dwarfed.
- **Composition device from the current:** the low horizon and the vast sky, edge to edge, no framing foreground; the storm mass around light; veiling (style doc §2, §7). I keep the low horizon (about four-fifths down) and give the sky three-quarters of the canvas, and I crop the sheet at the top-right edge so the cloth and the weather go on beyond the frame (awe needs something that can't be taken in at once: Keltner and Haidt 2003, emotion.md §7). I break the current's "tiny figures" rule on purpose: emotion.md §6 says a body raises agreement and a readable face raises it more, and the commission is about her. Research over convention, as the studio rules ask; the scale of the weather is carried by the sky and the crop instead.
- **From a current I have not used, the Hague School** (the "Grey School": "sky and light are the great magicians"; muted, warm greys; women and children on the dunes and beaches at Scheveningen; theartstory.org): the working woman on the coast in real weather, painted as a person and not an allegory. I take her plainness (heavy wool, real washing, real work) to keep her off the fantasy-cover crag, and I reject its grey quietness, which is my own hushed habit.
- **The cue that carries my feeling (emotion.md):** posture first. Joy is the head tipped back and the arms raised above the shoulders with straight elbows (Coulson 2004); fear is the head back with the forearms raised and the weight pulled away; the body, not the face, separates ecstasy from agony at high intensity (Aviezer 2012). So her raised arm is long and nearly straight, the chest open, the weight pushed INTO the wind, not away. A laugh needs the whole face (cheeks, crow's feet), never an open black mouth (habits.md). The art frame mutes joy (Gerger 2014): paint it bigger than seems needed.

**Chosen: between C and I.** Three-quarter length (cropped at mid-thigh by the crest), right of centre, seen from a little below, leaning hard LEFT into the wind with the raised arm almost straight up and to the right holding the corner of a sheet that bellies up and out of the top-right edge. Her face three-quarter to the left, into the light and the wind, tipped back, laughing. Light: a cold silver-lemon break low at the left under the cloud's edge, raking across her face and the linen. Low horizon (about y 1260 of 1600) with a strip of sea and rain veils at the right. Dark slate cloud from the upper left. Dark crest at the bottom.

### Colour studies (colour_studies.png)
1. Silver-lemon break, slate sky, smalt-blue dress: the light is right, but the blue dress dissolves into the blue sky; the figure loses.
2. Pearl grey, high key: no threat at all; picturesque prettiness (a nearest wrong look). Rejected.
3. Prussian storm, ochre crest, red-earth skirt: powerful, but it is my red-and-black drama code and my amber-against-blue reflex. Rejected.
4. **Chosen:** study 1's cold light and sky with a warm umber wool dress (sepia `#4b3a2a`, lit `#7a6048`): she separates from the slate by value and temperature, and the lit flesh (the only warm, high-chroma note) is the life in a cold world. Palette: lead white, smalt, Prussian, ultramarine, sepia, burnt sienna, indian red (only in the flesh), yellow ochre (crest grass, very muted), storm greys `#5c6470`, `#8d8f8c`; the break `#ebe6c9`, not amber.

### Habits I break, and how (at least three)
1. **Lone figure from behind / dark silhouette:** she faces us three-quarter, face lit and readable; she is a light shape on the dark sky, never a dark one on light.
2. **Eye level, middle distance, centred:** a low viewpoint looking a little up; she is big, right of centre, cropped by the crest; the sheet is cropped by the top-right edge.
3. **Hushed melancholy, bittersweet feeling:** one loud, glad feeling at high arousal: fierce glee.
4. **One warm amber light against violet:** a cold silver-white light, the white linen as the main light mass, slate and Prussian against it; the only warmth is her flesh.
5. **Radiant burst at the centre / radial pull:** the light is a low horizontal band at the left edge; the whole picture sweeps on a diagonal left to right with the wind; no radial centre.
6. **Frontal stiff body:** a hard lean and a twist through the torso; the arm stretched.
7. (planned for the painting phases) **Mannequin face, tube hands, uniform dab:** tonal studies for the face and both hands before they are painted; long wind strokes in the sky, knife in the linen lights, small brushes only at the head and hands; signature placed where the painting wants it (probably low left on the dark crest), not bottom right.

### The expectation ledger
- **Taught to expect:** everything streams left to right with the wind (cloud, rain veils, grass, hair, skirt, the sheet); long, soft, lost edges in the sky; a cold key of slate and grey; the light low and horizontal at the left.
- **The one break:** her face and her raised arm turn AGAINST the grain, to the left, up into the wind and the light; and there, where the lit corner of the sheet in her fist meets the darkest cloud, is the one hard, crisp edge and the warmest colour (the flush of her lit cheek and knuckles) in a cold picture. Check with `look edges`, `look temp` and `look thumb` that this is the strongest point.
- **Focal point:** her face and raised fist, about (760, 560) on the 1200x1600 canvas (to confirm on the blockout).

### Reading the subjects, and what the layer-2 research changes in my plan
I read layer 1 and the Romantic layer 2 for face, figure, hands-feet, hair, drapery, sky, landscape, water, light-shadow, space and objects. (My subject list: face, figure, hands-feet, hair, drapery, sky, landscape, water, light-shadow, space; objects only for the sheet. The studio refresh replaced subjects.json in the work dir with the library's merged file, so the list is kept here.)
- **Storm cloud colour: I was wrong, the research is right.** I had written "slate-indigo" and "Prussian". Every measured Romantic storm is a low-chroma coloured grey (#544c46, #595b5c; darkest about L* 30, never black). The cloud becomes a greyed indigo: Prussian/ultramarine broken with sepia and burnt sienna, about #3d424b to #4f545d, the zenith #363b45. The break is not white either: a cold silver-cream about #c8c5b3-#d0ccb9 (L* about 80), and the lit sheet stays below it.
- **The horizon: a stated choice.** My three-quarters sky (horizon 0.79) is beyond every measured work (median 0.55, highest 0.72). I lower it to **0.73** (y 1168), which sits with the figure branch's low viewpoint (the eye at knee height of the standing figure, heads and arms against the sky) and keeps the sky at three-quarters on the left. On the right the crest rises above the horizon line (we look up the slope at her), so there the land climbs to y 1065. Chosen on purpose: her body has to rise into the sky, and the scale of the weather has to be overhead, not in front.
- **Joy has no Romantic model.** No Romantic storm figure is glad, and loose hair then meant distress. So the face and the body carry the joy, and the hair is only half loose: pinned up in a bun with three damp ropes breaking out downwind, not a wild mane. The laugh is the Duchenne laugh of layer 1 (AU6 cheeks bunched, eyes narrowed into crescents, AU12 corners up and back, upper teeth, deep nasolabial fold, brows relaxed), painted with the current's means (side light, a few big planes, lost shadow edge into the hair). The pose is the Coulson joy pose: one arm at full stretch above the head, the other flung wide and open, the chest open to the weather. Nothing in the mouth may sneer (Baudelaire's satanic laugh is the wrong one).
- From figure/drapery layer 2: heavy working masses, no slimming; skirt pressed to the windward leg, billowing on the leeward side as one connected mass; the shawl is the flag, one long taut dark end; the sheet a big sail with a lit belly and a dark hollow, two or three folds from the fist, the brightest thing below the break.
- From light-shadow layer 2: a cold light with brown bounce makes the lit planes cooler than the shadows: the reverse of my amber-light habit, and correct here.

## Phase 2, development

### Reading critique 1
The critic felt "wind, release, a little wildness ... joy on the edge of a cliff, a woman giving herself to a storm": my feeling, and none of the nearest wrong ones. What failed: the mask face (a flat oval with slit eyes), the sheet read as a cloud (the biggest, brightest shape, no cloth logic, the fist touching it in a tangent), the body an upright brown column with no waist and no legs (she grows out of the hill), the break's brightest light at her hip, the digital smudges (blur band, pale rectangle, ruled sea streaks), the hands mittens. The critic's one change: make her the value and edge focus, light the face against a darkened strip of sky, dim and break up the right mass. I agree with every point: the painting is wrong where the critic missed my feeling only in the face.

## Revision (after publication): the head and the open hand

### Why
The owner looked at the published final and called the face "caricatural": a pale mask, lipstick-red lips in a gaping grin, crushed eyes. The visitor read the same ("the laugh reads as a grimace ... a clownish, almost manic leer") and named it a formula from my earlier pictures. Both critics before them had said mask. Looking at `state1/final.png` close: one even pale value over the whole face, the lips a red band laid on top, the eyes two drawn slits, no shadow side, no socket, no jaw. The feeling ("fierce glee in the gale") lived in the body and died in the face. Painters revise finished work, so I repainted her head, and her open hand. The old final is kept in `state1/final.png`.

### Research and references
- Read `face.md`, `sex-age.md` and `romanticism--face.md` again. What decided the repaint: joy is AU6 (cheeks bunched, eyes narrowed from below, crow's feet) more than the mouth; a laugh needs the cheeks, eyes and mouth corners to agree; the Romantic face is three to five value shapes with the shadow side thin, cool and lost into the hair, the lit planes warm and thick; ambiguity reads male, so the female cues (facial contrast, a narrow jaw, light on the lid band, the hair mass) must be present, as quiet shifts inside the form, never as marks on top.
- References (`refs/refs.json`, `refs/sheet.jpg`; Met and Cleveland open access, CC0/public domain; studied beside the canvas, never traced, never set as the schema). *Laughing cheek and eye:* Duchenne's plate 53 (a photograph; an induced, false laugh on an old man, so anatomy only: the cheek shoving the lower lid into a crescent, crow's feet, the nasolabial fold round the lifted cheek); Fenton's photograph of a laughing satyr cast (the closed-lip smile: the corner tucked into a cheek that bulges up toward the eye); a Classic Veracruz smiling head (the whole face smiling, eyes narrowed to slits under a quiet brow, lips only just parted). *Woman's head tipped back, three-quarter:* a 19th-century German drawing of a woman's head looking up (the jaw's underside and throat in soft halftone, never black; the far cheek one quiet plane); an 18th-century French red-chalk study of an old woman looking up (the shadow side as one plane, the brow shading the socket); a Hellenistic marble female satyr head (the closed-mouth smile only: it is barely tipped); a red-chalk head looking upward (how much of the nose's and jaw's underside a moderate throw shows). Her face is invented from none of them: they taught where the planes turn.
- The new head reference: a `scene` render of the MakeHuman mesh (`schema/scene_14.json`): a sturdy woman of 40, laughing with the cheeks (AU6 1.0, AU12 0.85, AU7 0.65, lids a little lowered, lips parted only 0.08), three-quarter to the left, tipped back moderately, gathered hair, the canvas's own camera and the low cold break from the left (azimuth -80, elevation 10). Four drafts to get there: the first rolled the head sideways with the body's lean and kept the eyes wide; the second turned her to profile and threw the head so far back that the jaw's underside took over (face.md's worm's-eye warning); the long-hair curtain fell on the windward side of her face (wrong for this wind). ### What I learned (for habits.md)
