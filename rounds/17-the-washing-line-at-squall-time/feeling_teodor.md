# Feeling

**Fierce glee in the gale: she is as big as the weather.**

What I need to say about her: she is not the small figure the storm is staged around, and not the "beautiful" half of the old pair (the sublime for the sky, the beautiful for the woman). For one minute on the hill, wrestling the washing off the line with the squall coming in, she and the storm are the same size, and she knows it, and it delights her: a body braced and laughing, the danger real and not minding it.

One overwhelming feeling, high arousal, positive. The threat is the weather's, not hers: it is there so that her delight has something to push against (the sublime's "delight" is the thrill of meeting danger and standing).

## THE NEAREST WRONG FEELINGS

1. **Fear, panic, struggle** (high arousal, dark sky, a body straining: the threat is the loudest thing in the picture, and an open mouth can read as a scream). What separates mine: the body is OPEN, not guarding: her raised arm is nearly straight, the chest turned up and into the wind, the head tipped back a little (Coulson's joy posture, not fear's raised forearms and pulled-back weight). The face laughs with the whole face: cheeks lifted, eyes narrowed with crow's feet, lips drawn back in a grin, the mouth only a little open (no wide black O). Her face is in the strongest light in the picture. The curves of the sheet and her body are long and swinging (curves are positive), the angles belong to the clouds only.
2. **Drudgery, grim labour** (a woman at the washing, bent, the domestic chore). What separates mine: she is upright and leaning INTO the wind, not bent over the basket; the action is play with the weather, not work; her free hand grips the corner high, above her head, like a sail she is holding for the pleasure of it.
3. **Cheerful genre prettiness** (a sentimental washday, picturesque charm, "a breezy day"). What separates mine: the sky is truly dangerous and takes most of the canvas: a slate-indigo squall front with rain veils already falling on the sea, the light a cold raking break under the cloud's edge, the scale of the weather enormous. The sublime must be in it, or the glee is just weather.

## THE NEAREST WRONG LOOKS

- **The postcard of this current:** the wanderer above the fog; an orange sunset vortex; a heroine on a crag posed like a fantasy cover (wind machine hair, swelling bosom, triumphant stance): kitsch grandeur. Or the woman as allegory (Liberty with a flag). She is a working woman on a hill with real washing, in a real squall; her clothes are plain and heavy, her body has weight.
- **Shampoo advert:** hair streaming prettily over a soft-focus face. The hair is a heavy, wet, tangled mass, partly whipping across; the face has age (about forty), weather in it.
- **My own habits:** a lone figure from behind (she faces us, three-quarter, face lit and readable); eye-level, centred, middle distance (low viewpoint, figure off-centre right and big, cropped by the crest); a hushed melancholy, a bittersweet feeling (loud and glad, single); one warm amber light in a dark room, amber against violet (a cold silver-lemon light raking under the cloud, white linen as the light mass, slate and Prussian against it); a radiant burst at the centre (the light is a horizontal band low at the left edge, no burst); one figure pulled toward a radial centre (everything sweeps across on a diagonal, left to right); mannequin face and tube hands (a tonal study for the face and both hands before they are painted); the uniform square dab (long wind-swept strokes in the sky, knife in the linen lights, small brushes only in the face and hands).
