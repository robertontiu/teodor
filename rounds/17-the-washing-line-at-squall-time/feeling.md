# The Washing Line at Squall Time

**Commission:** theme "She" (a female character the artist has a deep interest in), in Romanticism, oil on canvas (round 17). Painted by Claude Opus 5.5 with studio v8, after the engine and subject library upgrade. This covered the subject library (each subject researched as it really is, then as Romanticism renders it, before drawing), layers with roles, the stronger hand, revision tools, the figure rig and the finishing check. A first attempt was cancelled after phase 1; this one started from scratch. She is a woman Teodor invented.

**Teodor's feeling** (in his words, written before he painted; the full text, with the nearest wrong feelings, is in `feeling_teodor.md`):

> **Fierce glee in the gale: she is as big as the weather.**
>
> She is not the small figure the storm is staged around, and not the "beautiful" half of the old pair (the sublime for the sky, the beautiful for the woman). For one minute on the hill, wrestling the washing off the line with the squall coming in, she and the storm are the same size, and she knows it, and it delights her.

**Teodor, on finishing:**

> Two critics told me her face was a mask, and the owner told me she was a man. They were right, and the fault was in my own head study before any paint went down: a long shield of a face, a wedge nose, a heavy jaw, hair pressed flat like a cap, a body like a brown coat. So in this last phase I drew her again. Her face is shorter and rounder now, with a small chin and lips that are redder and darker than the skin around them, and her eyes are creased into laughter. Her hair is a heavy wet mass blowing downwind. Her bodice shows the bust and a waist pulled in high, and her hips flare into the skirt. She is a strong woman of about forty laughing into a storm while she holds a sheet against the dark. The flat bright band of light became a torn break under the clouds, and the hill now runs in front of her skirt so she stands behind its brow instead of growing out of it. Small edges kept coming out as ruled lines, and I scraped and wiped them out again and again. The picture ends quieter and softer-edged than Romantic oil paintings usually are.

**Research:** the Romanticism style doc; Burke's sublime and beautiful and how they were gendered ([Anthropoetics on Burke](https://anthropoetics.ucla.edu/views/vw401)); the [Met's essay on Romanticism](https://www.metmuseum.org/en/essays/romanticism); the emotion research on posture (an open chest and a raised arm read as joy, not fear); the Romanticism layer-2 pages for 12 subjects (`docs/styles/subjects/romanticism--*.md`); and, in the last phase, the new sex-and-age page (`docs/subjects/sex-age.md`). Teodor broke one convention on purpose: Romantic storm pictures show tiny figures against vast nature, but he made her large, because the emotion research says a readable face carries feeling better.
