# Visitor (a fresh critic; knew only the theme "Death"; read final.png, then the contact sheet of past work)

**1) First three seconds:** I'm startled and a little claustrophobic, and then it slides toward comedy. It looks like the view from inside a grave, or from the body on the floor, with the onlookers peering down. The unease doesn't last because it starts to read as a fisheye group selfie.

**2) What causes it:** The worm's-eye ring of faces around a blown-out white sky. The black disc at the centre, which works as a void, a pupil or an eclipse. The open mouth of the figure in red and the two hands reaching toward us. The phone held up by the top figure is the sharpest idea: someone filming a death. But it's under-stated, and it also pulls the picture toward a viral-video joke.

**3) Weaknesses:**
- **Drawing:** The faces are near-identical blobs with a stamped expression. The hands are rubbery: the left one has two sausage fingers, and the red arm is a tube with no elbow.
- **Values:** The top half is a flat white glare, so the black disc is the only dark accent and it floats without meaning. The bottom half is heavy black and red. The two halves don't connect.
- **Edges:** The halos around the heads look like cut-outs, a pasted look that sits badly with the soft airbrushed lower figures.
- **Brushwork:** The dabs in the sky look like a filter, not painted marks, and the finish is inconsistent across the canvas.
- **Composition:** It's crowded. Six heads all carry the same weight, and nothing tells you whose death this is or what the witnesses feel.
- **Color:** The red-black split is a stock "death" code.

**4) The one change:** Thin the crowd to two or three faces and make the expressions specific: grief, indifference, the person filming. Move the phone to the front as the emotional centre and cut the decorative black disc. That would turn a gimmicky viewpoint into a statement.

**5) Compared with past work:** The viewpoint is a real departure. There are multiple readable faces, a modern object and no lone silhouette. What it repeats: a radiant central light burst (as in 03 and 11), a strong backlit glare against dark masses (04, 08), the red-and-black palette (02, 03, 12), and figures painted as generic mannequins with no individual faces (all of them). The subject is new, but the light-burst habit and the generic figures are the same as before.
