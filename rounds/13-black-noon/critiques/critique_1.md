# Critique 1 (a fresh critic; knew only the theme "Death"; read crit_1.png)

**1) First three seconds:** A bit puzzled. It reads as an odd group portrait, or a circle of people looking down at someone. I don't feel grief or dread. The hand catches my eye, but more as a mystery than an emotion.

**2) What causes it:** The figures circle the viewer like mourners looking down into a grave, and that low point of view is the strongest idea in the piece. The open, limp hand laid across the red body suggests the one who has died or is dying. But the pale cream sky is so bright and flat that the mood comes out airy and almost cheerful. The red reads as cloth more than blood or anything solemn.

**3) Weaknesses:**
- **Composition:** The heads are scattered evenly and none of them leads. The yellow patch in the top-right corner is a loud distraction that pulls the eye off the canvas.
- **Values:** The sky is a large near-white area with nothing happening in it. There are only two zones, light above and dark below, with no middle tones to tie them together.
- **Edges:** Every edge is equally blobby. The hand is the only crisp, smooth shape, so it looks pasted on rather than painted in the same world.
- **Drawing:** The heads are stacked circles with no faces, necks or shoulders, and look like pom-poms. The hand's fingers are tube-like and stiff.
- **Gesture:** None of the mourners lean in. No bowed heads, no reaching. Without body language there's no grief.
- **Color:** The red is unfocused. The yellow fights everything else.
- **Finish:** The same uniform dab texture covers everything, so the surface reads as a filter, not as brushwork.

**4) The single best fix:** Make the mourners lean and bow their heads into the opening: tilted heads, shoulders curving inward, maybe one hand reaching down toward the limp hand. Then darken and cool the sky to a dim grey-violet so the light feels like it's fading, and remove the yellow.

(Studio manager's note: the critic got your idea, a ring of people looking down at us, and the craft points stand: faces with bone and expression seen from below, bodies that lean in and reach, the hands painted in the same world, varied marks. But do NOT take the advice to dim the sky to grey-violet: that is the old habit, the hushed fading light, and the client asked for shock. Keep the glare, and make it hurt: the noon must feel merciless, not airy. The critic felt puzzlement and no dread at all; the terror half has no form yet. Decide what the yellow is doing, and either make it mean something or remove it.)
