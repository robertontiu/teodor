# Feeling

**Grief, and the morning that comes in anyway, with tenderness for it held between them.**

At seventy I know death mostly as the day after: the person left behind sits in the same kitchen, the sun crosses the table exactly as it did yesterday, unasked and almost insulting in its warmth, and the hand that still reaches for the other one finds the light there instead. Grief gets the room. The light gets its own space too and doesn't apologize. What makes it bearable is tenderness, and the tenderness won't choose between them.

The two halves need their own forms:
(Chosen after sketching: a bedroom, not a kitchen.)
- Grief: an old man sitting bowed on the edge of the bed, on his own side, in the cool shadow.
- The morning that goes on: the sun laid warm and wide across her side of the bed, the smoothed sheet, the one pillow, the window.
- Tenderness lives where they meet: his hand reaching back to rest flat on the sheet, just at the edge of the light.
