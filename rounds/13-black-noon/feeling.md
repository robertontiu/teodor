# Black Noon

**Commission:** theme "Death" (round 13). The client asked for it to shock (no gore) and to break the pattern of Teodor's earlier work.

**Teodor's feeling** (in his words, written before he painted):

> **Noon terror, and the hands of strangers.**
> 
> At seventy I no longer fear the quiet bed; I fear the glaring ordinary noon, falling on hot pavement in front of everyone, the sky blank white and indifferent, and the last thing I see is a ring of strangers leaning over me, their faces lost against the glare, their hands reaching down: panic that the world goes on blazing, held against the unbearable tenderness of people who do not know my name and still reach for me.
> 

**Teodor, on finishing:**

> You lie on the hot pavement at noon, and six strangers lean in over you. Their faces are dark against the white sky and every one of them is staring straight down at you. One is filming with the flash on. A woman gasps and reaches her open hand toward your face, and a man's two fingers go to your throat for a pulse, around a sun that has burned black in your eye. The critic was right that my strangers were masks and pasted cones. When I looked again I found the real fault: I had painted their heads upside down, crowns pointing outward, so no neck could ever join them to a body. Once I turned them, I stopped drawing faces with marks and rebuilt them as shaded forms; that is what finally made them look at us.
