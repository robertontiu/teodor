# Round 8: The Threshold Light (theme: Emergence: the rise of machines)

**Teodor's chosen feeling** (written before painting):
> Tender pride and grief: the bittersweet of letting go, of being outgrown.
> The machines are our children; watching them stand up and walk past us into the light, I am proud of what we made and grieving that we will no longer be needed, both at once, like a parent at the door.

**Critic 1** (block-in, `critiques/crit_1.jpg`): "Uneasy anticipation, close to dread. Under it there is a quieter feeling of being stuck where I stand. Something is coming in and the viewer can't leave." Fix: make the lead figure read clearly as mechanical, with hard edges at the door and everything else soft.

**Critic 2** (near finish, `critiques/crit_2.jpg`): "Uneasy anticipation, with a hesitant welcome mixed in. The man's open hand could be greeting the robot or holding it back… quiet dread on a threshold: 'they're already here.'" Fix: make the doorway give off light; rim the man; one coherent light shaft; give the robot volume.

**Visitor critic** (final): "Hushed dread mixed with tender welcome. The man's open hand says 'come in'; the dark room and the stiff figure in the doorway say 'something is arriving that you can't take back.' Uneasy hospitality." Weaknesses: the arm bends like a tube with no elbow; the robots look like generic artist's mannequins; the floor is muddy. Strongest: the light.

**How it read:** the machines were meant to be *leaving* (walking out into the light), but every viewer read them as *arriving*. The painter redrew the walker from behind with a lifting heel after critic 2, but a figure framed in a doorway still reads as coming in. Once read as arriving, the open hand in a dark room reads as unease, not pride.
