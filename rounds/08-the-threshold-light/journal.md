# Journal of Teodor Lisk — commission: EMERGENCE: THE RISE OF MACHINES

## Stage 1 — Conception
The feeling is chosen and sealed away. I did not want the fear-of-robots poster, nor the shining-future poster. I wanted the old workshop truth: the thing you make with your hands one day stands up and does not need your hands any more.
- Sketch A (scene_A): an old man at his house while pale walkers cross a dusty plain toward a low sun. Too wide, too much sky; the man was lost behind his own wall. A landscape, not a moment.
- Sketch B (scene_B): a dark workshop, one bright doorway, a pale figure standing in it. The value structure hit me at once: one dark dominant mass, one hard bright rectangle. A passerby gets it in a second.
- Sketch C (scene_C): a kneeling woman, a child-sized pale machine walking toward great towers. Too many symbols, the towers say "science fiction" before they say anything human.
Chose B. A doorway is the oldest picture of leaving that we have, older than any machine. Everyone has stood in one, or watched someone through one.

## Stage 2 — Development
- B2: the maker was outside the frame and the lamp washed the whole room into one murky mid-value (my old sin). Dimmed it.
- B3/B4: lowered the eye to a seated man's height; brought the maker into the frame, lower left, back to us. Added three distant pale walkers beyond the door: this one is not the only one leaving; it is the first of many.
- B5-B6: widened the door so the walkers have room; the maker's raised hand was hidden behind his own body.
- B7-B10: swung his right arm out sideways so the hand reaches across the dark and stops just short of the light on the threshold floor. The gap between his hand and the walking figure is the painting. Softened the elbow so it does not read as pointing or accusing; it is an open reach that will not close.

- CRITIC 1: first three seconds "uneasy anticipation, close to dread; stuck where I stand; something is coming in and the viewer can't leave". Sees a figure walking IN through the door, more following, a dark silhouette with arm raised "as if guarding or bracing". Weaknesses: no telling human from machine; the near figure's head/shoulder/arm merge into a lump; the same square-dab edge everywhere, ragged door frame; muddy monochrome brown darks, dead right wall and lower half; doorway figures close in value. Fix: a clearly mechanical lead silhouette (angular head, a joint, a cold rim), hard edges at the door, everything else softer.
- My reading of the crit: the painting is wrong, not the critic. It reads backwards: they think the walker is arriving and the old man is defending the room. [my feeling] needs the walker unmistakably LEAVING, back to us, small and beyond the threshold, glancing back once; and the old man's hand must be an open, lowered, tender reach, not a brace. Also: show that he MADE it (lit bench, empty cradle), and make the machine undeniably made.

[my feeling] needs it small, out beyond the door, in stride, its back to us and the light eating its edges as it goes. And the light must be light: smooth, hot, spilling over the frame and onto him. The sky's impasto is the plaster: scrape it flat with the knife.
