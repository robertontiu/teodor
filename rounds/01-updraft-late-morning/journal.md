# Studio journal — Elias Varga

1. The ground is a warm yellow, almost too easy, like a morning that has not been earned yet. I wanted the top to open, so I washed a paler field across it, a sky that lifts rather than sits.

2. The pale band read like a lid, pleasant but static. I stacked a coral-orange field low, so the upper light has warmth to rise out of, the way heat rises off a summer field.

3. It had become a sunset, warm but drowsy, nothing in it moving. I threw one loaded red stroke up from the low left toward the light, the direction of a body getting up off the ground.

4. The single red line looked solitary, a stalk with nobody near it. I sprang three more strokes from the same root and let them fan open, magenta, flame and gold, like a hand flung wide.

5. The strokes were reaching but into nothing, just pale ground, and the dark halos made them muddy, like stems in a jar. I opened a Turner bloom of white-gold up where the gold stroke ends, so the reaching has somewhere to arrive.

6. The long strokes were slow and slick, and the image risked becoming a stem and a sun. I flicked three short marks out of the light, turquoise and pink, quick hooks that leap away like sparks.

7. The root at lower left had gone heavy and muddy, brown halos gathering like a bruise. I sank it under a warm apricot glaze and softened the halos higher up, so the strokes seem to spring from light rather than from dirt.

8. Everything leaned one way, a single shout up and to the right. I swung a sky-blue stroke in from the right edge and curled it back on itself, a turn of the body, so the movement circles instead of only fleeing.

9. The canvas looked like cut paper, too clean, and the lower orange lay flat and dead. I scrubbed in broken dry-brush passages of coral, lemon and pink along the bottom, all leaning upward, so the ground itself begins to shimmer.

10. The three dabs sat apart like things on a shelf, each casting its little shadow. I dragged a wide, nearly dry gold sweep along the bottom through all of them, so they become flecks inside one rising swell.

11. The upper left lay dull, a shelf the light never reached, and the lone yellow dab below was stranded. I let the bloom spill leftward in a pale glaze and drowned the stranded dab in warm apricot so it melts into the swell.

12. Nothing in the painting was sharp, it was all soft or fat, and the middle left was an empty yellow wall. I drew two thin indigo hooks there, tapered and fast, a skip in the rhythm, a little kick of the heel.

13. The ribbons stood each on their own, and the only passage that truly sang was the crumbling spray at the bottom. I dragged a nearly empty brush of pale light diagonally up through the strokes toward the bloom, a trail of glitter carried on an updraft.

14. The pale drag hit the canvas too full and left a white chisel, almost a quill, with a grey ghost along it. I sank its heavy head under warm orange and lifted the grey with gold, keeping only the glittering tail that rises into the light.

15. The blue turn happened once and nobody answered it; the upper left was warm but still. I sang it back smaller in turquoise up in the light, a little loop-the-loop, a refrain.

16. It was all ribbons at one volume, and the light at the top had lost its force because nothing beneath it had depth. I glazed a deeper vermilion into the bottom and lower right, a warm floor that makes the top glow brighter by contrast.

17. The new vermilion floor held, but it was one flat wash and did not vibrate. I flicked small rising notes of lilac, rose and turquoise into it, complementary sparks so the heat hums.

18. The middle band between light and heat had gone mustard-dull, and the right side of it sat empty. I laid a soft peach-gold glaze across it, so the three fields glow one into the next like rising warmth.

19. The bloom was only a soft haze, with no point where light was struck and certain. I laid one thick, broken stroke of near-white at its heart, curling up and over, the one place where the light is loaded rather than breathed.

20. The struck white came out cold, with a grey shadow, an object lying on the light rather than the light itself. I breathed warm pale yellow over it so its impasto stays but its edge melts back into the bloom.

21. The bottom had its glittering spray but the light gave nothing back; it only glowed. I pulled an almost bare brush of lemon up and out of the bloom toward the corner, so it throws off crumbs of brightness, overflowing.

22. The bare-brush pull landed full again and left a lemon chisel in a grey sleeve, cutting across the bloom. I drowned it in cream so only a faint warm streak remains, a trace of the light rushing out to the corner.

23. The peach ghost in the center-left still read as a blunt pencil, a shape rather than a movement. I threw a hot-pink arc in from beyond the left edge, up and over it and down again, the curve of a leap, to break its outline.

24. The right edge between the red and the blue was a mute orange gap where the rhythm stopped. This time I started the bare brush beyond the edge so its loaded head stays off the canvas, and only the sparkling crumbs drift in.

25. The crumbs drifted in well but their shadow left a dull olive band, a smudge in the warmth. A soft gold glaze along it, so the sparkle sits in light, not in dust.

26. From a step back, the light pours from the upper right and everything springs toward it; only the lower left sat quiet in its haze, as if someone sat out the dance. I flicked three small rising notes there, lemon, lilac and turquoise, to pull that corner onto its feet.
