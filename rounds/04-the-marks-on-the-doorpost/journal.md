# Journal

## 1 - sketches and ground
Two thumbnails: a figure in a doorway looking out at a road, and a figure on a shore before a bright sky with a small boat. The doorway wins: the house holds the one who stays, the light holds the one who goes. Toned the room cool and dark and cut a warm door of light into it.

## 2 - block-in
The first pass muddied the light; wet paint dragged the wall into it. Let it dry, then laid sky and field in the doorway separately, pale gold above, deeper ochre below, and began quieting the left wall so it is a cool dark field.

## 3 - room fields
Laid the right wall, lintel and floorboards as soft stacked fields so the door sits like a Rothko window in a dark stack. The room must feel lived in and quiet, the opening must be the only real light.

## 4 - the jamb
The left edge of the opening was ragged and read as torn paper, not a door. Cut it straight with the dark wall so the opening is architecture, and softened the hard corner of the lintel into the wall.

## 5 - clearing the light
Dark teeth of the first ground still bit into the opening. Filled them with the same gold so the doorway is one clean, generous field of outside.

## 6 - a thicker frame
The teeth would not go; so the jamb grows, a heavy dark post cut with the knife. The seam inside the light smudged away.

## 7 - the land outside
Gave the outside its road: pale, running straight from the threshold to a point on the horizon, with the ochre field brushed in strokes that fan out from that point. Low violet-warm hills on the horizon so the distance has a far side.

## 8 - sky
Re-laid the sky in soft round strokes, a little cooler and paler above, warmest and brightest just over the horizon where the road ends. The eye should be pulled out along the road to that glow.

## 9 - flattening
A ridge of old paint ran down the sky like a crack. Pressed it down with the knife, palette scraping, so the light is unbroken.

## 10 - sky again
The knife left a ladder of ridges in the sky. Covered the whole upper opening with flat, unloaded round strokes, no relief, letting them spill; the jamb can be recut afterwards.

## 11 - scraping down
Scraped the sky flat with the knife and no body of paint, the way you would take a passage back before repainting it.

## 12 - recutting the right jamb
The scraped sky had spilled over the right post. Knifed the wall back in, so the right edge of the opening is the hardest, cleanest edge in the room.

## 13 - settling the wall
Another opaque pass of the cool wall color over the grey residue so the room goes quiet again.

## 14 - the spill
Let the outside come in: a warm trapezoid of light on the floorboards, widening toward us. The room is cold, but this patch is the warmth of the day lying on the boards.

## 15 - filling the spill
The patch of floor light was broken into fingers. Filled it into a single plane, ochre warmed toward orange.

## 16 - the one walking
Placed the small figure on the road, head breaking the horizon into the palest sky so it reads instantly, stride open, a case in the right hand. Small, but the only vertical out there, and upright.

## 17 - firming the walker
The figure was a cluster of dabs. Drew it with deliberate strokes: head, back, two legs mid-stride, an arm down to the case, so it is a person going, not a stain.

## 18 - the one staying
Laid in the second figure, seen from behind, standing just inside the threshold, large and dark against the gold: the weight of the picture. Straight back, a long coat, feet planted on the boards.

## 19 - solid
The figure fell apart into stripes; the field showed through and it looked like grass, not a body. Rebuilt it with a few broad, deliberate strokes: head, neck, shoulders, the long column of the coat.

## 20 - pressing the figure flat
The ridges of the field underneath glinted through the coat like rain. Pressed the whole silhouette flat with the knife in the same cool dark, so the figure is a single quiet mass.

## 21 - the hand
Raised the figure's right hand, open, held at head height: the only part of that body that goes toward the road. The body stays planted on the boards; the hand is already out in the light.

## 22 - rim of light
Put a thin warm edge on the side of the head, along the raised forearm and down the coat, the light from outside touching the one who stays. Rembrandt's economy: a few millimetres of gold are enough.

## 23 - marks on the frame
Scratched a ladder of short pale ticks up the inside of the right door post, uneven steps climbing from knee height to above the shoulder, the last one a long jump from the others. Anyone who has kept them will know them.

## 24 - the shadow
The light coming in throws the standing figure's shadow back across the warm boards toward us, long and dark. What goes out the door leaves this behind in the room.

## 25 - deeper shadow
The shadow was a smear; gave it full body so it reads as a true cast shadow, joined to the feet, cutting the warm floor in two.

## 26 - the sun ahead
Put a low sun just beside the road on the horizon, brushed outward from its centre, Turner's dissolving glare, so the walker heads into the brightest place in the painting.

## 27 - repair
The glaze ran wild: the sun's haze spread over both door posts and the walls lost their weight. Repainted the right post and wall and the left post opaque and cool; the marks on the frame will have to be cut again.

## 28 - walker again
Redrew the walker over the glare, a little darker so the figure stands cut out against the sun: small, upright, clear.

## 29 - both again
A second, opaque pass over the walker and the raised hand, both had gone ghostly under the sun. They must be the two clearest dark notes against the light.

## 30 - the marks, recut
Darkened the pale wedge on the post and cut the ladder of ticks again, less even than before, each a slightly different length, the topmost brightest and set apart.

## 31 - scraping the wall
Old box-shaped ridges on the right wall drew attention to nothing. Scraped them down with the knife so the wall is an unbroken dark field again.

## 32 - opaque again
The knife had dragged sky across the post. Waited for dry and laid opaque wall color over it with a clipped edge, keeping the post's edge straight against the light.

## 33 - pressing the wall
Pressed the right wall flat with a clean knife so the plaster stops catching light in streaks.

## 34 - one wall
A knifed rectangle stood out from the rest of the wall. Scumbled dry-brush strokes across its border and laid a cool blue-black veil over the whole right wall so it is one field, colder than the light it frames.

## 35 - mending sky and floor
The wall strokes had flown into the sky and across the patch of floor light. Patched the sky with pale rounds and re-laid the floor light, clipped to its shape.

## 36 - shadow restored
The re-laid floor light had bitten into the long shadow; restored it whole, one dark tongue from the feet toward us.

## 37 - marks louder
At three seconds the ticks on the post vanished. Re-stated them thicker, longer, in a warm bone colour, so the ladder reads from across the room and the last high mark is unmistakable.

## 38 - the small chair
In the shadow against the left wall, a little wooden chair, child-sized, only its right edges catching the warmth that comes in from the door. It belongs to the room and to before.

## 39 - too big
The chair was an adult's chair; it said nothing about smallness. Painted it out, wall above, boards below, to put a truly small one in its place.

## 40 - scraping the ghost
The old chair still showed in relief like a pentimento. Scraped it down.

## 41 - melting the patches
Dry-brushed wall and floor colour across the edges of the scraped patches so they sink back into the dark.

## 42 - a small chair
Put in the small chair, knee-high to the figure at the door, a low back with two rails, its right edges warmed by the light from outside. Little enough that no grown person sits on it any more.

## 43 - sinking the box
The scraped patch round the chair still showed as a box. Brushed across its edges at angles with half-loaded wall colour, left, top and right of the chair, leaving the chair itself alone.

## 44 - knife on the seams
Ran the knife along the seams themselves, flattening the ridges that drew the box.

## 45 - veil on the left
A cool veil over the whole left side of the room to quiet the scrapes and push that wall back into the cold, so only the chair's warm edges come forward.

## 46 - grey and the small rail
Caught the chair's edges again in warm ochre so the eye can find it in the dark, and put a little grey into the hair at the crown of the standing figure; years on that head, few on the one on the road.

## 47 - the head
The head was a smooth egg. Gave it hair, greyish, with a warm silver edge where the outside light touches it from the right.

## 48 - head, simpler
The grey ring made a helmet. Painted the head back to one dark mass, slightly broader, with a gathered knot of hair at the back, and pressed it flat.

## 49 - the road shines
Laid bright loaded strokes up the road behind the walker so the path itself glows, the way ahead lit. And darkened the stray pale strip on the upper left post so the door holds its shape.

## 50 - a shadow that comes back
With the sun ahead of the walker, the walker's shadow falls back down the road toward the house. Laid it in, thin and long: the body goes, but something of it still stretches toward the door.

## 51 - firming the shadow, edge of sun
Made the long shadow more certain and put a hair of gold down the walker's sunward side, so the young one is touched by the light they are walking into.

## 52 - quieting the top
A cool dark veil over the lintel and the upper right wall where old scrapes still flickered. The room should be one held breath around the bright opening.

## 53 - last accents
Brought the highest marks on the post back up out of the veil, the top one brightest, and closed a small pale chip at the top of the left post. Anything more would be explaining. Stopping.
