# The Plum Crate at Noon

**Commission:** theme "At peace", in ukiyo-e, as a woodblock print on washi (round 18). Made by Claude Opus 5.5 with studio v8, after the engine and subject library upgrade: the subject library (each subject researched as it really is, then as ukiyo-e renders it, before drawing), the carved key line that swells and tapers, layers with roles, and the finishing check. A first attempt at this commission was cancelled before the upgrade; this one started from scratch.

**Teodor's feeling** (in his words, written before he printed; the full text with the nearest wrong feelings and looks is in `feeling_teodor.md`):

> **Let go in the middle of everything: a full, warm surrender, safe inside the noise.**
>
> Peace, to me, is not a quiet room or an empty shore: it is the moment a body stops guarding itself while the world goes on loudly around it, the market still shouting and feet still passing, and it doesn't matter, because the noise has become the thing that holds you.

**Teodor, on finishing:**

> The proof of the second state showed faults that a transparent film can't cover. The pigeons were cut like frying pans, the vermilion was shouting over the peace, and the passer-by was swallowing his boot. So I did what a printmaker does: I recut the blocks and pulled a third sheet. The stripes are now a bengara brick under the warm tint. Each pigeon is one closed outline, one pecking at the peel and one standing. The woman is cut clean at the edge with a quiet bag, and the orange under his fingers, printed twice, is the one jewel of colour. His pale, smiling, sleeping face lies on the deepest blue in the print. The noise of the market bends around him and the warm band of the street holds him up, which is the surrender I meant from the start.

**Three states:** the first proof and the second state are kept in `states/`. A printmaker can't scrape a print, so the blocks were recut and a new sheet printed each time.

**Research:** the ukiyo-e style doc and the Edo genre of "enjoying the evening cool" prints, in which ordinary people rest in public; the emotion research on posture (head tipped back and a body given to its support reads as rest, a forward-dropped head as sadness); and the ukiyo-e layer-2 pages for faces, figures, hands, hair, clothing, crowds, birds, fruit, objects, ground, light and space (`docs/styles/subjects/ukiyo-e--*.md`), which flagged where the picture had to extend the tradition (no print looks straight down; there are no fruit crates or pigeons in the corpus).
