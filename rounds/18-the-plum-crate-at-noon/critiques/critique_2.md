# Critique 2 (a fresh critic; knew only the commission "a public exhibition on a theme. The theme is: At peace"; read crit_2.png)

**1) First three seconds:** Warm and a little funny. It feels like a stolen nap in the middle of a busy day, a small private calm.

**2) What causes it:** The closed eyes, the slight smile and the head tipped against the plum crate. The hands rest loosely on a half-peeled orange. The figure lies on a diagonal while the stall stays upright and orderly, so he reads as someone who has let go inside a working place. The pigeons pecking at dropped peel add a quiet tenderness: the world keeps going, and nobody needs him for a moment. The mixed warm and cool palette (salmon awning, soft blue shirt, sage apples) is gentle, not loud.

**3) Weaknesses:**
- **Composition:** The steep top-down tilt puts everything on a strong diagonal and sets up a fight between the awning stripes and the paving, both of which are busy. The red-and-cream stripes are the loudest thing in the picture and pull the eye away from the face. The green figure cut off at the right edge, with its yellow bag, is an awkward fragment. It adds a second story (someone waiting for service?) that undercuts the peace and crowds the corner. The pigeons sit in a dead corner and feel pasted on.
- **Values:** Mostly mid-light and flat. Only the black hair, the shoes and the outlines carry darks, so nothing pulls the face forward. The face is the same pale value as the paving and the stall boards.
- **Edges:** The outlines are uniformly hard everywhere. There is no lost-and-found edge, so the stripes, the crates and the figure all sit on one plane. The doubled, offset contour on the shirt collar and shoulder reads as a registration error, not as intent.
- **Drawing:** The body has a scale problem. The legs are very long and the right leg vanishes into the green figure. The seated pose doesn't hold up: is he sitting on the pink crate or leaning on the stall? The hip and crate don't connect. The hands are rubbery, and the left forearm comes out of nowhere. The pigeons are flat, generic, frying-pan-shaped bodies with stuck-on heads. The apples are stamped repeats and lack variety.
- **Gesture:** The head tilt is good and carries the whole feeling. The rest of the body is stiff, more propped than slumped. A truly dozing body would sag more through the shoulders and knees.
- **Color:** Pleasant, but it has no temperature focus. The orange in his hand should be the warm jewel of the picture, but the stripes outshout it. The paper-texture overlay is uniform and a bit mechanical.
- **Finish:** Clean, illustrative and close to a comic panel. It is consistent, but the flat fills with texture on top look more like a print than a painting.

**4) The one change:** Calm the awning. Knock the red stripes down in value and saturation (or soften and lose them toward the edges) and cut out the green figure, so the dozing face and the orange become the clearest, warmest contrast in the picture. Right now the stripes are louder than the peace.

**Studio manager's note:**
- **Closer:** "a stolen nap in the middle of a busy day, a small private calm" is very near your feeling. "Warm and a little funny" is the remaining gap; the comedy comes from the propped, stiff body and the pasted-on details, not from the idea.
- **The stripes are the loudest thing.** The critic and your report agree: strongest colour 45 against a target of 25. You found a transparent overprint greys the cream, and the plank logic refused a tint on the red alone. Two honest printmaker answers:
  1. A tint block clipped to the red stripes only, using the new `segment` region picked from the schema, if the plank rule allows a separate block.
  2. A third state with a duller, darker red block (a brick or bengara red rather than vermilion), as you recut for state 2.
  
  Either way, the orange must be the warmest, most saturated note.
- **Don't follow the critic on "print rather than painting"** or on lost edges: it is a woodblock print, and that look is right. The collar's doubled contour is a different matter. It reads as a registration error; fix it, or own it consciously as one, but not by accident.
- **The pigeons (third time):** they still read as frying pans with stuck-on heads. Draw them from the animals layer 1 page, not from ovals:
  - a small head on a short neck lowered to peck;
  - a plump, egg-shaped body narrowing to the tail;
  - folded wing tips crossing over the tail, and a fanned tail end;
  - dark wing bars;
  - a small iridescent neck patch;
  - legs under the body's centre.
  
  And no inner outline ring.
- **The seat and the slump:** make the hip visibly on the crate (the crate's top edge meeting the seat of the trousers) and let the shoulders sag. The near leg must not vanish into the passer-by.
- **The passer-by:** keep the bustle if it matters to your feeling, but as a quieter, cleaner fragment: lower chroma (no yellow bag shouting), not touching his leg, cut cleanly by the frame. Or drop it if the pigeons and the dropped peel carry the world going on around him.
- **Hands:** rubbery fingers. Give them knuckles and a clear thumb, as in the ukiyo-e hands page: small, outlined, fingers curled.
