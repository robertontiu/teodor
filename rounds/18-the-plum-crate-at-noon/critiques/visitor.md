# Visitor (a fresh critic; knew only the commission "a public exhibition on a theme. The theme is: At peace"; read final.png and the contact sheet past_work.jpg)

**1) What I feel in the first three seconds:** It's warm and a bit funny. A man has nodded off at work on a sunny afternoon. I smiled before I thought about it.

**2) What makes me feel that:**
- **His face carries it.** The eyes are shut, the mouth has a small private smile, and the head leans back against the crate of plums.
- **The hands.** One hand rests lightly on a half-peeled orange on his lap. The peel bits on the pavement and the pigeons working through them say time has passed and nobody needs him.
- **A passer-by at the right edge** with leeks in a bag is busy while he isn't. That contrast is the joke, and the peace.
- **The surface helps.** Flat, quiet, paper-toned colour, a woodblock-style outline, and the paper grain soften everything.

**3) Weaknesses:**
- **Composition:** The whole scene is tilted about 35°, so the picture feels as if it is sliding off to the lower left. The red-and-cream striped stall front is the loudest thing in the picture: large, high-contrast, strongly diagonal, all movement. It fights the theme. The passer-by is cut by the frame into an awkward sliver, sitting on the edge rather than clearly in or out. The bottom-right corner is crowded while the upper-right pavement is empty.
- **Values:** Almost everything is a light-to-mid value. The only darks are the hair, the shoes and the plum crate, so there is no clear value path to the face. The face is pale on a pale collar against a pale crate, and it gets lost.
- **Space and drawing:** It's unclear whether he is sitting on the pink crate or lying back against the stall. The crate straddles the stripes and the pavement, and there is no cast shadow or base line where the stall meets the ground, so he floats. The legs read as standing straight rather than resting. The arms tangle at the belly, and the near forearm and hand are oversized and flat. The fruit is stamped in identical circles, which looks mechanical next to the hand-drawn figure. The upper pigeon lies flat on its belly and could read as dead.
- **Edges:** The outline has the same weight everywhere, with no swelling or tapering. The short interior strokes in the shirt and trousers look scattered rather than describing folds.
- **Colour:** The palette is pleasant and restrained, and the orange is a good focal accent. But the red stripes repeat the orange's warmth at a much bigger scale and steal its job. The orange glow along the bottom edge looks like a digital gradient and doesn't match the print idiom.
- **Finish:** The paper texture and the TEODOR seal are handsome. The rest is neat but a little sterile, especially the crates.

**4) The one change that would help most:** Quiet the stripes. Lower their contrast or darken the stall front into shadow. That would make the striped area a calm backdrop, let the face and the orange become the focus, and calm the picture to match its theme. A close second: put a cast shadow under the crate and the man so he sits on the ground.

**5) New departure or more of the same?** Clearly a new departure in medium and mood. It's flat, outlined, print-like and high-key daylight. The earlier work is all painterly brushwork and mostly dark silhouettes in doorways and windows, plus a few heated abstractions. It is also the artist's first real everyday genre scene with gentle humour.

It still repeats several habits:
- The tilted, top-down viewpoint with the figure rotated across the frame, as in 13, 14 and 14b.
- A simplified, mask-like face with closed eyes and a fixed smile, like the old couple in 15 and the swimmer in 16.
- One warm glowing accent as the emotional key: the orange here, the lit doorways and windows before.
- Weak, generic hands.
- A secondary figure pushed to the edge of the frame.
