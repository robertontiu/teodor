# Critique 1 (a fresh critic; knew only the commission "a public exhibition on a theme. The theme is: At peace"; read crit_1.png)

1) First three seconds: mild amusement and quiet calm. A man has drifted off at work and he's content. It reads more as "gentle comic" than "at peace". Something also looks a little off, because he seems to be tilting or sliding rather than resting.

2) Why:
- Closed eyes and a small smile on a pale, smooth face. The arms are folded around a half-peeled orange, so the hands are calm and busy at once. That's the strongest note of peace.
- The steep top-down view and the strong diagonal (crate stall, gutter, his body) set up a sense of falling or sliding. That works against rest.
- The orange peel on the floor and the pigeon going for it are a nice quiet narrative: the world carries on around a still man.
- Cut-off passersby' legs at the right edge give "stillness amid bustle". It works, but they also crowd him.

3) Weaknesses:
- Composition: the diagonal is too dominant and too even. Crates, blue band, gutter and figure all run parallel, so the eye slides off the lower right. The figure sits dead centre on that diagonal. The bottom-right legs and the unresolved grey shape in the corner are clutter that pulls attention to the edge.
- Pose and gesture: the body doesn't read as leaning, sitting or lying. The legs float in a long rigid line, the feet don't plant, and the small pink stool or triangle under him is unreadable. With no weight and no support he can't look relaxed. The figure is also very stiff, with tubular arms and a board-like torso.
- Drawing: the head and neck are awkward. The neck is a pale cylinder with a collar ring, and the ear and jaw sit oddly. The pigeons are flat, decoy-like, lack weight and are oddly scaled, with plate-like bodies.
- Values: nearly everything is mid-value with a uniform black outline. There's no light-dark structure to focus the face. The darkest masses (hair, shoes, blue band) compete with the face instead of framing it. The warm orange glow at the bottom edge is a good idea but it is disconnected from any light source.
- Edges: every edge is the same hard, even contour line. Nothing is lost or softened, so nothing recedes and nothing comes forward. The mechanical repetition of the crates and tile grid adds to the flatness.
- Colour: the fruit crates are the most saturated and busiest area, in the top-left corner, far from the subject. The figure's blues merge into the blue gutter band. The green trousers are dull. The palette is pleasant but the hierarchy runs the wrong way.
- Finish: the paper texture is applied evenly. The fruit is stamped and repetitive. The grey shape at the bottom right corner reads as unfinished.

4) The one change: give him real weight and support. Seat him solidly against the crate stall, with his back on it, knees bent and feet flat on the floor, on a readable crate or stool. Then make the face and hands the lightest, warmest, most resolved area, with the crates quieted down around them. A body clearly at rest, rather than sliding down a diagonal, turns the image from "dozing comic" into "at peace".

**Studio manager's note:**
- **The feeling half-lands.** The reading was calm and contentment, but "gentle comic", plus a sense of sliding. Your nearest wrong feelings (exhaustion, loneliness, collapse) were not read. The miss is new: comedy, and a body with no weight.
- **The body is the problem, and you named it yourself:** an upright torso, legs as straight pipes, no readable seat. A sleeper who is safe gives his weight to a support. Make the support unmistakable: his back against the crate wall, the upturned crate under him drawn as a box, his knees bent, his boots planted, and his head resting on a crate edge. Use the figure rig (`sit`, `lean` and a slack spine) for the tonal reference, then redraw the key line from it. One long slack S-curve, as your plan said, not a straight board.
- **Follow the critic on hierarchy, not on edges.**
  - **Edges:** ukiyo-e doesn't use lost or soft edges or modelled light. Keep the carved line, but let it vary by part, as you set up (finest at the face and hands).
  - **Hierarchy:** the crates are the loudest, most saturated and busiest area, far from the face. Ukiyo-e loves pattern, but give it a hierarchy. Quiet the crates near the focus (paler fruit blocks, or fewer distinct fruits where they meet his head), and keep the strongest colour accent (the orange) in his hands.
  - **Separation:** his blue shirt and apron melt into the blue gutter band. Separate them by value or hue.
- **The pigeons read as decoys or ducks.** Use the layer-2 rules: 2–4 flat tones by feather group, dark wing-bar scallops, an iridescent neck as a small patch, a head lowered below the back line when pecking, and real pigeon proportions (small head, plump body).
- **The pavement:** the faint light diagonal streaks across it read as cast light or a smeared texture. Ukiyo-e has no cast light. If they're paper texture or a stray block, remove or even them out.
- **The bottom-right corner:** the grey shape is unreadable. Either resolve it as a cut-off passer's bag or shoe, or clear it.
- **The report's "one role per passage" flag:** you're right not to fake layers for it. Flat single impressions are how woodblock works, and I'll fix the flag in the engine. Answer it only with real print layers (overprints, bokashi, embossing).
