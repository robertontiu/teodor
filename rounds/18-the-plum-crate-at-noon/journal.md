# Journal: commission 18, "At peace" (ukiyo-e, woodblock on washi)

## Phase 1, planning (no marks on the sheet)

P1 | research | read ukiyo-e.md in full, emotion.md (cue hierarchy, "Feeling -> visual cues", "Nearest wrong feelings"), the woodblock page and the manual. Web: the Edo genre of *yūsuzumi* / "enjoying the evening cool" prints (ukiyo-e.org listings; Smithsonian, MAK and V&A records), in which ordinary people at leisure are a full subject of the prints; the Met essay "The Nabis and Decorative Painting" (a current I have not used: Intimism). The National Diet Library's theme page on *nōryō* could not be fetched (https://www.ndl.go.jp/en/imagebank/theme/noryo), so I take nothing from it.

What I am taking from the research:
1. **Ordinary leisure as the subject, in its own present day.** The prints made "enjoying the cool" (people resting, fanning, sitting out) a subject of its own. So: no old Japan, but a contemporary market and a working man taking his nap at the stall. That is the ukiyo-e attitude without the postcard.
2. **Figure and pattern woven into one surface (Nabis, after the prints).** Shallow space, flat colour, bold pattern, the figure merging into its patterned surroundings, the interior as a refuge. My sleeper's patterned shirt rhymes with the fruit grid behind him, so the market "holds" him; the high viewpoint flattens the space into one tapestry.
3. **The cue that carries the feeling is posture first** (emotion.md): head tipped *back*, chest open, limbs long and given to the support (not the sadness posture of head and chest forward); the face visible from the front (here from above), with the whole face agreeing (smooth brow, soft cheeks, the corners of the mouth up). Then value: high key, paper the lightest, light near the head (light = good, up = good). Low arousal blurs (serenity / exhaustion / sadness), so lightness, warmth and the posture must separate them. Edge density is arousal: many hard edges and diagonals around him, few, long and curved in his figure.

P2 | feeling | chose: "Let go in the middle of everything: a full, warm surrender, safe inside the noise" (feeling.md). The poster version of "at peace" is an empty shore or a still pond; that is also my own hushed habit. Truer for me: a body that stops guarding itself while the world goes on loudly around it.

The value design: dark crate mouths and apron, middle fruit and trousers, light paper pavement and the lightest value of all on his face.

**Chosen: A**, borrowing from B: the fruit carries warmth and saturation at the edges of the picture (the busy, aroused half), while the sleeper is kept pale and low in chroma (pale blue shirt, paper-light face): the calm half is quieter than its surround. B was beautiful but its blue key leans to coolness and melancholy (my habit); C's olive wall went heavy and sour.
Problems the studies showed, to fix in the schema: the face came out as a cartoon smiley (round head, blush ovals, a drawn smile: the "anime-cute" pitfall). It must become a typified adult face in a fine, swelling key line, elongated, the cheeks and mouth corners carrying the smile, no blush ovals; the expression from a tonal/line study first. The lower-left triangle of pavement is a big empty block: give it the cobble pattern in key line, a curl of orange peel and two or three pigeons pecking near his boots (unafraid: the world is unbothered too). The passers-by are too small and stiff: larger, cut harder by the edge, one foot clearly lifted over his crossed boots so the crowd's path visibly bends round him.

P5 | habits broken (on purpose, at least three):
1. **The lone figure from behind, faceless:** his face is turned fully up to us, the largest and finest thing in the picture, and he is not alone (passers-by and pigeons share the space).
2. **Eye-level, middle-distance, centred:** a bird's-eye view, the figure close and on a hard diagonal, the awning, crates and passers-by cut by the frame.
3. **One warm light in a dark room / dusk:** flat midday daylight with no light source, no glow, no threshold; high key with the paper as the lightest value.
4. **The hushed melancholy, bittersweet feeling:** a single warm feeling, in a loud, colourful, busy place.
5. **A big empty block:** every field carries pattern (cobbles, fruit, shirt, awning stripes) and a few incidents.
6. **The signature in the bottom-right corner:** it will be cut into the keyblock where the picture wants it (planned: left edge, in the crate wall).
Not broken, and watched: warm against cool (here vermilion, orpiment and minium against Prussian and indigo, not amber against violet, but still warm/cool).

What the research changes in my plan, weighed:
- **The view.** No ukiyo-e print looks straight down; my plan said "bird's-eye". I build it instead as an *extension of the high oblique view*, and say so: an oblique parallel projection with two ground axes (u along the stall front, up-right; v into the street, down-right, the one dominant slant), verticals kept vertical, every receding edge parallel, no convergence and no texture gradient (the setts keep one size everywhere). It is steeper than any print I know, so it stays an extension, not a convention. The research is right and my plan was wrong: a true plan view would also have laid him out flat like a body on a slab (my nearest wrong feeling, death). The oblique view keeps him *sitting*.
- **The face.** The colour studies' cartoon smiley is gone. Built in the head's own coordinates, turned three-quarter toward his left and tipped back (crown up-left, -32 deg), resting on the stall cloth: closed eyes as relaxed downward-sagging arcs with a short crease, a crow's-foot tick, short level brows, a hooked nose, one nose-to-mouth line for his age, a slack mouth whose corner ticks up by a hair. No raised cheeks, no blush ovals; the tip back is told by the long throat line and the head lying against the cloth. Stubble as a flat pale blue-grey tint on the jaw (the commoner's convention, Met 55222, 36922).
- **Crates and pigeons.** Ukiyo-e has neither. Crates as boxes in parallel oblique projection with ruled slat lines and fruit as repeated flat rounds in overlapping rows, the gaps a flat darker tone; pigeons as 2-4 flat tones (grey body, dark head and tail, a flat green neck patch, two wing bars, pink legs), dot eye with no highlight, enlarged toward true scale (a pigeon is longer than a head).
- **No cast shadows; bokashi only in bands.** Agreed. The plan's one bokashi (light Prussian along the bottom edge of the pavement) stays the only one; there is no shadow under him, the crates or the birds.
- **What I changed on my own, from looking:** I dropped the striped awning. In this oblique geometry an awning over his stall would hide the fruit, and one in the top-right corner would need a third axis; the cut-off passer-by's coat and swinging bag take that corner instead. And I raised the stall cloth so the pale face lies on the darkest flat field in the picture (indigo), which is where the strongest contrast must be.
