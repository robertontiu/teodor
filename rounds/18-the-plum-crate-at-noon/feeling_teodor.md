# Feeling

**Let go in the middle of everything: a full, warm surrender, safe inside the noise.**

Peace, to me, is not a quiet room or an empty shore: it is the moment a body stops guarding itself while the world goes on loudly around it, the market still shouting and feet still passing, and it doesn't matter, because the noise has become the thing that holds you.

Two things held at once, and the second must have its own form: the **slack, sun-warm surrender** of one sleeping body (the loud half, carried by the posture and the face), and the **busy world that bends around it without waking it** (the passers-by stepping round his feet, the colour and pattern pressing in, all diagonal and moving, while he alone is round and still).

## The nearest wrong feelings, and what separates mine from them

- **Exhaustion / weariness** (a worn-out worker, collapsed): low arousal and a body at rest read alike. Separated by the posture: head tipped *back and up*, not dropped forward (forward head-and-chest is the sadness posture, Coulson 2004); chest open, hands loosely folded on the belly, legs stretched long and crossed at the ankle, the whole body *given* to the support rather than fallen. The face softly smiling at the corners, cheeks full, brow smooth. High key, warm saturated fruit colour around him, light on the face (light = positive, Meier et al. 2004).
- **Loneliness / isolation in a crowd**: one still figure among anonymous passing legs can read as the outsider. Separated by the link: the passers-by visibly *step around* his outstretched feet (one foot lifted over, a path bending), so the crowd accommodates him; he is part of the stall and the stall's colour runs through his shirt; nothing about him looks out of place or left behind.
- **Death / collapse** (a body lying in the street): a still body with closed eyes, seen from above. Separated by sitting upright on his stool, propped comfortably, one hand still loosely holding something (a half-peeled fruit), the small smile, the passers-by unconcerned, and no prone or crumpled limbs.

## The nearest wrong looks

- **The ukiyo-e postcard:** a kimono, a geisha, cherry blossom, a mountain, a wave, a lantern-lit street "of old Japan". None of that: a contemporary market, plastic crates, an ordinary man in a work shirt and an apron, painted in the grammar of the prints, the way the prints painted their own present day.
- **Pastiche of a known design:** no copied bijin pose, no famous bridge or rain scene. No fake seals, no Japanese writing.
- **Vector clip-art:** flat fields printed as perfect fills, an even wire line. The blocks must show grain and the key line must breathe (fine at the face, heavy at the drapery).
- **Bokashi as airbrush:** one graded band only (the cobbles fading into paper light, or the awning), not everywhere.
- **My own habits:** a lone figure from behind in the middle distance; eye-level centred staging; a warm light against a dark room; amber against violet; a hushed melancholy mood; a big empty block of one colour; a mask face that could be grimace or smile; old beside young; bottom-right signature.
