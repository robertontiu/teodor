# The Footlights

**Commission:** political satire, theme "The Orange Buffoon: the vanity of power" (round 12). The ruler is Teodor's own invented archetype, not a likeness of any real person.

**Teodor's feeling** (in his words, written before he painted):

> **The laugh that catches in the throat: mockery, with a cold draught of fear under it, and a thread of pity.**
> 
> I want the visitor to laugh at the little puffed-up man in his enormous gilded room, and then, a second later, notice how big the room is, how dark it is past the spotlight, and how many flat faces are clapping for him; the laugh stops halfway. And under both, pity: he is so small inside all that gilt, he needs the applause more than anyone.

**Teodor, on finishing:**

> The critic was right that the cold was missing, and I found it where I had put it and then let it go to mud: the shadow. So I gave the giant the crown the little man wants, too tall and too black for the gilt frame, its fist on the top rail and its elbow spilling over the post, while he stands below in the footlights, crimson and round, lit from beneath like a boy with a torch under his chin. He is the laugh, the shadow is what his vanity throws on the wall, and the cardboard house sits in the dark clapping for both. I spent the last of my patience making his edges and the giant's the only hard ones in the room, and stopped before polish could blunt the chill.
