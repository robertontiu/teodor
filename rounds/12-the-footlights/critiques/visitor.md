# Visitor (a fresh critic; knew only the commission "The Orange Buffoon: the vanity of power", satire; read final.png)

**1) First three seconds:** It's a theatrical gag. You see a small man and his huge ego, and I felt a warm, mild amusement more than any satirical bite.

**2) What causes it:**
- A squat, round figure in a red robe with a toy crown strikes a fist-up pose.
- Behind him his shadow on the gilded frame is enormous, crowned and imperial.
- The scale gap is the joke, and it reads right away.
- The spotlit stage and the silhouetted audience frame him as a performer, so his vanity becomes a show.

**3) Weaknesses:**
- **Composition:** Everything is centered and symmetric. The huge frame and stage are mostly empty texture, and the audience band at the bottom is a dark, inert third of the canvas.
- **Values:** Muddy mid-browns everywhere. The only real contrast is the shadow against the glow, and the figure itself barely separates from the warm light behind it.
- **Edges:** One blocky, dabbed stroke is used everywhere, so the frame, wall and floor all dissolve into the same mush. The shadow's edges are soft and lumpy where they should be crisp and graphic.
- **Drawing:** The figure is crude, a tube with stub arms. The shadow doesn't match its caster: different arm angle, different crown shape, and no believable light source. The audience heads are stamped clones.
- **Gesture:** The man is stiff and seen from behind, so there's no face and no expression of vanity. The shadow has more personality than he does.
- **Color:** Nothing on him is orange. Without the "orange buffoon" note, the theme gets lost and he reads as a generic king.
- **Finish:** Smeary and unresolved, with a digital-filter look.

**4) The one change:** Turn him three-quarters toward us and paint his face: a smug, preening orange profile caught in the spotlight, drawn with sharp edges. That gives the satire a target and a focal point, and the shadow joke still works.
