# Critique 1 (a fresh critic; knew only the commission "The Orange Buffoon: the vanity of power", satire; read crit_1.png)

**1) First three seconds:** Quiet, theatrical and a little ominous. It's a solemn figure in a spotlight, not a buffoon. I feel hushed expectation, not mockery.

**2) Why:** The heavy dark brown field, the single warm cone of light, and the rows of silhouetted heads all point at a small red figure on a lit stage. The hard yellow frame/gallows shape behind it adds a stern, almost ceremonial feeling. Nothing reads as vain or ridiculous.

**3) Weaknesses:**
- **Composition:** The giant yellow "U" or bracket takes over the picture. It's the brightest, biggest shape, and it reads as an unexplained structure (a gallows? a frame?). The figure is small and stuck where the bar crosses, so its head merges with the bar. The top half is mostly empty brown.
- **Values:** The figure's red sits in the midtones against a midtone background, so it doesn't pop. The stage lip and the bar are lighter than the subject.
- **Edges:** Everything uses the same choppy square-brush edge, so nothing is sharp where it should be (face, hat brim) and nothing softly dissolves.
- **Drawing/gesture:** The figure is an unreadable stack (cone hat, red ball, robe) with no face, hands or pose. There's no swagger, preening or bulk. The audience heads are stamped, repetitive outlines.
- **Color:** The theme says orange, and the subject is pink-red. Orange only appears in the background, so the colour story is backwards.
- **Finish:** It reads as a uniform filter texture rather than deliberate brushwork.

**4) The one change:** Make the figure the picture. Enlarge it to about a third of the height, paint it in a saturated orange with the lightest values on the face, and give it a readable vain gesture (chin up, chest out, admiring itself, maybe in a mirror or a gilded frame instead of the bar). Push the yellow bar back into the dark.

(Studio manager's note: the critic's craft points stand: the figure must become the picture, larger, with the lightest values and a readable vain gesture, and the frame must stop reading as a gallows. But the brief's rule stands too: do NOT paint the ruler's skin or face orange, and no yellow hair. Let the orange live in what surrounds and clothes his vanity: gilt, the glare of the light on him, an orange-gold costume or sash if you wish, never his skin. Weigh the critic's feeling against yours: they felt solemn expectation, not the laugh.)
