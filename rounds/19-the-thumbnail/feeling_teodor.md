# Feeling (round 19, Man and the Moon)

**Sly delight: a grown man putting out the moon with his thumb.**

Lying on his back in the afternoon grass, one eye screwed shut, arm straight up, he has hidden the whole moon behind a thumbnail, and he is pleased with himself the way a boy is: a private, almost smug pleasure in a game nobody outgrows. Underneath it, quieter, sits the size of the thing he is playing with: from where we stand the moon is not gone at all, it peeks out beside his thumb, white and hard, in a sky that goes on and on. The loud half is the grin; the quiet half is the moon that does not care.

## The nearest wrong feelings (and what separates mine)

1. **Rest, serenity, a lazy afternoon** (a man lying in grass is the "low arousal" picture, and low-arousal feelings blur together). Separated by effort and play in the body: the arm straight and taut, the hand held exactly, one eye clenched hard so the cheek bunches into crow's feet on that side, the other eye open and aiming; the mouth closed but pulled up at the corner. Nothing in him is slack.
2. **A "thumbs up", generic cheerfulness or approval.** A raised thumb seen from the side is the thumbs-up sign. Separated by the line of sight: the thumb's nail faces his eye, the arm points at the moon along the line from his open eye, the moon sits right at the edge of the thumbnail, and the shut eye says "sighting", not "signalling".
3. **Wonder or awe** (mouth open, eyes wide, overwhelmed). Separated by the closed eye and the closed-mouth smile: he is not overwhelmed, he is playing with it. The awe belongs to the viewer, who sees the moon he thinks he has hidden; it gets its own form in the deep sky and the moon's hard white edge, not in his face.

## The nearest wrong looks

- **The postcard of this current:** a giant moon disc with every crater rendered, a technical showpiece with no one in it; or pores and stubble rendered for their own sake ("look how real"), clinical and cold; the airbrushed plastic skin of overblended graphite.
- **The postcard of the theme:** a Romantic moon-gazer seen from behind; an astronaut; a silhouette against a huge rising moon; a werewolf night.
- **My own habits that could creep back:** a small lone figure in a field pulled to the centre; a big empty grey block of sky; the raised arm as the only gesture (here it must be a precise hand, not a fling); a mask face with a fixed smile; a hushed, melancholy dusk (this is a bright afternoon); a centred, polite eye-level view.
