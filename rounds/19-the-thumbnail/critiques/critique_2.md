# Critique 2 (a fresh critic; knew only the commission "a public exhibition on a theme. The theme is: Man and the Moon"; read crit_2.png, an unfinished state)

**1) First three seconds:** Quiet, lonely longing, with something slightly uncanny in it. Right away I also feel a dreamy, photographic blur, more than I feel a drawing.

**2) What makes me feel that, and who the person is:**
- **What does it:** A huge, soft, empty grey sky fills about two thirds of the sheet. The Moon is only a tiny bright dot high on the right. A single pale arm rises from the bottom toward it, against a tilted dark horizon band. The distance between the hand and the dot carries the whole emotion, and that part works.
- **The person:** Someone lies on their back, seen from behind and above the head. The face is upside down and foreshortened at lower left: the brow and eye socket are visible, and the hair may be pulled back or braided. I can't confidently tell the sex. I read them as young, maybe a teenager to 30. They are reaching or pointing up at the Moon. The expression is unreadable: the face is a smudge with one dark eye socket, so I can't tell wonder from sadness.

**3) Weaknesses, frankly:**
- **Composition:** The basic idea is good (big sky, small Moon, arm as a bridge). But the bottom third is a muddle. The head, the shoulder and the blurred mass on the right merge, and the arm comes out of nowhere. The tilted horizon cuts the arm at the elbow and fights it as a second strong diagonal. The Moon is so small and near the edge that it reads as a star or a speck of dust.
- **Values:** Nearly everything sits in a narrow band of middle greys. There is no real dark anywhere, so nothing anchors the image. The arm and hand are the lightest things on the page and beat the Moon. The Moon should be the brightest note.
- **Edges:** This is the worst problem. There are hard rectangular seams in the lower right, around x=720–1060, y=1340–1440 in the displayed image. They are straight-edged patches of different tone and texture that look pasted or masked, not drawn. The sleeve ends in a perfectly sharp cylinder at the wrist, like a mannequin joint. The face and right side are airbrush-soft while the hand is crisp, so the focus feels random.
- **Drawing:** The hand reads as plastic and doll-like. Its gesture looks like a hitchhiker's thumb or a "thumbs-up", not a reach or a point. The forearm is a tube with no wrist, ulna or tendons. The head's construction, upside down and foreshortened, is unclear. I can't place the nose, mouth or jaw.
- **Gesture:** The arm is stiff and vertical, so the yearning is stated rather than felt. Nothing in the body (shoulder lift, turned head, open fingers) follows through on the reach.
- **Finish / medium:** It doesn't read as graphite. There is no visible stroke direction or paper grain, and no line. It looks like a blurred photograph with stippled noise at the edges of shapes. In a show about drawing, that is a credibility problem.

**4) The one change:** Redraw the lower third as a drawing. Get rid of the rectangular patches and build the head, shoulder and arm as one connected, lit form, with real graphite hatching and one or two true darks (hair, eye socket, shadow under the jaw). Then make the face readable enough to carry an expression. Close behind that: open the hand into an unmistakable reach toward the Moon, and make the Moon a little larger and the brightest value on the sheet.

**Studio manager's note** (the owner is away; finish the drawing in this phase):
- **The reading is "lonely longing", the second time a wrong one.** The "sly delight" lives entirely in the clenched eye and the closed grin, and the face still reads as "a smudge with one dark eye socket", the sex uncertain, "teenager to 30". With one phase left, the face is the priority above everything except the seams. Make it a man in his forties who is plainly squinting one eye shut and grinning:
  - darks at the open eye, under the jaw and in the stubble zone;
  - the bunched cheek lit under the shut eye, with its crease line (not a second open eye);
  - the mouth corner pulled up.
  
  Check `look thumb` after every face episode. If after honest work it still reads as longing, record that plainly.
- **Don't follow the critic on these:**
  - **Moon:** don't enlarge it or make it the brightest note. Your research is clear: true size, darker than lit skin.
  - **Hand:** don't open it into a reach. The thumb is the idea.
  
  But do make the moon unmistakably the Moon, not dust: round, with its lit limb crisp.
- **The rectangular seams (lower right, around canvas x 1150–1700, y 2300–2400 at full size) are the worst defect.** Paint them out and re-blend across their edges, from the schema, as soft out-of-focus cloth. They read as pasted, which breaks rule 2's spirit even though it's strokes.
- **The wrist:** the sleeve ends as a perfect cylinder. Give the cuff an irregular edge and let the wrist emerge from it (ulna bump, tendon).
- **Graphite character:** in the in-focus passages only (fist, sleeve, sharp blades, the moon), let the pencil show (directional strokes, crisp edges, paper grain). Elsewhere the blur is right for the style, but add one or two true darks (carbon to your floor) to anchor the lower third: hair, eye socket, under the jaw.
