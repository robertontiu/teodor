# Critique 1 (a fresh critic; knew only the commission "a public exhibition on a theme. The theme is: Man and the Moon"; read crit_1.png, an unfinished state)

**1) Feeling in the first three seconds:** a foggy, dreamlike loneliness. Someone reaching or hitching a ride toward something very far away. It is a little uncanny, and it reads more like a dream than a drawing.

**2) What causes it, and who the person is:**
- **The arm and hand.** A single arm rises diagonally from the bottom edge into a heavy grey sky. The hand is the brightest thing in the picture and makes a hitchhiker's "thumb out" gesture.
- **The moon.** It is only a tiny white dot, upper right, very far from the hand. That gap is the emotional idea, and it's a good one.
- **The light.** Low, soft mist across a dark horizon band with a few white grass blades gives the dusk-and-silence mood.
- **The person.** There is a huge, blurred close-up of a face at lower left: one eye open and looking up and out. A pale textured disc covers half the face, like a visor, lens or moon reflection. It reads as an adult man, perhaps 30 to 50, though the sex and age are not certain. He is lying or leaning back and holding his thumb up at the moon, as if asking it for a ride.
- **The pale mass at right.** It could be a crouching body, a torso, or legs, or even a horse. I can't tell which.

**3) Weaknesses, frankly:**
- **The hand (finish and drawing).** It is a flat, pure-white cutout with a jagged, polygon-like outline and no tone inside. It looks pasted on rather than drawn, and it is the focal point. The thumb and fingers have no structure, foreshortening or shadow side.
- **The arm (drawing).** It is a uniform tube with a speckled texture. There is no wrist, elbow or muscle, and it ends at the top in a sawn-off cylinder below the hand, like a pipe with a glove on it.
- **Overall finish.** Almost everything is airbrush-soft. I see no graphite character: no line, no hatching, no direction of stroke. It reads as digital smudge rather than pencil.
- **Values.** Nearly the whole sheet sits in a narrow mid-grey range. The only real accents are the white hand and the dot moon. There are no true darks to anchor the foreground head.
- **Edges.** They are uniformly blurred everywhere except the hand, which is hard to the point of looking cut out. There is no edge hierarchy, so the eye has nowhere to settle except the cutout.
- **Composition.** The idea of a thumb aimed at a distant moon works. But the foreground face is unreadable, half-hidden by a confusing disc and cropped by the frame. The right-hand mass is ambiguous and competes for attention. The horizon is a stiff diagonal ruled band.
- **Gesture.** The arm has no tension or reach. It sticks up stiffly rather than straining.
- **The moon.** It is too small and too crudely shaped (a hexagonal dot) to feel like *the* Moon in a piece themed "Man and the Moon".

**4) The one change:** properly draw the hand and wrist in graphite. Give the thumb and knuckles structure, a lit side and a shadow side against the sky, a believable wrist joining the arm, and a soft-to-crisp edge instead of the flat white cutout. The whole picture hangs on that gesture, and right now it is the least drawn thing on the sheet. (Close second: give the foreground eye and head real darks so we know who is reaching.)

**Studio manager's note:**
- **The reading is one of your nearest wrong feelings** ("a generic thumbs-up", here a hitchhiker) plus a dreamy loneliness. The sly delight isn't there yet, because the two carriers of it, the clenched-eye grin and the thumb covering the moon, aren't drawn. The thumb is bare paper on purpose, and the face doesn't read. Both get most of phase 2.
- **Work passage by passage now, to finish, not layer by layer across the sheet.** This is how hyperrealists work (your style doc): the whole-sheet blends made the murk (spread 12 against 21, murky middle 71%, nothing under L* 40). Order:
  1. **The fist in its 4× detail, to near-finish:** the thumb's lit rim, the knuckle arc, creases, the nail plate with its free edge already indented, the sharp cast shadows, and the wrist joining the sleeve. That fixes "a glove on a pipe". It should be the sharpest, most finished passage on the sheet.
  2. **The face:** remove the hard oval rim, and make the clenched eye, the bunched cheek and the closed grin read at thumbnail size. Give him his darks: the open eye's lash line and pupil, the hair mass and the shadow under the jaw, in carbon down to your floor.
  3. **The arm:** a forearm in a sleeve, with the cuff, the wrist and a turn of form. Not a tube.
- **Values:** push the spread toward 21. The sky top at 43–45 and the moon at 74 are right per the research; the problem is the lower half. Give the near grass, the hair and the shadow sides real darks, and keep the sunlit skin the lightest flesh.
- **The right-hand mass** reads as "a crouching body, a horse". If it's his shoulder and chest under the shirt, make the cloth say so (collar, folds, buttons) or quiet it into the blur. It must not compete.
- **The moon:** keep its true size (the research is clear: no enlargement), but draw it in a detail as a round, waxing gibbous disc with faint maria, not a hexagonal dot. A small moon that is unmistakably the Moon is the point of the joke.
- **"Airbrush, no graphite character":** in the in-focus passages (fist, near sharp blades, the sleeve), let the pencil show (directional layers, crisp edges) before blending. Smoothness belongs to the out-of-focus zones only.
