# The Thumbnail

**Commission:** theme "Man and the Moon", in graphite hyperrealism, on a 2400×3200 vellum sheet (round 19). Drawn by Claude Opus 5.5 with studio v8, as the test of the engine's limits that Robert asked for. New in this round:
- the CC0 human mesh as the rendered reference;
- the detail pass (the fist at 4×, the face at 2–4×, the moon at 8×);
- openly licensed references, gathered with `tools/find_refs.py`;
- subject research, including a new layer-1 page on the moon and layer-2 rules for graphite hyperrealism.

**Teodor's feeling** (in his words, written before he drew; the full text, with the nearest wrong feelings, is in `feeling_teodor.md`):

> **Sly delight: a grown man putting out the moon with his thumb.**
>
> Lying on his back in the afternoon grass, one eye screwed shut, arm straight up, he has hidden the whole moon behind a thumbnail, and he is pleased with himself the way a boy is: a private, almost smug pleasure in a game nobody outgrows.

**Teodor, on finishing:**

> He lies in the grass with one eye pressed shut and puts the moon out with his thumbnail. The moon is drawn at its true size, a small gibbous disc with one hard lit edge, so we can see what he can't: it was never gone. This last sitting was mostly mending. I tore out the boxes my own clumsy regions had built into the chest. I redrew the eye as a lid squeezed shut over a lifted cheek, with crow's feet, and turned the corner of the mouth up. I drew the nail on the thumb so it faces his eye. What I couldn't mend was laid down in the first sitting: I blended the whole sheet before laying any darks, and I chose a head seen upside down from above the crown. So the drawing stays foggier and slower to read than a photograph, and I'd rather say so than pretend otherwise.

**Research:**
- The moon page, `docs/subjects/moon.md`, gave its true angular size: about 22 px through his lens, and no bigger.
- The graphite-hyperrealism layer-2 pages (`docs/styles/subjects/graphite-hyperrealism--*.md`) corrected the plan:
  - a daytime moon is darker than sunlit skin;
  - at f/16 the hand and the moon are both crisp;
  - there is no sharp blade among the near blur;
  - cast shadows from a low sun are sharp;
  - the sky is an orange-filter grey.
- The research on references (`docs/research/references.md`) set how he used them: twelve openly licensed hand and face studies, listed in `references.json`.
