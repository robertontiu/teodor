# Handoff after PHASE 2 (episodes 23-59; schema now v17 = scene_B9.json + overlay_C.svg, final)

## Where the painting stands
Phase 2 began by revising the schema after critique 1 (arm, head and fist were unreadable; no heat at the impact). New schema B9: a taller, wider window (transom at about y 545 on canvas, mullion at x 478), so the whole upper figure is a dark shape against cold glass. The man stands near the window, seen three-quarter from behind. His left arm is raised and bent, with the elbow thrown out over the lower left pane (I went further than the schema here) and the fist pressed at the cross of the frame (fist at about 522,560). His head is dropped and sunk below the hump of the near shoulder. His other arm hangs, and that hand is OPEN and slack (about 755,940), lit by the lamp. The chair is on its side lower right, with a cold pool and debris lower left.
I scraped down and re-toned (#552a1c) before the new block-in. The glass now has a halo: it is lightest around the knuckles and darker toward the frame. The cracks are crisp dark-and-light liner pairs running from the fist to the frame. There are dark holes at the knuckles, crushed white glass, warm slivers flung down-left, and the lamp is hot on the top of the knuckles. The right wall has been calmed into one warm plane, which also glazed the figure darker so it separates from the wall.
Gray check (ep 48): three groups. Cold window (lightest at the break), dark figure and room, lamp wall one step lighter.

## What each element does for the feeling
- Fist at the cross, the hot knuckles, cracks, holes and flying slivers: the explosion still in the body. This is the focal point, with the hardest edges, the highest contrast and the only hot colour.
- Head sunk below the shoulder, dropped against his own raised arm, back to us: what comes right after. He cannot look at it, or at us.
- Open hanging hand vs the closed fist above: the two halves of the moment, one hand still striking and one already let go. Keep the lit edge on the open hand; it is the second accent.
- Overturned chair and floor fragments: the room still ringing. Secondary, warm-lit.
- Cold window vs warm room: the heat inside against the cold outside. The cold light falls on the bowed head.

## Open problems (for phase 3)
1. Head and shoulder are crude: the skull is a round ball and the shoulder rim can read as a hat brim or strap. Wanted: a smaller skull tipped forward, the nape catching a little warm light, and the shoulder line as a broad plane with a broken (not continuous) warm rim. Dry before working here; wet-into-wet turned the glass streaky brown.
2. The upper arm and forearm are heavy and blocky: give them planes (lamp side vs shadow side) and a crisp edge against the glass. The elbow over the left pane must stay angular.
3. Fist: keep it the hottest, sharpest place. Add 1-2 tiny crisp highlights and one more dark shard at the knuckles; maybe a thin warm glaze around the fist after drying.
4. A stray pale/pink blob lower right (about 1100,1250-1350) and a few odd warm and blue chips at the top edge (blue ceiling strip top left, red bits top right): paint them out into the warm dark.
5. Floor debris lower left reads as paper scraps: make them a few crisp shards with cold tops and warm edges. Repaint the cold pool as a quiet grey plane.
6. Chair: legs read as planks. Make one or two lamp-lit edges crisp; the seat should read as a seat.
7. Rim light along the back and the back leg (liner, warm #a8652f to #d08446), broken, last.
8. Lower half of the window is evenly grey: glaze it slightly darker and cooler toward the sill so the halo near the break dominates.
9. Leg gap and legs: the feet need a contact shadow; the gap of light between the legs should be dim.
10. Never use free strokes without "clip": true and a narrow palette; that pass (ep 54) wrecked the figure.

## Plan for phase 3
Read critique_2. If the visitor still reads only "held in" or only despair, push the side they miss: more violence at the break (slivers, a sharper starburst) or more sinking at the head and shoulders. Then work in order: (a) dry and fix the head/shoulder; (b) arm planes; (c) the break and fist fine touches; (d) the stray blobs and edges of the canvas; (e) floor and chair; (f) rim lights, highlights, unifying glazes. Run the critic again before finishing. Then timelapse and final.png.


# Handoff after PHASE 3 (episodes 60-84) - FINISHED
Critique 2 got the aftermath but called the figure a leaning blob, the cracks polite, the right wall dead. Phase 3: glazed the right wall, top and left edges down so the room sinks to the window; cut the striking arm and the fallen head apart with a wedge of glass at its true value; pointed the elbow; pushed the skull below the shoulder line with a lit nape; rebuilt the hot knuckles with dark holes and crushed-glass glints; five long segmented cracks to the frame (removed one that ran over the arm); straight slivers falling down-left over the dark wall; floor scraps replaced by a few lying shards, chair legs given lamp edges; knife on the frame; lower panes glazed darker toward the sill (then sank the legs back into warm dark); right-wall seam and chips removed; pinpoint highlights at the break; a warm lamp glaze beside his back. Painting finished at episode 84; schema stayed v17.
