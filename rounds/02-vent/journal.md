# Journal — Teodor Lisk

**Sketchbook.** Three thumbnails: a red mass rising under dark air with knife slashes; a dark shore with a white burst breaking against it; a hot field split by a black wedge. The third hit me in the chest — the split, the pressure on both sides of it. I take the wedge, and borrow the burst from the second where the two forces meet.

**1. Block-in.** Covered the ground in hot reds and oranges, strokes leaning near-vertical. It is all one temperature and too even — it reads like autumn leaves, cozy. Nothing presses on anything yet.

**2. Block-in.** Brought a heavy near-black, blood-dark mass down from the upper left on a hard diagonal, so the red has something bearing down on it. Pressure needs a weight.
**3. Block-in.** The dark now sits on the red like smoke, but it is soft — brooding, not striking. I drive a black jagged wedge down through the middle with the knife, a cleft that splits the red field in two.
**4. Block-in.** The wedge reads like a cloaked shape standing in fire — too still, too mournful. The force must be outward. I set radiating strokes of hot cadmium exploding away from a point low and right of center, cutting across the dark.
**5. Block-in.** Now it blossoms — a firework, a chrysanthemum. Festive, wrong. In gray the values are scattered mid-darks with no structure. I rebuild the upper field: a heavy, tilted slab of black-crimson crushing down from above, its lower edge a slanting jagged line.
**6. Block-in.** The slab works — it bears down. But below, the radial petals still celebrate. I overpaint the lower field in deep cadmium and alizarin, strokes thrusting upward into the slab, so the red pushes back against the weight instead of blooming.
**7. Block-in.** The red is finally hot and blood-deep, and it does push — but it overran the slab, leaving only a thin dark lid. Flames under a ceiling. I let it set a little, then knife the dark slab back down hard, with a jagged, toothed lower edge biting into the red.
**8. Develop.** The slab now sits hard and heavy, edge like a clamped jaw. The meeting line is dead, just a border. I cut a molten seam along it — explicit zigzag strokes of white-hot yellow, thin and thick, the place where the pressure cracks open.
**9. Develop.** The seam sank into the wet red and vanished but for a ghost at the left. The paint must set. I dry it and lay the crack again, unmixed and fully opaque.
**10. Develop.** The crack burns only on the left and starves out by the middle — the brush ran dry. I lay the rest of it in short, reloaded jabs, the way a real crack runs: in fits.
**11. Develop.** Now it is a neat golden zigzag — a crown, a chart, ornament. Too polite, too composed. I knife dark wedges down across it at uneven intervals, chopping the rhythm, burying parts of it.
**12. Develop.** The knife chops hang like tabs of cloth off the seam — limp, the opposite of what I need. The two masses must collide, not touch. I drive hot red diagonals up and to the right across the seam into the dark, and black diagonals down against them: crossing thrusts.
**13. Develop.** Squinting, it reads now as one wall of fire under a black lid — energetic but undirected; the whole lower half is one value. Darkness must hold the heat. I dry and glaze deep alizarin-black over the bottom and the lower left, so the heat is cornered and forced upward toward the right.
**14. Develop.** The glazes settled like soft smoke blobs — too gentle, a dream rather than a blow. I firm the lower-left dark into a hard diagonal plane with flat bristle strokes laid along its edge, so the red is caught in a wedge between two black jaws.
**15. Develop.** Two black jaws now, the red wedged between, spikes biting upward. But there is no heart to the heat — no place where it is hottest. I load cadmium orange and yellow into the cores of the spikes at upper right, thick, driving the same up-right angle.
**16. Develop.** The yellow came in thin and bright like sparklers — a party in the corner, a fairground. Too light-hearted. I dry and pull it under a veil of deep cadmium red so the heat stays dense and red-hot, not glittering.
**17. Develop.** Now the spikes burn properly red, but the veil also lit the top-right corner, so the black lid there is gone and the thrust has nothing to strike. I bring heavy black back down into the corner, angled against the spikes.
**18. Develop.** The corner bites now; red spears stab into black. But the limp dark tabs from earlier still hang mid-right, like laundry. I bury them under thrusting crimson, same up-right angle as the spears, so everything in the red drives one way.
**19. Develop.** The laundry is gone, the right side surges. A sour green bruise sits in the lower-left dark, cooling it toward sickness. I overpaint it with black-crimson along the jaw's diagonal.
**20. Refine.** Gray shows a good skeleton: dark lid, dark jaw, a hot band slashing between. But the spears on the left are soft and dull, more glow than blade. I dry and cut black wedges down between them, parallel to their thrust, so they become teeth with hard edges.
**21. Refine.** Leaning close, the old pale zigzag still peeks through on the left with its tidy peaks, and the knife's hatching sits on it like a fence. I drive thick vermilion and crimson up across it, swallowing the ornament but leaving a torn scrap of the pale line.
**22. Refine.** The band surges now, a mass of red blades driving up-right. But the lower right is a placid pool of red with a soft dark smudge — it lets the pressure leak away. I firm that smudge into a hard rising black plane, so the red is squeezed into a channel and has nowhere to go but up.
**23. Refine.** Two black jaws below now pinch the red into a narrow throat at the bottom, and it flares upward — a blast from a small source, pressure finding its way out. The lid above is inert, though, a dead weight. I split it with a thin jagged fissure of red heat running up from the band into the dark, struck in short, broken segments.
**24. Refine.** Up close the fissure is a neon tube — even, rounded, manufactured. A real crack has breath and heat around it. I dry it and breathe a crimson glow into the black on either side, so the line looks like it was burned open from within.
**25. Refine.** The glow spread into a soft red cloud — heat, yes, but blurred, a haze where I wanted a split. I re-strike the core thin and uneven in hot orange, pressure jumping from stroke to stroke, so the fissure flickers between hairline and gash.
**26. Refine.** From across the room the fissure works like a lightning strike in the lid, but the red cloud around it is broad and pinkish, and orange leaks down the far left edge. I narrow the glow with black strokes pressing in from both sides, and close the left edge.
**27. Refine.** The black closed over the fissure, leaving only broken embers of it — heavier, more smothered, maybe truer. The red band itself is now a single hot hue that the eye starts to rest in. I scatter a few dark viridian flecks through it, so the red vibrates against its complement and cannot sit still.
**28. Refine.** Close up the flecks are tidy teal seeds — confetti, a festive sprinkle, the last thing I want. While they are wet I drag a dry smudging brush through them along the thrust, so they tear into bruised streaks inside the red.
**29. Refine.** The smudge raised scored, lighter streaks — scratched, clawed, good. The teal seeds still float on top. I dry and lay a thin alizarin glaze over the lower body of the red, sinking the flecks into bruise and deepening the red toward blood, keeping the upper-right spears hottest.
**30. Refine.** Now the red is blood-deep and clawed all over with scored slashes; the whole band seethes. But it has no point of impact — the eye roams. I lay a few short, blunt knife slabs of hot cadmium orange where the spears hit the lid at upper right: not sparkle, a blow.
**31. Refine.** The knife dragged black into the orange and left tan, hatched ribbons — muddy cloth, not heat. I go over them with a loaded round brush, short fat jabs of pure cadmium orange-red, wet into wet but barely mixing.
**32. Refine.** The impact burns at upper right now. But stepping back, the red mass between the black jaws closes to a point at the bottom and its upper-left swells round — the silhouette of an emblem, a symbol, which I will not have. I open the point: a jagged channel of red forcing up from below the frame, so the heat comes from underneath and through a crack rather than sitting in a shape.
**33. Refine.** The channel helps — the heat now rises from below like something forced through a vent. The upper-left contour of the red is still a soft swelling lobe. I jam an angular black wedge in from the left edge, point-first, to make that contour broken and toothed.
**34. Refine.** Squinting, it is one diagonal blaze, rising up and right from a narrow vent — no emblem now, just force. Everything in the red runs one way though; it has momentum but no clash inside it. I cut three crisp black lashes across the grain, against the thrust, like whip strokes.
**35. Refine.** The lashes came out as hairs — barely a whisper, and close up the scored streaks start to look like plumage. I strike the lashes again, heavier, a real brush full of black, tapering hard at both ends.
**36. Refine.** The lashes cut properly now, black against the red like welts. But they run nearly parallel and evenly spaced, ruled lines, too orderly. One more strike at a steeper, conflicting angle, short and brutal, to break their order.
**37. Refine.** The darks smoulder well, warm black with crimson in them. But the red tendrils in the vent below read as drips — falling, bleeding, the wrong direction for this. I put hot orange tongues inside the vent pointing upward, so the eye reads it as heat pushing up through the crack.
**38. Refine.** Values are right: a diagonal blade of light between two masses of black. But the raised streaks catch the light pinkish, and pink softens everything toward tenderness. I dry and veil the middle and left of the band with saturated cadmium red so the streaks burn instead of blush.
**39. Refine.** Now the band screams red, no blush left. But the veil softened the black lashes into dull maroon — the welts healed. I re-strike them on the dry surface, black and hard, not quite where they were, so they feel like fresh blows over old ones.
**40. Refine.** The fresh welts sit well. At the left edge, the black wedge I jammed in has gone a sour greenish, a cold note that reads as sickness rather than heat. I cover it with a warm black-crimson, keeping its pointed thrust.
**41. Develop.** The upper left smoulders, cracked and glowing — enough. The lid over the right is now the flattest passage, and it is all warm black, so the red has no cold to fight. I work loaded strokes of blue-black and dark violet into it, pressing down against the spears.
**42. Develop.** The cold violet in the lid makes the red hotter by contrast — the fight is now temperature too. Along the contact edge the boundary is everywhere the same; I scumble dry red into the black just above the spears in a few places, so the heat scorches into the dark there, and leave the rest hard.
**43. Refine.** The contact edge now frays into scorched orange fibres in places — burning into the dark. In the far bottom-right corner a scrap of red leaks out of the black jaw and lets the eye escape the frame. I close it with warm black.
**44. Refine.** The whole holds: a blaze driven up between black jaws, welted with lashes. The red's upper-left shoulder is still too round, a soft curve where everything else is blade. I bite two black teeth down into it from the lid, hard-edged, tapering to points.
**45. Refine.** The teeth landed in the dark above, not in the red — they only thicken the lid. I strike them again lower, starting in the black and driving down into the red's shoulder so the curve is broken.
**46. Final accents.** The shoulder is broken now; black prongs stab down into it and the red reads all edges and thrust. The impact at upper right needs one notch more heat at its very centre — a few short, thick jabs of near-white yellow, tiny, where the force is greatest.
**47. Stop.** Squinting one last time: a blood-red mass forced up through a crack, flaring against a black weight, welted across, heat breaking white at the point of contact. Every passage pushes. Another stroke would only be explaining it. I put the brushes down.
