# Journal (Teodor, round 14: HIGH)

## Phase 1: conception to block-in

E0 conception: read habits.md and looked at the contact sheet. Thirteen children of one mother: lone figure from behind, a lit door in a brown room, amber against violet, centered eye level, hushed. The first idea that came (a figure in a dark room before a blazing window, the light pouring in) is exactly the old sibling. Thrown away.
E0 research: Balla's Dynamism of a Dog on a Leash (1912) and the Futurists: a moving limb painted as many overlapping limbs, a fan of positions, so time smears inside one picture. Kusama's Infinity Nets and self-obliteration: the dot pattern that starts on the wall and climbs over her own body until she cannot tell where she ends. Matisse and the Fauves (Bonheur de vivre): arbitrary, unmodelled high-key color in flat fields, color as feeling not description, figures sprawled on ground. TAKING: (1) Balla's repeated limbs as a fan of ghost arms for the distortion of time; (2) Kusama's dots migrating from the ground onto and through the body, for the dissolving of edges; (3) Fauve daylight palette: acid green, lemon, rose madder, cobalt and turquoise, no umber, no amber-vs-violet.
E0 habits I am breaking on purpose: (1) eye-level, middle-distance, centered: the camera looks straight down from above, the body is off-centre and too big, cut by the top and right edges; (2) one warm light in a dark brown room / amber against cool violet: open noon daylight, acid yellow-green against cobalt, rose and lemon, no umber, no violet, no dark; (3) the lone small figure from behind in silhouette: a body close as a lover, lying face-up (head turned away), filling the frame; (4) the hushed bittersweet feeling: this one is rapture and vertigo, loud, with no grief in it; (5) the radiant burst and red-and-black: none; (6) uniform square dabs: the dots are single round hard dabs laid one by one against loose broken flats.
E1 conception, felt (arousal .5, tension .3, valence .6, care .6): idea A, a body lying in the grass seen straight down (I hang a grass wall and stand the mannequin against it, so the camera looks down from the sky), arms flung wide, one knee up. It reads at once as lying-in-the-grass-gone. But centered and polite, the figure small inside the field.
E2 conception: idea B, a dancer spinning on a rose field seen from high, three overlapping bodies in three phases of the turn, a fan of arms and a fan of shadows. The Balla device works in 3D; but tiny, centered, and the rose field is a candy poster.
E3 conception: idea C, A cropped hard: the body too big for the frame, hand leaving the canvas top-right, legs cut at the bottom. This is the one with force: we are hovering right over the person, close as a lover or as the sky. Choice: C's crop and overhead camera with B's fan of arms (time smearing) and Kusama's dots (edges going). A passerby feels the lift fastest from the flung, too-big body seen from above; the fan of arms and the dots will make it high and not just a nap in the park.
E4 development, felt (a .6, t .3, v .7, care .7): merged blockout D, the fan of arms in 3D. Six arms in white sleeves read as an octopus, a rubbery Shiva. Too many ghosts.
E5 development: E and F. Matisse's Blue Nudes gave me the answer to faces and clothes: the whole body one flat cobalt blue, the head a blue oval turned away, no features. One ghost per side, paler blue. Acid yellow-green field, shadow falling down-left. Camera slid left so the body sits right of centre: the left hand opens into empty grass, the right hand leaves the canvas at the top. Rendered final.
E6 development: SVG would not know the body, so I drew the overlay by the body's own distance field: the silhouette echoes outward in rings of rose, turquoise and lemon (the body's edge spreading into the world, Riley's repeated line), and Kusama's dots cover grass and body alike, densest at the body's edges, thinning to calm in the lower-left. First pass was confetti; thinned the dots and cut the rings to four. Schema G set.
E7 realization: toned rose madder ground (under the green it will vibrate, its complement), transferred the drawing at 0.6. The rings and dots stand on the rose like a map.
E8 block-in, felt (a .7 t .3 v .7 care .4): big flat match 45-90. The yellow-green lands in broken slabs and the rose ground sings through every gap; the blue body is already there as a thing. Too much rose, too square. Coverage next, bigger and more fluid.
E9 block-in, felt (a .65 t .25 v .75 care .45): second coverage pass. Energy is right, the rose flickers between green slabs like heat. But in gray the body and field sit almost one value, the legs are a blue-black heap and the shadow went olive-brown (mud, the old umber creeping back).
E10 block-in, felt (a .5 t .3 v .6 care .75): body pass with rounds, saturation up, value down. The blue body now stands; the outstretched left arm and the ghost arm above it read as a gesture caught twice. Legs still a dark heap; the shadows are olive mud.
E11 block-in, felt (a .45 t .2 v .5 care .7): tried to recolor the shadow with a limited cool palette through match, but match spread it into the pinks and greens too: a teal confetti, no mass. The shadow must be one shape.
E12 block-in, felt (a .4 t .15 v .45 care .8): free strokes in a poly gave a dead geometric slab and buried the outstretched arm. Wrong tool. Revised the schema instead (H): every cast shadow on the grass is now deep viridian-teal, never olive, so matching will lay it with the right shapes.
E13 block-in, felt (a .5 t .2 v .6 care .75): rematched arm and legs on schema H. Arm is back, the shadow is teal now. Stepping back I see a danger: two arms straight out, head dropped, one knee crossed: a CROSS. A passerby could read a crucifixion, the opposite of a rush. Lesson one again. The arms must be flung above the head, the body opened upward, and the ghost arms must sweep down from there like a snow angel.
E14 development, felt (a .6 t .35 v .7 care .7): scenes I, J, K. Both arms up read as a cheer, stiff as antennae; then asymmetric: one arm flung straight up off the top edge, the other out and off the right edge, the paler ghost arms swept down to the horizontal where the old cross arms were. The body now sits hard right and leaves the left half to the grass, the rings and the dots: the body's echo spreading into the world. Final render, rings, dots and teal shadows composited: schema K.
E15 development, felt (a .75 t .35 v .8 care .5): whole-canvas pass to the new schema. Lovely loose broken green, but the raised arm only half arrived, a blue stub floating at the top, and the old horizontal arm still shouts dark blue: the cross again.
E16 development, felt (a .6 t .3 v .8 care .85): raised arm painted on, ghost arm pale. Better: a person flung open, not hung. Compare shows the old cross-shadow still lying as a green-teal mosaic left of the torso where the schema has sunlit grass and rings.
E17 development, felt (a .55 t .2 v .8 care .8): cleared the old shadow back to sunlit grass and re-laid the legs. Value groups are distinct now: lemon-green light, cobalt middle, teal dark. But the dots came in as square pink slabs, and Kusama's dots must be round and deliberate.
E18 development, felt (a .5 t .25 v .85 care .9): rose and white dots laid one by one as round hard dabs, positions taken from the schema's net. Now the field shimmers; it is no longer confetti but a skin over the world, and it is starting to climb onto the body.
E19 development, felt (a .5 t .3 v .8 care .9): cobalt and lemon dots laid; lemon dots climb the body, densest at its edges. Thumb: a cobalt body flung open in a shimmering field; the dots read as a skin over everything. Weak: in thumb it may read as a figure standing or leaping, not lying on the ground (the shadow is too thin to tell the story); the pale ghost arms still sit horizontal and whisper the cross; the dots are spread evenly with no resting place; the rings have vanished; every edge is the same soft edge. End of phase 1.

## Phase 2: development

E20 critique 1 recorded: the critic felt "euphoric, but a party, confetti and a festival", read a standing figure, called the ghost arms a mistake, found every edge the same mush and nothing glowing; the manager adds that it reads as a figure on a cross and that a bloom behind the head is my old radiant-burst habit. They are right: the vertigo has no form and the cross is still in it. The cure is not touching up; it is turning the world over.
E20 development, felt (a .6 t .45 v .6 care .8): scene L and schema L. Ghost figures now rendered in red and magenta only to find where the arms swept; the body rotated 152 degrees and enlarged: it lies UPSIDE DOWN and diagonal, feet leaving the top-left, head low right of centre, arms flung toward the bottom and off the right edge. No standing reading, no cross: a body tipping into the ground. The ghost arms are no longer arms: they are the pale fans of grass pressed flat where the arms swept (a snow angel), and they bleach with light; that is the glow, coming from where the body has been, not a burst behind a head. Far field darkens to deep viridian in the corners; the upper-right is the rest. Dots now laid in rows that echo the body's outline outward (Riley's repeated line), rose, white, one turquoise row: a pattern, not confetti.
E21 realization, felt (a .75 t .4 v .7 care .45): big flat match 45-95 over the new schema. The old body still ghosting through the middle; corners went deep green at once.
E22 realization, felt (a .6 t .35 v .7 care .6): second coverage 28-60. Field right, but the body came out shredded, thin, no mass.
E23 realization, felt (a .5 t .35 v .65 care .8): rounds clipped to the body. Still shredded: match will not hold a big simple shape.
E24 realization, felt (a .45 t .3 v .7 care .85): painted the body deliberately, stroke by stroke from its mask, each stroke following the limb's length (tools/fillmask.py). Now it is a body: upside-down, diving, the feet out of the top-left. Cobalt dark at the edges, cerulean in the light.
E25 realization, felt (a .7 t .45 v .7 care .5): the grass laid as curved strokes wheeling around the head (tools/swirl.py): the whole field turns like a slow vortex. This is the vertigo I could not find in phase 1, and it comes from the hand, not from a symbol. But the pale pressed fans were eaten, and the stroke texture is too even, like combed fur.
E26 development, felt (a .55 t .25 v .85 care .65): pale pressed fans repainted thick along the swirl. They come back only as haze.
E27 development, felt (a .4 t .3 v .6 care .85): looked in gray: the cerulean torso sat at the grass's value and vanished. E28 development, felt (a .6 t .2 v .9 care .6): the pressed fans laid thick in cream and lemon-white, impasto: two pale wedges, one above the right arm, one beside the lower arm. The lightest value now lives where the body swept, next to the body. That is my glow.
E29 development, felt (a .55 t .3 v .8 care .8): sweep arcs (pale on pale, invisible) and a hair fan with the liner. The hair came out as an eyelash, a spiked sun: the radiant burst habit sneaking in by the back door. Out.

PHASE 3 (finalization)
Critique 2 recorded: "weightless free fall, a dizzy, bright rush ... euphoric, but a little like a candy wrapper." Weak: two tones, the cream fans compete with the head, every edge the same stipple, the head a featureless blob, hands stumps, the dots a pasted screen filter, mechanical dabs. Their one change: a value climax at the head, crisp there, dissolving toward the feet. The manager: take the climax and edge hierarchy, but no radiant burst; the blob head is my mannequin habit; dots must come out of the paint. As a painter I agree: the vertigo has arrived, the rapture has not. The rapture must be a person, not a pattern: a face turned into the light.
E52 finalization, felt (a .3 t .25 v .8 care .95): throat and upturned face laid with straight strokes from a formula: a tin can with a lid, a chef's hat. Mechanical, dead. Out.
E53 finalization, felt (a .25 t .2 v .8 care .95): repainted from a shaded schema (tools/p3/headsch.py): an egg on a stalk. A mushroom: the poster cliché I promised to avoid. Out.
