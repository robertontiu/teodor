# Critique 2 (a fresh critic; knew only the theme "High", as in the high of a drug; read crit_2.png)

1) First three seconds: weightless free fall, a dizzy, bright rush. Euphoric, but a little like a candy wrapper.

2) Why I feel that: the figure is upside down and diving on a diagonal, with its arms flung wide, so the body reads as surrendered to falling. The deep cobalt body against the acid yellow-green sets off a strong complementary buzz. The concentric dotted rings (pink and white) and the radiating dabs around the head feel like a pulse, or a world spinning outward. The pale cream patches read as a blown-out glare.

3) Weaknesses:
- Composition: the figure's diagonal runs edge to edge, so nothing is left to fall into. The left leg is cropped at the top-left corner and the right hand is cut off at the right edge. The torso sits near the centre with no tension around it.
- Values: the figure is almost all mid-to-dark blue with little modelling. The background is uniformly high-key, so the image flattens into two tones. The cream patches compete with the head for brightness.
- Edges: almost every edge is the same mottled, stippled border. There's no sharp edge to anchor the eye and no truly lost edge to give a sense of speed.
- Drawing: the head is a dark blob with no features or tilt. The anatomy is mushy: the shoulders and chest are unresolved, and the hands are stumps.
- Dots: they sit evenly over everything, the figure included, like a pasted overlay. This is the cheapest-looking element and reads as a screen filter, not paint.
- Finish: the mark-making is mechanical, with repeated rectangle dabs, so the surface reads as generated, not handled.

4) The one change: build a strong value climax at the head. Make one hot, near-white burst right where the head meets the rings, with crisp edges there, and let the dots and edges dissolve and blur toward the feet. That gives the eye a destination and makes the high feel like it peaks.

(Studio manager's note: the vertigo arrived: "weightless free fall, a dizzy, bright rush". The rapture still reads as "candy wrapper". Take the value climax and the edge hierarchy (crisp where it peaks, lost toward the feet), but find the peak without a radiant burst, which is your listed habit. The head as a featureless blob is the generic-mannequin habit; and the dots must come out of the paint, not sit on it like a filter.)
