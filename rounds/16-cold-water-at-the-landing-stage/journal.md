# Journal, commission 16: "Life" (Post-Impressionism, expressive mode, oil on canvas)

## Before the canvas

**Feeling** (feeling.md): gasped awake, the cold shock and the shout of joy in the same breath. An old man surfacing in cold river water, splashed full in the face by a child; he laughs and gasps at once.

**First idea thrown away**: an old man alone at a window at dawn, "still here". It was my old picture again (lit rectangle, lone figure, hush). Thrown out. A second idea, a crowded harvest table from above, echoed round 13's ring of faces; also thrown out.

**Research, what I take:**
1. Bathers in this tradition (Seurat's riverside bathers, smarthistory): bathers placed along a strong diagonal and made large and present, but faces hidden and the mood detached and timeless. I take the diagonal and the large near body, and refuse the detachment: my faces are turned to us and caught in one instant.
2. Fauvism (not a current I have used), *Bonheur de vivre* (Lumen): colour answering to emotion, not nature; curving arabesque contours repeated between bodies and setting; no recession of scale, so figures press forward. I take the flattened high view with the water as a field, and the contour that ties the spray's arc to the man's arm and the child's arms.
3. Post-Impressionism expressive (style doc): complementaries placed side by side, not mixed; impasto in proportion to the intensity of feeling; dark outlines restated last. I place cold ultramarine/viridian against shocked vermilion-pink flesh, with the thickest paint only at the spray and the face.
4. Emotion research: the posture decides (head thrown back, arms up and open = joy; fear shares "head back, forearms raised", so the hand goes to the crown slicking the hair, not up in defence, and the face is a creased-eye laugh). Light high in the frame = positive. Being moved = a relationship at a moment of intensified closeness: the spray is the visible link between the two.

**Habits broken on purpose** (at least three):
1. *Lone figure from behind, faceless, middle distance* → two people, both faces turned to us, the man large and close, cropped by the left edge and the waterline.
2. *Eye-level, middle-distance, centred* → a high viewpoint looking down into the water from the bank, hard crop, the main figure in the lower-left.
3. *One warm light in a dark room / amber against violet* → open daylight, no light source in the picture, a cold green-blue field with hot pink-vermilion flesh (red-green complement, not amber-violet).
4. *Hushed, melancholy, bittersweet* → loud: a shout and a splash; shock with delight, not grief with anything.
5. *Mannequin faces, tube hands* → individual faces from tonal studies (an old man's creased laughing face, grey stubble, white wet hair; a grinning child), a hand with joints slicking the hair.
6. *Radial burst / one figure pulled to centre* → no centre: a diagonal from child (upper right) to man (lower left) carried by an arc of thrown water.

**Thumbnails** (thumbs.png, ten): A high jetty view, B extreme close head, C worm's view (silhouette: my habit, rejected), D side eye-level (habit, rejected), E overhead with rings (radial, rejected), F two heads facing, G man on a ladder, H child aloft, I man big lower-left with the child's splash arcing to him, J child from behind in foreground (rejected: back view). **Chosen: I**, because the relationship is visible as one shape (the arc of water from the child's hands to the man's face), the main face is large and turned up to us, and the diagonal gives motion without a centre. Value plan: lights = flesh and spray; middle = water; darks = deep water at the bottom, the stage's shadow, the child's hair, the mouths.

**Colour studies** (colour_studies.png): 1 emerald/vermilion (lively, a bit warm, holiday), 2 ultramarine/orange (coldest, shock), 3 green-gold/rose (pretty, too sweet). **Chosen: a blend of 1 and 2**: water goes from sunlit green-gold far away (top) through emerald to deep ultramarine near the man (bottom), so the cold gathers around his body; flesh hot vermilion-pink with cobalt-violet shadows; the stage a small wedge of orange, the complement to all that blue.

**Focal point**: the man's face where the spray hits, around (370, 880). Hardest edges, thickest paint, the finest drawing there.

## Expectation ledger
- Teaches: a cold, high-middle key water field; long, low, broken horizontal marks in the water; curved, warm, soft-edged flesh; the eye travelling from the child (upper right) down the arc to the man (lower left).
- The ONE break: the thrown water itself, a hard, cream-white, knife-laid arc that cuts across the cold field with the only hard light edges in the painting, ending in a broken burst on the man's hair and brow. It should be the strongest point on the canvas.

## Phase 2: development

**Critique 1 recorded.** The critic read "summer, play, warm joy, grandfather and grandchild": my second nearest wrong feeling (cheerful summer leisure). The delight lands, the shock does not: nothing about cold, a gasp or a jolt. What tipped it: the water is a mid-value field of same-sized dashes, no real dark, so it neither bites nor lifts the skin; the flesh is one flat orange with no cold in it; the child is an emoji (round head, ^^ eyes), a cartoon that pushes the whole thing toward illustration; bare ground scattered like holes; a thin drawn waterline. The studio manager adds: let the cold bite, vary the marks, cover the ground, paint the child from a study, tighten the palette (the purples in the lower water are arbitrary). Keep the arm gesture, the high crop, the two faces, the daylight.
