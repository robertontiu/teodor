## What each element does for the feeling (gasped awake: shock + delight)
- **The man's face** (FOCAL POINT, around x 375 y 890, with the hand and burst just above at ~ 470 730): both halves: the laugh (creased eyes, lifted cheeks, open mouth) and the gasp (mouth open wide, head thrown back, raw cold-red skin, cold violet shadow side).
- **The hunched left shoulder**: the body's answer to cold; the shock half in posture.
- **The raised arm and the hand in the hair**: "surfaced and fine", a sweep of the wet hair, not a salute and not a cry for help. Kept from phase 1 (the critic called it the best thing) but raised and corrected.
- **The boy**: the cause and the play; eyes open on the old man, so the link between the two is visible as a look AND as the thrown water.
- **The thrown water**: the link and the expectation-break; hard, white, the only knife-laid lights.
- **The deep ultramarine water under and around him**: the cold given real area and weight; it presses on the warm body.
- **The sunlit far water, gold glints and the orange jetty at the top**: light high in the frame (positive), complement to the blues.

## Expectation ledger
- Teaches: a cool water field darkening toward us; long horizontal marks that grow broader toward the bottom and smaller toward the top; warm, soft, curved flesh against it; the eye travelling from the boy (upper right) along the thrown water to the man's hand and face (lower left).
- The ONE break: the thrown water, hard cream-white knife and loaded strokes, the only hard light edges, breaking on the hand and hair right above the face. The report's focal point now sits there (0.44, 0.45), with the hard edges gathered at it (33% near focus). In phase 3 the break must stay the strongest light, and the face beneath it must hold the strongest dark (the open mouth) and the sharpest drawing.

## Open problems (for phase 3)
1. **Lights are too few (9%) and the face went milky** under a veil in ep49. Re-establish the lit planes of the face, shoulders, forearm and the boy with OPAQUE, thick, light paint (fat 0.9, impasto 0.7-0.9): pale warm cream-pink on the lit forehead, left cheek, nose ridge; keep the cold violet side clean. Aim for 3 value groups (light flesh/spray, middle water, dark near water and jetty shadow).
2. **Face**: a ghost of a misplaced blue contour on the left cheek; laugh folds look drawn; water streaks on the forehead are two white sticks. Work the face last as the focal impasto: firm darks in mouth and eye creases, crisp edges here only. Restate the dark blue contour LAST and only at the true edge of the painted head (not the schema's).
3. **Thrown water close up**: the rising sheet at the boy's hands is two vertical posts; the burst is a sheaf of straws. Fewer, bigger, decisive knife-loaded marks; glassy cold blue-white in its shadows; a few drops falling, none in a ring.
4. **Near water**: deep and quiet but nearly an empty block; give it a few broad varied planes (knife or wide flat), a hint of the refracted body, 3-4 cold glints; keep the ring arcs broken, never wire-thin.
5. **Torso**: still a little flat and orange in the middle; goose-flesh stipple reads as blue dashes; chest runs as scratches. Simplify: fewer, better marks.
6. **Jetty shadow** is a ruler-straight band: break its lower edge with water strokes. White ground specks among the planks: close them.
7. **Report**: chroma and hue spread high (expressive mode allows loud, but keep loudest colour for flesh and spray); texture variation high.
8. Expressive finishing: dark outlines restated last (ultramarine/near-black) around the man, the boy and the hand; no varnish tone.
