# Critique 2 (a fresh critic; knew only the commission "a public exhibition on a theme. The theme is: Life"; read crit_2.png)

**1) First three seconds:** Warm, giddy joy. Then a softer second note: the old and the young sharing the same summer water. It reads as "life is play, at any age."

**2) What creates it:**
- The old man's wide-open laughing mouth and squeezed-shut eyes sit on the strongest warm/cool contrast in the picture: orange skin against blue water.
- The splash arcing from the boy to the old man's head ties the two figures together. That splash is the story of the painting.
- The boy's grin answers the old man's laugh.
- The white hair and wrinkles against the child's black hair give you the full span of a life without any words.
- The sunlit wooden dock in the corner says summer and holiday.

**3) Weaknesses, frankly:**
- **Composition:** The two figures sit on a loose diagonal, but the lower third is a big, flat field of dark navy horizontal dashes that does nothing. The dock in the top-left corner is cropped awkwardly and pulls the eye out of the frame. The green upper half is also empty.
- **Values:** The figures are mid-value orange on mid-value teal, so the old man's face doesn't pop as hard as it should. The brightest whites are spread thinly across the splash, the hair and the scattered water dashes instead of being concentrated in one place. The flat navy at the bottom is too heavy.
- **Edges:** The edges are uniformly soft and smeared everywhere: the figures glow, and so does the water. Nothing is crisp. The waterline across the old man's chest is a hard ruler-straight band, while the curved ripple lines at the bottom look like drawn vector arcs rather than paint.
- **Drawing:** The old man's raised arm is rubbery and over-long, and the hand on his head is mushy. His shoulders and torso are an amorphous orange mass with no structure. The facial features are cartoon outlines (eyebrow strokes, mustache squiggle, eye slits) laid on top of the paint rather than modelled. The boy's face is crude, with lopsided eyes, and his body is a blob.
- **Gesture:** The old man's gesture (head back, arm up) is good and alive. The boy is static, with no throwing arm. The splash seems to come from nowhere instead of from his hand, which weakens the cause and effect that drives the whole scene.
- **Color:** The warm-against-cool idea is sound. But the water is one monotonous blue-green dash texture throughout, with no light, no reflection of the figures, and no warm sunlight on the water to match the sunlit dock. The skin is one orange with pink patches, and it lacks cool shadow tones.
- **Finish:** The texture is uniform digital "brushy" dashes everywhere, which feels like a filter. The cartoon line overlays clash with the painterly passages. The bottom third looks unfinished.

**4) The one change:** Redraw and model the old man's head, arm and hand as properly painted forms rather than outlined cartoon features: real light from above, a crisp focal edge, the brightest light on the face and splash only. He is the emotional centre, and making him convincing would carry the whole painting. If only one more thing: give the boy a visible throwing arm so the splash clearly comes from him.

**Studio manager's note:**
- **This is the second fresh reader to land on your second nearest wrong feeling** ("life is play, at any age"). Still nobody feels the cold or the gasp. With one phase left, decide plainly whether the shock can still be made visible. The cheapest carriers are already in your own plan: spray that is hard and thrown, sharp knife-cut whites against the dark, goose-flesh violet in the shoulders that reads as cold rather than as dots, and the gasp in the mouth. If it can't be made visible, accept that the painting says delight and record the miss honestly.
- **Faces drawn on rather than painted:** the eye slits, brow strokes and moustache squiggle are lines laid over the paint. Your old face habit has come back. Model the features in paint, from your tonal study, and let the line go.
- **The milky glow** around the man (the episode 49 veil) is what makes every edge read soft. Restore the lights on the face and splash, and keep the crispest edges there.
- **The lower third:** the ruled navy dashes and the vector ripple arcs are filler and look digital. Paint the water wrapping around his body with long, loaded, varied strokes, darkest close to him, and drop the ruled lines.
- **The boy needs a visible throwing arm**, so the splash comes from him.
- **Report:** hard edges at the focal point z +3.4 (too concentrated: they are the drawn lines, which the fixes above take care of), texture variation z +1.3, chroma z +1.9 and hue spread z +1.3. Same advice as before: tighten the palette, don't mute the whole canvas. There are still only 2 value groups: you need a real light group on the face and splash.
