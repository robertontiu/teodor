# Critique 1 (a fresh critic; knew only the commission "a public exhibition on a theme. The theme is: Life"; read crit_1.png)

**1) First three seconds:** Summer, play, warm joy. A grandfather and a grandchild laughing in the water. It feels light, but busy.

**2) Why:**
- Both faces are laughing with closed eyes, and the old man's mouth is wide open.
- The man's arm makes a big diagonal sweep toward the child, and the white splash arc ties the two figures together.
- The warm orange skin stands out against cool green and blue water.
- The orange branch in the top left hints at a summer riverbank.
- The idea of old and young sharing one moment fits "Life" well.

**3) Weaknesses:**
- **Composition:** About 60% of the canvas is undifferentiated water texture. The upper half (green dashes) and the lower third (blue dashes) are filler. Both figures sit in the middle band, and the man is cropped hard at the left edge. The branch in the corner reads as a stray object, not as framing.
- **Values:** Nearly everything is mid-value. There is no real dark and no real light except the splash. The faces don't pop because the skin and the water have about the same value. The man's face has no clear light side and shadow side.
- **Edges:** Every stroke is the same short, hard-edged dash, all over the canvas. Nothing is soft or lost, so the eye gets no rest and there's no sense of depth. The figure outlines dissolve into blotchy patches, with white canvas showing through on the man's chest and face like holes.
- **Drawing:** The man's anatomy is unclear. The raised arm joins the head and shoulder in a confusing way, and the torso is a shapeless orange mass. The child is a cartoon emoji face (round head, ^^ eyes) in a different style from the man's more painterly face. The waterline across the man's chest is a thin drawn line, not water wrapping around a body.
- **Gesture:** The man's arm gesture is the best thing in the painting. The child is static, frontal and pasted on, with no response to the splash.
- **Color:** The green-to-blue split reads as two separate swatches, not one body of water with light and depth. The skin is one flat orange with no cool reflected light from the water. The purple strokes in the lower water are arbitrary.
- **Finish:** Bare canvas specks are scattered everywhere, especially at the left edge and through the water. They look unfinished, not intentional. The stroke size is uniform from foreground to background, so the space flattens.

**4) The one change:** Give the two figures a real value and edge hierarchy. Darken and quiet the surrounding water (larger, softer, darker strokes, with less texture in the top half). Model the man's face and arm with a clear light and shadow, keep the sharpest edges and brightest lights only on the two laughing faces and the splash between them, and finish the man's face, arm and body properly. Then the eye goes straight to the connection between the old man and the child, which is the whole point for "Life".

**Studio manager's note:**
- **The critic read your second nearest wrong feeling** (pleasant summer leisure). The delight lands; the shock does not. Nothing in the reading mentions cold, a gasp or a jolt. Your own remedy in feeling.md (cold given its own form and real area, spray thrown up like something hard, goose-flesh violet-blue in the shoulders, the gasp in the mouth) is still undone. Darkening the water, as the critic asks, can serve that: a deeper, colder body of water makes the cold felt and lifts the skin, but darker does not mean quieter or softer everywhere: the cold should bite.
- **It doesn't read as a painting yet.** One stroke shape repeated everywhere (a short, flat, hard-edged dash at one size), bare canvas showing through as holes, and a thin drawn waterline. Vary the marks the way a Post-Impressionist would: long directional strokes that follow this water (rings and ellipses around the body), broad loaded strokes, knife work in the spray, sizes that shrink with distance. Cover the ground except where you choose to leave it.
- **The child is a cartoon** (round head, ^^ eyes). Your old mannequin-face habit came back in a new form. Paint the child from a tonal study like the man, mid-jump or mid-throw as the schema intended, reacting to the splash.
- **Report:** value groups 2, average chroma z +2.1, hue spread z +1.5, hard edges z -1.0. The targets are museum photos of varnished paintings, and the expressive mode is legitimately louder than the style's average, so don't mute the whole canvas to hit the number. Do tighten the palette (the purple strokes in the lower water are arbitrary), keep the loudest colour for the faces and the splash, add a real dark and put the hardest edges at the focal point.
- **Keep:** the man's arm gesture, the high cropped view, two faces turned to us and the daylight palette. All of them break old habits.
