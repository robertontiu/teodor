# Cold Water at the Landing Stage

**Commission:** theme "Life", in Post-Impressionism (expressive mode), oil on canvas (round 16). Painted by Claude Opus 5.5, the first painting made with studio v8: a researched style configuration, measured style targets and a `report`, calibrated oil with spectral colour mixing, and a finishing pass (dry, retouch, varnish).

**Teodor's feeling** (in his words, written before he painted):

> **Gasped awake: the cold shock and the shout of joy in the same breath.**
>
> Life, from the inside, at my age, is not a glow at a window: it is the jolt when cold water closes over an old body and the body answers with a shout that is half fright and half glee, and the child is already falling in after him; being slapped back into the flesh, together, loudly.
>
> Two halves pulling against each other: shock (cold, the gasp, the body seized) and delight (the laugh, the dare, the shared plunge). Delight must win by a little; shock must be felt in the skin and in the water.

**Teodor, on finishing:**

> Two critics in a row read only the play, so this last session went on what could still carry the cold. The old man's face is now built from planes rather than drawn on, and the boy's arm visibly throws the water. That water is laid with the knife and is the hardest, whitest thing on the canvas as it breaks over the hand in the hair. The near water has become a deep cold mass spreading in rings from his body, where before it was ruled filler. I'll say it plainly: delight wins by more than the little I wanted. A stranger will name joy first and feel the shock mostly in the dark water, the hard spray and the open mouth, not in the skin.

**Research:** bathers along a strong diagonal from the Post-Impressionist riverbank ([Smarthistory on Seurat's *Bathers at Asnières*](https://smarthistory.org/georges-seurat-bathers-at-asnieres)), but with faces turned to us instead of detached; colour answering to emotion and contours that tie bodies to their setting ([Lumen on *Le Bonheur de vivre*](https://courses.lumenlearning.com/suny-arthistory2/chapter/matisse-bonheur-de-vivre/)); complementaries side by side and impasto in proportion to feeling (`docs/styles/post-impressionism.md`); and posture from the emotion research: head back and a hand slicking wet hair reads as joy, where forearms raised in defence would read as fear (`docs/research/emotion.md`).
