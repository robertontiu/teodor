# Journal — Teodor Lisk

**Sketchbook.** Two thumbnails. A figure from behind, planted, fists down, against a sky that has caught fire, with a chair knocked over at his feet; and a raised arm thrust into a radiating burst. The second is a poster; the first is a man in a room, and the room reacting to him. I take the first: the body tells the passerby before the color does, and the chair on its back says something already happened.

**1. Block-in.** Bare oxblood ground. I threw the wall in as strokes leaving one point behind where the head will be, and laid the floor dark and low. The whole upper canvas should push outward, like pressure behind a lid.
**2. The furnace.** The center was a dark hole and the floor too red. I put the hottest orange right where the head will stand, so the body will be cut out against it, and pressed the floor down to near black.
**3. Breaking the petals.** It had become a chrysanthemum, decorative and soft, which is the opposite of what I want. Knife slabs at clashing angles through the hot center, and dark blood-red slashed around the edges to close the room in.
**4. Fields instead of a flower.** I gave up the burst and stacked three fields: a heavy crimson ceiling, a scorching red middle, the black floor. Strokes all lean uphill and sideways, restless, but the masses are now quiet enough for a body to stand in front of them.
**5. Closing the room.** Everything was the same loud value, which deafens rather than presses. Dark glazes over the ceiling and both sides, so the red becomes a pressure coming from the middle, held in by the dark.
**6. The man.** Now the one thing a passerby knows in his own body: a man seen from behind, legs set wide, shoulders hiked up to his ears, arms held stiff away from his sides with the hands closed. Laid in as one dark shape against the hottest part of the wall.
**7. Solid body.** The first pass came out as a jointed puppet, the red leaking through every gap. I filled the shape with short dense marks and joined the head to the shoulders: no neck to speak of, the head sunk into the shoulders.
**8. Quieting the body.** The silhouette glittered with pale flecks; it looked like rock, not a man. A dark glaze over the whole figure so it becomes one undivided weight.
**9. Knife over the figure.** The flecks are ridges of thick paint catching the skylight. I scraped the body flat with the knife in dark, so it reads as a cut-out of night against the fire.
**10. Daylight between arm and body.** The arms had melted into the torso, so the hands meant nothing. I cut a sliver of the burning wall between each arm and the body, and knotted the hands into hard balls.
**11. Wider gaps.** The slivers were too timid to read at a distance. Opened them so the arms hang visibly apart from the body, held there by effort.
**12. The floor.** The bottom was a smear without a plane. A band of dull reflected red where the floor meets the wall, then boards running toward the light and going black near us, so we stand behind him in the dark.
**13. Floor again, held in.** My boards ran up into the wall and drowned his legs. Laid the floor flat and clipped, a low red band and black below; I will rebuild the legs on top.
**14. Legs back.** Rebuilt the planted legs and the two knotted hands over the new floor. He has to stand on it, not float in it.
**15. Scraped flat.** Knife over the body again so the legs are as solid as the shoulders.
**16. Burying the ghosts.** The botched floor left dark posts standing in the wall like a crowd. Painted the wall back over them, strokes leaning in toward him from both sides, and reopened the light between his arms and body.
**17. Softening the box.** The patches had a hard top edge like a sideboard. I dragged them up into the wall with a dry brush and glazed the outer sides dark again, so the heat stays centered on him.
**18. Crisp again.** The dragging blurred his outline into the heat, and a blurred man is a man dissolving, not a man holding. Scraped the figure back to a hard edge and reopened the arm gaps.
**19. Darker man.** He had gone rust-colored, half of the fire. A near-black glaze so he is the one thing in the room the heat does not get into.
**20. The chair.** A wooden chair knocked onto its back in the near right, legs in the air. Everyone has seen a chair that someone stood up from too fast; it tells the story the man's back can't.
**21. Light on the wood.** Dark on dark, the chair vanished. I let the fire behind catch the upper edges of the legs and the seat rim; a few thin hot lines and it stands up out of the floor, upside down.
**22. Heat off his shoulders.** The wall had gone soft, a sunset, and a sunset forgives. Thick, straight, loaded strokes thrown outward from his body in every direction, like a shock wave off him into the room.
**23. No frame.** The burst stopped at a hard box like a picture inside the picture. Darker, duller strokes carry the same outward motion into the corners, so the whole room is under the blast, not just a window of it.
**24. Falling off into dark.** Glazed the outer third down so the brightness grades away from him instead of ending at a line. The light belongs to his body now, the edges of the room are smoke.
**25. Stitching the seam.** Strokes laid right across the old box edge, half in and half out, in the middle reds. The burst should thin out, not be cut.
**26. Light on the floor.** In the value study his legs drowned in the floor. A reflected pool of red on the boards behind him, so the stance, feet wide, cuts sharply against it.
**27. Dissolving the disc.** The glow came down as a hard oval, a table nobody asked for. Dragged it up into the wall, down into the dark, and sideways, until it is just warmth lying on the boards.
**28. Standing him up again.** The smear swallowed his legs and hands. Rebuilt the planted legs, the forearms and the two fists, dense and flat, so the stance is the hardest thing in the picture.
**29. The chair, harder.** It had become a faint ladder. Thicker dark legs, and the light running along their tops to the cut ends of the feet, so you can count four legs in the air.
**30. A broken plate.** Pale shards scattered on the dark floor at the lower left, the only near-white in the picture. Something was thrown; the eye goes from his fists to the pieces.
**31. Shards with corners.** The first marks were dashes, not pieces of a plate. Built each fragment as a small sharp triangle, pale and catching the red.
**32. Shoulders up.** He stood too politely, a man waiting for a bus. Built the muscle between neck and shoulder up to the ears, so the head sinks between them, the way a body gathers itself before it does something.
**33. One dark.** The body was mottled with cold greens and blues, like a bruise. Glazed it to a single warm black so nothing distracts from the shape.
**34. The edge of the light.** Backlight running hard along the skull, over the hiked shoulders, down the arms to the knuckles. A crisp hot line is a held breath; it makes the body look clenched rather than merely dark.
**35. Stronger edge.** The rim was a whisper. Laid it thicker on the crown, the hunched shoulders and the knuckles, broken where the form turns, so the light grips him at the points of tension.
**36. Air between arm and ribs.** Opened the light again between his arms and his body. The arms held off the sides are what make a passerby's own shoulders tighten.
**37. Knuckles and slats.** Broke the light along the outside of each fist so they read as hands squeezed shut, not mittens; and put the spindles into the chair back where it lies on the floor, so no one mistakes it for a ladder.
**38. His shadow toward us.** With the fire behind him, his shadow has to come forward, straight at whoever stands in front of the picture. A dark wedge from his feet to the bottom edge, so the viewer is in its path.
**39. It comes off him.** The hottest light sat to one side, as if the fire were in the room and he were only watching it. Loaded yellow-orange strokes now leave his skull and shoulders, so the heat is his.
**40. Cracks.** The rays round his head had turned into a halo, too serene, almost holy. I cut dark jagged lines out from behind his skull through them, like plaster splitting, so the light is breaking something instead of blessing it.
**41. Deeper cracks.** They whispered. Went over them again in near-black, wider, so from across the room the wall reads as split.
**42. Clean legs, one shadow.** Up close the legs were bruised with stray blues and greens and the shadow was a row of ghosts. Scraped the legs to warm black and laid the shadow in as a single dark tongue running at us.
**43. Where wall meets floor.** The room had no corner, so the man stood in weather rather than in a house. A thin hot line where the floor ends, broken by his body, and he is home, which is worse.
**44. One body, again.** The floor line had cut across his hips and the torso was still mottled cold. Scraped the inside of the figure to one warm black, leaving the burning rim, and reopened the light between arms and ribs.
**45. Wrists.** A stray thread of floor-light was cutting both wrists. Closed it with dark so the arm runs unbroken into the fist.
**Stop.** Looked from the door of the studio: the stance, the hands, the chair on its back, the pieces of the plate, and the room going off around him. Another stroke would only be explaining. Forty-five passes.
