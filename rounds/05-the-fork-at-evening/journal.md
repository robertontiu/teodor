# Journal — the crossroads commission

## Level 1 (1x1)
Before: a toned ground, warm umber-grey, nothing yet. I feel the whole thing as two lights of equal worth with a small dark person between them, and I want the ground to hold neither yet.
Intended: the single cell got broad flat horizontal sweeps of umber, slate and near-black, then a thin lighter veil over the upper half so a sky can come later; neutral temperature so that neither side wins from the start.
After: a dim, even ground with a lighter top; nothing leans.

## Level 2 (2x2)
Before: a murky ground with stray greens and a blue patch; it holds nothing yet, which is right, but it is too uncertain in temperature.
Intended: cut the canvas into two equal worlds side by side. 0,0 a lamplit amber field, 0,1 a silver-blue morning field at the same value, so neither light is the stronger; 1,0 dark warm earth and 1,1 dark cool earth-and-water below them. The split runs straight down the middle, where someone will stand.
After: (see level 3 entry)
After (level 2): the two worlds appeared, but the overlap threw long strokes across the seam; warm left and cool right are there, muddled at the edges.

## Level 3 (4x3)
Before: two colored halves fighting through loose strokes; no place, no horizon, no road. A passerby would see only colored weather.
Intended: composition all at once. 0,0 and 1,0 amber sky; 0,2 and 1,2 silver-blue sky; the middle column split exactly in two clipped halves (warm 300-450, cool 450-600) so the seam falls where the person will stand; row 2 dark warm land left and dark cool land right, again split in the middle; row 3 dark ground across. Two glows of equal brightness low on the horizon, gold at left in 1,0 and pale silver at right in 1,2. A pale road rising from the bottom of 3,1 and splitting into a Y: the left arm ochre toward the gold, the right arm blue-grey toward the silver.
After: the gold/silver split carries well and the Y of the roads shows faintly, but the strokes are long and choppy and there is still no horizon.

## Level 4 (6x4)
Before: I feel the pull of both lights already, but there is nobody being pulled, and no places to be pulled toward.
Intended: shorter flat strokes re-state warm sky in cols 0-1 and cool sky in cols 2-3 of rows 0-1; row 2 brightens to the two horizon lights, with a darker water band in 2,2-2,3; rows 3-5 darken to earth, warm left, cool right. Motif jobs in parallel: the road and its two arms re-laid with clips, a small dark house with a pitched roof at the end of the left arm (2,0), a low boat hull on the water at the end of the right arm (2,3), and a dark standing figure seen from behind exactly at the fork in 3,1, head over the seam.
After: the figure stands at the fork and the split reads, but the figure is ragged, the house a smudge, and the horizon still soft.

## Level 5 (8x6)
Before: the two lights pull evenly and the figure stands on the seam, but the figure is ragged, the horizon soft, the house unreadable, the foreground a mess of dashes. In three seconds a passerby gets the two colors but may not get that a person is stuck between two good places.
Intended: rows 0-2 softened by smudge then refreshed with broken fan strokes, amber in cols 0-1 and silver in cols 4-5, so the sky becomes two calm fields; row 3 brightens to both horizon lights with a crisp dark land edge on the left and a pale water band on the right; rows 4-5 smudged warm/cool; rows 6-7 glazed dark to push the foreground down. Motif jobs in parallel: the house knifed solid at the end of the left road, a boat hull and a pale sail at the end of the right road, the figure refilled dark with a warm rim on its left side and a cool rim on its right.
After: the sky fields and the horizon lights came good, the boat's sail caught the light, but the smudge in 4,3 ran across the figure's neck and the foreground glaze left coloured dashes; the house is still scattered.
After (level 6): the house now stands solid with one lit window and a lit door, the sail is white against the silver, the foreground went quiet and dark, and the figure is solid, though flecked with light from the impasto and with a pale blotch on the head.

## Level 6 (12x8)
Before: the place finally existed in my head but not on the canvas: no solid house, a broken figure, and a noisy foreground pulling the eye down. 
Intended: rows 8-11 covered with quiet dark horizontal planks, warm in cols 0-3 and cool in cols 4-7, the bottom road re-laid in 8,3; rows 6-7 re-stated as warm earth left and cool earth right; the two road arms re-laid ochre and blue-grey. Motifs in parallel: the house filled solid with a gold window and a lit open door (5,1), the sail and hull of the boat (5,6), the figure and head refilled dark with the neck rebuilt (6,3). Many sky cells left alone; they already did their work.

## Level 7 (free accents)
Before: everything is in place: gold house left, white sail right, a person on the seam. But the person was speckled and had a pale blot for a head, and nothing in the paint said the person is pulled both ways at once, only that they stand between.
Intended: knife the head and body flat and dark; let the arms hang slightly open toward both sides; a thin gold rim on the figure's left edge and a thin silver rim on its right, so each light claims half of the body; two thin shadows running back from the feet in a V, one cast by each light; a gleam under the boat and a spill of gold from the house door down its road; a soft glaze bloom around the window and around the sail.
After: the figure is now one solid dark shape lit gold on one side and silver on the other, and the two shadows open from its feet like the road ahead of it, splitting.
