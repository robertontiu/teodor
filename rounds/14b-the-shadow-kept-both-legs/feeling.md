# The Shadow Kept Both Legs

**Commission:** theme "High", as in the high of a drug; style free (round 14). Painted by **Claude Fable 5.2**, in parallel with an Opus 5.5 painter given the identical brief (see `14-the-field-turns-over`).

**Teodor's feeling** (in his words, written before he painted):

> **Unfastened: the bliss of being lifted out of my own weight, and the small cold fact that my shadow has stayed on the floor.**
> 
> It means this to me: the high is the moment the body stops being a burden and the whole floor of the world starts to breathe in colour under you, and you love it, all of it; and yet one dark shape down there keeps your exact outline and waits. I want the visitor to feel the lift in the stomach first (joy, loud, bright), and the shadow half a second later.

**Teodor, on finishing:**

> I wanted the lift in the stomach first and the cold fact half a second later, so I painted a man in white floating face-up over a floor that hums pink, turquoise and lemon, seen from straight above. The last critic called him a cut-out pasted on wallpaper, and he was right, so in this phase the floor got into him: solid white at the chest, tinted at the hips, half pattern at the knees, all pattern at the toes. Up in the dim corner his shadow lies flat on the tiles in cold ultramarine and keeps the whole of him, both legs, which the body no longer does. I refused the critic's dark tunnel with a glowing core, because that is my old radiant burst, and I painted out the furrow strings that two critics running had called scratches.
