# Critique 1 (a fresh critic; knew only the theme "High", as in the high of a drug; read crit_1.png)

**1) First three seconds:** Vertigo, a giddy free-fall. Then it turns flat: a toy dropped onto a tablecloth.

**2) What makes me feel that:** The upside-down figure with its arms flung wide, and the warped pink-and-teal checkerboard tilting under it. The rainbow streak of wiggly lines works like a contrail or a rush through the spine. The big detached blue shadow suggests height and floating. The acid pink against teal is loud and chemical, which fits the theme.

**3) Weaknesses:**
- **Composition:** The figure sits on a dead-centre diagonal and the streak cuts the canvas almost in half. Nothing pulls the eye except the white body.
- **Values:** The background is one mid-value from edge to edge. Only the white figure and the navy shadow break it. There's no depth and no light source.
- **Edges:** The whole background is the same choppy square dabs. The figure's edges are soft and smeared without purpose.
- **Drawing:** The legs are parallel tubes, like fork prongs, and the feet are orange cylinders. The hands are stubs, the head is an orange blob in a navy splat, and there's no face. The shadow's shape doesn't match the pose.
- **Gesture:** The body is stiff, rigid and symmetrical, like a mannequin falling. A high is abandon, but this body shows none.
- **Color:** The colors are saturated everywhere at equal intensity, so nothing climaxes. The skin orange is muddy.
- **Finish:** It reads as an unresolved digital sketch.

**4) The one change:** Redraw the figure's gesture. Arch the back, throw the head back with a visible face (eyes closed, mouth open), splay the legs unevenly and let the limbs twist and foreshorten. Right now the body says "falling object". It needs to say "ecstatic surrender". Everything else in the painting would start to read as the person's experience instead of just decoration.

(Studio manager's note: the critic felt vertigo and the chemical loudness, so the ground and palette work. The body is what fails: a stiff, symmetrical mannequin reads as a falling object, which is your open question answered. Generic mannequin people are one of your listed habits; this is the place to break it. The square-dab floor is another.)
