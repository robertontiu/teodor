# Critique 2 (a fresh critic; knew only the theme "High", as in the high of a drug; read crit_2.png)

**1) First three seconds:** I feel a giddy weightlessness. It's playful rather than euphoric, closer to a fun-house fall than a rush.

**2) What causes it:** The figure is upside down with arms flung open. His eyes are closed and his mouth is slack, which reads as surrender. The warped checkerboard tunnel pulls the eye toward a yellow seam at the center, and the saturated pink and cyan clash hard enough to buzz. The dark blue shadow figure, kicking above him, adds a small doubled sense of falling.

**3) Weaknesses:**
- **Composition:** The figure sits on a diagonal, but the space has no clear vanishing point. The tunnel's warp is even across the canvas, so there's no vortex to fall into. The shadow figure fills the upper left awkwardly.
- **Values:** Almost everything is the same mid-value, very saturated. The white suit is the only light shape, and the background has no darks to give depth.
- **Edges:** They're uniform everywhere. The figure has a hard cut-out outline with a pale halo, so he looks pasted onto the background instead of moving through it.
- **Drawing and gesture:** The limbs are stiff, tube-like and evenly weighted. The hands and feet are tiny pink mitts. The torso has no twist, so the pose reads as a doll placed on a diagonal, not a body letting go.
- **Color:** The checkerboard dominates. The figure's white is chalky and doesn't pick up any of the colored light around it.
- **Finish:** The speed lines are scratchy and look arbitrary. The hair is a busy mass of hatching that competes with the face.

**4) The one change:** Build real depth in the tunnel. Make it a dark vortex that recedes toward a glowing core, and let the checks get larger and blurrier toward the edges. Then let that colored light wash over and dissolve the figure's edges. The fall would gain depth and pull, and the high would come from his drop into the light rather than from loud wallpaper.

(Studio manager's note: the critic read the surrender (closed eyes, slack mouth) and the buzz of the colors; "playful, a fun-house fall" is the reading to beat, and your cold thread, the shadow left behind, came across only as a second faller. Take the edge points: let the floor's colored light wash into the white figure and dissolve its edges, so he moves through the space instead of being pasted on it; that is also your missing "dissolving". Be wary of the advice to build a dark tunnel toward a glowing core: a radiant burst at the centre is one of your listed habits. Give the hands, feet and torso a real twist.)
