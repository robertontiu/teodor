# Journal: HIGH (round 14)

## Conception
- Read habits.md, looked at the contact sheet. It is a wall of umber rooms, lit doors, small backs, red-black bursts. Even Black Noon is black, white and red. Nothing in fourteen pictures is pink, or green, or seen from above, or happy.
- HABITS I BREAK ON PURPOSE (five):
  1. Eye-level, middle-distance, centred -> a plumb bird's-eye view straight down at a floor, the figure large, head-down in the frame, diagonal, cropped by the edge.
  2. One warm light in a dark brown room / amber against violet -> a high-key picture with no brown and no black: hot pink against mint, lemon against turquoise, the only dark a saturated ultramarine-violet shadow.
  3. Lone figure from behind in silhouette -> the figure is face-up and open to us, arms flung, big, lit flat from the front; the SILHOUETTE role is given to the shadow instead, as a second character.
  4. Hushed, melancholy, bittersweet-with-grief -> loud joy first (lift, vibration), with only a thin cold thread after it; not elegy.
  5. Uniform square dabs / soft filter -> three distinct hands: hard flat tile-marks laid like mosaic for the floor, long wet sweeps for the body, liner tremor (Michaux) at the furrow; planned hard edges at body and shadow.
  Also: no rectangle of light, no central radiant burst, no red-and-black.
- RESEARCH (WebSearch/WebFetch on Michaux's mescaline drawings; Riley and Hockney from what I know of them):
  1. Michaux: the drawings are dense, tangled seismograph scribbles around "a furrow without beginning or end"; he spoke of a vibratory motion that stayed in him afterwards. I take the FURROW: one seam running through the whole canvas where the world is pulled thin, and a trembling liner hand along it.
  2. Bridget Riley, Movement in Squares: a plain checkerboard whose squares narrow toward a fold until the flat plane seems to buckle. I take the COMPRESSED CHECKER as my floor, so the ground itself is doing the drug's work and I need no spirals or melting clocks.
  3. Hockney's pools (and Peter Getting Out...): the body seen from above in flat high-key colour, turquoise and pink, hard Californian light, the pattern of the water more alive than the person. I take the PLUMB VIEW and the palette key, and the permission to be flat and bright.
- Feeling written to feeling.md: unfastened; lift first, shadow after.
- Idea A (scene v1, v4, v5): plumb view, a white figure floating head-down over a checker floor that narrows into a furrow; the shadow thrown clear of the body. Idea B (scene v2): the coming down, a small curled figure on a huge pale floor with its shadow attached. Idea C (schema v3): pure abstraction, a Michaux furrow of tremor lines on acid colour.
- CHOSEN: A. B is quiet and sad, which is my old self in new clothes. C is handsome but a visitor gets vibration with no body to feel it in. A gives the lift in the stomach in one glance (a body with daylight between it and its shadow is floating, everybody knows that from below aeroplanes and birds), and the shadow carries the second, colder beat. Body made white (the lightest thing, weightless), floor mid-value pink/turquoise (dominant, loud), shadow ultramarine-violet (the only dark, the weight left behind). Limited palette: pink, turquoise, lemon at the furrow, white, ultramarine, a little coral skin.
- Technical note: the "floor" is a wall in the blockout, seen frontally, the figure standing 0.9 m before it; the picture is rolled 158 degrees so the figure hangs head-down. The checker and furrow are computed over the render (compose.py). Final blockout = scene_A3.json (5th version), schema v5.
