# Journal — round 15: LOVE, NOW AND IN THE AFTERLIFE

## Conception notes
Looked at the contact sheet: dark doorways, lone backs, amber against violet, a central burst, and lately one central figure in a radial field. Every one of them hushed and alone. This theme gives me two people, and I want their faces and hands.

Research (Stanley Spencer's Cookham Resurrection and Reunion; the Etruscan Sarcophagus of the Spouses; Bonnard painting Marthe in the bath for years, even after her death; Lucian Freud's beds seen from above; Alice Neel's frank faces and contour):
1. From the Etruscan spouses: the couple reclining together on their own tomb lid, SMILING, at ease, his arm around her. Death treated as a long dinner together, not a tragedy. I take the tone: matter-of-fact, almost amused.
2. From Spencer: the afterlife painted with ordinary people in ordinary clothes and ordinary bodies, tilted ground plane seen steeply from above, figures packed close to the frame. I take the steep top-down viewpoint and the tight crop.
3. From Bonnard and Freud: the bed and the bath as the place of a whole married life; high-key whites full of broken colour (lilac, lemon, cerulean, rose) instead of brown darkness; flesh painted as flesh, old and particular. From Neel: a firm drawn contour on the faces and hands.

Habits I mean to break: (1) lone figure from behind -> two people seen face-up, faces and hands doing the work; (2) eye-level middle distance centered -> straight down from above, cropped hard, bodies too big for the frame; (3) warm lamp in a brown room / amber vs violet -> cool white morning daylight, linen whites, cobalt and lemon; (4) hushed melancholy / bittersweet -> plain certainty and delight; (5) uniform soft marks -> a planned edge hierarchy, crisp drawn hands.

## Phase 3 — finalization
Critique 2 recorded: the critic felt gentle, sleepy warmth; the hands work, and found my idea alone (one asleep and smiling, one awake and laughing toward him). Weak: the blue band fills the bottom 40% with no meaning and reads as a grid of stacked rectangles; everything above is one light value, no darks behind the heads; edges uniformly soft; faces flat beige ovals with line features, like a vector cartoon; clothes stamped dabs; gesture stiff and frontal. The manager: the cozy reading is near my certainty, the delight needs her laugh to read as a laugh; the quilt grid, the line faces and the dabs must be painted out. I agree with all of it. The quilt as I pieced it was a quilt-maker's idea, not a painter's: it shouts in squares. Cloth over two old bodies lying side by side is the truer thing: their legs go on under it together.
