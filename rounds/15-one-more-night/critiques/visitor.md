# Visitor (a fresh critic; knew only the theme "Love, now and in the afterlife: a love that never ends"; read final.png, then the contact sheet of past work)

**1) First three seconds:** Gentle, slightly comic tenderness. It reads as an old couple in bed holding hands, not as an "afterlife". It is sweet, but it doesn't move me much.

**2) Why:** The clasped hands sit at the meeting point of white and blue, and they are the only place where the drawing is actually worked out. Her laughing open mouth and his closed-eye smile carry the warmth. The white-on-white pillows and lavender haze give a faint heavenly feel, but that comes across as a hospital ward or a cloud more than as eternity.

**3) Weaknesses:**
- **Composition:** The blue blanket is a flat, empty block covering the bottom 45% of the picture. The figures are squeezed into a band at the top and crowded against the left edge.
- **Values:** Almost everything is a high-key pale value. The only darks are the dot clusters beside his torso, which read as noise rather than shadow, so there is no focal contrast.
- **Edges:** Every edge is equally soft and blobby. The hands are the one exception.
- **Drawing:** The heads are flat peach discs with stamped-on features, and his head looks like a bald mask on a hood. The torsos are shapeless. The hands look like segmented tubes.
- **Gesture:** Both bodies are stiff and frontal. They don't lean toward each other, so the love lives only in the hands.
- **Color:** A pastel blue-lavender-peach palette with no temperature shift. The skin looks like plastic.
- **Digital look:** Yes. The round dab stamps are repeated in rows, especially the dark dots and the blue blanket. The swirl texture in the background looks like a filter. Her hair is drawn in thin, uniform vector-like strokes, and the signature is a typeset font.

**4) One change:** Cut the blanket down to about a quarter of the canvas and pull the figures down and toward each other. Have them lean in, foreheads or shoulders touching, with a real value structure: a dark field around them and the light falling on the faces and joined hands. That would put the emotion in the bodies and the light rather than in two stamped smiles.

**5) Versus past work:** In subject and palette it is a departure. It's the first pale, high-key, tender interior, with no silhouettes, no glowing doorway and no dark field. In handling it is more of the same: the same round-dab stamping and soft blur as 06, 07 and 14, the same mask-like disc faces as 12 and 13, figures placed at the centre and facing front, and the identical signature.
