# Critique 2 (a fresh critic; knew only the theme "Love, now and in the afterlife: a love that never ends"; read crit_2.png)

**1) What I feel in the first three seconds:** Gentle warmth, a little wistful, but mostly soft and sleepy. It reads more like a mild, cozy scene than a love that outlasts death.

**2) What causes that feeling:**
- The clasped hands in the centre are the emotional anchor, and they work.
- The man's closed eyes and slight smile suggest peace, maybe death or sleep. The woman looks at him open-mouthed and alive. That contrast between the living and the departed is the strongest idea in the picture.
- The pale cloud-whites, the halo-like hair and the blue "water" below suggest heaven or a crossing.

**3) Weaknesses:**
- **Composition:** The blue band fills the bottom 40% and holds no meaning. The figures are cut off and the heads sit high, so the painting is split horizontally into "people" and "blue rectangles".
- **Values:** Almost everything above the band is the same light mid-high value. Figures, clothes and clouds merge, the faces don't stand out, and nothing is truly dark except the blue.
- **Edges:** Uniformly soft and mushy, with no hard edge where the eye should land (face contours, the hands).
- **Drawing:** The faces are flat beige ovals with thin line features, like a cartoon placed on top of the painting. Her mouth and eyes look pasted on. The bodies are shapeless masses.
- **Gesture:** Stiff and frontal. They sit side by side and don't lean toward each other.
- **Color:** Pleasant but timid. The flat flesh tone clashes with the textured surroundings.
- **Digital look:** The bottom is clearly a pattern: stacked horizontal rectangles in a grid, plus blurry smudges that look like a filter. The "dab" strokes on the clothing look like a stamped brush texture, not paint. The faces look like a vector graphic.

**4) The one change:** Repaint the faces and hands as real painted form, with light, shadow and a darker value behind them, and turn the heads toward each other. Then crop or remove most of the blue rectangle band so the two of them, and the hands, carry the picture.

(Studio manager's note: real progress: the hands work, and the critic found your idea on their own, one asleep and smiling, one awake and laughing toward him.
