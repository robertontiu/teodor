# Critique 1 (a fresh critic; knew only the theme "Love, now and in the afterlife: a love that never ends"; read crit_1.png)

**1) First three seconds:** a soft, tender meeting, a little dreamlike. Then I get confused, because the figures read as blurred dolls more than people.

**2) What makes me feel that:** the cool blue figure on the left leans toward the pale white figure on the right, and its hand reaches toward her hand at the center. The cloudy lilac and white sky suggests heaven. The deep blue band at the bottom, with yellow and white dots, reads as a night sky or the earth below.

**3) Weaknesses:**
- **Composition:** The bottom band is cut by a flat, nearly straight horizontal line. It looks pasted on and splits the picture in two. The hands, which should be the focal point, sit low and get lost in the middle of the canvas.
- **Values:** The top two-thirds is all mid-light pastel, so nothing stands out. The only real contrast is that saturated blue band, which pulls the eye away from the couple.
- **Edges:** They are uniformly choppy everywhere, with no hierarchy between hard and soft. The faces are smeared and the features are unreadable.
- **Drawing and gesture:** The heads are blobs with pink scribbles. The hand at center right looks like a flat orange paddle. The left figure's second hand floats with no arm.
- **Color:** The saturated blue and lemon yellow clash with the pastel palette.
- **Digital look:** The sky is made of many uniform, same-size rectangular dabs laid in parallel. That reads as an algorithmic stroke filter, not paint. The dots in the blue band look like stamped graphic clip-art.

**4) The one change:** Rebuild the two hands and the faces as the clear focal point. Give them the darkest darks and the sharpest edges, with drawn fingers actually touching. Let the rest of the canvas go soft. At the same time, dissolve the hard edge of the blue band into the figures' robes so the two worlds blend into each other instead of being stacked.

(Studio manager's note: the critic felt the tenderness, but read two standing figures in heaven, not an old couple lying in bed seen from above: the bed, the pillows and the quilt don't read yet, and the quilt's top edge is a ruler line. Your "certainty and a little delight" hasn't arrived; faces with real features (eyes, a smile) are where it lives. Take the hands-and-faces point fully, drawn with deliberate strokes. The rectangle-dab "sky" (the sheets) and the stamped quilt dots read as a filter and clip-art: by the rule, the canvas must look painted.)
