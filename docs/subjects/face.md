# Face (layer 1)

The head and face as they really are: a skull with a few big masses on it, planes that take light in a fixed order, features that sit at predictable places, and expressions that are whole-face patterns of muscle action, not drawn-on marks. Researched 2026-11. Every value names its source; values marked **judgement** have no published source and are editorial choices for Teodor. Read with `figure` (where the head sits on the body), `hair` and `light-shadow`. The studio's plane-built heads (`scene` people, `docs/MANUAL.md`) follow this page.

## 1. What it is, structurally

**A ball and a face.** The cranium is "a simple ball shape" with a flat side plane where the ball is cut off at the temple ([Loomis method, Fine Art Tutorials](https://finearttutorials.com/guide/loomis-method/)). The face hangs in front of and below the ball: brow, cheekbones, the nose wedge, the barrel of the teeth (the muzzle) and the jaw.

**Proportions (a head one unit tall, crown to chin).**
- The face divides into thirds: "from the hairline to the brow", "from the brow to the nose" and from the nose to the chin; "the hairline is halfway between the brow and the top of the head" (Loomis, Fine Art Tutorials). The studio uses hairline 0.20 below the crown, brow 0.47, nose base 0.74, chin 1.0 (**judgement** fit of the thirds to a full head).
- The eyes sit at about half the head's height (**judgement**, the common teaching; the Loomis source above places them in the middle third without a number).
- The mouth line sits about a third of the way from the nose base to the chin (**judgement**).
- The ears lie between the brow line and the nose line, behind the middle of the head (Loomis places the ear in the side plane of the ball).
- The head is about 0.65 as wide as it is tall and about 0.85 as deep (**judgement**, used in the builder).
- Hands are as long as the face ([Body proportions, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_proportions)): a useful check whenever a hand touches a face.

**The planes.** Painters simplify the head into flat planes so they can decide where light turns into shadow. John Asaro's "Planes of the Head" is the best-known model: a sculpted "tool for rendering the human head", whose left side shows "the basic structure of the head's planes as seen in a round younger face" and the right side "a more complex structure that is characteristic of maturity" ([1stDibs listing of the Asaro head](https://www.1stdibs.com/furniture/decorative-objects/sculptures/planes-head-john-asaro/id-f_1136476/); history in [Drawing the Portrait, ch. 3](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/drawing-the-portrait/9798888141427/xhtml/chapter3.xhtml)). Loomis's simplest breakdown has "front, side, and bottom planes" ([James Gurney, Plane heads](https://jamesgurney.substack.com/p/plane-heads)). The planes that matter most for value: the forehead front and its turn to the temple; the brow ridge and the socket under it; the cheekbone corner where the front of the face turns to the side; the nose's front, sides and underside; the muzzle; the chin and the underside of the jaw.

## 2. Seen from different viewpoints

- **Front:** symmetry is the trap; tilt and light break it. The wings of the nose line up roughly with the inner corners of the eyes (**judgement**).
- **Three-quarter:** the far eye shortens and tucks behind the nose bridge; the far cheek's contour shows the cheekbone corner; the near side plane of the ball becomes large (Loomis side plane).
- **Profile:** a chain of forms: forehead slope, brow, the dip at the nose root, the nose's projection, upper lip, lower lip, chin, all set on a line that leans back from forehead to chin (**judgement**).
- **From below (worm's-eye):** the underside planes dominate: the jaw's underside, the nose's underside with the nostrils, the brow's underside. Features crowd toward the top of the head; the chin and jaw become large (**judgement**, follows from foreshortening; see `figure`).
- **From above:** the forehead and the top of the nose dominate, the mouth and chin shrink, the eyes sit low on the head shape (**judgement**).
- **Head thrown back (laughter, a shout):** the face foreshortens; seen from below, the mouth may be the only feature clearly visible. Pull the camera to the side of the face or lower the throw so cheeks and eyes stay readable (lesson from the studio's own tests).

## 3. Under light

- **Choose the light, then assign one value per plane.** "I chose a light direction first and then found my planes and assigned them their values"; "it's either one value or the other, light or shadow, no guesswork"; "using just two or three values for halftone is enough to describe subtle form changes" ([Marco Bucci, painting the head from imagination](https://www.proko.com/lesson/painting-the-head-from-imagination-lighting-without-reference-with-marco-bucci/notes)).
- **Where the shadows go under a high light:** "eye sockets and their contents are often almost entirely in shadow"; the nose casts a shadow across the face; "the lips will also cast a shadow down onto the chin"; and places that get little bounced light go darker still (ambient occlusion) (Bucci, same page).
- **Colour zones of the face:** forehead cooler and lighter, the middle third (cheeks, nose, ears) warmer and redder, the lower third cooler and, in men, greyer with beard (**judgement**, the common portrait-painting teaching; the studio's heads tint these zones slightly).
- **Eyes are forms, not marks.** The eyeball is a sphere under a lid; the upper lid's thickness and its cast shadow give the dark accent we read as "the eye". Painting a dark outline around the eye makes a mask (**judgement**, matches the round-16 critics: "features drawn on").

## 4. Variation

- **Age.** Children: a bigger cranium over a smaller face, so the eyes sit lower on the head; a small nose; full cheeks ([Tuts+, drawing different ages](https://design.tutsplus.com/tutorials/human-anatomy-fundamentals-drawing-different-ages--cms-21905): "the face takes up less of the head" in children; baby eyes are "wider apart than an adult's"). Old age: the nose tip droops as cartilage is lost and "the earlobe droops"; skin loses elasticity and "hangs at the jaw", cheeks sag into jowls (Tuts+). The Asaro head's mature side has more, smaller planes (1stDibs).
- **Sex.** Men: a stronger brow ridge, a squarer and wider jaw, a larger nose; women: a smoother brow, a narrower jaw, fuller lips (**judgement**, the common teaching; no measured source fetched).
- **Individuality.** No two faces share proportions: vary nose length, jaw width, eye spacing and lip fullness between people in one picture (habit "faces near-identical", `docs/habits.md`).

## 5. Expressions (facial action units)

The Facial Action Coding System names each visible muscle action as an action unit (AU) ([FACS, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facial_Action_Coding_System)):

| AU | name | muscle |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | inner brow raiser | frontalis, medial |
| 2 | outer brow raiser | frontalis, lateral |
| 4 | brow lowerer | corrugator, depressor glabellae and supercilii |
| 5 | upper lid raiser | levator palpebrae superioris |
| 6 | cheek raiser | orbicularis oculi, orbital part |
| 7 | lid tightener | orbicularis oculi, palpebral part |
| 9 | nose wrinkler | levator labii superioris alaeque nasi |
| 10 | upper lip raiser | levator labii superioris |
| 12 | lip corner puller | zygomaticus major |
| 15 | lip corner depressor | depressor anguli oris |
| 17 | chin raiser | mentalis |
| 20 | lip stretcher | risorius with platysma |
| 23 | lip tightener | orbicularis oris |
| 25 / 26 / 27 | lips part / jaw drop / mouth stretch | depressor labii, masseter relaxed / pterygoids, digastric |
| 43 | eyes closed | levator relaxed |

The prototype combinations (same source): happiness 6+12; sadness 1+4+15; surprise 1+2+5B+26; fear 1+2+4+5+7+20+26; anger 4+5+7+23; disgust 9+15+17.

**Smile and laugh.** A genuine (Duchenne) smile is AU6 + AU12, the eyes crinkling with the mouth; a "Pan-Am" smile moves only the mouth ([iMotions, Duchenne smile](https://imotions.com/blog/learning/research-fundamentals/duchenne-smile/)). Laughter is "a Duchenne Display with an open mouth, possibly jaw relaxation, and raised lips", and in intense laughter a frown (AU4) can join; perceived joy followed AU6 intensity, perceived intensity followed AU12 ([Frontiers in Psychology 2014](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.01306/full)). Darwin's description of laughter: mouth corners "drawn much backwards, as well as a little upwards", "the upper lip is somewhat raised", "the cheeks are drawn upwards", "wrinkles are thus formed under the eyes", and "a well-marked naso-labial fold is formed" ([Darwin 1872, ch. 8](https://brocku.ca/MeadProject/Darwin/Darwin_1872_08.html)).

**Fear and surprise.** In fear the brows are "straighter and more horizontal", in surprise "raised and curved"; "the upper eyelid is also lifted higher in fear than in surprise"; "the lips are tensed and stretched in fear" while in surprise the mouth is looser and more open ([Paul Ekman Group, fear](https://www.paulekman.com/emotions/fear/)).

**Sorrow.** "The raising of the inner ends of the eyebrows" makes them oblique, with "transverse furrows ... across the middle part alone of the forehead", and "the corners of the mouth are drawn downwards" ([Darwin 1872, ch. 7](https://brocku.ca/MeadProject/Darwin/Darwin_1872_07.html)).

**Telling a laugh from a scream** (the habit "faces between expression and grimace"): both open the mouth and can close the eyes. The difference is everywhere else.

| | laugh | scream / fear |
|---|---|---|
| cheeks | lifted, bunched under the eyes (AU6) | flat or pulled down |
| eyes | narrowed from below by the cheeks, crow's feet | wide, white above the iris (AU5), or squeezed by AU7 with the brows knit |
| brows | relaxed, at most slightly lowered | raised and drawn together (1+2+4), furrowed forehead |
| mouth corners | up and back (AU12): an upturned crescent, upper teeth showing | stretched sideways and down (AU20): a squarer opening, lower teeth showing |
| nasolabial fold | deep, curving round the lifted cheek | long and straight |
| body | loose, head back or forward, shoulders up and shaking (**judgement**) | braced, recoiling, hands up (**judgement**) |

**Calm sleep:** lids closed and smooth (AU43), brows and mouth slack, lips may part slightly (25), jaw relaxed; no frown, no squeeze (**judgement**: sleep has no FACS prototype).

## 6. How painters observe and construct it

1. The ball and the face plane, tilted and turned to the head's real angle (Loomis).
2. The thirds and the centre line on that form, then the big planes (Asaro).
3. Decide the light; assign one value to each plane: light, two or three halftones, shadow (Bucci).
4. Only then the features, as small planes in those values: the socket's shadow before the eye; the underside of the nose before the nostril; the shadow under the lower lip before the lip line.
5. Expression last, as a change to the masses (cheek, jaw, brow), not as lines drawn on top.

In the studio: build the head in the `scene` blockout with an expression preset (or your own AU mix), check it from a second angle, and paint it from the blockout. The plane shading (`planes`) gives the value grouping of steps 2-3. Plan face strokes in the head's own coordinates (`docs/habits.md`, craft notes).

## 7. Common mistakes

- **The emoticon or mask:** an evenly lit oval with features drawn on as lines. Check: squint; do the socket, the nose's underside and the jaw's underside read as shadow shapes?
- **A grimace that could be a laugh or a scream:** only the mouth was changed. Check the table in section 5: cheeks, eyes, brows and mouth corners must agree.
- **Faces near-identical:** the same proportions on every person.
- **Eyes outlined, iris as a dot:** the eye is a lit sphere under a lid.
- **Features floating:** the mouth not following the curve of the muzzle, eyes not set in the socket.
- **Lost face from below:** a head thrown back from a worm's-eye view hides the expression; turn the head or move the camera.

## 8. Checks for Teodor

1. Squint at the head: do three to five value shapes (light front, shadow side, socket, under-nose, under-jaw) describe a head before any feature?
2. Is the light direction the same on the face as on everything else?
3. Name the expression's action units and check each is visible: for a laugh, lifted cheeks and narrowed eyes as well as an open mouth.
4. Cover the mouth: does the upper face alone still say the feeling? Cover the eyes: does the lower face?
5. Are no two faces in the picture built on the same proportions?
6. Is no feature outlined?
