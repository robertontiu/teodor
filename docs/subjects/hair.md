# Hair (layer 1)

Hair on the head (and the same rules for beards and eyebrows) as it really is: a mass of fibres that sits on the skull, groups into locks, and catches light in a way no other material does. Researched 2026-11. Every value names its source; values marked **judgement** have no published source and are editorial choices for Teodor. Read with `face` (the hairline and the head's thirds) and `light-shadow`.

## 1. What it is, structurally

**The fibre.** A scalp hair is a thin transparent fibre with a pigmented interior and a surface of overlapping cuticle scales that tilt toward the tip. Optically it behaves like "a transparent elliptical cylinder with an absorbing interior and a surface covered with tilted scales" ([Marschner et al. 2003](https://w1.graphics.cornell.edu/pubs/2003/MJC+03.pdf)); measured fibres had a cross-section eccentricity of about 0.7:1 to 0.8:1 (same paper). This is why hair shines in bands and why light hair glows when lit from behind (section 3).

**How much of it.** About 223 terminal hairs per cm² of normal scalp, range 175–300 ([BioNumbers 116431](https://bionumbers.hms.harvard.edu/bionumber.aspx?id=116431), citing Robbins, *Chemical and Physical Behavior of Human Hair*). It grows about 0.35 mm a day, 1 cm a month, 15 cm a year, and 10–15 % of hairs are resting at any time ([StatPearls, Physiology, Hair](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK499948/)). For a painter: hair is never sparse enough to show individual strands across the mass; it is a continuous material with texture.

**The mass sits on the skull, it is not painted on it.** The outer silhouette of the hair is "always larger than the skull because of thickness and volume" ([Skyrye Design, How to draw hair](https://skyryedesign.com/?p=36957)). Draw the skull first and the hair as a shell over it ([Proko, How to draw hair](https://proko.com/course-lesson/how-to-draw-hair/notes): "Think of the hair as a 3 dimensional form, just like the nose and lips"). Thickness of the shell: close-cropped 0.5–1 cm, ordinary short-to-medium hair about 1.5–3 cm above the crown, curly or coily hair far more (**judgement**, from observation; no measured source found).

**Where it grows from.** The hairline bounds the forehead (the top of the face's upper third; see `face`), runs down in front of the ears to the sideburns, behind the ears and across the nape. Hair radiates from a whorl at the crown and falls away from any parting; over the forehead it grows forward, at the sides down and back, at the nape down (**judgement**, observational convention in drawing manuals; the direction matters because the highlight runs across it, section 3).

**Hierarchy of groups.** Hair clumps into locks: ribbons with width, depth and curvature, lit on one side and shaded on the other (Skyrye, paraphrased). Read it in four levels: the whole mass → a few big groups (3–7 at portrait distance, **judgement**) → locks inside each group → a few separate strands at edges and in the light. Design "volumes, not strands" (Proko).

**Curl.** A worldwide study sorted hair into eight shape types using three measurements: curve diameter, curl index and number of waves ([Loussouarn et al. 2007, *Human Biology*](https://digitalcommons.wayne.edu/humbiol/vol79/iss3/1/)); in practice from straight through wavy and curly to tightly coiled. Curl changes the whole structure: straight hair hangs and follows gravity and the skull; wavy hair forms repeated crests; curly and coily hair holds its volume away from the head, and its silhouette is built from many small rounded forms rather than long locks (**judgement** for the description; the types are Loussouarn's).

## 2. Seen from different viewpoints

- **Front:** the mass frames the face; its thickness shows beyond the temples and above the forehead. The parting, if any, is a short foreshortened line on top (**judgement**).
- **Profile:** the mass runs from the forehead hairline over the crown to the nape; the ear sits in front of the back half of the mass; the shell is thickest at the crown and back (**judgement**).
- **Three-quarter:** the far side of the mass overlaps the far temple and cheek; the near side shows the hairline in front of the ear.
- **From above:** the parting and crown whorl are visible; locks radiate from them, so the highlight band becomes a ring or arc around the crown (Skyrye: the highlight "must curve with the roundness of the skull").
- **From below:** the underside of the mass, the nape and the shadow the hair throws on the neck dominate; the top highlight disappears (**judgement**).
- **Distance:** at portrait distance locks and a few strands read; at figure distance the hair is one shape with one shadow and one light, and a soft edge; in a crowd it is a value patch on the head (**judgement**, consistent with Proko's "don't try to draw every single strand").

## 3. Under light

- **Two highlights, not one.** Measured on real fibres, the main highlight (surface reflection, "R") is white, i.e. the colour of the light, and is shifted 5–10° toward the root from the mirror direction; a second, *coloured* highlight (light that enters the fibre and reflects off its back wall, "TRT") is shifted toward the tip, and in a head of hair appears "just above the primary, sometimes appearing more as a colored fringe on the primary than a separate feature". Black hair shows the white highlight but not the coloured one (all from [Marschner et al. 2003](https://w1.graphics.cornell.edu/pubs/2003/MJC+03.pdf)). Paint the main highlight in a near-white of the light's colour, and a narrower band of saturated hair colour beside it on the tip side.
- **The highlight is a band across the hair, following the skull.** Because each fibre shines where its axis is at the right angle to light and eye, the highlight runs across the strands and wraps around the head's curvature. A straight horizontal stripe makes the head look flat (Skyrye). Straight hair: one smooth band, widest over the most prominent curve; wavy: one crest highlight per wave; curly: small scattered highlights on the outer surface of each curl (Skyrye).
- **Backlight.** Light-coloured hair (blond, brown, grey, white) scatters strongly forward, which "causes blond, brown, gray, and white hair to look very bright when lit from behind" (Marschner). A backlit head gets a glowing rim on the hair that is much brighter than the rim on the skin; dark hair gets a thin bright edge only (**judgement** for the dark-hair part).
- **Shadow side.** Treat shadows as flat, connected graphic shapes with low texture and contrast; texture lives in the halftones, which are gradations toward the shadow on one side and the highlight on the other; add dark accents in the shadows to separate locks (Proko). The darkest marks are where one lock passes over another, under the mass at the nape and behind the ears (Skyrye).
- **Cast shadows.** The mass throws a shadow on the forehead, temples and neck; a fringe casts a shadow band across the forehead whose edge is soft and broken (**judgement**).
- **Hair against skin.** Don't outline the hairline: "show some gradations, otherwise it will look like a wig" (Proko). Skin shows through thin hair at the temples and hairline, so the value steps from skin into hair (**judgement**).

## 4. Variation

- **Colour.** Black hair: low diffuse value, form told almost entirely by the white highlight and the edges. Blond and red: a large, coloured diffuse component, translucent edges, a strong coloured secondary highlight (Marschner observed the diffuse background "larger and more colored for the blond hair"). Grey and white: translucent, highlight contrast low, edges glow in backlight (Marschner on light hair; contrast is **judgement**).
- **Age.** Babies: fine, short, sparse, the skull shows through. Adults: full mass. Older people: greying (mixed fibres read as a lighter, cooler, less saturated mass, not as grey paint), thinning at the temples and crown in many men (**judgement**, observational).
- **Type.** Straight, wavy, curly, coily (Loussouarn's eight types). Each changes silhouette, highlight pattern (above) and how blended the surface looks: blend straight and wavy hair lightly, leave curly hair's marks unblended (Skyrye).
- **Length and how it's held.** Short hair follows the skull closely (Skyrye). Long hair hangs from the crown, rests on and breaks over the shoulders, and divides into two or three big falls (**judgement**). Tied hair (ponytail, bun, braid) is pulled into tension lines that converge on the tie point, like drapery to a support point (**judgement**, by analogy with `drapery`).
- **Wet.** Wet materials are darker: water reduces surface reflection and traps scattered light so more is absorbed ([Lekner & Dorf 1988, *Applied Optics*](https://www.wgtn.ac.nz/scps/staff/pdf/darkerwhenwet.pdf); sand's albedo halved from 0.18 to 0.09 when wet). Wet hair is darker and more saturated, clumps into fewer, narrower points, lies flat against the skull, and shows sharp small highlights on the water film (darkening is sourced; the rest is **judgement**).
- **Wind and motion.** The hair streams downwind from its roots: the ends move most, the roots don't move; on the windward side it presses flat against the head and reveals the skull; the leeward silhouette breaks into separate locks and lifting ends; a lock in motion is a tapering ribbon, wider near the root (**judgement**, by analogy with Leonardo on cloth "pressed and clinging to the limbs … by the action of the wind", [Notebooks §391](https://www.fromoldbooks.org/R.bak/section-7/item-391.html)). Running or turning, long hair lags behind the head and swings past it on the stop (**judgement**).
- **Beards, moustaches, eyebrows.** Same order: mass on the form, groups, light. Eyebrows grow up and out from the inner end, then out and down (**judgement**); a beard follows the jaw and has its own thickness over it.

## 5. How painters observe and construct it

Order of work in manuals: a linear lay-in of the mass, then the design of light and shadow, then accents, then texture (Proko). In five steps: skull volume and parting; silhouette as one continuous shape ("do not draw individual strands at this stage"); flat value zones (highlight band, midtones, shadows under layers, behind the ears, at the nape); tones from light to dark; highlights and detail last (Skyrye).

For a painting (**judgement**, consistent with the above):
1. Block the hair as one shape in its shadow value, wider than the skull, with its silhouette designed: a few big groups, one or two places where it breaks into locks.
2. Lay the light shape and halftones in the direction of growth; keep shadows simple.
3. Lay the highlight as a band across the strands, following the skull's curve; then break it where locks overlap; add the narrow coloured secondary band beside it.
4. Soften the silhouette in a few places into the background, keep it firm where it overlaps the face or a dark ground. Grade the hairline into the skin.
5. Last: separations between locks, a few strands only in the light and at the edges, "no more than five to eight per area — the goal is softness at the edge, not a corona of lines" (Skyrye). A strand is a confident tapering stroke from root to tip (Proko: "better to draw a quick confident strand slightly out of place, than a wobbly ugly stroke in the right place").

Squinting is the main way to see the mass: with eyes half closed, hair resolves into a few shapes of light and dark.

## 6. Common mistakes

- **Spaghetti hair:** strands before form. "Ignoring the form and going straight for the texture results in spaghetti hair" (Proko).
- **Wig or helmet:** an outlined hairline (Proko), or a smooth uniform shell with no thickness variation, no breaks in the silhouette and no locks (**judgement**).
- **Hair painted onto the skull:** the mass no wider than the head, so the head looks shaved under a coat of paint (Skyrye on silhouette size).
- **Flat stripe highlight:** a straight band that ignores the skull (Skyrye), or highlights placed along the strands instead of across them (**judgement**, from the optics).
- **Detail in shadow:** strands painted in the shadow mass (Skyrye on short hair; Proko).
- **Repetitive marks:** even parallel strokes all the same width and spacing (Proko: "avoid repetitive strokes"). Teodor's "uniform marks" habit (`docs/habits.md`) shows up here first.
- **A corona of strays** around the whole silhouette (Skyrye).
- **Hair that ignores physics:** long hair that floats with no gravity or wind, or wind that moves the hair one way and the cloth another (**judgement**).
- **Same-hair-for-everyone:** in a group, every head with the same colour, length and shape; hair is one of the strongest carriers of individuality and age (**judgement**; see `crowd`).

## 7. Checks for Teodor

1. Squint: the hair reads as one mass with at most 3–7 big light and shadow shapes, and the silhouette is wider than the skull.
2. The main highlight runs across the strands and curves with the skull; its colour is the light's, with a narrower band of the hair's own saturated colour beside it.
3. No strands or texture inside shadow shapes; strands only in the light and at the silhouette, a handful per area.
4. The hairline is graded into the skin, never outlined; the hair casts a shadow on the forehead or neck where the light demands it.
5. Gravity and wind agree with the rest of the picture (cloth, smoke, trees): the hair hangs, or streams downwind from its roots.
6. Each person's hair differs in type, colour, length and mass.
