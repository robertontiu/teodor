# Figure (layer 1)

The human body as it really is: a proportion canon that shifts with age, sex and build; a gesture that runs through the whole body before any part is drawn; weight that has to land somewhere; and forms that shorten and overlap as they turn toward the eye. Researched 2026-11. Every value names its source; values marked **judgement** have no published source and are editorial choices for Teodor. Read with `face`, `hands-feet`, `drapery` and `light-shadow`. The studio's people (`scene` objects of type `person`, `docs/MANUAL.md`) are built on this page.

## 1. What it is, structurally

**The canon, in heads (one head = crown to chin).**
- "An average person is generally 7-and-a-half heads tall"; 8 heads is the idealised figure and 8½ the heroic one, with the extra length in chest and legs ([Body proportions, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_proportions)).
- "The half-way mark is a line between the greater trochanters", just above the pubic arch; legs (floor to crotch) are "three-and-a-half to four heads long"; arms about three heads; "hands are as long as the face" (same source).
- Landmarks used by the studio's rig for an adult (heads from the floor; **judgement**, the common drawing canon consistent with the above): crown 7.5, chin 6.5, shoulder joints 6.0, nipples about 5.5, elbow and navel about 4.6, hip joints 3.85, wrists 3.4, fingertips mid-thigh, knees 2.0, ankles 0.3. Upper arm 1.45 heads, forearm 1.15, hand 0.75; thigh 1.85, shin 1.6.

**By age.** "At birth the head measures about 4 times into height", "at 5 years ... about 6 times", "in the adult about 7 1/2 times" ([USF, relation of head to height by age](https://etc.usf.edu/clipart/52100/52139/52139_head.htm)). Another teaching gives toddler about 5 heads, "5.5 heads in early childhood, to 6 heads between 7 and 9", adolescents "6.5 to 7 heads" ([Tuts+, drawing different ages](https://design.tutsplus.com/tutorials/human-anatomy-fundamentals-drawing-different-ages--cms-21905)). Children's legs are shorter in proportion: the midpoint of a toddler falls near the navel, not the crotch (**judgement**; the rig's hip-height fraction rises from 0.41 of stature at age 2 to 0.51 in adults). Old age: slumping "makes the neck look shorter and the arms fall lower than usual"; pectorals droop (Tuts+); the rig adds a stoop and a forward head after 55 (**judgement**).

**By sex and build.** The hip-to-shoulder circumference ratio averages 1:1.03 in women and 1:1.18 in men; the female waist-to-hip ratio in art has stayed within about 0.67-0.80 for 2,500 years (Body proportions, Wikipedia). Men: shoulders about two head-heights wide, hips narrower; women: shoulders narrower, hips as wide or wider, the waist higher and narrower (**judgement** for the head-unit widths). Builds (slim, average, heavy, muscular, frail) change the widths of trunk and limbs, not the lengths (**judgement**).

How a body reads as a woman or a man, a child or an old person, and how strongly (waist-to-hip ratio, shoulders, muscle and fat, posture, silhouette at a distance): see [`sex-age`](sex-age.md).

**The masses.** Three big masses (head, ribcage, pelvis) joined by a flexible spine and neck, with the limbs hung from the shoulder girdle and the pelvis. The ribcage is an egg, the pelvis a box, and they twist and tilt against each other (common teaching, see section 5).

## 2. Gesture, weight and balance

- **Line of action.** "An imaginary line that runs down the spine"; "the more curve you put into that line, the more attitude, force and/or movement the image will communicate"; find it in the first seconds and hang the torso and hips on it ([Line of Action, gesture basics](https://line-of-action.com/article/gesture-basics-1-line-of-action/)).
- **Curves and straights.** Use only C curves, S curves or straight lines, as few as possible; zig-zags for tension, long flowing curves for relaxed poses; asymmetric, alternating curves through head, ribcage, pelvis and legs carry the eye, "symmetry can make a figure stiff"; exaggerate to tell the story ([Proko, how to draw gesture](https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/how-to-draw-gesture/notes)).
- **Contrapposto.** "A human figure standing with most of its weight on one foot", "its shoulders and arms twist off-axis from the hips and legs"; when one hip is higher the opposite shoulder is lower ([Contrapposto, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contrapposto)).
- **Balance.** "To balance a pose you need to think of what's known as the center of gravity": the support must sit under it or be spread around it; in a running pose "the center of gravity is directly above the supporting foot"; when the pelvis tilts, "the rib cage rotates the other way creating the opposite angle in the shoulders"; drop the plumb line from the centre of the big masses rather than from the pit of the neck ([Proko, balanced poses](https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/how-to-draw-balanced-poses)).
- **External forces.** Wind, a rope, another body: a figure leaning into a gale is balanced by the wind's push, so its centre of gravity can lie ahead of the feet; the windward foot braces, the body is a straight diagonal from heel to head (**judgement**, follows from the balance rule).
- **Lying down.** Weight spreads over the back of the head, shoulder blades, sacrum, calves and heels; the lumbar curve lifts off the ground; relaxed feet fall outward; arms behind the head lift the ribcage and flatten the belly (**judgement**, observation).

## 3. Seen from different viewpoints

- **Foreshortening:** "objects appear to be shorter or more compressed than they actually are" as they recede, "objects appear to get smaller as they recede", and "objects at the front will overlap objects behind"; break the body into simple boxes and cylinders and "draw through" them ([Draw Paint Academy, foreshortening](https://drawpaintacademy.com/foreshortening/)).
- **Worm's-eye:** feet and legs large, head small; the undersides of chin, breasts and arms are seen; verticals converge upward (**judgement**).
- **Overhead:** the body flattens into its silhouette on the ground; the face, chest and the tops of thighs and feet are visible; the cast shadow does the modelling (**judgement**).
- **Rolled camera:** a tilted horizon makes a still figure feel off balance; the line of action reads against the frame, not against the ground (**judgement**).

## 4. Under light

Treat the big masses first: one light side and one shadow side to the ribcage, the pelvis and each limb, with the core shadow where the cylinder turns; cast shadows where one form overlaps another (arm on torso, chin on neck); occlusion in the armpit, between the legs and where a body meets the ground (**judgement**; see `light-shadow`). Skin under a high sun takes the colour of the light on its lit planes and of the sky in its upturned shadow planes (**judgement**).

## 5. How painters observe and construct it

1. The line of action, then the tilts of the shoulders and the hips against each other (Proko gesture; contrapposto).
2. The three masses on the line, in perspective (boxes or eggs), and the balance: where does the weight land?
3. Limbs as tapered cylinders with overlaps at the joints; check lengths with the head unit.
4. Hands and feet as blocks (see `hands-feet`), then the face (see `face`).
5. Light and shadow on the masses, then the drapery over them (see `drapery`).

In the studio: build the figure in `scene` as a `person` (proportions by age, sex and build), pose it with a preset and joint overrides or reach a hand to a target, and render it from the picture's camera. Check the line of action, the balance and the overlaps in the blockout before painting.

## 6. Common mistakes

- **Mannequin:** separate tubes meeting at visible joints, a straight spine, symmetric arms. Check: is there one line of action through the whole figure, and do shoulders and hips tilt against each other?
- **Floating or falling:** the weight lands nowhere. Drop a plumb line from the big masses.
- **Frontal and stiff** (habit, rounds 15-16): two figures side by side facing front. Turn them toward each other, overlap them.
- **Half-committed gesture:** an arm raised 30 degrees when the feeling wants 90. Exaggerate (Proko).
- **Wrong scale of parts:** short arms, small hands, a long torso. Measure in heads; hands are face-length.
- **Children drawn as small adults:** use the age canon.

## 7. Checks for Teodor

1. Draw the line of action over the blockout: is it one clear C, S or diagonal?
2. Do the shoulder line and the hip line tilt in opposite directions (for a standing weight-bearing figure)?
3. Is the centre of gravity over the support, or held by a force you can see (wind, a hand, a rope)?
4. Measure: is the figure the right number of heads for its age, and do fingertips reach mid-thigh?
5. Where forms turn toward you, do they overlap and shorten?
6. In a group, do the bodies lean toward or away from each other, rather than standing frontally?

## 8. Masses on the limbs, knees and elbows (added 2026-11-26)

A limb is not a smooth tube: each segment carries a few masses, and the joints are built from bony landmarks, not balls (critics' "mannequin", section 6).
- **Knee:** the patella is "a flat, rounded triangular bone" that "covers and protects the anterior articular surface of the knee joint" and gives attachment to the patellar tendon ([Patella, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patella)). So the knee reads as a block wider than it is deep, a flat plate in front, and the tendon running down to the shin, with the inner condyle a little fuller (**judgement** for the proportions).
- **Thigh:** the quadriceps in front with the teardrop of vastus medialis just above the inner knee, the hamstrings behind (**judgement**, the common anatomy teaching).
- **Calf:** "the medial gastrocnemius head is low and heavy", the lateral head ends higher; "the soleus muscle belly descends lower and is wider than gastrocnemius" and "can even be seen in front view, behind the tibia" ([Proko, how to draw the calf](https://proko.com/course-lesson/how-to-draw-the-calf-anatomy-for-artists/notes)). The ankle bone is higher on the inside (see `hands-feet`).
- **Arm:** the deltoid caps the shoulder; biceps in front, triceps behind; the forearm's extensor mass on the thumb side and flexor mass on the palm side, both in its upper half, tapering to a flat wrist; at the elbow, the olecranon's point behind and the inner epicondyle's knob (**judgement**, common teaching).
- **As planes:** under one light each mass gives one plane change (light to halftone), which is what a painter puts down; the studio groups the body's light (`body_planes`, default 0.35, **judgement**) as it does the head's. Women and children show the masses less (**judgement**).

**Drapery in wind (studio note).** A skirt pressed by the wind onto the windward leg stays there: cloth that touches the body with the wind pushing it in does not slide along it (static friction), so the skirt wraps the near leg and shows its form, and streams only from the leeward side (see `drapery`).

**Sex and build in the rig (studio note, 2026-11-26).** The rig used to pinch every waist (its narrowest girth fell in the gap between two ellipsoids: a waist breadth 0.42 of the hip breadth in women, 0.67 in men) and gave an 8-year-old girl adult hips. Now the torso is built from girths in meters: biacromial breadth 41.1 / 36.7 cm (men / women, scaled by stature; `sex-age` section 2), waist 85 / 72 cm and hip 96 / 100 cm at 1.77 / 1.64 m (**judgement**, chosen for the measured waist-to-hip ratios 0.89 / 0.72), and per build multipliers that put women's extra girth on hips and thighs and men's on the belly. `figure.body_stats` measures them as a tape does (the convex hull of a horizontal slice). Measured, waist-to-hip ratio: women slim 0.71, average 0.72, sturdy 0.77, heavy 0.81, at 75 0.77; men slim 0.86, average 0.89, sturdy 0.93, heavy 0.96, at 75 0.92; children of 8, either sex, 0.81. Hip breadth 34.4 cm (woman) and 32.8 cm (man), so biacromial over hip breadth is about 1.06 / 1.25. Sex enters from puberty (from about 10, full at 16); a child is built halfway between. Women also get a bust, a slimmer neck, a lower trapezius, slimmer arms, wrists and hands and smoother arm masses; men a thicker neck with the trapezius rising high and crisper muscle masses. A skirt hangs from a waistband on the waist and is pushed out by the hips; a fitted bodice is the body's own surface.

Muybridge's *Animal Locomotion* plates at the Met (public domain; women, object 266434, and men, 266429) were tried as silhouette references: in back views the bideltoid breadth over the hip breadth read by eye about 1.5 for the woman and 1.6 for the man, but the plates are soft and the figures turned, so these are not used as targets.

## 9. Studio note: the mesh rig (2026-11-26)

`scene` people are now MakeHuman's base mesh (CC0) on its 163-bone skeleton with its skin weights (`studio/v8/human.py`), posed with the same presets and joint conventions as before: every segment's frame is measured on the rest mesh, the pose gives each segment's posed frame exactly as the primitive rig built it, and the mesh follows by linear blend skinning (the forearm carries 60% of the wrist's pronation, the deltoid's cap 30% of the arm's raise; judgement). Reaching (two-bone IK to a target or a landmark), the contact of a palm with a surface and of each finger joint with it, the lean into a wind and the anchors all work as before, against a distance field built from the posed mesh (1.2 cm voxels; also used for ambient occlusion, and for cloth and hair to rest on the body). The masses of section 8 are now the sculpted mesh's own. Checked on the gallery scenes (`docs/gallery/figures_gale.jpg`, `figures_sleeper.jpg`, `figures_swimmer.jpg`): the wind pose, a sleeper with one hand on a cat and one under his head seen from above with the camera rolled, a swimmer with a hand pushing back his hair. **Still short:** linear blend skinning can pinch a joint bent far (it held up on the gallery poses and on `kneel`, `sit`, `throw`, `lie_side`, `hand_on_head`, but was not tested past them); `sit` and `sit_ground` have no seat, so the lowest point (a foot) goes to the ground and the hips hang in the air, as with the primitive rig; a lone `hand` cut from the mesh does not rest on other objects.
