# East Asian ink painting (Chinese and Japanese ink-wash painting)

Researched 2026-11. Every value names its source; values marked **judgement** have no published source and are editorial choices for Teodor. Covers Chinese literati landscape (*shanshui*, *wenren hua*) and Japanese ink painting (*suibokuga*, colloquially *sumi-e*), which share tools, strokes and aims.

## 1. In one paragraph
Ink painting is "painting in *sumi* (black ink) in dark and light shades on silk or paper", which "uses gradations of ink to create a sense of light and shade, as well as modulated brushstrokes and lines to create a sense of volume and rhythm"; it shares with calligraphy its brushes and its admired qualities, "strength and resilience of line, economy of brushstrokes and ink, and expressiveness" ([JAANUS, *suibokuga*](https://projects.mcah.columbia.edu/jaanus/record/suibokuga)). In China, landscape became its own genre by the late Tang and "embodied the universal longing of cultivated men to escape their quotidian world"; scholar-officials (the literati) used calligraphic brushwork "for self-expressive ends", and painting moved towards "a means of conveying the inner landscape of the artist's heart and mind" ([Met, Landscape Painting in Chinese Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/TOAH/HD/clpg/hd_clpg.htm)). The literati "viewed painting, like calligraphy, as an extension and expression of the man, not the depiction of things or nature" ([Asian Art Museum, *Brushstrokes: Styles and Techniques of Chinese Painting*](https://education.asianart.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/6/2019/09/Brushstrokes.pdf)). In Japan ink painting rose among Zen priests in the late Kamakura period and became the favoured mode of Muromachi painters, valued for "its austerity and immediacy" (JAANUS). Painters such as Guo Xi, Mi Fu and Sesshū Tōyō are named only as historical context. Teodor uses two **modes**: `mode: "literati"` (structured landscape built from texture strokes and washes) and `mode: "xieyi"` (the free, spontaneous, splashed/broken-ink manner of Chan/Zen painting and *haboku*).

## 2. How it carries feeling
- **The brush as the painter's state.** "In the quality of the brushwork the artist captures *qiyun*, the spirit resonance, the raison d'être of a painting" (AAM *Brushstrokes*). Xie He's first principle (c. 550 CE), spirit resonance, is "the nervous energy transmitted from the artist into the work"; the second, "bone method", is "the way of using the brush" and its link to personality ([Cultural Heritage of China, Painting theory](https://ibiblio.org/chineseculture/contents/arts/p-arts-c01s03.html); same list in [HKU University Museum, *Huihua Liufa*](https://ulearninglabs.umag.hku.hk/en/dictionaries/huihua-liufa/)). The literati believed "the true character of the author was revealed in his brush strokes" ([JAANUS, *bunbō shihō*](https://projects.mcah.columbia.edu/jaanus/record/bunboshiho)). This maps directly onto Teodor's felt-state hand.
- **Season, weather and mood.** Guo Xi's essay (11th c.) linked seasons, times of day and weather to moods: "Winter mountains covered by storm clouds; people depressed"; contemplation of such pictures "evokes in men corresponding ideas/feelings" (J. Cahill's lecture summary of Guo Xi, [UC Berkeley IEAS lecture 7B](https://ieas.berkeley.edu/sites/default/files/aparv_lecture7b.pdf)).
- **Entering the landscape.** Landscapes are "meant to be entered into": paths, pavilions and small figures invite the viewer to travel (AAM *Brushstrokes*); Guo Xi ranked paintings fit to contemplate, to ramble through and, finest, to live in (Cahill).
- **Withdrawal, integrity, longing.** Old trees, bamboo and rocks "became emblems of [the painters'] character and spirit"; a thatched hut stands for reclusive retreat; political loyalty and withdrawal could be conveyed (Met). Bamboo is "a symbol for the scholar-gentleman who bends but does not break in the wind" (AAM).
- **Immediacy and sudden insight (xieyi).** Chan ink painting "captured the quality of sudden enlightenment": "Brushstrokes were swift and spontaneous with rough textures and strong contrasts of light and dark ink" (AAM). A flung-ink landscape is "the messenger for a moment's emotional or spiritual state" and an exercise "in seeing meaningful detail slowly emerge from what at first seems unclear" ([Cleveland Museum of Art, *Haboku* Landscape](https://www.clevelandart.org/art/148810)).
- **Emptiness as presence.** Water and snow are painted "by not painting them -- that is by leaving areas blank" (AAM). The unpainted ground is part of the image.
- **Fit for Teodor's feelings:** solitude, longing for elsewhere, resilience, quiet joy, grief held with dignity, awe at scale, a moment of clarity (**judgement**). Poor fit: claustrophobic, saturated or violent feelings.

## 3. Media, supports and grounds
- **The Four Treasures:** "Brush, inkstick, paper, and inkstone" ([Smithsonian National Museum of Asian Art, Four Treasures](https://asia-archive.si.edu/learn/chinas-calligraphic-arts/four-treasures)).
  - **Brush:** hair of deer, goat, sable, rabbit, wolf and others (Smithsonian; AAM), with a stiffer core ("heart"), a mantle of shorter hairs that holds ink ("belly") and long outer hairs that come to a sharp point ("coat"); "tiny, fairly stiff wolf hair brushes for outlining" to "immense resilient brushes"; long tapering brushes for swirling movements, short stumpy ones for a blunt line (AAM *Brushstrokes*).
  - **Ink:** pine soot or lampblack mixed with glue, pressed into sticks; ground with water on the inkstone; "the resulting liquid ink varies in blackness according to the amount of water that is added" (Smithsonian).
  - **Inkstone:** a fine, smooth yet abrasive surface for grinding and a reservoir (Smithsonian).
  - **Paper and silk:** paper of bamboo, hemp and mulberry bark ("rice paper" is a misnomer: Smithsonian). Sized paper (alum on one side) "preferred for dry brush, contrasting ink, or fine line work"; "Unsized paper, in contrast, was used for wet ink painting of the expressive style" (AAM). Yuan literati appropriated paper because it "was more receptive to movement and pressure of the brush" (AAM). Silk was used for court and earlier painting (AAM; JAANUS).
- **Formats:** hanging scroll, handscroll (read "like a book", never hung), album leaf, fan (AAM).
- **Teodor default:** ink on paper. Literati mode: sized paper `#f1ece0` (**judgement**: warm off-white xuan); xieyi mode: unsized absorbent paper `#f3efe6` (**judgement**). Alternative support: aged silk `#d8caa6` (**judgement**: silk darkens with age, AAM). Light colour washes in watercolour (being added) as a second medium.

## 4. Palette
- **Principle:** ink is the colour. "In Chinese color theory, black contains all colors; thus theorists believe that people can conceive all colors in the various tones of ink" (AAM). Colour, when used, is light washes, often "complementary shades of blue-green and red-orange" (AAM); "brushwork is the true bones and flesh of Chinese painting -- color is secondary" (AAM).
- **Ink tones (approximate, judgement; scale of dilution on warm paper):** charred/dense black `#151413`, dark `#2e2c2a`, middle `#5a5753`, light `#8d8a84`, pale `#bdb9b0`, the faintest wash `#d9d4c9`. A "five tones of ink" scale is traditional (**judgement**: no reachable source named it).
- **Light colour (approximate, judgement):** pale indigo/blue-green `#8fa3a0`, mineral green `#6f8f7a` (thin), ochre `#c9a66b`, burnt sienna wash `#b07a50`, (Lan Ying's snow landscape uses "yellow ochre and burnt sienna washes": AAM).
- **Seal red** `#b0302a` (cinnabar seal paste, Smithsonian on seals) is not a paint colour; Teodor's signature takes the seal's place (see Finishing).
- **Avoid:** opaque or saturated colour; Western blue-green "blue-green landscape" mineral style unless deliberately chosen (that is the professional, not the literati, tradition: AAM); pure grey digital gradients (**judgement**).

## 5. Light and value
- No single light source: "Strong effect of light-and-shadow, although no consistent light source (never in Chinese painting)" (Cahill). Light and dark are made by ink tone and by leaving paper bare (JAANUS; AAM).
- **Atmosphere:** mist and blank bands separate near, middle and far; distant ranges "faintly visible" in pale ink (AAM). Guo Xi: "mist and clouds give them air of beauty" (Cahill).
- **Value key:** high: most of the surface is paper or pale wash, with the darkest ink concentrated in a few accents (**judgement**, consistent with "economy of brushstrokes and ink", JAANUS).

## 6. Drawing and form
- **Line first.** Early painting was called "making boundaries"; outline was essential; figure lines have named types: "iron-wire lines", "floating silk-thread lines", "rippled-water lines" and others, from a Ming list of eighteen (AAM).
- **Texture strokes (*cun*)** give rocks and mountains "geologic credence": long and short hemp-fibre (the most common), raveled rope, cloud-head, small and large axe-cut, bands dragged in mud, nail-head; Mi dots for "a blurry, atmospheric effect" (AAM *Brushstrokes*, Chart C). Each has a brush angle: hemp-fibre "with an upright brush held in the center of the handle", axe-cut "with a slanted brush", raveled rope "with the slanted brush", nail-head "pressed down at an angle" and finished upright (AAM).
- **Dots (*dian*)** for distant foliage and surface texture; outline patterns for kinds of tree leaves (AAM).
- **Boneless (*mogu*)**: a leaf or petal in one or two strokes of wet ink or colour, no outline; several shades loaded on one brush (AAM).
- **Abbreviated brush (*genpitsu*)** in Zen painting: sketches whose immediacy mirrors spontaneous enlightenment (JAANUS).
- Form follows *type*: a mountain's structure, a pine's character, not one observed view. The painter observes, then paints from the image stored in the mind (AAM; Guo Xi via Cahill: "When I have stored up impressions of them in my mind, then with the eye unconscious of the silk and the hand unconscious of brush and ink...").

## 7. Composition
- **Mountain and water.** *Shanshui hua*, "mountain water painting": "Mountains rise up; water flows down"; yang and yin, hard and flowing (AAM).
- **Hierarchy:** a main mountain with subordinate mountains; the pine stands out among trees (Guo Xi via Cahill).
- **Three distances** (Guo Xi): three ways of achieving distance (Cahill). Standard reading: *high distance* (looking up from the foot to the peak), *deep distance* (looking from the front into the folds behind), *level distance* (looking across from a near hill to far hills) (**judgement**: definitions from the commonly cited Bush & Shih translation, not fetched in this session).
- **Shifting viewpoint:** a handscroll unfolds as a journey; a hanging scroll stacks near, middle and far vertically with mist between (AAM descriptions of handscroll and slide 6).
- **Path and figure:** roads, figures and buildings lead the eye (Cahill); small figures gaze or travel; a pavilion invites rest (AAM).
- **Emptiness:** large blank areas for water, mist, sky (AAM).
- **Inscription and seal** are part of the composition, placed to balance it and "in a more active part of the painting rather than in an empty space" (AAM).
- Formats: tall hanging scroll (e.g. 1:2.5 to 1:4) or a wide handscroll; Teodor may use 800x2000 portrait or 2400x800 landscape (**judgement**, proportions from typical scroll formats such as the Cleveland *haboku*, image 59.5 x 26.9 cm).

## 8. Marks and edges
- Brush angle is the main variable: upright (centre) tip for fine and round lines, slanted (side) brush for broad, flat, broken strokes (AAM).
- **Flying white (*feibai*):** pressure separates the hairs, "leaving streaks of white spaces. The effect is of speed and vigor" (AAM). **Dry brush:** "Ink is used sparingly with little moisture in the brush" (AAM).
- **Splashed ink:** "A very wet, free application of ink resulting in ink blobs, broad strokes, or saturated areas of wash" (AAM). **Broken ink:** "'breaking' the wash with deeper or lighter ink tones while the first layer of ink or color is still wet" (AAM); in Japan *haboku* and *hatsuboku* came to mean "the same technique of laying on successive ink washes" (JAANUS).
- Wet edges on unsized paper bleed soft and fuzzy; strokes on sized paper keep crisp edges (AAM on paper sizing and Qi Baishi's "soft, fuzzy edges").
- **No correction.** "Once the painting is started it is not normally possible to alter a wrong stroke" (Cultural Heritage of China, ibiblio); each stroke is final.

## 9. Process (the order of work)
1. **Prepare:** grind ink to the needed tones; choose brush and paper (Smithsonian; AAM).
2. **Observe and store:** look at the subject, then paint from the image in the mind, not from a model (AAM; Guo Xi via Cahill). The composition is held "in the mind's eye" before the first stroke (ibiblio).
3. **Structure (literati):** main forms in pale-to-middle ink outline: the main mountain, rocks, trees, from near to far (**judgement** on near-to-far order; outline-first from AAM's "making boundaries").
4. **Texture:** *cun* strokes on rocks and mountains, matched to the kind of rock (AAM).
5. **Washes:** graded pale ink (and, if used, light ochre/blue-green) to give volume and separate planes, applied in layers "alternated with strokes of water to blur their edges" (AAM on Lan Ying); blanks left for water, mist and snow (AAM).
6. **Dots and accents:** moss dots, foliage dots, the darkest accents last to bring the picture to life (AAM: dots "enliven the surface") (order **judgement**).
7. **Inscription and seal:** a poem or note and the seal, placed to balance the composition (AAM).
- **Xieyi variant:** pale tones first, then "darker and darker ink while the first was still a little wet", then accents and flying-white lines to tie the composition (AAM, on Qi Baishi; broken ink definition).
- **Teodor phases:** drawing = steps 1-2 (a composition schema is allowed, but `transfer` should be very faint or omitted, since the tradition paints from the mind); lay-in = 3-4; development = 5; finishing = 6-7.

## 10. Finishing
- Finished means alive, not complete: the qualities sought are "vitality of spirit, intensity of realization and freshness of perception" and freshness and spontaneity (ibiblio); economy of brushstrokes and ink (JAANUS). Overworking is the main failure (**judgement**).
- Last acts: dots and dark accents; inscription; seal (AAM). Mounting as a scroll was the presentation (AAM formats).
- **Teodor:** his signature is his seal: `sign` placed in an active area that balances the composition, not in an empty corner (AAM's seal-placement rule), in a dark ink tone. No fake seals or invented Chinese/Japanese characters (**judgement**: fake script would imitate a real writing system badly; an inscription only if it is real text Teodor writes in a script he can write correctly, or none).

## 11. Pitfalls for Teodor
- **Orientalist postcard:** panda, red sun, crane against a moon, pagoda, a fisherman in a straw hat, misty Guilin karsts copied from tourism images, bamboo-and-plum stencil; and "Zen" decor minimalism (one stroke, one stone). Teodor paints his own observed world (a street, a tree outside, a northern coast) with the brush methods (**judgement**).
- **Fake calligraphy and seals:** gibberish characters, red squares added for "authenticity" (**judgement**).
- **Western habits:** a single light source and cast shadows; linear perspective; filling the whole surface; correcting strokes by overpainting (Cahill; AAM; ibiblio).
- **Grey mush:** too many mid-tone washes with no dark accents and no white; lose the "strength and resilience of line" (JAANUS) (**judgement**).
- **Nearest wrong feelings:** generic serenity ("spa calm") instead of a specific mood of a season; melancholy as a default. Guo Xi tied each season and weather to a distinct mood (Cahill).
- **Habits:** the lone figure seen from behind is common in this tradition too (small figures gazing); if used, it must be small and in a landscape that is the subject.

## 12. Engine mapping
- **Ink (being added):** the native medium. It needs: ink tones as dilution of one black (not separate colours); absorbent unsized paper with wet bleeding and soft fuzzy edges vs. sized paper with crisp edges; a pointed round brush whose width swells with pressure and whose angle (upright vs. slanted) changes the mark; several tones loaded on one brush (boneless); flying white from pressure and dryness (the oil engine's dry-brush breaks from low paint or speed are the right model); permanence: no lifting, no covering a dark with a light.
- **Watercolour (being added):** light ochre/blue-green washes; graded wash "alternated with strokes of water" (blend with water).
- **Until ink exists (oil fallback, poor fit):** `new "#f1ece0" --medium oil --support panel`; `strokes` with `liner` sizes 2-8 for outlines; `free` with `round` small sizes, `direction` per rock face, short `length` for *cun*; `glaze` in feathered regions for washes; `round` small dabs for dots; `impasto` 0 everywhere.
- **Felt bias:** literati: arousal -0.1, tension 0.0, care +0.25; xieyi: arousal +0.3, tension 0.0, care -0.2 (**judgement**: literati brushwork is deliberate but must stay alive; xieyi is swift). The JSON default is literati; both are under `modes`.
- **Gaps:** ink medium and paper absorption are not yet available; no brush-angle parameter (upright vs. slanted tip); no multi-tone loading in one brush; no rule that forbids covering (ink permanence); no scroll formats beyond custom canvas sizes; no inscription capability (deliberately not needed); `transfer` should be optional and faint for this current.
