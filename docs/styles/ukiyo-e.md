# Ukiyo-e (Japanese woodblock prints and their painted counterparts)

Researched 2026-11. Every value names its source; values marked **judgement** have no published source and are editorial choices for Teodor.

## 1. In one paragraph
*Ukiyo-e*, "pictures of the floating world", is the name given to paintings and prints of the Edo period (1615-1868) that primarily depicted the transitory world of the pleasure quarters and the social life of Edo (Tokyo); *ukiyo* was originally a Buddhist term for the impermanence of life, which came to mean pleasures "embraced all the more for their ever-changing nature" ([Asian Art Museum, The ukiyo-e woodblock printing process](https://education.asianart.org/resources/the-ukiyo-e-woodblock-printing-process/)). Subjects were courtesans and Kabuki actors, then famous views and, later, historical events; full-colour single-sheet prints (*nishiki-e*, "brocade pictures") became possible in 1765 ([Met, Woodblock Prints in the Ukiyo-e Style](https://www.metmuseum.org/en/essays/woodblock-prints-in-the-ukiyo-e-style)). Each print was the work of four experts: designer, carver, printer and publisher (Met). Suzuki Harunobu, Kitagawa Utamaro, Katsushika Hokusai and Utagawa Hiroshige are named only as historical context. Teodor paints *in the visual language* of ukiyo-e (key line, flat colour, graded bands) with the woodblock-print look medium, or as a painted ukiyo-e (*nikuhitsu*, "from the brush") in ink and colour; he does not reproduce a print workshop's mechanical output (AGENTS.md: "It must be a painting").

## 2. How it carries feeling
- **Impermanence savoured.** The floating world's pleasures are loved because they pass (AAM). The feeling is a light, poised awareness of transience: a season, weather, a moment of a performance, a journey (**judgement**, from the term's meaning).
- **Line and silhouette, not modelling.** Feeling lives in the contour's rhythm (a sleeve, a wave crest, a bent figure in rain) and in posture and gesture; faces are typified (**judgement**; the AAM confirms the "fine line drawing" bordering "large flat areas" and an "essentially flat image").
- **Colour as mood and season.** Flat colour fields and soft graded skies (*bokashi*) set time of day and weather; blue-only prints (*aizuri-e*) show how a single colour could carry a whole series ([Vermeulen et al. 2020, *npj Heritage Science* 8:62](https://heritagesciencejournal.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s40494-020-00406-y)).
- **Weather and nature against small humans** in landscape prints: rain, snow, wind, waves as the emotional agents (**judgement**).
- **Fit for Teodor's feelings:** nostalgia, the sweetness of a passing moment, travel and solitude on the road, the drama of a storm, playful wit, a held breath before something happens (**judgement**). Poor fit: heavy grief or claustrophobic darkness: the medium has no deep chiaroscuro.
- Avoided: cast shadows, chiaroscuro, tonal modelling of volume, visible painterly brushwork in the print (AAM: even Western shading borrowed into prints stayed "essentially flat").

## 3. Media, supports and grounds
- **Prints:** water-soluble colours "derived from plant and mineral sources" until the late nineteenth century, applied in large flat areas (AAM); key-block lines in *sumi* ink, "made from soot and bound in *nikawa*" (animal glue) ([Fitzwilliam Museum, Yoshitoshi glossary](https://yoshitoshi.fitzmuseum.cam.ac.uk/glossary/)); damp paper rubbed onto cherrywood blocks with a *baren* (AAM). Paper: *hōsho*, "highest quality thick kōzo paper" (Fitzwilliam). White: *gofun*, shell-white calcium carbonate (Fitzwilliam). Special effects: blind embossing (*karazuri*), mica (*kira*, *kirazuri*), imitation wood grain (*itame mokuhan*), textile embossing (*nunomezuri*) (Fitzwilliam; Met captions for *karazuri* and white mica).
- **Paintings (*nikuhitsu*):** ink and colour on silk or paper, as hanging scrolls (**judgement**: the AAM confirms ukiyo-e "paintings and prints"; no technical source on the paintings was reached).
- **Formats:** *ōban* about 39 x 26.5 cm (the standard), *chūban*, *aiban*, *hosoban* (narrow, about 33 x 15 cm), *hashira-e* (pillar print), diptychs and triptychs (Fitzwilliam; AAM).
- **Teodor default:** the woodblock-print look medium on a kōzo-paper support, paper colour `#efe7d4` (**judgement**: warm, slightly aged hōsho); canvas proportion close to *ōban*, e.g. 1060x1560 portrait or 1560x1060 landscape (**judgement**, from the ōban ratio 390:265). Second medium: ink with gouache (as a proxy for water-based colour with *gofun*) on paper for a painted ukiyo-e.

## 4. Palette
- **Principle:** a limited set of flat, water-based colours printed one block at a time, minimum about ten impressions for an average *nishiki-e* (AAM), with a key line and graded bands. Blues were indigo and dayflower before Prussian blue (introduced in the early nineteenth century through trade) caused a "blue revolution" (Vermeulen et al. 2020).
- **Documented colourants** (Vermeulen et al. 2020; Fitzwilliam): *sumi* black, indigo, dayflower blue, Prussian blue, safflower red (*beni*), vermilion, minium (red lead), iron oxide red, orpiment yellow, yellow organic colourants, *gofun* white, mica.
- **Colours (approximate hex, judgement; aged print appearance, not fresh):** sumi line `#22201e`, Prussian blue `#1f4e79`, light Prussian `#7fa7c4`, indigo `#3a4f66`, safflower pink `#d9849b`, faded beni `#c9a2a0`, vermilion `#c8442e`, minium orange `#d86a3a`, orpiment yellow `#dcb54a`, olive green (blue over yellow) `#6f7d4a`, grey `#9a958a`, gofun white `#f3eee4`, paper `#efe7d4`.
- **Avoid:** oil-paint chroma and impasto; gradients anywhere except as deliberate *bokashi* bands; black shadows; Western atmospheric blue haze over everything (**judgement**).

## 5. Light and value
- No modelled light, no cast shadows; value is a property of flat areas (AAM: "essentially flat image").
- *Bokashi*: "grading pigment on a block by hand-wiping"; *atenashi bokashi*, "shading without definition", by mixing water and pigment on the block with a brush (Fitzwilliam). Used for skies at the top edge, water, the horizon and the ground (**judgement** on placement).
- Night and weather are signalled by colour fields (dark blue sky, grey rain lines) rather than by light sources (**judgement**).
- Value key: high to middle; paper white is the lightest value (**judgement**).

## 6. Drawing and form
- **The key line carries form.** The designer drew the composition "in black ink on a thin sheet of paper" (*hanshita-e*), pasted face down on the block and cut around (AAM; Fitzwilliam). Lines are clean, even or gently swelling, closed around colour areas (**judgement** on line character).
- Figures are elongated and typified (*bijin-ga*, beautiful women), actors' faces given characteristic features; *ōkubi-e* are large-head bust portraits (Fitzwilliam). Proportions follow type rather than anatomy (**judgement**).
- Pattern: textiles rendered as flat pattern that does not follow the folds (**judgement**).

## 7. Composition
- Strong diagonals, high or low viewpoints, dramatic cropping of foreground objects at the frame, asymmetry, empty bands of graded sky; a series logic (one view in a set of many) (**judgement**; generally observed and the reason for the Japonisme interest, but no reachable source stated it).
- Narrow formats (*hosoban*, *hashira-e*) force vertical, cropped compositions (Fitzwilliam formats).
- A cartouche for title and the artist's signature appear on prints (AAM: publisher's seal and signature); Teodor signs only with his own signature and adds no fake seals or censor marks (**judgement**: fake seals would imitate real records).

## 8. Marks and edges
- **Print:** every edge is hard; colour areas abut a key line; slight registration shifts and the paper's fibre and baren texture are visible in good impressions (**judgement**); *bokashi* bands are the only soft transitions (Fitzwilliam).
- **Painted ukiyo-e:** fine, controlled brush line with a pointed brush; flat colour washes inside it (**judgement**).

## 9. Process (the order of work)
The historical workshop (AAM; Met):
1. Publisher chooses the subject; designer draws the composition in ink on thin paper.
2. The drawing is pasted on cherrywood; the carver cuts away everything but the lines (key block).
3. Key-block proofs in *sumi*; the designer colours a proof to show where each colour goes.
4. A separate block per colour, each with *kentō* registration marks.
5. Printing, one colour at a time, lightest and largest areas generally first (**judgement**: common practice, not stated by the sources reached); *bokashi* wiped on the block; embossing and mica last (**judgement** for order).
Teodor's analogue, through brush acts only:
- **Drawing phase:** the composition as a line schema (`ref` SVG of the key lines), with flat colour zones planned per "block".
- **Lay-in:** flat colour areas one colour at a time, lightest first, each confined to its zone (`clip`), with the paper left showing where intended.
- **Development:** *bokashi* bands; pattern areas; then the key line drawn over everything in sumi.
- **Finishing:** small corrections of the line; optional embossing/mica effects when the medium supports them; sign.
- Note: the historical print laid the key line first and colours after; for a brushed analogue, line-last keeps the line crisp over the colour (**judgement**; the painted ukiyo-e also drew line first, so either order is defensible).

## 10. Finishing
- No varnish; the print was finished when the last block was printed. Embossing (*karazuri*, *kimedashi*) and mica (*kirazuri*) were the luxury finishing touches (Fitzwilliam; Met captions).
- Teodor: stop with flat, clean areas; resist adding shading or texture to "improve" it (**judgement**).

## 11. Pitfalls for Teodor
- **Orientalist postcard:** the Great Wave, Mount Fuji, cherry blossom, geisha, torii, rising sun; Japan as costume. Teodor paints his own subject (his own city, weather, a contemporary scene) in the ukiyo-e language, as the prints themselves showed their own present day (**judgement**, from ukiyo-e's subject being contemporary Edo life: AAM).
- **Pastiche of a series or a named design;** no fake seals, publisher marks or Japanese signatures (**judgement**).
- **Vector clip-art flatness:** machine-perfect fills read as a graphic (AGENTS.md); flat must still be brushed, with the paper's fibre and small irregularities (**judgement**).
- **Gradient abuse:** *bokashi* everywhere becomes airbrushing; keep it to one or two bands (**judgement**).
- **Nearest wrong feelings:** decorative prettiness without the bite of transience; tourist exoticism; cute "anime" stylisation of faces (**judgement**).

## 12. Engine mapping
- **Woodblock-print look (being added):** the native medium: flat water-based colour with paper fibre, hard edges, a key line in sumi, *bokashi* gradation on a "block", slight registration offsets, baren texture, blind embossing and mica as `finish` ops. Until it exists: gouache (being added) on paper is the nearest proxy; oil is a poor fit (impasto, raking light, weave).
- **With current oil (fallback only):** `new "#efe7d4" --medium oil --support panel`; `free`/`match` with `flat` brushes, `impasto` 0, `cover` near 1, `edge: "hard"`, `clip` to `poly` zones, felt `care` high; *bokashi* via `glaze` with a feathered `rect` region at the top edge; key line with `strokes` and `liner` sizes 2-5 in `#22201e`.
- **Ink (being added):** the key line and a painted ukiyo-e's line work.
- **Felt bias:** arousal -0.1, tension 0.0, care +0.45 (**judgement**: a clean, deliberate, unhurried hand).
- **Gaps:** no woodblock medium yet; no paper fibre texture; no embossing or mica render; no registration-offset model; no "one colour per block" constraint across the canvas; schema tools for a key-line SVG exist (`ref`), but there is no zone-fill planning helper.
