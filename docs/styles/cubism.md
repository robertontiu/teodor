# Cubism (Analytic and Synthetic Cubism)

Researched 2026-11. Every value names its source; values marked **judgement** have no published source and are editorial choices for Teodor. Most museum pages were only partly reachable in this session (see Sources: not reached), so more values are judgement calls than in other docs.

## 1. In one paragraph
A Paris current of about 1907-1914 that turned away from copying nature and from traditional perspective, modelling and foreshortening, and stressed the two-dimensionality of the canvas ([Met, Cubism](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/cubism)). Historians split it into two stages ([Tate, Analytical cubism](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/art-terms/a/analytical-cubism)). **Analytic** (about 1908/1910-12; the Met calls the 1910-12 phase "hermetic") dissects the subject "viewpoint-by-viewpoint, resulting in a fragmentary image of multiple viewpoints and overlapping planes", with a simplified palette "so the viewer was not distracted from the structure of the form" and a dense centre (Tate). **Synthetic** (about 1912-14) began with textures, patterns and pasted paper, has "simpler shapes and brighter colours", and is "about flattening out the image and sweeping away the last traces of allusion to three-dimensional space" ([Tate, Synthetic cubism](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/art-terms/s/synthetic-cubism)). Favoured subjects: still lifes with musical instruments, bottles, glasses, newspapers and playing cards; landscapes were rare (Met). Picasso, Braque and Gris are named only as historical context.

## 2. How it carries feeling
Cubism is not an expressive current: the feeling is carried by **structure, rhythm and the act of looking**, not by gesture or colour drama.
- **Contemplative attention.** A restrained, near-monochrome palette keeps the eye on structure (Tate); the viewer reassembles the object from facets, so the felt register is slow, intellectual, absorbed (**judgement** drawn from Tate's "not distracted from the structure of the form").
- **Rhythm and density.** Planes and lines interweave and thicken towards the centre (Tate, Analytical cubism; [Tate, Cubism](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/art-terms/c/cubism)): the feeling of a crowded, humming room or a piece of music rather than a story (**judgement**; the instrument still lifes support the musical reading).
- **Wit and the everyday.** Lettering, newsprint and imitation wood grain bring "the reality of everyday life" into the picture ([Tate, Papier collé](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/art-terms/p/papier-colle)); Synthetic Cubism's flat bright shapes read as playful and lighter (**judgement**).
- **Fit for Teodor's feelings:** quiet concentration, nostalgia for a place or a room, a café or music memory, fractured or divided attention, the coexistence of several moments. Poor fit: raw grief, terror, ecstasy (**judgement**).
- Avoided: atmospheric perspective, single-point perspective, illusionistic modelling of a whole volume, expressive colour (Met).

## 3. Media, supports and grounds
- **Oil (and charcoal) on canvas** is the default. Braque's *Bottle of Marc* (1911-12) is "oil and charcoal on canvas" on fine-weave canvas with "a thin white ground layer, composed of barium sulfate, lead white, gypsum, and linseed oil" that "approximated the high-quality wove paper" the two painters favoured ([Met, Braque *Bottle of Marc*](https://www.metmuseum.org/articles/georges-braque-bottle-of-marc-vieux)). Oval formats appear (same source).
- **Mixed matter:** sand mixed into paint appears in Synthetic works (Braque, *Still Life with Banderillas*, "oil and charcoal with sand on canvas", caption in the [Met essay](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/cubism)).
- **Papier collé:** pasted wood-grain paper and newspaper drawn over with charcoal, late 1912-early 1913 (Tate, Papier collé). Teodor cannot paste (AGENTS.md "It must be a painting"); he paints the *look* of pasted, flat, cut planes instead (**judgement**).
- **Teodor default:** oil on `canvas`, white ground `#ece8dc` (**judgement**: a smooth, slightly warm white for the Met's lead-white/barium ground). Second medium: charcoal on paper for the drawing-like works; gouache is a plausible proxy for flat Synthetic planes (**judgement**).

## 4. Palette
- **Analytic principle:** near-monochrome "browns, grays, or blacks" (Met) / "muted tones of blacks, greys and ochres" (Tate, Cubism). Many areas left as bare white ground (Met, *Bottle of Marc*).
- **Analytic colours (approximate, judgement):** ground white `#ece8dc`, lead white `#efe9dc`, warm grey `#9a9184`, cool grey `#7d7f7c`, yellow ochre `#b8904f`, raw umber `#6b5a45`, burnt umber `#57402e`, ivory black `#262422`. Umber and black are documented in the stencilled letters (Met, *Bottle of Marc*).
- **Synthetic principle:** "simpler shapes and brighter colours" (Tate). Flat fields, often patterned (dots, stripes, wood grain).
- **Synthetic additional colours (approximate, judgement):** muted green `#5f7a5a`, cerulean/cobalt blue `#4a6b93`, brick red `#a3473a`, mustard yellow `#c9a542`, newsprint cream `#e5dcc4`, wood-grain brown `#8a6643`.
- **Avoid:** rainbow saturation, gradients across a whole object, Fauve colour, glossy illusion; in Analytic mode, any strong hue at all beyond a single small accent (**judgement**).

## 5. Light and value
- No single coherent light source: each facet carries its own small light-to-dark shift, so light becomes a rhythm across planes rather than a description of space (**judgement**, consistent with the Met's "multiple or contrasting vantage points" and Tate's "overlapping planes").
- **Value key:** middle, with a narrow range in Analytic work (pale ground to umber-black accents); Synthetic work is flatter, with value contrast between whole shapes rather than within them (**judgement**).
- Darkest, densest marks are few and placed as accents: in *Bottle of Marc* the stencilled "B" is "the darkest and most thickly painted element" (Met).

## 6. Drawing and form
- Objects "dissected into a multitude of small facets" then reassembled to suggest the subject (Met); "breaking objects and figures down into distinct areas" (Tate, Cubism).
- **Contour:** charcoal outlines of the main elements, some strengthened with thin oil; charcoal on top of, beneath and beside the paint (Met, *Bottle of Marc*). Contours are left open so planes slide into each other (**judgement**: the art-historical term is *passage*; no reachable source confirmed it in this session).
- **Proportion:** faceting and multiple viewpoints are allowed to distort; recognisable "clues" (a scroll of a violin, a bottle neck, a pipe, letters) anchor the subject (Met: "subjects were usually still discernible").
- **Synthetic:** form is built from flat cut shapes that "suggest objects, often through cut shapes or graphic details" (Met).

## 7. Composition
- Vertical, upright formats and ovals; the subject on a table-top seen from several angles at once (Met; oval stretcher: Met, *Bottle of Marc*).
- A dense, interlocking central scaffold of planes that thins out towards the edges, where the bare ground may show (Tate: "density of the image at the centre"; bare ground: Met).
- A loose grid of near-verticals and diagonals (**judgement**).
- Lettering (fragments of newspaper titles, words like a bar or dance name) placed flat on the picture plane (Met: "combined representational motifs with letters").

## 8. Marks and edges
- **Analytic:** small, short, parallel strokes stacked inside each facet, roughly one brush-width; flat or small filbert brushes; thin paint, some near-imperceptible washes (Met, *Bottle of Marc*: varied paint density, "nearly imperceptible washes"). Stroke size and the parallel hatching are **judgement**.
- **Edges:** one edge of a facet hard, the opposite edge lost into its neighbour (**judgement**, the *passage* effect).
- **Lines:** fine charcoal and thin oil lines; stencilled letters ("daubing layers of umber and black paint through a stencil", Met).
- **Synthetic:** larger flat areas with uniform paint, crisp edges, pattern applied over flats; textured passages with sand (Met caption).

## 9. Process (the order of work)
Based on the one detailed technical account reached (Met, *Bottle of Marc*); the rest is **judgement**.
1. **Ground:** thin, smooth white ground (Met). Teodor: `new "#ece8dc"`.
2. **Charcoal drawing:** outlines of the major elements in fine charcoal (Met). Teodor: draw the scaffold as a schema of planes and use `transfer` lightly in a dark grey; build the schema from a still-life blockout seen from two or three viewpoints and recut into facets (**judgement**).
3. **Selective oil:** strengthen some lines with thin oil; lay thin washes; leave many areas as bare ground (Met).
4. **Facet building:** small parallel strokes per facet, light at one edge to dark at the other, densest at the centre (**judgement**, Tate's central density).
5. **Return to drawing:** more charcoal on top of and beside the paint (Met).
6. **Accents and letters:** the darkest, thickest marks last, including stencilled letters (Met).
- Synthetic variant: flat planes laid first, then pattern, texture, letters and drawn contours over them (**judgement**).
- Teodor phases: drawing = steps 1-2; lay-in = 3; development = 4-5; finishing = 6.

## 10. Finishing
- Finished meant an integrated structure, not a polished surface: bare ground, charcoal and thin paint were left visible (Met, *Bottle of Marc*).
- Last acts: darkest accents and lettering (Met), a check that the centre is the densest area (Tate), no unifying glaze (**judgement**: a glaze would fake an atmosphere Cubism refused).
- Varnish: not established for these works in reachable sources; Teodor leaves the surface matte (**judgement**).

## 11. Pitfalls for Teodor
- **Pastiche:** a guitar, a bottle and the word "JOURNAL". Teodor chooses his own objects and words from the feeling (**judgement**).
- **Shattered glass:** random triangles laid over a realist picture. Facets must come from viewpoints and the object's structure, and the planes must interlock (Tate: "structured dissection").
- **Kaleidoscope colour:** Analytic must stay near-monochrome (Met, Tate).
- **Graphic-design flatness:** Synthetic Cubism done as machine-perfect vector shapes. Every plane must be painted with the brush, with visible paint (AGENTS.md).
- **Nearest wrong feelings:** cold detachment (structure without the warmth of the everyday), chaos or anxiety (fragmentation read as breakdown). The current's feeling is absorbed, rhythmic, witty (**judgement**).
- **Habits:** a lone figure, a lit doorway, a hushed bittersweet mood all break the still-life logic; choose a table-top world.

## 12. Engine mapping
- **Oil (exists, v8):** `new "#ece8dc" --medium oil --support canvas`; `transfer` 0.3-0.5 in `#3a3632` as the charcoal drawing; `match` with `brush: "flat"` sizes 8-24, `edge: "hard"`, low `impasto` (0.1-0.25), `threshold` high (0.08-0.12) so the ground stays bare; `free` with `direction` set per facet (one angle per region, `spread` 5-10) and `clip` inside a `poly` region per facet; `blend` along one edge of a facet only, for the lost edge; `liner` for thin lines and letters; `glaze` not used.
- **Synthetic:** `free` with `flat`/`knife`, larger sizes, `cover` near 1, `clip` to `poly` planes; pattern (dots, stripes) with `strokes`; wood grain with `strokes` and a small round (**judgement**; historically painted with a graining comb, which was not confirmed in this session).
- **Charcoal (being added):** needed for the drawing on top of paint; currently a canvas has one medium, so charcoal-over-oil is a gap.
- **Gouache (being added):** a proxy for Synthetic flat planes.
- **Felt bias:** arousal -0.15, tension +0.1, care +0.3 (**judgement**: deliberate, structured, unhurried).
- **Gaps:** two media on one canvas (charcoal over oil); no stencil tool; no sand/texture additive; no pasted paper (by rule: paint its look instead); facets need a planning helper that recuts a schema into planes from several viewpoints (a schema script is allowed: AGENTS.md).
