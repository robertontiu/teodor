# Fauvism

Researched 2026-11. Every value names its source; values marked **judgement** have no published source and are editorial choices for Teodor.

## 1. In one paragraph
Fauvism names the work of a group of painters in France from about 1905 to 1910, "characterised by strong colours and fierce brushwork"; the critic Louis Vauxcelles called them *les fauves* ("the wild beasts") at the 1905 Salon d'Automne ([Tate, Fauvism](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/art-terms/f/fauvism)). Its first paintings, made together at Collioure in the south of France, used "bold, non-naturalistic colours (often applied directly from the tube), and wild loose dabs of paint", with forms simplified so the work "appears quite abstract" (same source). It extended Post-Impressionism's expressive colour and Neo-Impressionism's divided colour, rejected traditional three-dimensional space, and used "flat areas or patches of colour to create a new pictorial space" (same). The Courtauld's curators call it a short-lived "moment" rather than a movement ([Courtauld, Derain technical report](https://courtauld.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/Report-Derain-by-F-Whitlum-Cooper-and-Cleo-Nisse.compressed.pdf)). Henri Matisse and André Derain are named only as historical context.

## 2. How it carries feeling
- **Colour is the feeling.** "Bright emotive colours, simple shapes and painterly mark-making" ([Tate](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/art-terms/f/fauvism)); colour is non-naturalistic and chosen for intensity, not description.
- **Light without shadow.** "There are hardly any shadows"; volume and depth come from contrasts of colour ([Museo Thyssen, Restoration of Derain's *Waterloo Bridge*](https://www.museothyssen.org/en/node/25199)). The feeling is radiance, heat, exhilaration, the force of light.
- **Breathing space.** White ground left showing lets paint "flicker beneath ... like dappled light" and gives "breathing space" to a dense picture ([Courtauld](https://courtauld.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/Report-Derain-by-F-Whitlum-Cooper-and-Cleo-Nisse.compressed.pdf)).
- **Complementary vibration.** Complementaries used side by side "make each other look brighter" ([Tate](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/art-terms/f/fauvism)).
- **Direction of stroke as energy.** Vertical strokes radiating from the sun, horizontal overlapping dabs for moving water ([Thyssen](https://www.museothyssen.org/en/node/25199)).
- **Judgement:** Fauvism's register is joy, vitality, sensual pleasure and heat; it is not anguish (that is Expressionism, which Tate notes it is often compared with).
- Avoided: modelling in tone, brown shadows, illusionistic depth, finish.

## 3. Media, supports and grounds
- **Oil on canvas** (default). Watercolour and drawings exist (Derain's preparatory drawing close in form and colour to the painting: [Thyssen](https://www.museothyssen.org/en/node/25199)), but oil is the core.
- **Grounds, two practices:**
  - **Bright white ground left exposed**: a commercial two-layer ground, the upper one higher in lead white "probably to enhance the luminosity of a layer that he intended to leave unpainted in many places" ([Thyssen](https://www.museothyssen.org/en/node/25199)); an artist-applied lead-white priming that "shows through in the final work" in numerous places ([Courtauld](https://courtauld.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/Report-Derain-by-F-Whitlum-Cooper-and-Cleo-Nisse.compressed.pdf)).
  - **No ground at all** with thick single layers of colour, "typical of Derain's fauvist painting practice", e.g. *The Boats at Collioure* (1905) ([Courtauld](https://courtauld.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/Report-Derain-by-F-Whitlum-Cooper-and-Cleo-Nisse.compressed.pdf)).
- Teodor default: bright white `#f6f3ea` (**judgement**, lead-white priming); the raw-canvas option `#cdb892` (**judgement**, unprimed linen colour).

## 4. Palette
- **Principle:** saturated hues almost straight from the tube, juxtaposed not blended; complementary pairs; colour does the modelling; white ground as the lightest light ([Tate](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/art-terms/f/fauvism); [Thyssen](https://www.museothyssen.org/en/node/25199)).
- **Pigments (technical studies):** vermilion, naphthol red, red earth, zinc yellow, chrome orange, ultramarine, emerald and chrome green ([Thyssen](https://www.museothyssen.org/en/node/25199)); French ultramarine, iron-oxide red/pink, zinc white in the 1905-06 layer; viridian, Prussian blue, earths, red lake in a later, darker picture ([Courtauld](https://courtauld.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/Report-Derain-by-F-Whitlum-Cooper-and-Cleo-Nisse.compressed.pdf)).
- **Approximate hex (judgement, for those pigments):** vermilion `#e23d28`, naphthol red `#d42a3c`, pink (iron red + white) `#e88a8a`, zinc yellow `#f5dc3a`, chrome orange `#f08a1c`, ultramarine `#2b3a9a`, emerald green `#2fae72`, chrome green `#3d7a3a`, violet (ultramarine + red) `#7a3a8a`, white ground `#f6f3ea`.
- **Avoid:** black and earth browns as shadow, greys, tonal mixtures, glazes.

## 5. Light and value
- Light is everywhere and intense; "hardly any shadows" ([Thyssen](https://www.museothyssen.org/en/node/25199)). Value key **high**, value contrast low; chroma contrast high (**judgement** following the Thyssen description).
- Where light is strongest, paint is thickest (the sun corner of *Waterloo Bridge*: Thyssen).

## 6. Drawing and form
- Forms simplified to the point of abstraction ([Tate](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/art-terms/f/fauvism)).
- Initial layout in a thin blue painted line ("Derain often used a thin blue painted line as a way of marking out an initial composition": [Courtauld](https://courtauld.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/Report-Derain-by-F-Whitlum-Cooper-and-Cleo-Nisse.compressed.pdf)) or very light glazes of ultramarine ([Thyssen](https://www.museothyssen.org/en/node/25199)).
- "Thin dark fluid lines to indicate contours" keep shapes from eliding even with wet-in-wet paint ([Courtauld](https://courtauld.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/Report-Derain-by-F-Whitlum-Cooper-and-Cleo-Nisse.compressed.pdf)).
- No compositional changes: the painter remained faithful to the initial idea and the preparatory drawing (X-ray, [Thyssen](https://www.museothyssen.org/en/node/25199)).

## 7. Composition
- Flat pictorial space built of colour areas, no traditional perspective ([Tate](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/art-terms/f/fauvism)).
- Divided into zones with their own stroke direction and density, e.g. sky vs. river; the middle band painted thinner and smoother "to give depth" ([Thyssen](https://www.museothyssen.org/en/node/25199)).
- Subjects: harbours, landscapes, river views, windows, portraits; **judgement:** open, light-filled motifs suit the current better than interiors at night.

## 8. Marks and edges
- **Tools:** flat bristle brushes for "patches of colour", fine brush for thin dark contour lines ([Courtauld](https://courtauld.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/Report-Derain-by-F-Whitlum-Cooper-and-Cleo-Nisse.compressed.pdf)).
- **Strokes:** "a mosaic of loose brushstrokes that allow the canvas to show through"; strokes "thickly applied, rarely overlapping, and ... a certain distance apart" in sky and water, thin and continuous in the middle band ([Thyssen](https://www.museothyssen.org/en/node/25199)). Thin scumble over the priming in places ([Courtauld](https://courtauld.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/Report-Derain-by-F-Whitlum-Cooper-and-Cleo-Nisse.compressed.pdf)). Impasto from "very high" (no-ground paintings) to thin.
- **Direction:** per zone (vertical from the light source, horizontal for water) ([Thyssen](https://www.museothyssen.org/en/node/25199)).
- **Edges:** hard by juxtaposition; contour lines where shapes would merge.

## 9. Process (the order of work)
From the two technical studies (Teodor phases in brackets):
1. **Sketch or drawing** from the motif, close in form and colour to the painting ([Thyssen](https://www.museothyssen.org/en/node/25199)). [schema]
2. **White ground** meant to stay visible ([Thyssen](https://www.museothyssen.org/en/node/25199); [Courtauld](https://courtauld.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/Report-Derain-by-F-Whitlum-Cooper-and-Cleo-Nisse.compressed.pdf)). [`new "#f6f3ea"`]
3. **Layout** with a thin blue line or light ultramarine glazes ([Courtauld](https://courtauld.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/Report-Derain-by-F-Whitlum-Cooper-and-Cleo-Nisse.compressed.pdf); [Thyssen](https://www.museothyssen.org/en/node/25199)). [phase 1, `liner`]
4. **Key line first** (the horizon and bridge), then the zones, each with its own stroke direction ([Thyssen](https://www.museothyssen.org/en/node/25199)). [phase 1-2]
5. **Direct, mostly wet-in-wet** patches of nearly pure colour, few layers, no reworking ([Courtauld](https://courtauld.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/Report-Derain-by-F-Whitlum-Cooper-and-Cleo-Nisse.compressed.pdf); X-ray: [Thyssen](https://www.museothyssen.org/en/node/25199)). [phase 2]
6. **Contour lines** in thin dark fluid paint where shapes would merge (Courtauld; order after the patches is **judgement**). [phase 3]

## 10. Finishing
- No varnish tone: the yellowed natural-resin varnishes on these pictures were judged non-original and removed, restoring luminosity ([Courtauld](https://courtauld.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/Report-Derain-by-F-Whitlum-Cooper-and-Cleo-Nisse.compressed.pdf); [Thyssen](https://www.museothyssen.org/en/node/25199)).
- "Finished" means the colour chord is complete and the ground still breathes; stop early rather than fill gaps (**judgement**, following the deliberately exposed ground).
- No glazes, no blending, no last darks except contour lines.

## 11. Pitfalls for Teodor
- **Pastiche:** red trees, green faces, a "wild colour" filter over a naturalistic picture. Fauve colour is structured (complementary pairs, zones, light), not random (**judgement**).
- **Filling every gap:** covering the white ground kills the light.
- **Mud from wet-in-wet:** high `wetmix` with complementaries greys them; keep strokes apart.
- **Nearest wrong feelings:** decorative prettiness (holiday poster), and the darker anguish of Expressionism; Teodor's hushed, bittersweet dark interior is the opposite of this current.
- Engine: `do_match` closes the surface; it must run with low `cover` and coarse grid, or be replaced by `free` strokes.

## 12. Engine mapping
- **Oil (exists):** `new "#f6f3ea"`; layout with `liner` in ultramarine `#2b3a9a`, thin; zones as `rect`/`poly` regions, each with `free` strokes, `flat` size 18-35, `length` [15, 40], `direction` per zone (`{"away": [sun]}` or `{"angle": 0}`), `wetmix` 0.25-0.4, `pickup` 0.15, `cover` 0.95, `impasto` 0.5-0.8 thickest near the light; `count` modest so ground stays visible; contour lines with `liner` in dark blue or red-violet. No `glaze`, no `blend`.
- **Raw-canvas option:** `new "#cdb892"` with thick single layers (`impasto` 0.8).
- **Felt bias:** arousal +0.4, tension 0.0, care -0.2 (**judgement**: fast, confident, unfussy; valence left to the painter, though the current leans positive).
- **Gaps:** no spacing control so strokes stay "a certain distance apart" (only `count`); no zone-wise palette rule; raw unprimed canvas absorbency is not modelled.
