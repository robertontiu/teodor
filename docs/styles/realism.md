# Realism (19th-century French Realism)

Researched 2026-11. Every value names its source; values marked **judgement** have no published source and are editorial choices for Teodor.

## 1. In one paragraph
A French current that flourished from about 1840 to the late nineteenth century, after the Revolution of 1848 and under the Second Empire. It wanted "a truthful and objective vision of contemporary life", rejecting both the idealised classicism of the Academy and the exotic themes of Romanticism, and based its work on direct observation of the modern world ([Met, Nineteenth-Century French Realism](https://www.metmuseum.org/toah/hd/rlsm/hd_rlsm.htm)). Its subjects were ordinary people and places: rural labourers (stonebreakers, gleaners, sheepshearers), third-class travel, provincial funerals, the artist's own region, given the monumental scale once reserved for history painting (same source). Gustave Courbet, Jean-François Millet and Honoré Daumier are named here only as historical context.

## 2. How it carries feeling
- **Weight and posture, not expression.** Hardship is carried by bent backs, heavy bodies and work, not by theatrical faces: Millet's bent gleaners carry physical strain; Daumier's weary figures carry daily hardship ([Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/toah/hd/rlsm/hd_rlsm.htm)).
- **Monumental plainness.** Ordinary people at large scale and with sculptural presence; generalised figures rather than portrait detail (Millet "applied paint heavily and generalized his figures": same source).
- **Material presence of the world.** Rock, earth and water are made physically present through thick, troweled paint whose handling follows each material ([Met, *The Source of the Loue*](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/436023)).
- **Restraint.** No allegory, no idealisation, no sentiment pushed by colour; the feeling comes from the dignity and gravity of the subject. When Realism slips, it slips into sentimentality (the Met notes the sentimental quality of Millet's generalised figures). **Judgement:** the felt register is sober: gravity, endurance, solidity, quiet dignity; anger only when the subject is injustice (Daumier's *Rue Transnonain*, "unsparing in its grim depiction": Met).
- Avoided: idealised beauty, smooth academic finish, mythological pretext, bright decorative colour.

## 3. Media, supports and grounds
- **Oil on canvas** (default) and **charcoal / black conté crayon on paper** (second). Courbet drew portraits "in charcoal or black pencil" (*Young Man Sitting*, charcoal on paper, c. 1847, [Google Arts & Culture / Musée du Louvre-Orsay record](https://artsandculture.google.com/asset/young-man-sitting-study-self-portrait-known-as-at-the-easel/5gGgmfez-G4BOQ)); around 1850 Millet began making independent, highly finished drawings in black conté crayon ([Morgan Library, Millet drawing](https://www.themorgan.org/drawings/item/247412)). Lithography (Daumier) is out of scope.
- **Ground: dark.** Courbet's seascape is painted "atop a dark ground that breaks through to the surface" ([NGA, *The Black Rocks at Trouville*](https://www.nga.gov/content/ngaweb/Collection/art-object-page.154844.html)); Bomford describes "a very dark underlying colour" in *The Diligence in the Snow* (quoted in [thehistoryofart.org, Courbet style and technique](https://www.thehistoryofart.org/gustave-courbet/style-and-technique/), which warns against assuming it for every canvas). Teodor default: a dark warm brown ground `#3a2e24` (**judgement**: value chosen to read as "very dark" and warm; no measured colour found).
- **Drawing paper:** charcoal "requires paper with some texture" ([Met, Charcoal](https://www.metmuseum.org/en/perspectives/materials-and-techniques-drawing-charcoal)); Teodor default a warm off-white laid paper `#e9e1cf` (**judgement**).

## 4. Palette
- **Principle:** a restricted, earth-dominated tonal palette organised by value, with local colour subdued and a few strong accents (**judgement** grounded in the dark grounds and the Met's "deliberately simple style"; the Met essay does not discuss colour, and no Realist pigment survey was reachable).
- **Colours (approximate, judgement):** lead white `#ece4d2`, yellow ochre `#c08a3e`, raw sienna `#a8743a`, burnt sienna `#8a4a2a`, raw umber `#5a4630`, burnt umber `#4a3324`, vermilion accent `#c2412b`, Prussian/ultramarine dull blue `#3c4a5c`, viridian-earth green `#4d5a3a`, ivory black `#1c1a18`.
- **Black is allowed** (unlike Impressionism): ivory and bone blacks stayed in general use in the period ([eclecticlight, pigments after 1850](https://eclecticlight.co/2015/10/06/pigments-technique-%e2%86%92-style-4-after-1850/)).
- **Avoid:** high-key prismatic colour, coloured shadows as a system, saturated decorative fields, pastel tints.

## 5. Light and value
- Contrast of light and dark guides the eye ([Getty, *Landscape near Ornans*](https://www.getty.edu/education/teachers/classroom_resources/curricula/landscapes/ib_ornans.html)). Value key **low to middle**, with lights built up thick out of a dark ground (dark ground: NGA; "lights thick, darks thin" ordering is **judgement**, consistent with dark-ground practice).
- Shadows stay warm, transparent and close to the ground colour; lights are opaque and loaded (**judgement**).
- Charcoal: masses of grey are spread by stumping, and highlights lifted with an eraser ([Met, Charcoal](https://www.metmuseum.org/en/perspectives/materials-and-techniques-drawing-charcoal)); Millet's finished sheets model form by light and shadow ([Morgan](https://www.themorgan.org/drawings/item/247412)).

## 6. Drawing and form
- Form built as solid mass and weight; figures generalised and sculptural rather than detailed ([Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/toah/hd/rlsm/hd_rlsm.htm)).
- Proportion broadly observed, but academic rules of scale and perspective could be broken: Courbet's *Young Ladies of the Village* "breaks conventional rules of scale and perspective" (Met). Space may feel flattened; depth comes from overlapping forms rather than atmospheric perspective ([Getty](https://www.getty.edu/education/teachers/classroom_resources/curricula/landscapes/ib_ornans.html)).
- Drawing rhythm can echo the action (Millet's repeated rounded lines mirror the motion of churning: [Morgan](https://www.themorgan.org/drawings/item/247412)).

## 7. Composition
- Ordinary subjects at monumental scale; frieze-like rows of figures or a single labourer filling the frame ([Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/toah/hd/rlsm/hd_rlsm.htm)).
- Landscape: cliffs, rocks and water close to the viewer, replacing classical framing devices; a curving path or overlapping forms for depth ([Getty](https://www.getty.edu/education/teachers/classroom_resources/curricula/landscapes/ib_ornans.html)).
- Eye level, a viewer standing among the subject; no heroic low angle (**judgement**).

## 8. Marks and edges
- **Tools:** brushes of several kinds plus the palette knife: "sculpted by a variety of brushes as well as by his famous palette knife"; "smears of the knife define the foreground" ([NGA](https://www.nga.gov/content/ngaweb/Collection/art-object-page.154844.html)). Paint "vigorously trowelled" on, the strokes adjusted to the qualities of each object ([Met, *The Source of the Loue*](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/436023)).
- **Visibility:** handling visible, thick in the lights and in rock/earth textures (impasto); thinner and quieter in figures' faces and in shadow (**judgement**).
- **Edges:** firm where mass meets mass, lost into the dark ground in shadow (**judgement**).
- **Charcoal:** stumped masses (finger, chamois, cork), lifted highlights, linear accents on top ([Met, Charcoal](https://www.metmuseum.org/en/perspectives/materials-and-techniques-drawing-charcoal)).

## 9. Process (the order of work)
Oil (Teodor phases in brackets):
1. **Studies** from observation; for some Realists extensive preparatory drawings and anatomical study (Bonheur: [Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/toah/hd/rlsm/hd_rlsm.htm)). [blockout / schema]
2. **Dark ground** laid over the canvas ([NGA](https://www.nga.gov/content/ngaweb/Collection/art-object-page.154844.html)). [`new` with the ground colour]
3. **Lay-in of the big masses** in thin dark-to-middle values, letting the ground serve as the shadows (**judgement**, follows from the dark ground breaking through). [phase 1, large flats and rounds]
4. **Building the lights** with loaded brush and knife, each material handled in its own way ([Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/436023)); compositions could be revised over many layers (at least eight paint layers and three successive compositions in *The Wounded Man*: thehistoryofart.org summary of technical study). [phase 2]
5. **Accents and finishing** (below). [phase 3]

Charcoal:
1. Light block-in of the figure's masses; 2. stumped tonal masses; 3. darks reinforced; 4. lights lifted with eraser/bread; 5. linear accents and contour; 6. fixative ([Met, Charcoal](https://www.metmuseum.org/en/perspectives/materials-and-techniques-drawing-charcoal); order is **judgement** consistent with the techniques it lists). A drawing could be "highly finished" or a quick sketch with loose strokes ([Morgan](https://www.themorgan.org/drawings/item/247412)).

## 10. Finishing
- Oil: "finished" meant a resolved, weighty image, not a polished academic surface: critics found the style crude ([Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/toah/hd/rlsm/hd_rlsm.htm)). Last stage: a few lightest knife accents in lights, the darkest accents, and a natural-resin varnish that warmed the whole (varnishing was customary practice the Impressionists later rejected: [Met, Impressionism](https://www.metmuseum.org/toah/hd/imml/hd_imml.htm)). Teodor: a very light warm unifying glaze is allowed (**judgement**).
- Charcoal: highly finished sheets in the 19th century, with textural effects and scraping; fixative applied to finished drawings ([Met, Charcoal](https://www.metmuseum.org/en/perspectives/materials-and-techniques-drawing-charcoal)). Signed in the same medium at lower right ([Morgan](https://www.themorgan.org/drawings/item/247412)).

## 11. Pitfalls for Teodor
- **Brown-gravy pastiche:** everything umber, a museum-brown filter. Keep a real value structure and a few true local colours (**judgement**).
- **Sentimental genre picture:** the noble peasant with a tear, sunset glow. Realism drifts into sentimentality (Met on Millet); the target is gravity, not pity.
- **Photographic hyper-realism:** "Realism" is not photo-realism; the surface is heavy, generalised, handled.
- **Nearest wrong feelings:** melancholy nostalgia (Teodor's own habit: hushed, bittersweet, lit doorway in a dark room, see `docs/habits.md`), heroic propaganda, academic polish.
- Engine: knife impasto and the dark ground exist; charcoal does not exist yet.

- **Oil (exists):** `new "#3a2e24"`; `do_match` with `brush: "flat"`/`"round"` sizes 40-90 for the lay-in, low `impasto` in shadows; `brush: "knife"` with `impasto` 0.7-0.9 for lights, rocks, earth and water; `glaze` (low opacity, warm) only as a finishing unifier; `liner` sparingly for the last darkest accents.
- **Felt bias:** arousal 0.0 (deliberate, not gestural), tension +0.1, care +0.2 (**judgement**).
- **Charcoal (being added):** needs stick vs. compressed charcoal, stumping (`smudge` is a proxy), lifting/erasing (no subtractive tool exists), paper tooth (the canvas `tex` is a weave, not paper), fixative as a finishing step.
