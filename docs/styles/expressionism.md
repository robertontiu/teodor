# German Expressionism

Researched 2026-11. Every value names its source; values marked **judgement** have no published source and are editorial choices for Teodor.

## 1. In one paragraph
German Expressionism was an early twentieth-century movement "that emphasized the artist's inner feelings or ideas over replicating reality", characterised by "simplified shapes, bright colours and gestural marks or brushstrokes"; its two main groups were Die Brücke (Dresden, then Berlin) and Der Blaue Reiter (Munich) ([Tate, German expressionism](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/art-terms/g/german-expressionism)). Brücke, founded in Dresden in 1905, developed "a radical anti-traditional style characterised by vivid non-naturalistic colour and emotional tension", blending old German art and non-European sculpture with Post-Impressionism and Fauvism ([Tate, Brücke](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/art-terms/b/brucke)). Its art "confronted feelings of alienation from the modern world by reaching back to pre-academic forms of expression including woodcut prints, carved wooden sculptures" ([The Art Story, Die Brücke](https://www.theartstory.org/movement/die-brucke/)). This configuration follows the Brücke strand (figurative: painting, charcoal and the woodcut look); Der Blaue Reiter, which moved toward abstraction and a spiritual value of colour (same source), would be a separate style. Ernst Ludwig Kirchner, Erich Heckel and Karl Schmidt-Rottluff are named only as historical context.

## 2. How it carries feeling
- **Inner state over appearance.** Landscapes and portraits "become dramatic expressions of the artist's inner feelings or emotions"; colours "intense and non-naturalistic", forms "distorted", brushwork "free", paint "generous and highly textured" ([Tate, Expressionism](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/art-terms/e/expressionism)).
- **Tension and alienation.** "Emotional tension" ([Tate, Brücke](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/art-terms/b/brucke)); alienation from the modern city ([The Art Story](https://www.theartstory.org/movement/die-brucke/)). Street scenes carry nervous excitement: hatched lines "become sharp rays in the gouges of woodcut", a figure "nearly electrified as the excitement of the street is transferred to his body" ([MoMA, Kirchner and the Berlin Street, working process](https://www.moma.org/momaorg/shared/pdfs/docs/learn/courses/Kirchner.pdf)).
- **Directness and strong contrast.** The woodcut was chosen as "the most direct form of expression", making "powerful statements with strong contrasts" ([The Art Story](https://www.theartstory.org/movement/die-brucke/)).
- **The other pole:** freedom, the body in nature (bathing at the Moritzburg lakes: [Tate, Brücke](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/art-terms/b/brucke)); the feeling then is raw vitality, not anguish.
- **Matte intensity.** Brilliance without gloss ([artdaily on the Kirchner colour exhibition / Doerner Institut research](https://artdaily.com/news/70440/images/close.gif)).
- Avoided: polish, academic modelling, harmonious naturalistic colour, prettiness.

## 3. Media, supports and grounds
- **Oil on canvas** (default), **charcoal on paper** (second), and the **woodcut look** (black ink on paper or flat colour blocks) as a third mode. Kirchner's Street Scene work alone includes "countless notebook sketches, some thirty large drawings in pen and ink and wash, as well as in pastel and charcoal, and over twenty prints" ([MoMA](https://www.moma.org/momaorg/shared/pdfs/docs/learn/courses/Kirchner.pdf)); the woodcut "became one of their most important artistic mediums" ([Städel Museum, The Mysteries of Material](https://www.staedelmuseum.de/en/mysteries-material)).
- **Oil paint handling:** the Brücke painters thinned their paints "as far as at all possible"; to get a matte surface Kirchner used little binder and added wax dissolved in benzine, and painted onto a ground that bonded with it ([Kunsthalle Mannheim, conservator K. Radermacher, via Rheinneckarblog](https://rheinneckarblog.de/10/die-maltechnik-von-kirchner/65884.html); translated). Ground colour not found in reachable sources; Teodor default a light, slightly warm white `#efe9dc` (**judgement**).
- **Charcoal paper:** off-white `#e6dfcf` (**judgement**). **Woodcut:** printed on wove paper ([LACMA, K. G. Brücke portfolio 1910](https://collections.lacma.org/object/71942)); paper `#ece4d0`, ink `#141210` (**judgement**).
- Wood itself mattered: its "unevenness and grain, but also its differing hues and degrees of hardness" ([Städel](https://www.staedelmuseum.de/en/mysteries-material)).

## 4. Palette
- **Principle:** vivid, non-naturalistic, high-chroma colour used for tension and contrast ([Tate, Brücke](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/art-terms/b/brucke)); newly available aniline (tar) colours alongside tube paints ([Rheinneckarblog / Kunsthalle Mannheim](https://rheinneckarblog.de/10/die-maltechnik-von-kirchner/65884.html)); matte, not glossy.
- **Approximate hex (judgement):** acid green `#7cc242`, chrome yellow `#f0c419`, vermilion `#e2382a`, magenta/aniline pink `#d23c8c`, orange `#ef7a1e`, ultramarine `#2a3a9e`, Prussian blue `#1e3a5f`, violet `#6a3a8c`, black `#141210` for contour and woodcut, raw ground `#efe9dc`.
- **Colour preview:** colour solutions were tried in pastels before paintings ([MoMA](https://www.moma.org/momaorg/shared/pdfs/docs/learn/courses/Kirchner.pdf)).
- **Avoid:** glossy depth, browns as harmonisers, naturalistic flesh, smooth gradations.

## 5. Light and value
- No consistent light source; value contrast high and colour contrast high; lights and darks allude to "flickering signs or headlights" without evidence of their sources ([MoMA](https://www.moma.org/momaorg/shared/pdfs/docs/learn/courses/Kirchner.pdf)).
- Woodcut: two values only, black and paper (**judgement** following "strong contrasts": The Art Story).
- Charcoal: grey wash or shading to emphasise rhythm, hatching for contour ([MoMA](https://www.moma.org/momaorg/shared/pdfs/docs/learn/courses/Kirchner.pdf)).

## 6. Drawing and form
- Simplified and distorted forms ([Tate](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/art-terms/e/expressionism)); angular, grainy, stark (the medieval woodcut lineage the group revived: [The Art Story](https://www.theartstory.org/movement/die-brucke/)).
- Underdrawings exist beneath the paint and layers were reworked; "in contrast to the stereotypical notion of the immediacy of Expressionist painting, Kirchner's work proves to be precisely planned and carefully developed" ([artdaily / Doerner Institut](https://artdaily.com/news/70440/images/close.gif)). Yet no detailed preparatory drawings: "forms arise and undergo change during the process of work" ([MoMA](https://www.moma.org/momaorg/shared/pdfs/docs/learn/courses/Kirchner.pdf)).
- Elongated figures, mask-like faces (**judgement**, commonplace in the literature, not verified here).

## 7. Composition
- Crowded city streets with tilted, compressed space, or nudes in nature (**judgement** for tilt; subjects from [Tate, Brücke](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/art-terms/b/brucke) and [MoMA](https://www.moma.org/momaorg/shared/pdfs/docs/learn/courses/Kirchner.pdf)).
- Rhythm of figures in motion carried by repeated marks ([MoMA](https://www.moma.org/momaorg/shared/pdfs/docs/learn/courses/Kirchner.pdf)). Woodcut compositions come out reversed from the drawing on the block (same source).

## 8. Marks and edges
- **Paint:** free, generous, textured ([Tate](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/art-terms/e/expressionism)), yet thinned and matte in Brücke practice (Mannheim); **judgement:** resolve as thin, dry, dragged strokes of strong colour, hatched, with dark angular contours; the "generous" texture comes from stroke energy, not oil impasto.
- **Charcoal and pen:** broad strokes "to add dramatic emphasis", "quick hatchings to define contours", grey wash for rhythm ([MoMA](https://www.moma.org/momaorg/shared/pdfs/docs/learn/courses/Kirchner.pdf)).
- **Woodcut look:** hatching becomes "sharp rays in the gouges" ([MoMA](https://www.moma.org/momaorg/shared/pdfs/docs/learn/courses/Kirchner.pdf)); stark, angular, grainy ([The Art Story](https://www.theartstory.org/movement/die-brucke/)); wood grain visible in flat blacks ([Städel](https://www.staedelmuseum.de/en/mysteries-material)). Edges hard, straight-sided, splintered (**judgement**).

## 9. Process (the order of work)
Oil (Teodor phases in brackets):
1. **Seeds:** quick sketchbook impressions, sometimes charcoal, pastel colour trials, prints of the same motif ([MoMA](https://www.moma.org/momaorg/shared/pdfs/docs/learn/courses/Kirchner.pdf)). [schema]
2. **Underdrawing** on the canvas ([artdaily / Doerner](https://artdaily.com/news/70440/images/close.gif)). [`transfer` + `liner`]
3. **Thin, lean colour fields** in vivid hues, drying fast and matte ([Mannheim](https://rheinneckarblog.de/10/die-maltechnik-von-kirchner/65884.html)). [phase 1-2]
4. **Rework and restate**, forms changing in the process; reworked paint layers ([artdaily](https://artdaily.com/news/70440/images/close.gif); [MoMA](https://www.moma.org/momaorg/shared/pdfs/docs/learn/courses/Kirchner.pdf)). [phase 2]
5. **Hatching and dark contours** (**judgement** on order). [phase 3]

Charcoal: block-in of rhythm with broad strokes, hatching for contours, grey wash or stumping for movement, emphasis lines last ([MoMA](https://www.moma.org/momaorg/shared/pdfs/docs/learn/courses/Kirchner.pdf); order is **judgement**).
Woodcut look: drawing reversed onto the block; cut away the whites with gouges, leaving black relief; ink and print by press or by hand ([Tate, Woodcut](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/art-terms/w/woodcut)). Teodor cannot cut, so it paints the result: flat black masses, then cuts as hard-edged white strokes painted on top (**judgement**).

## 10. Finishing
- **No varnish:** Kirchner used no final varnish, which gives depth but also gloss, "which the Brücke painters all wanted to avoid" ([Mannheim / Rheinneckarblog](https://rheinneckarblog.de/10/die-maltechnik-von-kirchner/65884.html); translated). Teodor: no glaze, matte surface (low `impasto`).
- "Finished" left the energy of the process visible; the paintings were "precisely planned and carefully developed" nonetheless ([artdaily](https://artdaily.com/news/70440/images/close.gif)).
- Charcoal: fixative (see `realism.md`, Met charcoal source). Woodcut: printing is the finish; wood grain and uneven inking accepted.

## 11. Pitfalls for Teodor
- **Horror-poster pastiche:** green faces and screaming masks as a filter. Distortion must come from the felt state, not a stock look (**judgement**).
- **Oil gloss and impasto:** Brücke surfaces were matte and thin; thick shiny paint is wrong.
- **Random scribble:** the paintings were planned; the energy is in the stroke, not in chaos.
- **Nearest wrong feelings:** Fauvist joy (same colours, opposite tension), generic angst, and Teodor's hushed bittersweet habit.
- Engine: no woodcut medium and no subtractive cut; charcoal not built yet.

## 12. Engine mapping
- **Oil (exists):** `new "#efe9dc"`; `transfer` + `liner` underdrawing in dark blue; colour fields with `flat` size 25-45, `impasto` 0.1-0.2 (lean, matte), `wetmix` 0.15, `load` 0.6 so speed-driven dry-brush breaks appear; hatching with `free`, `round` size 6-12, `length` [25, 70], `curve` low, `direction {"angle": 70, "spread": 12}`; contours with `liner` in `#141210` or ultramarine. Felt state drives distortion (high tension switches to jagged angular polylines when valence is negative, which suits this current).
- **Felt bias:** arousal +0.4, tension +0.5, care -0.2 (**judgement**).
- **Charcoal (being added):** needs broad side-of-stick strokes, hatching, wash/stumping, fixative.
- **Woodcut look (gap):** no relief-print medium. Proxy: `liner`/`flat` with `edge: "hard"`, black on paper, white "cuts" painted over; wood-grain texture and uneven inking are missing; a future `ink` medium could carry a woodcut mode.
- **Gaps:** no matte/gloss surface parameter (render always adds a slight specular on height); no subtractive tool; Blaue Reiter would need its own entry.
