# Impressionism

Researched 2026-11. Every value names its source; values marked **judgement** have no published source and are editorial choices for Teodor.

## 1. In one paragraph
A group of independent artists in Paris launched Impressionism with an 1874 exhibition and held eight exhibitions between 1874 and 1886, united mainly by independence from the official Salon ([Met, Impressionism: Art and Modernity](https://www.metmuseum.org/toah/hd/imml/hd_imml.htm)). Their work is recognised for "short, broken brushstrokes that barely convey forms, pure unblended colors, and an emphasis on the effects of light", for shadows and highlights rendered in colour rather than neutral greys and black, for newly available synthetic pigments, and for refusing the golden varnish that toned paintings down (same source). Subjects: modern leisure (boating, bathing), boulevards, cafés, theatres, dancers, landscape under specific weather and hour (same source). Claude Monet, Pierre-Auguste Renoir and Edgar Degas are named only as historical context.

## 2. How it carries feeling
- **Light and air as the subject.** The feeling is of a moment's light, weather and atmosphere, carried by colour temperature (warm lights, cool coloured shadows) rather than by drama of value ([Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/toah/hd/imml/hd_imml.htm)).
- **Spontaneity.** "The artists' loose brushwork gives an effect of spontaneity and effortlessness", even when the composition was planned (Met).
- **Detachment.** Urban scenes take "a detached, objective view" rather than a sentimental one (Met). The feeling is perceptual (freshness, brightness, a passing instant, sometimes the chill of snow or fog), not narrative.
- **Vibration of colour.** Unblended adjacent strokes and complementaries make the surface shimmer; in pastel Degas set oranges against blues and reds against greens "side by side ... in overlapping vertical strokes that exposed the underlying colors" ([Met, Precious Powder: Degas](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/precious-powder-degas)).
- Avoided: black shadows, brown tonal unity, high finish, story-telling and sentiment.

## 3. Media, supports and grounds
- **Oil on canvas** (default) and **pastel on paper** (second).
- **Oil ground:** off-white, single layer of lead white and chalk on commercially prepared canvas, in two technically examined Monets ([Art Institute of Chicago, *Stacks of Wheat (End of Summer)*](https://publications.artic.edu/node/135599); [AIC Monet catalogue, cat. entry with technical report](https://publications.artic.edu/api/epub/135564/content.xhtml)). Impressionists preferred the white ground ([Wikipedia, Ground (art)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_(art))). Teodor default: `#ebe5d6` (**judgement**, an off-white).
- **Pastel support:** Degas chose low-quality machine-made wood-pulp paper for its colour and rough texture, sometimes light green (now discoloured to warm grey), glued to millboard ([Met, Degas](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/precious-powder-degas)). Teodor default: a mid-light toned paper `#c9c3ae` (**judgement**).

## 4. Palette
- **Principle:** pure, mostly unmixed spectral colours plus white; shadows and highlights in colour; little or no black ([Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/toah/hd/imml/hd_imml.htm); *Lavacourt under Snow* analysed as "mostly unmixed pigments": [ColourLex / National Gallery](https://colourlex.com/project/monet-lavacourt-under-snow)).
- **Pigments found in technical analysis** (AIC and National Gallery): lead white, cadmium yellow, chrome yellow, vermilion, red lake, emerald green, viridian, cobalt blue, (artificial) ultramarine ([AIC *Stacks of Wheat*](https://publications.artic.edu/node/135599); [AIC catalogue](https://publications.artic.edu/api/epub/135564/content.xhtml); [ColourLex](https://colourlex.com/project/monet-lavacourt-under-snow)). Cadmium yellow was costly; Monet adopted it after commercial success in the 1880s ([eclecticlight](https://eclecticlight.co/2015/10/06/pigments-technique-%e2%86%92-style-4-after-1850/)).
- **Approximate hex (judgement, for those pigments):** lead white `#f1ece0`, cadmium yellow `#f2c230`, chrome yellow `#e9a800`, vermilion `#e0452b`, red lake `#b0304a`, emerald green `#3fae7a`, viridian `#2f7a64`, cobalt blue `#2f55a8`, ultramarine `#2b3a8f`.
- **Typical mixtures (sourced):** snow as cobalt blue + lead white over an ultramarine underlayer; reds as vermilion over cobalt + red lake + white ([ColourLex](https://colourlex.com/project/monet-lavacourt-under-snow)). Even a grey roof is several bright pigments mixed ([AIC](https://publications.artic.edu/api/epub/135564/content.xhtml)).
- **Avoid:** black and earth browns as shadow colour; muddy over-mixing; glazes.

## 5. Light and value
- Outdoor, specific light (hour, season, weather); value key **high**, compressed into the upper half; colour temperature, not value, does most of the modelling ([Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/toah/hd/imml/hd_imml.htm); "high key" is **judgement** following the white ground and the rejection of black).
- Shadows coloured (blues, violets, greens), never neutral (Met).

## 6. Drawing and form
- No underdrawing found by infrared reflectography; forms laid in directly with thin paint in subdued colours ([AIC *Stacks of Wheat*](https://publications.artic.edu/node/135599)).
- Strokes "barely convey forms" ([Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/toah/hd/imml/hd_imml.htm)): contour dissolves into touches; figures are a few gestural strokes of incompletely mixed colour ([AIC](https://publications.artic.edu/api/epub/135564/content.xhtml)).
- Degas's pastel line remained a drawing tool: "linear marks, squiggles, zigzags, and stumped powder" ([Met, Degas](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/precious-powder-degas)).

## 7. Composition
- Modern leisure and urban life, often with a planned composition under the spontaneous handling ([Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/toah/hd/imml/hd_imml.htm)).
- Horizon set early and the whole worked up together ([AIC](https://publications.artic.edu/node/135599)).
- **Judgement:** casual, cropped, off-centre framing and high or oblique viewpoints (a commonplace of the literature, not verified in a fetched source here).

## 8. Marks and edges
- **Tools:** flat-ferrule brushes about 1.0 and 1.5 cm wide ([AIC](https://publications.artic.edu/node/135599); [AIC](https://publications.artic.edu/api/epub/135564/content.xhtml)). At Teodor's 1200x1600 canvas for a ~65x80 cm picture, that is roughly 18-28 px (**judgement**, scale conversion).
- **Strokes:** short, broken, unblended ([Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/toah/hd/imml/hd_imml.htm)); an "open network of strokes that leaves small areas of ground and earlier paint layers exposed"; late thin strokes dragged over the surface ([AIC](https://publications.artic.edu/node/135599)). Paint often applied quite thickly with brush marks left visible ([eclecticlight](https://eclecticlight.co/2015/10/06/pigments-technique-%e2%86%92-style-4-after-1850/)).
- **Edges:** soft and broken everywhere; no hard contour except for a few accents (**judgement**).
- **Pastel:** adjacent unblended strokes, overlapping verticals, squiggles, zigzags, some stumping ([Met, Degas](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/precious-powder-degas)).

## 9. Process (the order of work)
Oil (from AIC technical reports; Teodor phases in brackets):
1. **Off-white ground** ([AIC](https://publications.artic.edu/node/135599)). [`new "#ebe5d6"`]
2. **Lay-in** of the main elements with thin paint in subdued colours; horizon set early ([AIC](https://publications.artic.edu/node/135599)); main forms placed early and the composition "built up with little deviation from the initial lay-in" ([AIC](https://publications.artic.edu/api/epub/135564/content.xhtml)). [phase 1]
3. **Build up the whole together**, "moving back and forth between different areas"; surrounding fields (water, beach) brought up around the forms ([AIC](https://publications.artic.edu/node/135599); [AIC](https://publications.artic.edu/api/epub/135564/content.xhtml)). [phase 2]
4. **Wet-in-wet upper layers**, figures painted into the wet surroundings ([AIC](https://publications.artic.edu/api/epub/135564/content.xhtml)); later colours over dry paint in places (*Stacks of Wheat*). [phase 2, `dry` sparingly]
5. **Reinforce and accent:** sails reinforced, reflections last, intense local colour on top of figures, thin dragged strokes late, corners reinforced ([AIC](https://publications.artic.edu/api/epub/135564/content.xhtml); [AIC](https://publications.artic.edu/node/135599)). [phase 3]

Pastel: build in layers of linear and stumped marks; fixative only in selected areas as a barrier between layers, never over the whole, because it darkens and dulls ([Met, Degas](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/precious-powder-degas)).

## 10. Finishing
- **No golden varnish** ([Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/toah/hd/imml/hd_imml.htm)); the AIC Monets are now shown unvarnished ([AIC](https://publications.artic.edu/node/135599)). Teodor: no unifying glaze; finishing is a last pass of accents and reflections, with ground allowed to show.
- "Finished" meant the effect of light was complete, not that the surface was closed (**judgement**, following "open network of strokes").
- Pastel: no overall fixative (Met).

## 11. Pitfalls for Teodor
- **Chocolate-box pastiche:** water lilies, parasols, pretty pastel pinks; every stroke the same comma. Vary stroke size and direction with the form and keep the composition planned (**judgement**).
- **Mush:** over-blending kills the broken colour; keep mixtures incomplete and strokes unblended (Met, AIC).
- **Grey-black shadows** from habit: forbid black.
- **Nearest wrong feelings:** sweet nostalgia; generic "pleasant"; Teodor's hushed lit doorway. Impressionism's feeling is freshness and a specific moment.
- Engine: `do_match` follows the schema's gradient, which can make strokes too uniform and too obedient.

## 12. Engine mapping
- **Oil (exists):** `new "#ebe5d6"`; lay-in `do_match` with `flat`, size 30-50, thin (`impasto` 0.2, `cover` 0.7); build with `do_match`/`free`, `flat`, size 18-28, `wetmix` 0.6-0.8 (wet-in-wet), `color_jitter` ~0.05 for incompletely mixed colour, `cover` < 1 so ground shows; reflections and accents with small `flat`/`round`; no `glaze`, no `blend`.
- **Felt bias:** arousal +0.2, tension -0.1, care -0.1 (**judgement**: quick, relaxed, not fussy).
- **Pastel (being added):** needs toned paper tooth, stick marks that skip the tooth, stumping, selective fixative as a layer barrier.
- **Gaps:** no plein-air light model (the schema must state hour and weather); no per-stroke complementary placement (only jitter); no paper texture for pastel.
