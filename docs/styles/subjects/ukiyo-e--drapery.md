# Ukiyo-e: drapery (layer 2: the subject in the style)

How ukiyo-e draws cloth. The outline of the garment is a long, clean key line. Folds are a few lines, or blind embossing, never shade. Textile pattern lies flat across the folds, its motifs keeping their size and shape. Garments are stacks of flat colour areas, one per piece of cloth. The focus is a patterned shirt, an apron and the trousers of a seated figure. Read [the layer-1 page](../../subjects/drapery.md) first, then [ukiyo-e--figure](ukiyo-e--figure.md) and [ukiyo-e--objects](ukiyo-e--objects.md) section on textiles. Researched 2026-11 from public-domain prints at the Met and the Cleveland Museum of Art (CMA). Values marked **judgement** have no published source.

## 1. The departure from layer 1, in one paragraph
Layer 1 hangs cloth from support points and names seven fold types. It models each fold as a small cylinder with a lit top, halftone, shadow and valley, lets material set fold size, and keeps a hierarchy of one or two fold systems per section. Ukiyo-e keeps the **support points, the direction of fall and the hierarchy**, and removes the modelling. A garment is a **flat colour area inside a long key-line contour**. Folds are **a few lines**: long ones for pipe folds, short hooked ones at the hem or a bent joint. In white cloth they are **blind-embossed** instead of drawn (CMA 1985.309: "embossing of the fabric folds, lending a three-dimensional effect"). **Pattern is laid flat over everything.** Crests, flowers, checks and dots keep the same size and shape across folds and turns, and are cut off by the outline and by overlapping cloth rather than wrapped round the form (Met 55800, 55810, 37176, 37163, 55185; CMA 1956.753).

## 2. Pattern ignores the folds
- **Motifs do not foreshorten.** Round crests stay circles on a lap seen at an angle (Met 55810). Butterflies keep one size from shoulder to hem (Met 55800). Large flowers on a black robe run straight through the folds (CMA 1956.753). Late Meiji prints treat Western dresses the same way: rosettes and diamond lattices on a bustle dress keep their size and shape through every gather (Met 55185).
- **Motifs are cut, not bent.** The garment's outline, an overlapping sleeve or a fold line slices a motif through. The motif does not curve away round the form (Met 55185, 37176, 55810).
- **Stripes and checks:** these run straight within each panel and change angle where the cloth turns (Met 36922, the porter's blue checked jacket; Met 55222, checked jackets in a crowd; see `ukiyo-e--objects.md`). On a large, softly draped panel they may bend gently along its main fall (Met 55810, a striped robe curving round the lap). Either way their **spacing stays constant** and they are never shaded. This adds a nuance to the objects page: bending along the panel's fall is allowed, compressing toward a turn is not.
- **Pattern is printed as its own block**, often a darker or lighter flat colour on the garment's ground, with reserved paper motifs or fine key-line motifs (Met 55800, white butterflies on black; CMA 1956.753) (**judgement** on the block logic, consistent with the style doc's one colour per block).

## 3. The outline of cloth and its folds
- **Contour:** one long, smooth, continuous line, heavier than the face's lines. Its curves carry the cloth's weight: long sagging arcs for soft cloth, straighter runs and corners for stiff cloth (Met 55810, 54869; CMA 1956.753; Met 37176).
- **Folds:** a few lines, starting at a support point (shoulder, elbow, waist, knee) and running with the fall. Each ends in a taper or a small hook. They are drawn *over* the pattern (Met 55810; CMA 1956.753, long pipe folds down the skirts).
- **Hems and edges:** layered garments show at the hem, sleeve and collar as **parallel bands of flat colour** (lining, under-robe), each with its own key line (Met 55800, the sleeping attendant's red and grey layers; CMA 1985.309). Hem flips and zigzags are a few short broken lines.
- **White or plain cloth:** folds by blind embossing (*karazuri*) and a few lines, or lines alone (CMA 1985.309; style doc section 10). Teodor has no embossing yet, so lines alone it is (**judgement**, per the style doc's engine gaps).

## 4. The brief's clothes
- **Patterned shirt:** the shirt is one flat ground colour inside its outline. The pattern is a second flat colour (or paper reserve) laid evenly over the whole shirt at one motif size, cut off by the outline, the collar, the sleeve seams and the apron's edge. Fold lines at elbow, armpit and waist are drawn over the pattern in key line, and the collar and button placket are key lines (**judgement**, applying section 2; Met 55185 and 55182 show Western garments under the same rule).
- **Apron:** a separate flat panel in its own colour, overlapping the shirt and trousers with its own clean contour. Its ties and strings are key-line loops and bands, like an obi's ties (Met 55800, CMA 1985.309, bow ties as looped lines). Any pattern or lettering on it lies flat. One or two long lines show where it sags over the lap (**judgement** for the apron itself; no apron was among the works measured).
- **Trousers of a seated figure:** one flat colour per leg area inside the contour. The bent knee is told by the **angle of the outline**, not by shading. Two or three short compression lines sit behind the knee and at the groin, and one or two long lines run down the shin to the hem (Met 36922 and 36521, porters' close-fitting indigo leggings as flat colour with knee and calf in the outline; Met 55182, Western trousers as a flat pale area with a few fold lines; **judgement** for the seated case).

## 5. What stays true to life
Support points and the direction of fall, the order of overlap (apron over shirt over trousers), the place where cloth bunches (inside a bent knee or elbow) and the hierarchy of a few big folds over small ones all stay true. Material still shows in the contour: soft cloth sags in long curves, stiff cloth breaks at angles (**judgement**).

## 6. Checks for Teodor
1. Is each garment one flat colour inside a long, clean contour?
2. Are folds a few lines starting at support points, drawn over the pattern, with no shading?
3. Does every motif keep one size and shape across the folds, cut off by outlines and overlaps rather than wrapped?
4. Do stripes or checks keep constant spacing (straight per panel, or bending only along a panel's main fall)?
5. Do the apron, shirt and trousers overlap in the right order, each with its own contour and colour?
6. Is the seated figure's knee told by the outline's angle, with only a few compression lines?
