# Ukiyo-e: hands and feet (layer 2: the subject in the style)

How ukiyo-e draws hands and feet. They are made small. Refined hands are slender and the fingers curl back. Working hands and feet are blunt and drawn with a few lines. An object in the hand is a flat shape that the fingers overlap. Feet are a fan of small toes, and sandals are a few strap lines. Passers-by's feet are cut hard by the frame. Read [the layer-1 page](../../subjects/hands-feet.md) first, and [ukiyo-e--figure](ukiyo-e--figure.md). Researched 2026-11 from public-domain prints at the Met and the Cleveland Museum of Art (CMA). Values marked **judgement** have no published source. Ratios "read by eye" come from grid overlays of the museums' images and are ±0.1.

## 1. The departure from layer 1, in one paragraph
Layer 1 makes the hand as long as the face, builds it as a palm block with fingers of three stacked cylinders on an arched row of knuckles, and models it with a core shadow on each finger and dark gaps between them. The foot is about one head long, a wedge with an arch. Ukiyo-e **shrinks both**. The hand reads at about **half the face's length**, and the foot at about **0.7 of a head** (by eye: CMA 1956.753, Met 55810, CMA 1985.309, Met 36922). Both are drawn as **outline only**, with knuckles and toes as small hooks and lobes. There is no shading, no tendons and no nails. Hands are often half hidden in a sleeve. Hands and feet are not anatomy studies. They are small accents of skin at the ends of the clothing silhouette (**judgement** on the wording; observed in every print below).

## 2. Hands
- **Refined hands (beauties, young men):** long tapered fingers drawn as separate slender strokes. Fingertips often curl back or bend at the last joint. The thumb is small and the palm hardly drawn (Met 55810, the hand resting on the floor; CMA 1956.753, a hand at the chin; Met 54869).
- **Working hands:** blunter and mitten-like. Two or three lines split the fingers, and a hook marks a knuckle on a grip (Met 36922, porters gripping carrying-poles; Met 55222).
- **Hidden hands:** a hand is often half inside a sleeve, or holds an object *through* the sleeve's cloth, so only the fingertips show (Met 37163, Shun'ei: a crystal ball held up through the sleeve, fingers as curled segments with small knuckle hooks).
- **Relaxed hands:** fingers loosely curled in a gentle fan, the hand lying on a lap, the floor or another hand (Met 55810; CMA 1985.309, the sleeping attendant's hand by her face).

## 3. A hand holding a fruit
- The held object is a **flat disc or shape in its own flat colour** with a key-line contour, and at most one internal line for its form (a spiral, a stem, a cleft). Nothing is shaded (Met 37163: the ball is a flat yellow disc with a spiral line).
- The fingers **overlap** the object's contour as curled segments: two or three on the near side, the thumb on the other, with a small gap of object showing between them (Met 37163; **judgement** for a fruit, as no fruit-holding hand was found among the works looked at).
- A sleeper's grip is loose. The fruit rests in the cupped palm on the lap, the fingers part-open, the wrist bent (**judgement**, from layer 1's relaxed grip and the slack sleepers in Met 55800 and 55810).

## 4. Feet and footwear
- **Bare feet:** a small outline with a fan of toes as small lobes, the big toe set apart by one line, the ankle bone a tick, the heel one curve. There are no nails, tendons or sole shading (CMA 1985.309 and Met 55800, women's feet; Met 36922 and 36521, porters' feet).
- **Straw sandals (*waraji*, *zōri*):** the sole as a flat band under the foot and the straps as two or three lines across the instep and between the toes (Met 36922, 36521, 55222).
- **Western shoes and boots in Meiji prints:** a flat dark shape with a sole line and buttons or laces as dots and short lines, without any sheen (Met 55185, the girl's buttoned boots). This is the model for modern footwear (**judgement** that it transfers).
- **Socks or *tabi*:** a flat white or coloured shape with the split toe as one line (**judgement**, common in Edo prints; not among the works measured here).

## 5. Feet cropped by the frame
- Ukiyo-e cuts near figures hard at the frame edge, through the body if needed. Porters cut at the waist or shoulders at the bottom edge (Met 55222), and a crowd of silhouettes cut at the bottom and sides (Met 55624). The cut is a **straight edge with full detail right up to it**. Nothing fades or softens toward the frame (see `ukiyo-e--space.md`, `ukiyo-e--crowd.md`).
- **Passers-by's feet entering the frame (the brief):** draw each foot and shoe complete up to the edge, in the same key line and flat colour as a whole figure. Let one or two be cut mid-foot or at the ankle, so the frame clearly slices them (**judgement**; no print looked at crops *only* feet, so log it as an extension of the crop rule).

## 6. What stays true to life
The number of fingers and toes, the thumb's opposition, the direction a grip wraps, and the joints' bending order all stay true. So do the big toe on the inside and the ankle bone higher on the inside. The change is in size and drawing, not in structure (**judgement**).

## 7. Checks for Teodor
1. Is the hand about half the face's length and the foot about 0.7 of a head?
2. Are hands and feet outline only, with hooks and lobes and no shading, nails or tendons?
3. Is the fruit a flat colour shape overlapped by curled fingers, with a sliver of fruit between them?
4. Is the sleeper's hand loose, with fingers part-open?
5. Is footwear a flat shape with a sole band and a few strap or lace lines, and no sheen?
6. At the frame edge, are feet cut by a straight edge with full detail right up to it?
