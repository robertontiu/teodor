# Ukiyo-e: animals (layer 2: the subject in the style)

How ukiyo-e prints render animals, with town birds in focus: small birds (sparrows, the commonest ukiyo-e town bird), pigeons or doves, seen from above and pecking at the ground. Read [the layer-1 page](../../subjects/animals.md) first for the bird's real structure, and [the style doc](../ukiyo-e.md) for line, colour and process. Researched 2026-11 from public-domain prints at the Met and the Cleveland Museum of Art (CMA) and museum texts; values marked **judgement** have no published source.

**Honest gap:** neither museum's public-domain print collection returned a pigeon print (Met search "pigeons": one figure print with no bird in its title; CMA: none). The rules below come from the town birds the prints do show (sparrows above all, plus a hawk, swallows, roosters and ducks) and transfer to a pigeon as **judgement**.

## 1. The departure from layer 1, in one paragraph
Layer 1 builds a bird from its skeleton, models it under light (countershading, sheen on dark plumage, a wet highlight in the eye) and sets it on a ground that receives its shadow. Ukiyo-e keeps the anatomy and the species markings, but drops the light: the bird is a flat silhouette closed by a fine line, its plumage a few flat tones arranged by feather tract, and it sits on blank paper or a flat band of colour with no cast shadow. The prints are *kachō-e* ("bird-and-flower pictures"), a genre that ukiyo-e took over from Chinese and Japanese painting and that Hokusai and Hiroshige made a major print subject (RISD; Jaanus; CMA on Hiroshige's "keen observation of nature").

## 2. Proportion and structure: what stretches or flattens
- **Anatomy stays true:** the sparrow's round head, short conical bill, cheek spot and brown back, the hawk's hooked bill and fanned primaries, the rooster's comb and sickle tail all read correctly in every print looked at (Met 36718, 54517, 56178, 55733; CMA 111658, 152546). Proportions are not stylised the way human figures are (**judgement**, from these works).
- **Scale is enlarged to fill the sheet:** two or three sparrows fill a small surimono or a narrow print, and a hawk spans a third of a vertical print over a whole landscape (Met 54517, 36718, 55733). The bird is the subject at near life size or larger, not a detail in a view.
- **The body is a closed flat shape.** Volume is suggested only by the overlap of feather groups and, at most, a soft band of tone; there is no form shadow on the belly or core shadow under the wing (all works listed).
- **Viewpoint:** mostly profile and three-quarter; birds seen from above appear where the bird bends to the ground or flies below the viewer: the hawk over Fukagawa Susaki is seen from above its back, its wing feathers laid out as a fan (Met 55733; Portland Art Museum: the print lifts the viewer "to a height far beyond that of human scale"); a sparrow peers up from behind a roof tile (Met 54171); a rooster bends its head down to the chicks (CMA 152546).

## 3. What it simplifies, idealises or leaves out
- **Left out:** cast shadows, the ground's texture under the bird, reflected light, the eye's specular highlight, atmospheric softening (all works listed).
- **Simplified:** plumage is grouped by tract (cap, cheek, mantle, wing coverts, primaries, tail) and each tract is one flat tone; individual feathers appear only as a row of short dark marks or scalloped edges along the coverts and flight feathers. Hiroshige's hawk turns the back and wing coverts into a regular scale pattern (Met 55733); the sparrows of Shunman and Hiroshige carry fine dark tips over a flat brown (Met 54517, 36718).
- **Idealised:** the birds are healthy, neat and complete; no moult, no mess. Pairs and small groups are composed as a rhythm of repeated shapes (two or three birds turned different ways) (**judgement**, from Met 54517, 56178; CMA 111658; CMA on paired ducks "usually shown in pairs").
- **Setting** is a seasonal emblem rather than a place: bamboo, plum, chrysanthemum, snow (Met 36718, 54517; CMA 111658). For a town picture this is the place where Teodor substitutes his own street (pavement, crate, crumbs) as flat shapes (**judgement**).

## 4. Conventions
- **Blank or flat ground.** The bird stands on nothing, or on a flat band of colour; the paper itself is snow, sky and ground (Met 56178, 54517; CMA 111658, where falling snow is white dots over a flat grey).
- **The poem space.** Surimono and many kachō-e leave a large empty area of paper for poems (Met 54517, 54171). Teodor leaves the empty space but writes no imitation calligraphy (style doc section 11).
- **Motion as shape, not blur.** Flight is shown by a clear spread silhouette at the top or bottom of the stroke (Met 36718, 55733). The only motion streaks found are in a preparatory sketch, where Hokusai's bathing sparrows have wings drawn as radiating strokes inside ripple rings (Met 56178, "sketch for a woodblock print"): that is the painted, not the printed, look.
- **Pecking:** the head goes down below the line of the back, the tail rises, legs are short angled lines; the bird is seen a little from above so the back and folded wings show as one oval (Met 56178; CMA 152546; **judgement** for the general rule).

## 5. Colour, edge and mark
- **Key line:** fine, even sumi line round the head, bill and legs; plumage edges are often drawn in a darker tone of the plumage colour rather than black (Met 54517, 36718; **judgement** on frequency).
- **Plumage colour:** 2 to 4 flat tones per bird: a warm brown (`#8a5a3c`, **judgement**), a darker brown or sumi for markings, the paper or *gofun* white for cheeks and belly, a grey for the hawk's or pigeon's back. Countershading is kept as *local colour* (light belly, dark back) but not as light: the belly is a flat pale area, not a lit plane (**judgement**, from the works listed).
- **Bokashi** may run across a wing or tail to show its fan of feathers, darker at the tips (Met 55733, 36718; CMA text on Hiroshige's ducks: "variety of brushstrokes and ink tones used to depict the duck's plumage").
- **Eye:** a dark dot or a dot ringed by a thin light circle; no specular highlight (all works listed).
- **Edges:** all hard, except bokashi inside a feather group.

## 6. What stays true to life
Species markings and the correct count of visible parts (two legs, a folded wing that ends at the tail, a fanned tail), the posture of feeding, the "backward knee" that is really the ankle (layer 1), and the relative size of birds to one another. A pigeon keeps its small head, its iridescent neck band as a flat green-violet patch, its two dark wing bars and its pink legs (**judgement**, from layer-1 anatomy; no pigeon print found).

## 7. Checks for Teodor
1. Each bird is a closed silhouette that reads at a glance in notan; species markings are right.
2. Plumage is 2 to 4 flat tones by tract, with feather edges as short dark marks along coverts and primaries only.
3. No cast shadow, no highlight in the eye, no modelled belly.
4. Birds seen from above show the back and folded wings as one oval, the head bent below the back line when pecking.
5. Several birds form a rhythm: different turns of the head, one overlap at most, space left between groups.
6. The ground under them is paper or one flat tone (or the flat pattern of the pavement, see `ukiyo-e--architecture.md`), not modelled.
