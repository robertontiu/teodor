# Ukiyo-e: light and shadow (layer 2: the subject in the style)

How ukiyo-e handles light: flat daylight, no modelled light, no cast shadows, with time of day and warmth carried by flat colour and *bokashi*. Read [the layer-1 page](../../subjects/light-shadow.md) first, and [the style doc](../ukiyo-e.md) sections 4 and 5. Researched 2026-11 from public-domain prints at the Met and the Cleveland Museum of Art (CMA) and museum texts; values marked **judgement** have no published source.

## 1. The departure from layer 1, in one paragraph
Layer 1 splits every form at the terminator into a light family and a shadow family, adds a core shadow, reflected light and a cast shadow whose edge depends on the source, and tints light and shadow with the colour of sun and sky. Ukiyo-e removes the whole system. Each surface has one flat colour that is its **local colour**, the paper is the lightest light, and the "light" of the picture is the overall key of its colours: a dawn is a pink band at the horizon under a blue band at the top, a night is a dark sky with the same flat figures beneath it, rain is ruled lines and graded greys. The Asian Art Museum's description of the prints as an "essentially flat image" (style doc) holds for every work looked at here, with one documented exception (section 5).

## 2. What replaces modelling
- **Flat local colour.** A white robe is paper or *gofun* everywhere; a dark robe is one indigo; there is no lit side (all works listed).
- **Value as a property of the thing, not the light.** Depth and distance are carried by **flat value steps**: "three printing blocks were required to produce the three shades of gray used to suggest distance in the thickets of bamboo" (CMA on Hiroshige's *Driving Rain at Shōno*).
- **Bokashi as the only gradient.** Graded bands at the top of the sky (*ichimonji*-type band) and at the horizon; along water and the ground; inside a fruit or a wing at most (Met 36922, 55733; Portland Art Museum on Susaki: "a grey band at the top of the sky and a deep blue band at the bottom").
- **The paper as light and snow.** White snow comes "from the paper itself" (Portland); snowflakes are carved out of the block so "the carved areas are represented by the unprinted white paper" (CMA, *Evening Snow at Kambara*).

## 3. Time of day and warmth through flat colour
Measured on Met web images of aged prints (approximate):
- **Dawn** (Hiroshige, *Morning View of Nihonbashi*, Met 36922): dark blue band at the top `#2f4f68`, grading down to a warm pink-peach horizon `#edc7a6`; the figures and bridge under it stay flat and unshaded.
- **Winter day** (Hiroshige, *Jūmantsubo*, Met 55733): a grey band at the top `#595453`, a grey-green middle sky `#809a9a`, deep blue sea `#374e6e`, paper snow `#f3e6c7`.
- **Night:** a black or deep blue sky, a moon as a flat disc, everything below still in local colour (Met 36669 uki-e theatre street under a black sky; CMA 1930.202 moon with halo; CMA 1992.73 "stars dust the evening sky").
- **Rain:** "thin, ruler-straight diagonal lines" over the scene (CMA on *Night Rain at the Azuma Shrine*), and grey value steps (CMA 1948.306).
- **Warmth** is carried by the choice of the sky band and the ground tone (peach and ochre for warm, grey and Prussian blue for cold), not by a tinted light falling on the figures (**judgement**, from the works above).

## 4. Flat daylight for the brief
For a plain midday market under flat daylight (**judgement**, built from the above): paper-light ground or a single pale warm tone; one bokashi band at most, at the top edge or along the far edge; every object in local colour; the time and warmth told by that band and by the overall key (pale, warm, high).

## 5. The exception, and what to keep from it
Some late Edo designers borrowed Western shading: Kuniyoshi "employed Western-style shadows beneath the figures and the palanquin" in a night scene (CMA 1930.202). The museum text calls it out because it is unusual. Teodor's default is no cast shadows; a cast shadow is a deliberate departure, flat and hard-edged if used (**judgement**).

## 6. What stays true to life
Which things are dark and which light *in themselves* (indigo cloth, white plaster, wet stone); the sky's order of colour at dawn and dusk (warm low, cool high); the presence of weather.

## 7. Checks for Teodor
1. No form shadow, core shadow, reflected light or highlight anywhere.
2. No cast shadow (unless a deliberate, flat exception).
3. At most one or two bokashi bands, at edges (sky top, horizon, ground edge).
4. Time of day and warmth can be named from the sky band and the overall key alone.
5. Distance shows as flat value steps, not haze.
6. The paper is the lightest value.
