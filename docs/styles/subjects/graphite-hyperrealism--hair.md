# Graphite hyperrealism: hair (layer 2)

How graphite hyperrealism departs from the layer-1 hair ([docs/subjects/hair.md](../../subjects/hair.md)). Read with the style doc ([docs/styles/graphite-hyperrealism.md](../graphite-hyperrealism.md)), its calibration ([docs/calibration/graphite-hyperrealism.md](../../calibration/graphite-hyperrealism.md)) and [graphite-hyperrealism--face.md](graphite-hyperrealism--face.md) (section 3: the photographs measured and the method). Researched 2026-11-26. **[J]** marks a judgement call. The current is in copyright: technique and optics sources and public-domain photographs only; no rule rests on one artist.

## 1. The twist in one paragraph
Layer 1 paints hair as masses first and strands only at the light and the silhouette. Hyperrealism agrees on the order and then, **in focus**, goes much further than painting: many individual strands, light strands reserved by indenting or lifted with an eraser pen, the sheen band broken into strands crossing it. **Out of focus** it goes the other way, further than layer 1: no strands at all, only soft masses and a blurred sheen, because a lens erases them. Which of the two the drawing does is decided by the photograph's focus, not by the importance of the hair **[J]**, from depth of field ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depth_of_field)).

## 2. In focus (for reference; this brief's hair is out of focus)
- Order: "making the general values first", then strands; thin strokes "following the direction of the strands"; blend with a brush; layer darker graphite; lift highlights with an eraser ([Skillshare, hair and beard](https://www.skillshare.com/en/classes/drawing-realistic-hair-and-beard-with-graphite/1384601388)). "Always remember that we must make thin strands" (same).
- Light strands over dark: indent before tone ("use the metal tip of this tool to dig into the paper"; same source, and Sibley via style doc §8), or lift with a fine eraser such as an eraser pen for "thin strands of hair" ([Veronica's Art](https://veronicasart.com/?p=5729)). In the engine: `stylus` before tone, `electric_eraser` at 2-3 px after (style doc §8; manual).
- Avoid layer 1's "spaghetti hair": strands before form (layer 1 §6).

## 3. Out of focus (this brief)
- **No strands.** Blur removes fine detail first ("details that are only visible on a well-focused object", [Dessindigo](https://dessindigo.com/en/blog/drawing-tutorial/draw-blur-effect)); the ruler's out-of-focus passages keep only 0.6-1.2 L* of structure at 0.25-2 mm (face page §3). Keep the hair's big value shapes: the mass, its shadow side, one or two soft lighter bands where the sheen was **[J]**, from the measurement.
- **Soft edges, graded by distance.** "The only hard edges are to be found at the focal point" ([Wildlife Art Store, depth](https://www.wildlifeartstore.com/create-depth-in-your-drawing/)); blur grows gradually with distance from the focal plane ([Wikipedia, Depth of field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depth_of_field)). The hairline and the silhouette melt over a few millimetres; the farther from the hand's plane, the wider the melt **[J]**.
- **Bright points become discs.** A specular glint on a hair or a dew-wet blade out of focus becomes "an image of the aperture, generally a more or less round disc" ([Wikipedia, Bokeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bokeh)): if any glint survives, it is a soft round light patch, never a streak **[J]** for hair.
- **Values: dark hair is very dark.** On photograph B (face page §3) dark hair in shade has a median of L* 4 and lights up to L* 40-54 (p95-p99.9), with little texture (mid 1.8): a near-black mass whose only structure is in its lights. Graphite cannot reach those darks as an area (L* 42; carbon L* 32: calibration, "What still limits", 2). Lay the hair's darks with `carbon_pencil` first and blend them less; keep the lighter sheen band so the mass has form **[J]**.
- **Strokes vanish.** Lay the tone in bands, then blend with a large `brush` (60-90 px, the manual's size for out-of-focus passages), clipped to the hair mass for its inner tones but **unclipped across its edge** so the edge itself blurs into the grass **[J]**, from the engine's masked blending (calibration "Masked blending").

## 4. For this brief (short dark hair pressed into grass, out of focus)
- **Pressed into grass.** The back of the head rests on the ground, so the hair at the back is flattened and compressed against the grass; on the side it fans slightly where the blades push through **[J]**. Out of focus this reads only as: a dark mass flattened at its contact, with grass blades (soft, lighter or darker bands) crossing its edge, and an occlusion dark where the head meets the ground (see `figure`).
- **Short hair.** The silhouette is close to the skull, a little wider than it (layer 1 §7, check 1); no locks lift.
- **Grey hairs** don't resolve out of focus: see `sex-age` (the grey goes to the stubble near the focal plane; at the temple at most a slightly lighter mottle) **[J]**.
- **Hairline.** Graded into the forehead's skin, softer than the face's own edges because the hair lies farther from the focal plane; never outlined (layer 1 §7, check 4).

## 5. Conflicts flagged
- Layer 1's "strands only in the light and at the silhouette, a handful per area" (check 3) is too much for an out-of-focus mass: following the optics, draw **no** strands here.
- Hyperrealist hair tutorials teach strand-by-strand rendering; that applies only inside the depth of field. This brief puts hair outside it.

## 6. Checks for Teodor
1. Squint: the hair is one dark mass with at most two or three soft value shapes, a sheen band among them.
2. No strand, no hard edge, no indent in the hair.
3. Its edges melt into the grass over a few millimetres, wider than the face's edges.
4. The hair's darks are carbon, laid first and blended lightly; the mass is not grey.
5. Grass blades cross the edge of the mass, as blurred bands.
