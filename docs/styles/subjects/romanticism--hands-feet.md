# Romanticism: hands and feet (layer 2)

How Romanticism departs from the layer-1 hands and feet ([docs/subjects/hands-feet.md](../../subjects/hands-feet.md)). Read with the style doc ([docs/styles/romanticism.md](../romanticism.md)) and [romanticism--figure.md](romanticism--figure.md). Researched 2026-11-26. **[J]** marks a judgement call. Works are examples of what several artists share; no rule rests on one artist. Little scholarship addresses Romantic hands directly; this page rests mostly on looking at the works below, so most of its rules are observations **[J]**.

## 1. The twist in one paragraph
In the Romantic figure the hand is the end of the gesture, not a study piece. It sits at the tip of an arm flung up or out along the picture's diagonal (the *Raft*'s arms "gesture upward in a strong diagonal" and its top figure "waves a piece of red and white fabric" [1]; Liberty "raises the tricolor" [2]), often seen small against the sky or the sea, and it is painted as a few planes and a silhouette: an open fan, a gripping knot, a block. What it holds (cloth, rope, a branch, a mane) tells the force more than the fingers do. The academic ideal of the elegant, finished, sculptural hand (the académie's "firm contours reinforced by gently blended modeling" [3]) gives way to working, gripping, gesturing hands with broken contours.

## 3. How it departs from layer 1
**Proportions.** True to life (a hand about a face long). Raised, gesturing hands are not shrunk; working hands are broad and heavy (Géricault's blacksmith, the water-carrier) **[J]**.

**Simplified.** At figure scale the hand is a silhouette and two or three planes: lit back or palm, shadow side, the dark gaps between fingers (layer 1's "darkest accents"). An open hand is a fan of a few strokes; a gripping hand is a knot, the fingers one mass wrapped over the held thing, the thumb a single opposing stroke. Nails and knuckle detail are left out **[J]**, observed on *Christ Asleep*, Ophelia and Goya's album.

**Conventions.** (a) The raised hand at the top of the composition, against light sky or sea, at the end of the main diagonal (*Raft*, *Christ Asleep*, Ophelia, Liberty [1][2]). (b) The grip shown by what it grips: the cloth taut or flying, the branch bent, the rope straight (Ophelia, the *Raft*'s cloth, Goya's staff). (c) Hands in shadow merge with the sleeve or body; only the hand in the light is articulated **[J]**.

**Colour, edges and marks.** A lit hand against a dark ground gets a found, warm edge on the lit side and a lost edge where it turns; a hand against a light sky reads as a dark silhouette with a thin warm rim **[J]**. Strokes follow the fingers' direction; shadows carry the surround's colour, not black [4].

**Feet.** Mostly hidden or summarised: under drapery, in waves, in shadow. When seen, bare working feet are broad and flat, planted or braced (Goya, Delacroix's water-carrier, Géricault's blacksmith); a kicked or lifted foot is a wedge of two planes **[J]**.

**What stays true to life.** The construction underneath (palm block, knuckle arc, thumb from the wrist, layer 1 sections 1 and 5) and contact: a gripping hand must wrap and press. The Romantic hand simplifies the surface, not the structure.

## 4. For this brief (a raised hand gripping a wet sheet against the wind, seen from below; a lower hand on a taut line)
- **Raised hand from below:** we see the palm side and the underside of the wrist and forearm; the fingers wrap forward over the sheet's edge, so their tips are hidden and the hand reads as a knot with the thumb opposing **[J]**, after Ophelia's grip from below. Construct it fully (layer 1), then paint the light side and the shadow side and the dark gaps, nothing more.
- **The force is in the sheet:** the wet cloth bunched in the fist, pulled taut from the grip downwind, heavy and dark where wet. The forearm's tendons and the flexor mass show the strain in one or two strokes **[J]**.
- **Against the sky:** the hand sits at the top of the figure's diagonal against light cloud: a darker shape with a warm lit rim from the low raking light.
- **The lower hand on the line:** the line runs straight and taut through the fist; the knuckles face the pull, the wrist bent against it; one plane of light on the back of the hand, the rest in shadow merged with the sleeve **[J]**.
- Both hands broad and working, the same scale as the face (layer 1).

## 5. Conflicts flagged
None with the research. One caution: layer 1's habit "tubes, mitts, paddles" can hide behind Romantic simplification. A silhouette is only Romantic if the construction under it is right; check the knuckle arc and the thumb in the blockout before simplifying **[J]**.

## 6. Checks for Teodor
1. The raised hand sits at the end of the main diagonal and reads against the sky.
2. The grip is shown by the sheet and the line: bunched, taut, pulled.
3. Each hand is a silhouette plus two or three planes, with dark finger gaps; no nails, no outline.
4. The construction underneath is correct (palm block, knuckle arc, thumb from the wrist).
5. Hands are face-sized and working-broad.
