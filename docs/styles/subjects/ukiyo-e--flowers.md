# Ukiyo-e: flowers and fruit (layer 2: the subject in the style)

How ukiyo-e and *surimono* render fruit and still life, with flowers alongside: fruit in crates seen from above, a half-peeled citrus and a curl of peel. Read [the layer-1 page](../../subjects/flowers.md) first, and [the style doc](../ukiyo-e.md). Researched 2026-11 from public-domain prints at the Met and the Cleveland Museum of Art (CMA) and museum texts; values marked **judgement** have no published source.

## 1. The departure from layer 1, in one paragraph
Layer 1 models fruit as solids under one directional light: a specular highlight on glossy skin, a reflected light, a cast and a contact shadow, and a tabletop in perspective. Ukiyo-e still life drops all of that. Fruit is a flat shape of local colour on bare paper, given a little roundness only by a soft *bokashi* grade inside the shape; there are no highlights and no shadows, and the "table" is either absent or a flat band of colour. The genre for still life is the *surimono*, privately commissioned prints for New Year greetings, poetry circles and events, often "about eight inches square" and printed with "metallic pigments, embossing, and burnishing" (Harvard Art Museums), whose subjects include "morsels of food, tobacco pouches and pipes, books, dolls and flower arrangements", making small everyday things "appear large and elegant" (RISD, *Small Pleasures*). Fruit in commercial ukiyo-e appears in the same way inside larger designs (Hiroshige's fish series pairs a fish with an eggplant, Met 55897).

## 2. Proportion and structure: what stretches or flattens
- **Shapes and counts stay true:** a citrus is round with a stem and a dimple, a cut orange shows its segment wheel, a peeled mandarin its lobed segments, a persimmon its four-lobed calyx (Met 54727, 54971, 54973).
- **Scale is enlarged:** one orange can fill a third of the sheet (Met 54971); objects are shown "large and elegant" (RISD).
- **Volume is flattened** to an outline plus one soft grade. Measured on the Met images (web JPEGs of aged prints, so approximate): Shunman's persimmon goes from `#f3d6a9` at the centre to `#e3ad74` at the lower edge, a grade darker toward the rim; Shinsai's large orange is a nearly even `#e29e71` with a slightly darker rim (Met 54973, 54971). The grade is the only modelling.
- **Viewpoint is high and oblique:** fruit is seen from a little above, plates and boxes in an oblique view whose parallel edges stay parallel (see `ukiyo-e--space.md`) (Met 54973, 54832; CMA 1949.153).

## 3. What it simplifies, idealises or leaves out
- **Left out:** specular highlights, reflected light, cast and contact shadows, the receding tabletop (all works listed; style doc: "essentially flat image").
- **Simplified:** citrus skin texture becomes a few faint wrinkle lines or a light printed texture inside the flat colour (Met 54971); a cut face is a flat pale disc with the segment lines and the pips as small marks (Met 54727).
- **Idealised:** fruit is fresh, whole or cleanly cut; arrangements are loose groups that overlap a little and leave the paper open around them, with room for poems (Met 54727, 54971, 54973).
- **Containers** are drawn as pattern-carrying frames: a blue-and-white dish with its pattern printed flat on the rim (Met 54973), a woven basket as a grid of fine lines (CMA 1949.153, 1943.5).

## 4. Conventions
- **Fruit on bare paper.** The commonest setting is none: fruit floats on the sheet (Met 54727, 54971). When a ground is shown it is a flat band of colour that fades upward by *bokashi*: Shunman's green band runs from `#dbd2b1` at the bottom edge into the paper `#f3e1c3` (Met 54973, measured).
- **Peel as ribbon.** Shunman's half-peeled persimmon shows the peel as one continuous ribbon that spirals off the fruit onto the ground, outside skin coloured, inside paler, with a knife laid across (Met 54973). The cut flesh is a paler flat area with soft radiating lines. This is the closest public-domain model for a half-peeled orange and a curl of peel.
- **Seasonal and celebratory meaning:** oranges, dried persimmons and nuts as New Year foods (Met 54971 title), chrysanthemums for autumn (CMA 1949.153 text), peonies as an actor's emblem (CMA 1943.5 text). Teodor's market fruit carries the season through its kind and colour, not through emblems he does not need (**judgement**).
- **Mixed media of finish:** surimono add metallic pigments and embossing (Harvard); the dried persimmons' white sugar bloom is a stippled pale layer over the dark fruit (Met 54971, 54727; **judgement** on technique).

## 5. Colour, edge and mark
- **Outline:** fruit is often drawn without a black key line, its edge made by the colour itself or by a thin line in a darker tone of the fruit colour; sumi is kept for hard man-made things (knife, dish rim, basket) (Met 54727, 54971, 54973; **judgement** from these three works by two artists).
- **Colour:** soft, slightly faded oranges and pinks, an olive or grey green for leaves, generous paper. Measured hexes (approximate, aged prints): orange `#e29e71`, persimmon flesh `#f3d6a9`, paper `#f7e4c5` to `#fcf3e4`.
- **Bokashi** inside the fruit shape: darker at the rim or on one side, never a highlight spot.
- **Edges:** hard at the silhouette, soft only inside the grade.

## 6. What stays true to life
Species shape and colour, the stem and calyx, the segment count of a citrus (usually 10 to 12; **judgement**), the order of overlap, the logic of a peel that comes off in one spiral, the weave of a basket.

## 7. Fruit in crates, seen from above
No ukiyo-e crate of fruit was found; the nearest are baskets of mushrooms and peonies and fishmongers' tubs (CMA 1949.153, 1943.5; Met 36922). From them (**judgement**): the crate is an oblique box of flat wood colour with its slats as parallel lines (see `ukiyo-e--objects.md`); the fruit inside is a packed field of repeated round shapes, each a flat colour with the same soft grade, overlapping in rows so the repetition becomes a pattern; the crate's far edge hides the tops of the back row; no shadow between fruits, only a darker gap colour or the paper.

## 8. Checks for Teodor
1. No highlight, reflected light or shadow on any fruit; roundness is one soft grade inside a flat shape.
2. Fruit edges are drawn by colour or a thin coloured line; sumi only for hard objects.
3. The ground is paper or a single flat band fading upward.
4. The peel is one continuous ribbon, outside and inside in two flat tones.
5. Repeated fruit in a crate reads as pattern: same size, same grade, rows overlapping.
6. Shapes and counts stay true (segments, calyx, stem).
