# Ukiyo-e: face (layer 2: the subject in the style)

How ukiyo-e types and draws faces: beauties and gentlemen as a shared ideal type, working people and old men with a few individual marks, sleepers with a single closed-eye line, and every feature a fine key line on a flat face. Read [the layer-1 page](../../subjects/face.md) first, and [the style doc](../ukiyo-e.md) section 6. Researched 2026-11 from public-domain prints at the Met and the Cleveland Museum of Art (CMA) and from museum and reference texts. Values marked **judgement** have no published source. Ratios "read by eye" were taken from grid overlays of the museums' images and are ±0.1.

## 1. The departure from layer 1, in one paragraph
Layer 1 builds the head as a ball and planes. It chooses a light, gives each plane one value, and lets expression move the masses (cheeks, brows, jaw). Ukiyo-e keeps almost none of that. A face is **one flat area**: paper white, or one pale flat tint, with no plane breaks, socket shadows or nasolabial modelling. Its features are **a few very fine key lines**: a slit or single arc for the eye, a hooked line for the nose, a short mouth. This descends from the court-painting formula *hikime kagibana*, "slit eyes, hooked nose", in which faces show "essentially identical features" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hikime_kagibana); [JAANUS, *hikime kagihana*](https://projects.mcah.columbia.edu/jaanus/node/4084): "eyes in straight lines", "a hooked-line nose and a small, dot-like mouth"). Beauties are paraded "with hardly any distinctions in their facial features" ([NGV, *Japanese woodblock prints: a mass medium*](https://www.ngv.vic.gov.au/essay/japanese-woodblock-prints-a-mass-medium/)). Actors, old men and working people get **individual marks**: brows, wrinkles, stubble, a grin or a frown. Even then they are drawn as glyphs on the flat face, never as modelled form (observed: Met 55222, 36922, 54339).

## 2. The ideal type and the commoner
- **Ideal type (beauties, young men, lovers):** a long oval face. The eyes are narrow slits, each an upper-lid line with at most a short lower line and a dot or short stroke for the pupil. The nose is one long fine line ending in a small hook. The mouth is tiny, its width read by eye at about 0.6-0.8 of one eye's length (CMA 1956.753; Met 54869, 55810). Layer 1's mouth is about 0.85 of the pupil distance, roughly 1.7 eye lengths. So the ideal mouth is less than half its real size. Faces of young men and women differ mostly by hairstyle and dress (Met 54869, *Fan Vendor*: the young man's face has the woman's slit eyes and small mouth).
- **Commoners (porters, fishmongers, travellers):** the same flat face and fine line, with **more and blunter marks**. Brows are short thick strokes. Eyes are short dashes or arcs, sometimes a dot for the pupil. The nose is a hook or a bulb in profile. The mouth is a short line turned up or down, often wider than the ideal type's. The face can grin, frown or grimace (Met 55222, Eisen, a whole crowd of distinct faces; Met 36922 and 36521, Hiroshige; CMA 1930.190, Hokusai). Individuality comes from the **shape of the skull and jaw and from two or three marks**, not from modelled anatomy. This contrast is observed in all the commoner prints looked at. No museum text states it as a rule (**judgement** that it is a convention rather than a habit of these artists).
- **Old age:** a few extra key lines, such as crow's-feet, a furrow on the brow and a line from nose to mouth. Hair and beard are drawn in fine parallel lines instead of a black mass (Met 54339, Gakutei, *A Sage Fallen Asleep over His Books*: closed eyes as two curved lines, wrinkle strokes, a grey beard carved line by line).
- **Actors** were given characteristic, recognisable features ([viewingjapaneseprints on Kiyonaga](https://viewingjapaneseprints.net/texts/ukiyoe/kiyonaga.html): actors' faces "distinctly different from each other"). That is likeness (*nigao-e*) and is not wanted here. AGENTS.md forbids the likeness of real people.

## 3. The sleeper and the closed eye
- **Closed eye = one line.** The court formula drew closed eyes "in black ink as narrow lines" ([Japanese Wiki Corpus, *Hikime-Kagibana*](https://www.japanesewiki.com/culture/Hikime-Kagibana.html)). In the prints the closed eye of a sleeper is a single curved line, sagging downward like a relaxed lid. Sometimes a second short line marks the crease above it. There are no lashes, no shadow and no swelling of the lid (Met 55800 and CMA 1985.309, Harunobu's sleeping attendants; Met 55810, Harunobu's dreaming courtesan; Met 54339, Gakutei's sleeping sage).
- **The faint smile of sleep** is in the mouth line alone. The corners tick up by a hair, and the lips may part to a tiny gap (Met 55810: the dozing courtesan's half-smile). The cheeks do not rise. Layer 1's AU6 cheek-raise has no way to show itself on a flat face, so the smile must stay **slight**, or it reads as a grin (**judgement**).
- **Head pose of sleepers:** the head drops onto a hand, the arms, a desk or a shoulder, and the face turns three-quarter or near profile (Met 55800, 55810, 54339; CMA 1985.309). None of the sleepers looked at has a face turned straight up and seen full-face from above.

## 4. Viewpoints: few, and the features do not foreshorten
- The court formula "allows only two main viewpoints" (Wikipedia, *Hikime kagibana*). Prints use three-quarter and profile overwhelmingly. Full-front faces are rare and kept for special cases (**judgement**, from the works looked at: no frontal face among the ordinary figures).
- **Head tipped or seen from above:** the skull shape grows and the features slide toward the chin, but each feature keeps its frontal glyph. The eye is still a slit and the nose a hook, with no foreshortened nostrils or eyelid planes (Met 55222: the bowed porter at the bottom edge and the men looking down, their pates large and their features crowded low).
- **The brief's face (a man asleep, head tipped back, face turned up to a viewer above):** this is layer 1's "lost face from below" problem in reverse, and ukiyo-e has no exact model. Use its own solution. Turn the head a little to one side so the face reads **three-quarter**. Keep the closed-eye arcs, the nose hook and the mouth line in their normal places on a flat face. Show the tip back only through the outline: the chin and jaw line uppermost, the throat as one or two lines. Do not foreshorten the features (**judgement**; record it in the journal as an extension of the convention).

## 5. Colour, line and edge
- **Flesh:** women and refined faces are paper white or white ground (Met 54869, CMA 1956.753). Working men are paper or one pale warm tint, and Hiroshige's porters a stronger ruddy tint (Met 36922, 36521). The tint is flat and the same on face and limbs. There is no rosy cheek, except a soft pink *bokashi* on cheeks and lids in some late Meiji prints (Met 55182), which belongs to that later phase, not the classic convention (**judgement** on the scope).
- **Shaved or stubbled skin:** a flat pale grey or blue-grey tint, such as a shaved pate or a stubbled jaw (Met 55222, 36922).
- **Line weight:** the face carries the **finest lines in the print**, thinner than the garment outlines. The head carver, the master, cut the face and hair, and might cut a nose line "much narrower than it appeared in the design" ([JAANUS, *kashirabori*](https://projects.mcah.columbia.edu/jaanus/node/3270)). The face's outline is one continuous thin line, broken only where the hairline's fine strands take over.
- **Red accents:** the lower lip (women) and sometimes the inner eye corner or the eyelid line get a touch of red (*beni*) (Met 54869; CMA 1985.309). For men, keep the mouth a line in sumi or a dull red (**judgement**).

## 6. What stays true to life
The face's oval and the placement of features on it (eyes near the half line, mouth closer to the nose than to the chin), the turn of the head, and the overlap of hair over forehead and ears all stay true. The expression stays legible: a commoner's frown or grin reads at once (Met 55222). The type changes the features' size and drawing, not their order.

## 7. Checks for Teodor
1. Is the face one flat area (paper or one tint) with no shading, socket or cheek modelling?
2. Are the features fine single key lines, thinner than any garment outline?
3. Closed eyes: one relaxed downward arc each, with no lashes and no lid shadow?
4. Is the smile only a tick at the mouth's corners, with no raised cheeks?
5. Is the face three-quarter or profile, and are the features drawn whole rather than foreshortened?
6. Does the man read as an individual through skull, jaw, brows and two or three marks, rather than as a beauty's face?
7. Is any stubble or shaved skin a flat grey tint?
