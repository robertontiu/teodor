# Graphite hyperrealism: figure (layer 2)

How graphite hyperrealism departs from the layer-1 figure ([docs/subjects/figure.md](../../subjects/figure.md)). Read with the style doc ([docs/styles/graphite-hyperrealism.md](../graphite-hyperrealism.md)) and the sibling pages [face](graphite-hyperrealism--face.md) (section 3: the photographs measured and the method), [hands-feet](graphite-hyperrealism--hands-feet.md) and [drapery](graphite-hyperrealism--drapery.md). Researched 2026-11-26. **[J]** marks a judgement call. The current is in copyright: technique and optics sources and public-domain photographs only; no rule rests on one artist.

## 1. The twist in one paragraph
Layer 1 constructs the figure from canons, gesture and masses, and corrects foreshortening by drawing through boxes and cylinders. Hyperrealism takes the figure as the camera saw it: the proportions are the lens's, including its distortions; the gesture is whatever the photograph froze; the frame is a **close crop** that cuts the body where the photograph cuts it (style doc §7: "close crops, extreme close-ups ... filling the frame"; perspective is "the lens's, not constructed"). The body is not idealised and not stylised: a real build, real skin, real clothes. Layer 1's construction becomes the check, not the method **[J]**.

## 2. How it departs from layer 1
**Proportions follow the lens.** "Objects close to the lens appear abnormally large relative to more distant objects", and this comes from distance, "not by the lens per se" ([Wikipedia, Perspective distortion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perspective_distortion)). A hand held toward a close camera is drawn larger than the face, a forearm foreshortens hard, and canons (head heights, hand equals face) only hold for parts at the same distance. Hyperrealism keeps that enlargement rather than correcting it; it is one of the "photographic limitations" the style capitalises on (style doc §2) **[J]** for "keeps rather than corrects".

**Crop instead of whole figure.** The body is a fragment: head, shoulder, arm. The frame's edges cut through forms without apology (a shoulder cut by the edge, an elbow out of frame) **[J]**, after style doc §7. Layer 1's line of action still has to run through the fragment, or the crop reads as a body part on a table **[J]**.

**Weight and contact are shown photographically.** A lying body shows weight by what the ground does: grass pressed flat and bent under the head and shoulder, blades in front of the edge of the shoulder and arm, a dark occlusion line where body meets ground ("occlusion ... where a body meets the ground", layer 1 §4). In the photograph, contact is the darkest value of the passage; in graphite, carbon **[J]**.

**Focus ranks the parts.** The focal plane picks one part (here the hand: `hands-feet`); every other part softens with its distance from that plane, gradually ("a gradual reduction of clarity ... as they move away from the POF"; "the DOF beyond the subject is always greater than the DOF in front of the subject", [Wikipedia, Depth of field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depth_of_field)). The ruler's out-of-focus passages keep their values and lose texture (face page §3: mid texture 0.6-1.2 against 1.5-2 for sharp smooth skin and 4-7 for sharp textured passages).

**Masses and anatomy.** Unchanged and unsimplified: the forearm's muscle masses, the deltoid's cap, the clavicle, the neck's sternomastoid show as they do in the photograph, as tone, without contour lines (style doc §6) **[J]**.

**What stays true to life.** Layer 1's anatomy, gesture and weight; hyperrealism only refuses to idealise or to correct the lens.

## 3. For this brief (lying on his back in grass, the arm straight up; only head, shoulder and arm in frame)
- **Camera and lens.** The scene reads as a photograph taken from close above or beside him, the raised hand nearest the lens. Then the hand is enlarged, the forearm foreshortened toward the hand, the shoulder and head smaller and farther **[J]**, from perspective distortion. Build the blockout with the real camera distance, and let the scene's perspective set the sizes; don't "fix" the hand to face size.
- **The arm straight up.** Seen from near the hand, the arm is a strongly foreshortened cylinder: the elbow's bony points and the forearm's flexor and extensor masses overlap in depth (layer 1 §3, §8). The sleeve slides down toward the shoulder (see `drapery`).
- **Focus order.** Sharpest: the fist (`hands-feet`). Then the forearm and sleeve, softening toward the elbow. The face is softer still but must keep its expression readable (big values, the crow's feet as forms, the catchlight: face page §6). Hair and grass at the head: the most blurred (see `hair`). If the camera is above the hand looking down at him, the head lies behind the hand, in the larger rear zone of depth of field, so it can stay more legible than near-foreground grass **[J]**.
- **Contact with the grass.** Occlusion darks under the jaw's side, the back of the head and the shoulder; grass blades crossing the edge of the shoulder and the hair; any blades between the camera and the figure, closer than the hand, are the most blurred thing in the picture (soft bands, no edges) **[J]**, from depth of field.
- **Gesture.** One line of action from the tilted head along the shoulder up the raised arm to the thumb: the aim. The crop keeps it inside the frame **[J]**.

## 4. Conflicts flagged
- Layer 1's checks ("each hand about a face long") contradict a lens-true picture when the hand is nearer the camera. The research of the current (perspective from the lens) decides: follow the scene's perspective; use layer 1's proportions to check the hand and head at their own distances, not against each other.

## 5. Checks for Teodor
1. Sizes come from the scene's camera: the hand nearer the lens is larger than the face.
2. One line of action runs through the cropped fragment.
3. Contact with the ground is the darkest value of its passage (carbon), with grass pressed and crossing the edges.
4. Focus ranks the parts: hand, forearm, face, hair and grass, with gradual blur and no part out of order.
5. No contour lines on the body; all edges are tone against tone.
