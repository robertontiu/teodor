# Graphite hyperrealism: face (layer 2)

How graphite hyperrealism departs from the layer-1 face ([docs/subjects/face.md](../../subjects/face.md)). Read with the style doc ([docs/styles/graphite-hyperrealism.md](../graphite-hyperrealism.md)), its calibration ([docs/calibration/graphite-hyperrealism.md](../../calibration/graphite-hyperrealism.md)) and the sibling pages [hands-feet](graphite-hyperrealism--hands-feet.md), [sex-age](graphite-hyperrealism--sex-age.md), [hair](graphite-hyperrealism--hair.md). Researched 2026-11-26. **[J]** marks a judgement call. The current is in copyright, so no drawing of it is measured or named; this page rests on technique sources (tutorials, optics, anatomy, dermatology) and on **public-domain photographs measured as the ruler** (section 3), as the style doc does. No rule rests on one artist.

## 1. The twist in one paragraph
Layer 1 builds the face as planes and masses and paints features as small planes in the plane's value. Hyperrealism keeps all of that underneath, then adds what a lens records and a painter usually leaves out: the skin's surface (pores, fine lines, stubble, sheen on the oily zones), the eye's wet optics (catchlight, wet rims, a sclera that is grey, not white), and the photograph's focus (one sharp plane, everything else graded into blur). It departs from layer 1 in three ways: (a) **no simplification of surface** at the focal plane; (b) **no line at all**: every edge is a meeting of two tones, sharp or soft by the lens, never by the hand ([Proko forum](https://proko.com/community/topics/making-realistic-faces): "push the painting away from lines, especially in the lightest parts"); (c) **values come from the photograph, not from a plane model**, including the photograph's deep darks (style doc §5). What stays true is layer 1's structure: the planes, the thirds, the expression as a change in the masses. Texture painted onto a wrong structure is the commonest failure (section 6).

## 3. The ruler: two photographs measured
Library of Congress, FSA/OWI Black-and-White Negatives: **A** = "Floyd Burroughs, cotton sharecropper. Hale County, Alabama", 1936, [item 2017762300](https://www.loc.gov/item/2017762300/) (8x10 negative, master scan 9790 x 12237 px; hard sunlight; a man of about thirty with several days' stubble, a cotton work shirt); **B** = "Destitute pea pickers in California. Mother of seven children. Age thirty-two", 1936, [item 2017762891](https://www.loc.gov/item/2017762891/) (master scan 6849 x 8539 px; soft overcast light; a hand at the face, an out-of-focus tent pole and tent). Only value, texture and focus were measured; nothing of either person's likeness is used.

Method (scripts kept in the task's scratch folder, deleted after): 8-bit grey to CIELAB L* (sRGB curve); boxes picked by eye on gridded crops; per box the median and percentiles, plus two texture bands: **fine** = std of (L* minus a Gaussian blur of 0.25 mm) and **mid** = std of the 0.25 mm blur minus a 2 mm blur. Scale from the interpupillary distance (A: 1310 px for about 62 mm, so about 21 px/mm, ±15% because the head is slightly turned) and the eye fissure (B: about 390 px for 31 mm, so about 12.6 px/mm, ±20%). Film grain sits inside the fine band: on B the out-of-focus pole (no detail) gives fine 2.1 at L* 46, so fine-band skin values of 2-2.6 are mostly grain; the mid band is the honest texture measure.

Stubble on A (dark marks below -8 L* after removing a 1 mm blur, at least 15 px): **0.3 visible hairs per mm²** on the jaw and upper lip (0.04 on the clean cheek: noise and pores), visible length median **0.75-1.0 mm** (quartiles 0.5-1.5 mm), width **0.15-0.19 mm** (includes the lens's blur), each hair at least 11 L* darker than the skin around it. Pores ([Flament 2015](https://www.dovepress.com/facial-skin-pores-a-multiethnic-study-peer-reviewed-fulltext-article-CCID), cheeks of women): area 0.03-0.22 mm² on average (a diameter of about 0.2-0.5 mm), 10-90 per cm², covering 1-16% of the cheek; the youngest group had smaller pores, and in several groups size "plateaus from 40 years of age"; men were reported to have fewer pores (cited, not tested).

**What the ruler says.** (1) Smooth lit skin has little texture at 0.25-2 mm (mid 1.5-2 L*); pores at photographic resolution are a faint, even stipple at about the grain's amplitude, never dots that read one by one. (2) Texture is **not uniform**: it doubles to triples where the skin folds or grows hair (crow's feet 5.1, stubble 4.4-4.6) and stays at full strength in shadow (shadow cheek 2.1, as much as the lit cheek). (3) The sclera is **mid grey**, 26 L* darker than the lit forehead and darker than the lit cheek; only the catchlight is as bright as, or brighter than, the brightest skin (A: 83.5 against the forehead's 81.7 at p95). In shaded eyes (B) the catchlight is dimmer than the lit skin. (4) Out of focus the 0.25-2 mm structure falls to 0.6-1.2, below even smooth skin.

## 4. How it departs from layer 1
**Proportions and structure.** Unchanged: the photograph's (or rendered scene's) geometry, transferred, not constructed by canon (style doc §6). Layer 1's construction is the check, not the method: transfer from the reference, then verify thirds, the plane head and the action units against it **[J]**.

**What is added (not simplified).**
- **Skin as a value field with texture laid inside each value band.** Lay the plane's tone first, smooth; then texture: "make a base layer with the general values", then add irregular marks, "avoid making mirror circles" (Skillshare, skin texture). Pores are "small oval spots" with their highlight on the side away from the light (same source). At the studio's 5 px/mm a pore of 0.2-0.5 mm drawn 1.5 x life is 1.5-4 px: the scale of a `mech_03` touch, not a drawn circle.
- **Wrinkles as forms, not lines.** "Avoid making lines that are too sharp" (Skillshare, skin texture); a wrinkle is "a dark line with a highlight running along one side" ([Wildlife Art Store, depth](https://www.wildlifeartstore.com/create-depth-in-your-drawing/)): a small valley with a lit lip, its darkness graded, not an outline. Crow's feet fan from the outer canthus and deepen with AU6; on A the crow's-feet zone has a dark p5 of 41.6 against a skin median of 57.5: the deepest creases are about 15 L* below their skin, not black.
- **Stubble as a zone plus a few hundred marks.** First a grey tone over the beard zone (the lower third is darker and cooler: layer 1 §3), then short, firm marks: "a gray base, then ... relatively short firm strokes" that "vary in direction" (Skillshare, hair and beard). Measured: 0.15-0.2 mm wide, 0.5-1.5 mm long, about 0.3 per mm², at least 11 L* darker than the skin between. Hairs grow in tracts and lie in groups that share a direction (seen on A's jaw: hairs on the jawline sweep down and forward, under the lip they point down) **[J]**. Grey hairs in the stubble are indented with the `stylus` **before** any tone, as white beards are embossed "before starting the shading" (Skillshare, hair and beard; Sibley, style doc §8).
- **The eye as wet optics.** The pupil sets the darkest value: "the darkness of the pupil will define the contrast for the rest of the drawing" (Tuts+); the catchlight is reserved, not erased, and the iris fibres "go around the reflection" (Tuts+); iris fibres radiate from the pupil, "some darker than others", with a soft iris edge (Tuts+). The eyeball "is roughly a sphere, so don't make the shadows flat"; the sclera is shaded and only lifted locally (Tuts+), and the ruler puts it at L* 50-70 in sun, never paper white. The lids "have a certain thickness" (Tuts+): the lower lid's wet rim catches small speculars; the inner corner (caruncle) "is wet and shiny" with small highlights (Tuts+). Eyelashes: 90-160 on the upper lid, 7-8 mm typical, longest at the centre; 75-80 on the lower lid, 5-6 mm; in up to six imperfect rows ([Wikipedia, Eyelash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eyelash)); they "go slightly down ... and then they curve up", "stick to each other, creating sharp clumps", and "don't grow in one thin row" (Tuts+). On A at about 1:1 the lashes do not resolve one by one: the upper lid reads as a dark band (L* 37) with a few clumps at its outer end. Draw the band, then a few tapered clumps, not a fringe of evenly spaced hairs **[J]**, after the ruler.
- **Sheen.** Skin is not matte: the nose tip on A has a median of 45.6 but a p99.9 of 80.6, a specular spot as bright as the lit forehead; the forehead, nose and the lit lip carry small, sharp-edged speculars; dry zones (cheek, temple) carry none **[J]** (regional skin oil is common teaching; the measured paper was not reachable). Lift them last with `precision_eraser` or `electric_eraser` with `"seek": true`.

**Edges and focus.** Sharp edges only in the focal plane; elsewhere the blur grows gradually with distance from it ("a gradual reduction of clarity in objects as they move away from the POF", [Wikipedia, Depth of field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depth_of_field)); at close range the depth of field is "proportionally much smaller" (same). "The only hard edges are to be found at the focal point" ([Wildlife Art Store, depth](https://www.wildlifeartstore.com/create-depth-in-your-drawing/)). Texture is the first thing blur removes: "details that are only visible on a well-focused object" ([Dessindigo](https://dessindigo.com/en/blog/drawing-tutorial/draw-blur-effect)); the ruler's out-of-focus passages keep their values but drop to 0.6-1.2 L* of mid texture (section 3). A face behind the focal plane keeps its big value shapes, its expression and its darkest accents, and loses pores, single stubble hairs and lash clumps first **[J]**, from the measurement.

**Value.** The photograph's range (style doc §5): pupil and lash line are the darkest of the face (A: L* 13; B: L* 3), far below what graphite reaches as an area (L* 40) and close to carbon's (L* 32). Use `carbon_pencil` for pupil, nostril and the lash-line darks; never burnish 8B there (shine) (style doc §4).

## 5. Keeping the face alive, not plastic, not "pores for their own sake"
The two failures sit at opposite ends; both are failures of hierarchy.
- **Plastic (overblended):** a tonally correct face with no surface. Overblending "can flatten a drawing" (Wildlife Art Store, style doc §8); stumps overused give "muddy and flat values" ([Skillshare, shading fabric folds](https://www.skillshare.com/en/classes/shading-folds-of-fabric-with-graphite-pencil-how-to-get-a-realistic-effect/2141936883)); a base layer that "still looks very straight, very clean and organized" needs irregular marks (Skillshare, skin texture). Blending a face also **makes it younger**: averaged faces look younger because averaging erases wrinkles (Burt and Perrett 1995, cited in [`sex-age`](../../subjects/sex-age.md) §4). The check: the mid-band texture of lit skin should not fall below the ruler's 1.5-2 L* at the focal plane, nor rise above about 2.5 on smooth zones **[J]**, from section 3.
- **Pores for their own sake:** every pore drawn, at equal contrast, everywhere, in focus and out, which reads as a disease or a graphic pattern. The ruler says pores are near the grain's amplitude; the texture that carries a face is the **folds and hair** (2-3 times the pore band) and they sit where the expression puts them. Draw texture by zone and by focus: strongest at the crow's feet, the beard zone and the lips' vertical lines in the focal plane; faint on the forehead and the cheek; none out of focus **[J]**, from section 3.
- **Alive:** (a) catchlights, because "eyes without catch lights are often said to appear dull or lifeless" ([Wikipedia, Catch light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catch_light)); one catchlight per open eye, the same light in both, shaped like the source (a window is a square, the open sky a broad soft band, a small bright source a point) (same source: softboxes "produce large, square highlights"); (b) wet edges: lower-lid rim, caruncle, the lips' line; (c) the grey sclera (white eyes read pasted on: **[J]**, from the measurement); (d) the expression's action units all present (layer 1 §5), since a hyperreal surface on a frozen or partial expression looks like a mask; (e) slight asymmetry of the two halves, which every photograph has **[J]**.

## 6. For this brief
An invented man of about 45, near three-quarter, lying on his back; the near eye clenched shut with crow's feet and a bunched cheek, the far eye open and aiming; a closed-mouth grin; stubble. Focal plane on the raised hand (see `hands-feet`).
- **Expression in action units (layer 1 §5).** Near eye: AU6 (cheek raiser) + AU7 (lid tightener) + closed lids (AU43 or a hard wink, AU46): the lower lid pushed up by the bunched cheek, the crow's feet fanning from the outer corner over the cheekbone, folds under the eye, the brow's outer end pulled down. Far eye: AU6 + AU7 lighter, the fissure narrowed by the grin and by aiming (eye opening around 0.23-0.26 of its width, layer 1 §9's smile and laugh values, **[J]** for aiming). Mouth: AU12 (corners up and back) with lips closed, maybe AU24 pressed; the nasolabial fold deepens and curves round the lifted cheek. The grin's asymmetry follows the wink: the near side's cheek higher **[J]**.
- **Where the texture goes.** Focus: the head is behind the hand, so it is softer than the knuckles. Keep enough sharpness to read crow's feet as forms and stubble as a grey zone with a scatter of marks at the jaw nearest the camera; let pores go **[J]**. If the head lies near the focal plane, use the ruler's stubble numbers above.
- **The clenched eye** has no catchlight and no sclera: the lash line is a pressed dark crescent (carbon), the lashes clumped and pointing down and out; the wet line between the lids may carry one tiny specular **[J]**. **The open eye** carries the picture's life: one catchlight, the sky's or the moon's shape, reserved from the start (indented or never toned), a grey sclera, a soft iris edge, a wet lower rim.
- **Lying on his back:** gravity pulls the cheeks and jowls toward the ears; the nasolabial fold flattens a little and the face looks younger and wider than upright **[J]** (soft tissue follows gravity). Do not "correct" this with drawn lines.
- **Stubble at 45:** see `sex-age`: a few grey hairs indented before tone at the chin and the jaw angle.

## 7. Conflicts flagged
- [Wildlife Art Store, depth](https://www.wildlifeartstore.com/create-depth-in-your-drawing/) advises being "generous with contrast" and adding contrast "even if ... not apparent in the original photo". That contradicts the style's ruler (the drawing "looks like the photograph": style doc §1, §10). **Follow the photograph's values**; graphite already loses the deepest darks, so the risk is too little contrast, not too much.
- The Tuts+ eye tutorial and common teaching blend the sclera then lift; the ruler shows the sclera under hard sun is still a mid grey with a shaded top under the lid. Lift only to the reference's value with `"seek": true`, not to white.
- A finished hyperrealist face is drawn at 1:1 or larger (style doc §3). At the studio's 5 px/mm, life-size stubble (0.15 mm) is under 1 px: individual hairs are only drawable if the face is at least about 1.5-2 x life **[J]**. If the face is smaller, stubble is a tone with a sparse scatter of marks, not hair-by-hair.

## 8. Checks for Teodor
1. Squint: the head reads as layer 1's three to five value shapes before any texture.
2. No outline anywhere; every feature edge is two tones meeting.
3. The pupil and lash line are the darkest marks of the face (carbon); the catchlight is the brightest and is paper or near it.
4. The sclera is grey (around L* 50-70 in sun), shaded under the upper lid.
5. Texture by zone: strongest at crow's feet, beard zone and lips; faint on forehead and cheek; continued in shadow; absent out of focus.
6. Every action unit of the expression is visible (AU6 cheeks, AU12 corners, the near eye's AU7 squeeze and crow's feet).
7. At viewing distance no mark reads as a mark; at 1:1 the stubble reads as hairs only in the focal plane.
