# Ukiyo-e: hair (layer 2: the subject in the style)

How ukiyo-e draws hair. The mass is flat black with a hard edge. The hairline is a comb of very fine parallel lines (*kewari*) cut by the master carver. Grey or thin hair is lines only. Short hair is a black cap with a few direction lines. The focus is short hair seen from above. Read [the layer-1 page](../../subjects/hair.md) first, and [ukiyo-e--face](ukiyo-e--face.md). Researched 2026-11 from public-domain prints at the Met and the Cleveland Museum of Art (CMA) and from carving texts. Values marked **judgement** have no published source.

## 1. The departure from layer 1, in one paragraph
Layer 1 treats hair as a shell of locks over the skull. It has a hierarchy of mass, groups, locks and strands, two highlights (a white one and a coloured one, shifted along the strand), a highlight ring round the crown when seen from above, and soft edges where it meets skin and sky. Ukiyo-e removes the light and keeps the **shell and the edge**. The mass is a **flat, solid sumi black** (or a flat grey for grey hair). It has a hard contour against the background and **no highlight** at all (Met 54869, 55800, 55810; CMA 1956.753). Where hair meets skin, a soft edge is made from hard marks: a fringe of **fine parallel lines**, each strand cut separately, thinning into the paper of the face. This *kewari* ("hair-splitting") work cut "the outline of individual strands of hair as seen on a woman's hairline", in lines "less than a millimeter in width" ([Waraku, on *kewari*](https://intojapanwaraku.com/rock/art-rock/1665/)). The face and especially the hair were the master's job: "Most difficult of all was the hair (*kebori*)", and the head carver's name is the one recorded on a print ([JAANUS, *kashirabori*](https://projects.mcah.columbia.edu/jaanus/node/3270)).

## 2. The hairline: fine parallel lines
- **Construction:** the designer's drawing only marked where the hair lines were to go, and the carver cut them directly ([David Bull, *Where are we going?*](https://www.asahi-net.or.jp/~xs3d-bull/where/going06.html): copyists "would simply indicate the area where the lines were to appear"). The lines are cut so each tapers to a point. The first cut is "about 1mm 'out' from the line" where the hairs end ([David Bull, carving the hair](https://woodblock.com/encyclopedia/entries/015_02/015_02.html)).
- **Measured on Met 55800** (Harunobu's sleeping attendant; print 21 cm wide, image at about 0.07 mm/px): fringe lines about **0.15-0.2 mm wide**, about **0.5-0.8 mm apart**, and about 1-3 mm long. That is roughly **1/15-1/20 of the face's length** apart (rough reading; the lines are oblique to the pixel grid).
- **Direction:** the lines follow the growth direction. They run back and up from the forehead and temples, and down in front of the ear and at the nape, all parallel within one patch and fanning gently round the curve of the head (Met 55800, 55810; CMA 1985.309; Met 55222, men's temples).
- **Where it sits:** where the hair meets skin (forehead, temples, sideburns, nape). Against the background or the clothing, the edge is a clean hard contour of the black mass (all works seen).

## 3. Men's hair, short hair and grey hair
- **Edo men:** a shaved pate as a flat pale grey or blue-grey tint, a black topknot, and side hair as black masses with *kewari* fringes at the temples (Met 55222, 36922).
- **Short Western haircuts (Meiji):** a **black cap** over the skull with a clean outer contour, a *kewari* fringe at forehead and temples, and a few **grey or lighter lines inside the mass** for the direction of the locks (Met 55182, a man with a short cut and moustache). A girl's bob is drawn the same way (Met 55185).
- **Grey or thinning hair and beards:** drawn as **fine lines only**, not a black mass, letting the paper or skin show between them (Met 54339, the sleeping sage's grey hair and beard).
- **Stubble:** a flat grey tint, not lines (Met 36922; see `ukiyo-e--face.md`).

## 4. Short hair seen from above (the brief)
No print looked at shows a short-haired head from straight above, so this is an extension (**judgement**; log it):
- The hair is a **flat black shape** that fills the top of the head's silhouette. Its outer contour is the skull's outline plus a little thickness (layer 1's shell).
- A few **fine lines inside the mass** radiate from the crown whorl and run forward to the forehead and down at the sides, in a lighter ink or as thin reserved lines (after Met 55182). There is **no highlight ring**.
- A parting, if any, is a thin line of paper or skin tint.
- **The hairline:** with the head tipped back toward a viewer above, the forehead hairline faces the viewer. Draw it as the *kewari* fringe of short parallel lines thinning onto the forehead's paper tone. Draw the temple and sideburn hairlines the same way, smaller.

## 5. What stays true to life
The hair's shell sits outside the skull's outline. Growth directions radiate from the crown whorl. The hairline frames the face's upper third (layer 1). The ears sit below and in front of the side hair. Only the light is removed (**judgement**).

## 6. Checks for Teodor
1. Is the mass flat black (or flat grey for grey hair), with no highlight band or ring?
2. Is the edge against the background a clean hard contour?
3. Is the hairline against the skin a fringe of very fine parallel lines, roughly 1/15-1/20 of the face's length apart, following the growth direction?
4. Short hair from above: are there a few fine direction lines radiating from the crown inside the black cap?
5. Is grey hair drawn as lines, not mass, and stubble as a flat grey tint?
