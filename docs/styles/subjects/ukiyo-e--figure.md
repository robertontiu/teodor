# Ukiyo-e: figure (layer 2: the subject in the style)

How ukiyo-e draws bodies. Proportion follows a type, not anatomy. The pose is carried by the silhouette of the clothes. Anatomy shows only on bare working limbs, as a few lines. Seen from above, the figure is drawn on a tilted, parallel floor rather than foreshortened. The focus is on a slack, seated or reclining sleeper seen from above. Read [the layer-1 page](../../subjects/figure.md) first, then [the style doc](../ukiyo-e.md) section 6 and [ukiyo-e--space](ukiyo-e--space.md) for the high view. Researched 2026-11 from public-domain prints at the Met and the Cleveland Museum of Art (CMA), with museum and reference texts. Values marked **judgement** have no published source. Head counts "read by eye" come from grid overlays of the museums' images and are ±0.3 head.

## 1. The departure from layer 1, in one paragraph
Layer 1 builds the body as three masses (head, ribcage, pelvis) on a line of action, gives it a proportion canon (7½ heads), lands its weight over the support, models each mass with a light and a shadow side, and foreshortens limbs as boxes and cylinders. Ukiyo-e keeps the **line of action and the overlap order**, and drops most of the rest. Proportion is set by the period's **type** and the figure's role. The body is mostly a **flat silhouette of clothing** drawn in key line. Masses are not modelled, and folds can be shown by blind embossing instead of shade (CMA 1985.309: "embossing of the fabric folds, lending a three-dimensional effect"). Bare limbs get two or three lines for knee and calf. A high view is drawn as an **oblique parallel projection**, in which the floor tilts up behind the figure, instead of as a foreshortened body (JAANUS, [*fukinuki yatai*](https://projects.mcah.columbia.edu/jaanus/record/fukinukiyatai): "the viewer looks inside from above"; see `ukiyo-e--space.md`).

## 2. Proportion by type, not by anatomy
- **Beauties vary by period:** Harunobu's are "child-like" ([viewingjapaneseprints, Kiyonaga](https://viewingjapaneseprints.net/texts/ukiyoe/kiyonaga.html)), "younger and thinner" ([Wikipedia, Torii Kiyonaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torii_Kiyonaga)). Kiyonaga's became "taller and slimmer" (viewingjapaneseprints), "exceptionally tall" (Wikipedia). Utamaro's standing women in CMA 1956.753 read about **8 heads**, crown to chin, not counting the coiffure (by eye). With the coiffure they are about 6.6 heads.
- **Working men in landscape and street prints are compact:** about **6-6.5 heads** (Met 36922, the blue-jacketed porter, by eye). Heads are large, legs short and sturdy, and the back is bent under a load (Met 36521, 55222; CMA 1930.190).
- **What the works share:** the canon is a choice that tells you the type. Refined figures are long and slight, and working people are short and solid. Every work keeps hands and feet small (see `ukiyo-e--hands-feet.md`). For a contemporary adult man as an ordinary person, use the compact commoner build, about 6½ heads (**judgement**).

## 3. The body as a clothed silhouette
- **The garment's outline is the figure's outline.** Torso, hips and thighs are seldom drawn as anatomy. The shape of the clothing (shoulder line, sleeve, lap, hem) says where they are (Met 55810, 54869; CMA 1956.753).
- **The line of action survives in the contour:** one long curve from the nape down the back to the lap or hem carries the pose (Met 55810, the dozing courtesan's bowed back; CMA 1930.190, the haulers' bent backs). Layer 1's C and S curves still apply, drawn as **the silhouette's main line** (**judgement** on the wording).
- **Bare working limbs:** knees as a small curve or hook, a calf bulge in the outline, ankle bones as a tick, all in key line on flat skin tint, with no muscle masses modelled (Met 36922, 36521).
- **Weight and contact:** the figure is grounded by overlap. The body sits *over* the floor line, the mat or the bench edge. There is no cast or contact shadow (see `ukiyo-e--light-shadow.md`; Met 55800, CMA 1985.322).

## 4. Seated, slumped and reclining sleepers
- **Sleepers in the prints:** an attendant curled on the floor with her head on her arms (Met 55800; CMA 1985.309), a courtesan dozing upright with her head bowed onto her chest (Met 55810), and a scholar slumped forward over his books (Met 54339). Every pose is **slack and folded**. The head drops, the shoulders round, and the arms lie loose or pillow the head.
- **Seated figures read as a mound of cloth.** The lap and the spread hem make a broad base, and the back is one curve (Met 55810; CMA 1985.322, travellers at an inn, one seen from behind as a rounded shape tied with an obi).
- **Slackness is in the curves.** Long sagging contours, limbs that rest on something, and hands that lie open. A tense figure has angular breaks and zigzag hems (Met 36521 running porters; **judgement** on the contrast, consistent with layer 1's gesture rules).

## 5. Seen from above: tilt the floor, not the body
- The high view is oblique: floor lines, mats and benches run as **parallel diagonals** that rise up the sheet behind the figure (Met 55800, the sleeping attendant on a floor of parallel diagonals; CMA 1985.322; Met 55222).
- On that floor the figure is drawn from a **raised three-quarter view**. Near parts overlap far parts, and the silhouette is compressed along the line of sight. But the near limb is **not enlarged**: a near knee and a far knee are about the same size (**judgement**, from the works seen; no print shows strong perspective scaling of a body).
- **Straight down:** no print looks vertically down at a figure. As in `ukiyo-e--space.md`, a straight-down view is an extension of the convention. The figure becomes a flat silhouette seen from its top. The crown of the head, the shoulders, the lap and the feet show as clear shapes, and the parts are told apart by overlap and by the flat colour of each garment (**judgement**; log it).

## 6. What stays true to life
The line of action, the tilt of head, shoulders and hips against each other, the overlap order, the place where weight rests, and the relative length of limbs within one figure all stay true. Lumping or warping the anatomy beyond the type reads as caricature, not ukiyo-e (**judgement**).

## 7. Checks for Teodor
1. Is the proportion a chosen type (compact commoner, about 6½ heads), used the same way on every figure of that type?
2. Does one long contour carry the slack pose (bowed head, rounded back, loose arms)?
3. Is the body a clothed silhouette in key line, with no muscle or volume modelling?
4. Are bare limbs drawn with only a hook at the knee, a bulge at the calf and a tick at the ankle?
5. Is the figure grounded by overlap, with no contact shadow?
6. From above: does the floor tilt on parallel diagonals, while the body is not perspective-scaled?
