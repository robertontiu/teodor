# Graphite hyperrealism: hands and feet (layer 2)

How graphite hyperrealism departs from the layer-1 hands and feet ([docs/subjects/hands-feet.md](../../subjects/hands-feet.md)). Read with the style doc ([docs/styles/graphite-hyperrealism.md](../graphite-hyperrealism.md)) and [graphite-hyperrealism--face.md](graphite-hyperrealism--face.md), whose section 3 holds the photographs measured as the ruler and the method. Researched 2026-11-26. **[J]** marks a judgement call. The current is in copyright: technique sources and public-domain photographs only; no rule rests on one artist.

## 1. The twist in one paragraph
Layer 1 builds the hand as palm block, knuckle arc and thumb wedge, and lets the dark gaps between fingers do the reading. Hyperrealism keeps that construction and then gives the hand the most surface of anything in the picture when it sits in the focal plane: the skin's creases at every joint, the nail as a hard glossy plate with its cuticle and free edge, tendons and veins under thin skin, the fine grain of the back of the hand. A hand held close to a lens is a classic hyperrealist subject because the lens enlarges it and the depth of field isolates it ([Wikipedia, Depth of field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depth_of_field): at close range depth of field is "proportionally much smaller"; style doc §2, §7). What separates a living hand from a catalogue of wrinkles is hierarchy: "Create a hierarchy with your values and edges"; "details like veins are tertiary forms"; "if it doesn't serve a purpose, it shouldn't be there" ([Proko, details for realistic hands](https://www.proko.com/lesson/how-to-draw-hands-details-for-realistic-hands)).

## 3. How it departs from layer 1
**Proportions.** True to life and to the lens: a hand nearer the lens than the face is drawn larger than "a face long" by perspective (layer 1's check holds only at equal distance) **[J]**, from perspective.

**Creases.** "Skin folds follow the action of the hand, like folds in clothing follow the action of the body"; on the backs of the fingers "the distal joint can be simplified to a single, straight line", "the middle joint has a spiral pattern", and "the creases point in the direction of the knuckle" (Proko). Keep "other creases ... secondary and as light as possible" (Proko). Draw each main crease as a valley with a lit lip on the light side (the face page's wrinkle rule, [Wildlife Art Store, depth](https://www.wildlifeartstore.com/create-depth-in-your-drawing/)), its darkest point short and inside the joint, fading at both ends; never a uniform line **[J]**. When a finger flexes, the skin over the back of the joint stretches and its creases shallow, while the palm side closes into deep folds **[J]** (follows from the skin's slack).

**Nails.** The nail "follow[s] that cylindrical top plane of the rest of the finger" and "acts as a natural cross-contour line", telling whether the finger comes toward the viewer (Proko). Hyperrealism adds the surface: a hard, smooth plate with a specular highlight along its curvature (glossier than skin), a lighter free edge that turns off the plate, the cuticle and lateral nail folds as soft skin overlapping hard plate, and often the pale lunula at the base **[J]** (common observation; no measured source fetched). The nail's edge is the hardest edge in a hand (plate against skin or air), so in the focal plane it carries the sharpest transition of the picture **[J]**.

**Tendons and veins.** "Tendons are going to be much straighter in their path from wrist to fingertip"; "veins will meander", are "tucked into depressions between bones" and have "a slightly darker ... local color, whereas tendons will match the skin tone or even be a little lighter" (Proko). In graphite: tendons are lit ridges with a soft shadow on their far side; veins are soft, slightly darker raised bands, never outlined **[J]**, applying Proko's values in monochrome.

**Marks.** Fine skin grain inside the light, laid after the plane's tone (face page §4); creases with a `mech_03` and a `tortillon`, blended clipped to the finger (`clip: true`) so the silhouette stays crisp; nail speculars and the lit lips of creases lifted last with `precision_eraser` or `electric_eraser` and `"seek": true`; a single hair or two on the back of the hand or wrist indented with the `stylus` before tone (style doc §8) **[J]**.

**Feet.** The same rules (creases by joint, nails as glossy cross-contours, tendons straight, veins meandering) apply; feet are not in this brief and were not measured.

**What stays true to life.** Layer 1's whole construction, and contact: a fist's fingers press each other, the flesh of one finger is dented by the next, cast shadows sit between them (layer 1 §3, the darkest accents).

## 4. For this brief (a hand at arm's length, a loose fist with the thumb up and the nail toward his eye; knuckle creases, nail edge and tendons are the focal plane, the sharpest edges in the picture)
- **The focal plane.** Put the sharpest edges in the picture here and nowhere else: the thumbnail's free edge and side, the knuckle creases of the thumb and the curled index finger, the extensor tendons at the back of the hand and wrist. Everything else softens with its distance from this plane (face page §4); the curled fingers further from the camera already soften a little across the fist **[J]**, from depth of field at close range.
- **Thumb up, nail toward the eye.** An extended thumb raises the tendons that bound the anatomical snuffbox: the extensor pollicis longus on one side and the paired extensor pollicis brevis and abductor pollicis longus on the other; the hollow "is most visible ... during thumb extension" ([Wikipedia, Anatomical snuff box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatomical_snuff_box)). That is the tendon passage to draw sharp: two ridges and a small triangular hollow at the base of the thumb, on the radial back of the wrist. The thumb's back shows the creases of its joint (Proko's straight distal crease); the nail faces him, so from the camera it is seen at an angle as a curved plate whose free edge is the sharpest line in the picture **[J]**.
- **Loose fist.** The knuckles (the heads of the metacarpals) form an arc of four bumps (layer 1), with skin stretched lighter over them and short creases over the middle joints of the curled fingers pointing to the knuckle (Proko); the fingertips tuck into the palm with dark gaps between them (carbon at their deepest) **[J]**.
- **A man of 45.** A few more and deeper creases than a young hand, a little vein relief, a couple of hairs at the wrist and on the backs of the proximal phalanges; no age spots or deep wrinkles (those say 60+) **[J]**, after layer 1 `sex-age` §4 (over-ageing).
- **Light.** If the light comes from the sky he faces, the hand's back and nail are lit and the palm side faces him in shadow; the silhouette against the sky may be darker than the sky: then the sharp edge is a dark-on-light silhouette edge, kept sharp with a hard pencil drawn along the schema's edge, a tool the calibration lists as missing ([calibration](../../calibration/graphite-hyperrealism.md) "What still limits", 3) **[J]**.

## 5. Conflicts flagged
- Layer 1 treats a hand as a silhouette and planes with the gaps as darkest accents; that holds out of focus. **In the focal plane** the style adds surface detail layer 1 leaves out; the planes still come first (tone before texture).
- The calibration notes that edges between two dark tones get lost and that a contour-sharpening tool is missing. The brief's "sharpest edges in the picture" depends on it: plan the crisp edges as reserved lights (indent or never tone the nail's free edge and the creases' lit lips) or as masked blending (`clip`), since they cannot be sharpened afterwards **[J]**.

## 6. Checks for Teodor
1. The sharpest edges in the whole picture are on the thumbnail, the knuckle creases and the tendons, and nowhere else.
2. Each crease is a valley with a lit lip, darkest inside the joint, fading at both ends; distal creases straight, middle-joint creases spiralling, all pointing to the knuckle.
3. The nail reads as a hard glossy plate (specular, light free edge, soft cuticle overlap) and as a cross-contour of the thumb.
4. Tendons straight and light; veins soft, slightly darker, meandering; neither outlined.
5. Texture on the back of the hand is about two to three times the cheek's in the focal plane, and fades with distance.
