# Romanticism: hair (layer 2)

How Romanticism departs from layer-1 hair ([docs/subjects/hair.md](../../subjects/hair.md)). Read with the style doc ([docs/styles/romanticism.md](../romanticism.md)) and [romanticism--face.md](romanticism--face.md). Researched 2026-11-26. **[J]** marks a judgement call. Works are examples of what several artists share; no rule rests on one artist.

## 1. The twist in one paragraph
Neoclassical hair is dressed and sculptural: bound, curled, parted, with a firm edge and an even, polished light (the Met's David and Girodet portraits, below). Romantic hair is let loose. Unbound, dishevelled or wet hair is one of the current's signs of passion, distress and abandon to nature or feeling; in the theatre of the period a woman entering "with disheveled hair" signalled madness or violation, and "the white dress, loose hair, and wild flowers" conveyed distraction [1]. In paint, hair becomes one dark mass with few groups, its edge lost into a dark ground or the weather, a handful of locks broken out in the light with visible strokes that follow the fall. It moves with the same weather as the cloth and the sky.

## 3. How it departs from layer 1
**Proportions.** The mass sits on the skull as in layer 1 (wider than the head); loose hair makes it larger and less contained, spreading onto shoulders, into water or wind (Ophelia, Fuseli, Rebecca) **[J]**.

**Simplified.** Fewer groups than layer 1's 3-7 at portrait distance: one dark mass, two or three big falls, a few locks only where the light catches them (Pierret, Desdemona, Ophelia) **[J]**, observation. No individual strands except one or two at the lit edge. Within the shadow mass there is no texture at all, which layer 1 also asks (Proko), pushed further.

**Conventions.** (a) Loose hair as a sign of feeling and of nature's force [1]. (b) The edge lost into a dark ground on the shadow side and into the weather on the leeward side (Desdemona, Pierret, Fuseli); found only against the lit face or a light sky. (c) Wet hair as dark ropes following the water or wind, with light gaps between them (Ophelia).

**Colour, edges and marks.** Hair is among the darkest values near the face, so the lit face reads against it (Ophelia, Desdemona) **[J]**. Strokes follow the direction of growth and fall, long and tapering, visible; the light on hair is a warm band of a few strokes across the locks (layer 1's highlight band, painted briskly), not a polished sheen. Shadows in hair carry the surround's colour, not black (the period's coloured shadows [2]).

**What stays true to life.** The physics of layer 1: roots fixed, ends move most; wet hair darker, clumped, flat to the skull at the windward side; one wind for hair, cloth and sky. The highlight still runs across the strands.

## 4. For this brief (long, dark, half loose, damp, streaming downwind in ropes)
- **Half loose is right for the current;** loose hair belongs to the Romantic figure. The period's code reads dishevelled hair as distraction or distress [1]; here the open face, the braced body and the raised arm must turn the same sign into release and joy **[J]**.
- **Ropes downwind:** damp hair clumps into a few dark, tapering ropes (layer 1 "wet"), streaming from the skull on the leeward side in the wind's direction, parallel to the sheet's flight; on the windward side it presses flat against the head and shows the skull.
- **One mass, few ropes:** paint the whole hair as one dark shape first, then three to five ropes breaking out downwind, each a long tapering stroke, a few light gaps between them where the sky shows through **[J]**.
- **Edges:** lost where the hair's shadow side meets a dark cloud; found where the ropes cross a light sky; the tied part keeps a firmer shape near the head.
- **Light:** the low raking light gives a warm band across the crown and the near ropes, a few strokes, and sharp small lights on the damp surface (layer 1); the rest is dark and cool.

## 5. Conflict flagged
Mild only: in the period's code, loose hair on a woman signals distraction [1]. The brief's feeling (joy) is carried by the face and pose; nothing in the research forbids loose hair in joy, so the brief stands **[J]**.

## 6. Checks for Teodor
1. Squint: the hair is one dark mass, with at most three to five ropes breaking out downwind.
2. No texture inside the shadow mass; strokes only in the light and at the leeward edge.
3. The ropes stream in the same direction as the sheet, the cloth and the clouds.
4. Edges lost into dark ground or weather, found only against light sky or the face.
5. Damp hair darker and clumped, with a few sharp small lights; no corona of strays.
