# Ukiyo-e: objects (layer 2: the subject in the style)

How ukiyo-e renders made things, with a street market in focus: stacked crates, a striped awning with a scalloped edge cut by the frame, a shopping bag. Read [the layer-1 page](../../subjects/objects.md) first, and [the style doc](../ukiyo-e.md). Researched 2026-11 from public-domain prints at the Met and the Cleveland Museum of Art (CMA) and museum texts; values marked **judgement** have no published source.

## 1. The departure from layer 1, in one paragraph
Layer 1 treats objects as simple solids whose material is read from how light meets them: specular highlights on glaze and metal, translucency in glass, cast and contact shadows, ellipses in perspective. Ukiyo-e keeps the solid and its construction but not the light: materials are told apart by **flat colour and pattern** (wood grain lines, weave grids, tile scales, textile stripes), boxes are drawn in an oblique view whose parallel edges stay parallel, and nothing casts a shadow. Shop fronts, their curtains (*noren*) and their goods are a standing ukiyo-e subject because the prints showed contemporary Edo life (style doc; CMA on teahouses "with shop curtains hanging from their eaves").

## 2. Proportion and structure: what stretches or flattens
- **Boxes in oblique parallel projection.** Stacked boxes, counters and chests show a front face and a top or side face as parallelograms with parallel receding edges (Met 51136, a fan shop interior with stacked boxes; Met 36922, fishmongers' tubs and baskets; Met 54973, a dish). The early Western-perspective prints (*uki-e*) are the exception (see `ukiyo-e--space.md`).
- **Objects keep true construction:** a hoop on a tub, a lid, a carrying pole, a basket rim, a fan's ribs are all drawn as they are built (Met 36922; CMA 1949.153).
- **Foreground objects can be enlarged and cut by the frame:** shop curtains run off the top edge, gates off both sides (Met 54670, 36922); a boat's oar fills the foreground (CMA 1992.73 text: the design "foregrounds the oar of a passing boat").

## 3. What it simplifies, idealises or leaves out
- **Left out:** highlights, reflections, cast shadows, the darkening of an object's turned side (all works listed). Even metal and lacquer are flat colour (**judgement**, from Met 51136, 54973).
- **Simplified:** each object is one or two flat colours plus a line pattern that names the material: woven basket as a fine grid, wood as parallel grain lines, tiles as a scale or grid, cloth as stripes, checks or a crest (Met 36922, 54670, 55222; CMA 1949.153).
- **Idealised:** goods are neat, stacked squarely, new; clutter is arranged as rhythm (**judgement**).

## 4. Conventions
- **The shop curtain cut by the frame.** Kiyonaga's Echigo-ya curtains hang from the top edge in long panels printed with a large crest and run out of the picture; the awning above is a strip of bamboo with a fringe (Met 54670). Hokusai's Mitsui shop shows signboards and roofs cut by the frame (Met 55287). This is the ukiyo-e model for a market awning cut by the frame.
- **Textile pattern does not bend much with the cloth:** stripes and checks run straight within each panel or fold face and change direction at the fold, rather than curving and shading round it (Met 36922 blue checked jacket; Met 54670 curtains; style doc: "textiles as flat pattern", **judgement**).
- **Repeated objects as pattern:** rows of hanging lanterns, stacked boxes, a crowd of tubs and baskets carried on poles (Met 36669, 51136, 36922).
- **Carried goods:** bundles, baskets and tubs hang from poles or are carried in the hand as flat silhouettes with a pattern (Met 36922; CMA 1985.315 baskets of shellfish).

## 5. Colour, edge and mark
- **Key line** in sumi round every made object, straight and even; ruled straight lines for architecture and boxes (all works listed).
- **Colour:** natural wood and straw as pale ochre or paper colour, iron and tiles as grey or blue-grey, cloth in the strongest colours of the picture (indigo, vermilion, Prussian blue) (Met 36922, 55222, 54670; palette from the style doc).
- **Pattern** is drawn on top of a flat ground colour in a second colour or in line; it stops cleanly at the object's outline.
- **Edges:** all hard.

## 6. What stays true to life
Construction (slats, hoops, handles, seams), the number of repeated parts, the way a stack rests (each box on the one below), the logic of a cut-off curtain hanging from a rail.

## 7. For the market brief (**judgement**, built from the conventions above)
- **Stacked crates:** each crate an oblique box: front face in pale wood colour, slat joints as ruled sumi lines, the open top showing its contents as a flat field (see `ukiyo-e--flowers.md` section 7); stacks drawn with the same receding angle for every crate; the gaps between crates a darker flat tone, not a shadow.
- **Striped awning with a scalloped edge, cut by the frame:** stripes as flat alternating bands of two colours, straight on the flat part of the awning and changing angle only where the cloth folds over its frame; the scalloped hem as a repeated motif of identical arcs in key line; the frame cuts the awning on two sides so only a band of it enters the picture, the way the Echigo-ya curtains enter from the top edge.
- **Shopping bag:** a flat silhouette with one or two colours and any print or logo as flat pattern; handles as two key-line loops; no sheen on plastic (if it is plastic, its material is told by a lighter flat colour and a few crisp fold lines).

## 8. Checks for Teodor
1. Every box shares one receding angle; receding edges stay parallel.
2. Materials are told by pattern and colour, never by highlights.
3. Stripes run straight within each face of the cloth and change direction at folds.
4. The awning and at least one foreground object are cut by the frame.
5. No cast shadows; gaps between stacked things are a flat darker colour.
6. Key lines are clean, even, closed.
