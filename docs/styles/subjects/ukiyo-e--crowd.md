# Ukiyo-e: crowd (layer 2: the subject in the style)

How ukiyo-e implies a crowd. It uses a few near figures cut hard by the frame, a dense overlap of flat, distinct shapes, anonymous flat silhouettes, and a far band of tiny repeated heads. The middle distance is left out, and nothing is shaded or blurred. The focus is passers-by implied and cropped by the frame edge. Read [the layer-1 page](../../subjects/crowd.md) first, then [ukiyo-e--space](ukiyo-e--space.md) (crops, scale jumps) and [ukiyo-e--figure](ukiyo-e--figure.md). Researched 2026-11 from public-domain prints at the Met and the Cleveland Museum of Art (CMA), with museum texts. Values marked **judgement** have no published source.

## 1. The departure from layer 1, in one paragraph
Layer 1 builds a crowd out of small groups. Their spacing says their relationships, their heads line up on the eye level, they shrink steadily with distance, and their detail falls off from near faces to far silhouettes. Ukiyo-e keeps the **groups, the overlap and the fall-off of detail**, but replaces the steady recession with a **jump in scale**. Large near figures, often **cut by the frame**, sit against a far **band of tiny repeated figures**, with little in between (CMA 1956.753: "People crowding the Ryōgoku Bridge" drawn as a row of tiny heads behind large foreground figures; Met 55624; see `ukiyo-e--space.md`, scale jumps). The edge cuts figures without hesitation. Japanese designers were known for "artificially compressing compositions and cropping figures", which Degas took up so that "the figures are cropped almost cruelly" ([Art UK, *When East inspired West*](https://www.artuk.org/discover/stories/when-east-inspired-west-the-extraordinary-influence-of-japanese-art)). Distance is told by flat, paler steps rather than haze (CMA 1948.306: three blocks give "three shades of gray used to suggest distance").

## 2. Ways ukiyo-e implies a crowd
- **Cut by the frame.** Near figures are sliced at the bottom or side edge, sometimes down to a head and shoulders or a back. The cut is a straight edge with full detail up to it (Met 55222: porters cut at the bottom edge; Met 55624: grey silhouettes cut at the bottom and sides).
- **Dense overlap of distinct shapes.** Bodies overlap in a tight cluster. Each has its own key line, a different flat colour or pattern, and a different pose and facing, so the mass reads as many people without any shading (Met 55222; Met 36922, the vendors' cluster in the foreground).
- **Anonymous flat silhouettes.** Passers-by can be single flat tones in a few values of grey, with no features or interior lines, overlapping each other (Met 55624). This is the strongest way to imply people without making them characters.
- **Hidden behind something.** Only heads, hats, spears or banners show above a bridge crest, a wall or a palanquin (Met 36922: a procession's heads and spears over the bridge; Met 55369, Hokusai: a traveller half hidden behind a palanquin).
- **A far band of tiny repeats.** A row of dots or tiny figures along a bridge or street, in one flat value (CMA 1956.753; Met 55624 background).
- **A frieze.** Travellers spaced along a road in a line, each separate, their repeated hats and loads making a rhythm (Met 55369; Met 36521 and CMA 1948.306).

## 3. Rules that hold across the works
- **No middle distance:** near figures and far figures, with little between them (CMA 1956.753, Met 55624; **judgement** that it is the rule rather than a frequent choice).
- **Groups of two and three** inside the mass: pairs talking, carriers sharing a pole (Met 55222, 36922; CMA 1956.753). This matches layer 1's group sizes.
- **Detail falls off in steps:** near figures get faces and pattern, middle figures (if any) flat colour and outline, far figures a single value (Met 55222, 55624; CMA 1948.306).
- **Rhythm by repetition:** the same hat, head or load shape repeated with small variations (Met 36521, 55369; **judgement** on the wording).
- **From above,** heads become round shapes over shoulders, with the ground showing between figures (Met 55222, high view; layer 1, high view).

## 4. The brief: passers-by implied and cropped by the frame
- Let the passers-by **enter from the frame edges only**: a foot and trouser leg, the hem of a coat, the edge of a bag, perhaps a shoulder. Cut each by a straight frame edge with full detail right up to it (Met 55222, 55624; see `ukiyo-e--hands-feet.md`).
- **Subordinate them by flatness and value, not by blur.** Give them flat colours lower in chroma than the sleeper, or one flat grey each as in Met 55624. Keep them free of pattern, or give them only a simple pattern (**judgement**).
- Show **direction of motion** by stride and a flipped hem, all moving along the frame edge rather than toward the sleeper (**judgement**; layer 1's figures leaning into their motion).
- Vary the pieces you show (one foot, two feet in step, a hem, a basket) so the edge reads as many people, not one person repeated (**judgement**; layer 1's individuality).

## 5. What stays true to life
Group structure (pairs, threes), overlap order, a consistent ground plane for feet, the size of like things at one depth, and the direction people walk all stay true (**judgement**; matches `ukiyo-e--space.md` "keep").

## 6. Checks for Teodor
1. Are passers-by cut by a straight frame edge with full detail up to it, and never faded?
2. Do they read as several people (different pieces, colours or greys), not one person repeated?
3. Are they subordinate through flatness and lower chroma, not through blur or haze?
4. If a far crowd appears, is it a band of tiny repeated shapes in one flat value, with no middle distance?
5. Does each overlapping figure have its own key line and flat colour, with no shadows between them?
