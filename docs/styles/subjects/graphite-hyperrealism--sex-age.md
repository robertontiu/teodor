# Graphite hyperrealism: sex and age (layer 2)

How graphite hyperrealism departs from the layer-1 page on sex and age ([docs/subjects/sex-age.md](../../subjects/sex-age.md)). Read with the style doc ([docs/styles/graphite-hyperrealism.md](../graphite-hyperrealism.md)) and [graphite-hyperrealism--face.md](graphite-hyperrealism--face.md) (section 3: the photographs measured). Researched 2026-11-26. **[J]** marks a judgement call. The current is in copyright: technique sources, perception research and public-domain photographs only; no rule rests on one artist.

## 1. The twist in one paragraph
Layer 1 finds that at thumbnail size sex and age read from structure, hair value and facial contrast, and that wrinkles do not survive reduction. Hyperrealism is viewed close, at or above life size, so the cues layer 1 calls weak at a distance become strong: **skin surface carries age**. "Texture and wrinkles explain a large share of perceived age" (Fink and Matts 2008), and smoothing a face makes it younger: averaged faces look younger "because averaging erases wrinkles" (Burt and Perrett 1995) (both via layer 1 §4). The style's own failure, the overblended "plastic" surface (style doc §11), is therefore also an **age** error: it takes years off and, with large eyes and a smooth jaw, can turn a man into a boy **[J]**, from those findings. Its second risk is the opposite of every other style's: a hyperreal face from a photograph of a real person **is** that person's likeness (rule 5), so the reference has to be invented.

## 2. How it departs from layer 1
**Age is in the surface, measured by zone.** At the focal plane the drawing shows what the photograph shows: fine lines at the outer eye and under the lower lid, the nasolabial fold, the vertical lines of the lips, forehead lines at rest, pores and coarser texture on the cheek and nose (pore size grows from the youngest group and in several groups "plateaus from 40 years of age": [Flament 2015](https://www.dovepress.com/facial-skin-pores-a-multiethnic-study-peer-reviewed-fulltext-article-CCID)). The ruler (face page §3) gives the amplitudes: smooth lit skin 1.5-2 L* of mid texture, folds and hair zones 4-5. A forty-five-year-old sits between the young smooth ruler and the old: the texture is present everywhere in focus, deepest where the expression folds the skin **[J]**.

**Facial contrast in monochrome.** Layer 1: facial contrast falls with age and lower contrast also reads more male (Porcheron et al. 2013; Russell 2009, via layer 1 §1, §4). In graphite the colour cues are gone, so contrast is the eye and lip darks against the skin's value. For a man, keep the eye region's contrast moderate (no darkened lash line beyond what the photograph shows) and the lips close to the skin's value (A, face page: lips sit inside the skin's range, their line a dark crease) **[J]**.

**Grey hair is drawn as reserved white.** A grey hair in a dark mass or in stubble is a white line: indent it with the `stylus` before any tone, as white beards are embossed "before starting the shading" ([Skillshare, hair and beard](https://www.skillshare.com/en/classes/drawing-realistic-hair-and-beard-with-graphite/1384601388); Sibley via style doc §8), or lift it last with an `electric_eraser` at 2-3 px. How much: among people aged 45-65, 74% have some grey, averaging 27% of the hair (Panhard et al. 2012, via layer 1 §4); "a forty-year-old has a little grey at most" (layer 1).

**Male cues that need no exaggeration.** The beard zone's tone and stubble, the brow ridge, the jaw angle and the thicker neck (layer 1 §5) all come from the reference's structure; hyperrealism adds the beard's texture (face page §4: 0.15-0.2 mm marks, about 0.3 per mm², in grouped directions).

**What stays true to life.** Layer 1's structure cues and their weights. Hyperrealism exaggerates nothing; it only resolves what is there.

## 3. Avoiding a specific real person (rule 5)
- The reference is a **rendered scene** (the studio's people, `docs/MANUAL.md`) or an invented composite, never a photograph of a real man: at this fidelity a traced photograph is a likeness **[J]**, follows from the style's transfer method (style doc §6).
- Vary the face's proportions away from the studio's defaults and away from any face in the measured photographs: nose length, jaw width, eye spacing, lip fullness (layer 1 `face` §4, "individuality"). Nothing of the two measured photographs' geometry is used; only value, texture and focus numbers.
- Do not add identifying marks a viewer could match to someone (a distinctive scar, tattoo or mole pattern) **[J]**.

## 4. For this brief (an adult man in his mid-forties, with stubble, first lines and a few grey hairs; he must not read as a boy or as a specific real person)
- **Not a boy.** Keep the adult structure: a brow ridge with the eye set under it, a defined jaw angle, a thick neck, an adult nose. Keep texture at the ruler's level in focus; don't blend the face smooth; let the grin's folds (AU6 crow's feet, the deepened nasolabial fold) be crisp forms, with faint lines that persist beyond them (under the lower lid of the open eye, at the forehead) **[J]**. Stubble is the strongest adult-male cue the brief allows: lay the grey beard-zone tone first, then the hairs where focus allows.
- **First lines, not old lines.** Crow's feet and the nasolabial fold come from the grin; at rest a 45-year-old has only fine lines there. So: the folds the expression makes are deep; the folds age alone makes (jowls, deep marionette lines, neck bands) are absent or faint (layer 1 §4: "over-ageing" adds twenty years) **[J]**.
- **A few grey hairs.** Mostly in the stubble at the chin and the jaw angle nearest the focal plane (indented white, a handful per cm² among the dark ones), and a few at the temple **[J]**. The hair on his head is out of focus (see `hair`), where single grey hairs do not resolve: there grey can only appear as a slightly lighter, mottled patch at the temple. Put the age cue where the focus can carry it.
- **Lying down.** Gravity draws the soft tissue toward the ears and smooths the nasolabial region, which looks younger (face page §6); compensate with texture, not with lines **[J]**.

## 5. Conflicts flagged
- The brief's grey hairs and its out-of-focus hair conflict: single grey hairs cannot read in a blurred mass. Following the optics (depth of field), the grey moves to the stubble near the focal plane. Flagged for the orchestrator.
- Layer 1's "age at thumbnail size" (wrinkles don't survive) is right for the thumbnail critic, wrong for viewing distance in this style. Check both: the thumbnail must read "adult man" from structure and hair value; the close view must read "mid-forties" from surface **[J]**.

## 6. Checks for Teodor
1. At thumbnail size: adult man from structure (brow, jaw, neck) and the beard-zone tone.
2. Close up: fine lines under the open eye and at the forehead; the grin's folds crisp; skin texture present in focus.
3. A handful of white (indented) grey hairs in the stubble near the focal plane; no white hair elsewhere.
4. No over-ageing: no jowls, no deep neck bands, no age spots.
5. The face's proportions are the invented reference's, varied from the studio's defaults; no traced photograph of a real man.
