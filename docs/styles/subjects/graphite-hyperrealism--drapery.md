# Graphite hyperrealism: drapery (layer 2)

How graphite hyperrealism departs from the layer-1 drapery ([docs/subjects/drapery.md](../../subjects/drapery.md)). Read with the style doc ([docs/styles/graphite-hyperrealism.md](../graphite-hyperrealism.md)) and [graphite-hyperrealism--face.md](graphite-hyperrealism--face.md) (section 3: the photographs measured and the method). Researched 2026-11-26. **[J]** marks a judgement call. The current is in copyright: technique sources and public-domain photographs only; no rule rests on one artist.

## 1. The twist in one paragraph
Layer 1 builds folds from support points, tension and gravity and paints each fold as a small lit form. Hyperrealism keeps the fold logic and, in focus, adds what the camera records of the material: the weave as a fine regular texture, wear and soiling, frayed edges, seams and stitching, and the full value range of white cloth in sun, from near paper-white on the lit planes to near black in deep valleys. Cloth is one of the style's classic test subjects (style doc §7: crumpled paper and fabric), because its folds show whether the values are right **[J]**. Out of focus, cloth loses its weave and keeps only its fold values (face page §3).

## 2. What the ruler shows (photograph A of the face page: a worn white cotton work shirt in hard sun)
| measure | value |
|---|---|
| lit flat cotton (shoulder): median / p95 / p99.9 L* | 68.9 / 79.2 / 83.5 |
| rolled sleeve, lit: median, p5 - p95 | 66.2, 52.0 - 76.3 |
| fold zone over the shoulder: p5 L* (deep valleys) | 16.6 |
| weave: FFT peak period (sleeve, shoulder) | **0.81-0.83 mm**, peak 84-129 x the annulus median (forehead skin, same test: x15, no weave) |
| fine-band texture, sleeve vs forehead skin | 3.5 vs 1.8 L* |
| mid-band texture (folds, soiling), lit shoulder / fold zone | 5.2-8.3 / 6.1 |

Read: (1) white cotton in sun is as light as the lightest skin and the catchlight (p99.9 83.5), and its deep fold valleys go nearly to black (L* 17): the full photographic range inside one white shirt. Shadows on white cloth are darker than one expects: "at least a middle gray" ([Skillshare, shading fabric folds](https://www.skillshare.com/en/classes/shading-folds-of-fabric-with-graphite-pencil-how-to-get-a-realistic-effect/2141936883)). (2) The weave is a **regular** texture with a measurable period, unlike skin's irregular grain: in focus it is visible as a fine directional pattern that follows the cloth's surface. This was a coarse work cotton; a modern shirting's weave is finer (**[J]**: a fine poplin's threads are well under 0.3 mm) and at the studio's 5 px/mm it would fall below one pixel unless the sleeve is drawn larger than life. (3) No sharp specular: cotton is matte (layer 1 §3: a highlight is "not present on matte materials"); the lightest values are broad lit planes, not streaks **[J]**, seen on the gridded crops.

## 3. How it departs from layer 1
**Fold forms, photographic values.** Layer 1's fold types and tension logic hold. The values are the photograph's: lit planes high, halftones broad and soft (cotton), core shadows in the valleys deep, reflected light inside the shadow "about two steps below" the highlights (Skillshare, shading fabric folds). "Keep edges light where light hits the fabric, and darken edges only where a shadow sits beneath them" (same). Rolls soft, creases hard (layer 1 check 4).

**Surface.** In focus: the weave as a fine periodic tone (a `mech_03` hatch at the weave's spacing and direction, then a light brush pass that leaves the period visible), seams as small ridges with a lit side and a stitched line of tiny reserved lights (indented before tone), wear and soil as soft irregular stains, frayed edges as short indented or lifted threads against a dark ground **[J]**, after the ruler's crops and style doc §8 (indenting). A pumice stone under the paper is one draughtsman's way to give clothing texture ([Veronica's Art](https://veronicasart.com/?p=5729)); in the engine, the weave is drawn, not rubbed.

**Blending.** Stumps overused give "muddy and flat values" (Skillshare, shading fabric folds); "small circular strokes help blend without creating directional edges" (same). Blend each fold clipped to its own plane so ridges stay crisp where the photograph has them **[J]**, from masked blending in the calibration.

**Out of focus.** Weave, seams and soil vanish; fold values and soft fold edges remain (face page §3: out-of-focus structure 0.6-1.2 L*) **[J]**.

**What stays true to life.** Every layer-1 rule on support, tension, gravity and material.

## 4. For this brief (the sleeve of a pale cotton shirt slipping down the raised forearm)
- **Fold logic.** With the arm straight up, gravity pulls the sleeve down the forearm toward the elbow and shoulder: the cuff falls open away from the wrist as a loose ellipse, the sleeve's slack gathers at the elbow in stacked compression folds (zigzag or diamond buckles), and long pipe folds run from the gathered mass up toward the cuff **[J]**, applying layer 1's fold table (layer 1 §4: "Raising an arm: the sleeve slides down"). The forearm shows bare between the cuff and the wrist, its skin edge meeting the cuff with a soft cast shadow inside the cuff's opening.
- **Pale cotton in light.** Lit planes near the lightest skin values; the valleys of the gathered folds as dark as the photograph says (white cloth's shadows "at least a middle gray", deep valleys near black); no sharp highlight anywhere on the cloth.
- **Focus.** The cuff is close to the hand and nearly as sharp: weave visible only if the sleeve is drawn large enough (see section 2); the gathered folds near the elbow, farther from the focal plane, keep their values and lose their texture **[J]**.
- **Contact.** Where the cuff rests on the forearm the cloth casts a narrow dark line and the skin under it is in shadow; where the sleeve touches the arm it reveals the forearm's mass (layer 1 check 5).

## 5. Conflicts flagged
- None with the research. One caution: the ruler's shirt is a coarse 1930s work cotton; its weave period (0.8 mm) is not a target for a modern pale shirt. Use it only for the principle (a regular, directional, measurable texture) **[J]**.

## 6. Checks for Teodor
1. The fold logic follows layer 1: sleeve slid down, gathered at the elbow, cuff open, pipe folds toward the cuff.
2. The lit cotton is as light as the lightest skin; deep valleys reach the drawing's darks.
3. No specular streak on the cotton; halftones broad and soft.
4. In focus, the weave is a fine regular texture following the cloth; out of focus it is gone.
5. Fold ridges crisp where the cloth creases, soft where it rolls.
