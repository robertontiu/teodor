# Abstract Expressionism (gestural/action painting and colour field)

Researched 2026-11. Every value names its source; values marked **judgement** have no published source and are editorial choices for Teodor.

## 1. In one paragraph
A New York current of the early 1940s whose first generation flourished from 1943 to the mid-1950s; its painters valued "spontaneity and improvisation" and gave "the highest importance to process" ([Met, Abstract Expressionism](https://www.metmuseum.org/en/essays/abstract-expressionism)). It aimed at art that, "while abstract was also expressive or emotional in its effect", inspired by the Surrealist idea that art should come from the unconscious and by automatism ([Tate, Abstract expressionism](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/art-terms/a/abstract-expressionism)). Two broad groupings (Tate): **action painters**, who "attacked their canvases with expressive brush strokes", worked "in a spontaneous improvisatory manner often using large brushes to make sweeping gestural marks", or poured and trailed paint; and **colour field** painters, who "created simple compositions with large areas of colour intended to produce a contemplative or meditational response". Jackson Pollock, Willem de Kooning, Franz Kline, Lee Krasner, Mark Rothko, Barnett Newman and Clyfford Still are named only as historical context. Teodor treats the two as **modes**: `mode: "gestural"` and `mode: "colour-field"`.

## 2. How it carries feeling
- **The act as the content (gestural).** Rosenberg (1952): "What was to go on the canvas was not a picture but an event" (quoted by the [Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/en/essays/abstract-expressionism)); the action painters "directly placed their inner impulses onto the canvas" (Tate). The process involved "splashing, using gestural brushstrokes and dripping paint onto canvas rather than carefully applying it" ([Tate, Action painters](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/art-terms/a/action-painters)). Feeling travels through the speed, pressure and rhythm of the mark, which viewers covertly simulate (`docs/research.md`, Freedberg & Gallese 2007). This is the current closest to Teodor's felt-state engine.
- **Embodied memory.** De Kooning's marks re-enacted remembered bodily gestures: the line of a horizon "releasing his breath", a singer's stance; "De Kooning's memory was embodied memory" ([Tate, *Women Singing II*: Process and Memory](https://www.tate.org.uk/research/in-focus/women-singing-ii/process-and-memory)).
- **The sublime and the intimate (colour field).** Rothko and Newman aimed for the "sublime" rather than the "beautiful"; Rothko's luminous soft-edged rectangles were meant to provoke a quasi-religious experience, "even eliciting tears"; "I paint big to be intimate" (Met). Newman: "we are making it out of ourselves, out of our own feelings" (Tate).
- **Enveloping scale.** Works were monumental and meant to be seen close, so viewers were "virtually enveloped" (Met).
- **Gravity.** The painters were "troubled by man's dark side" and "anxiously aware of human irrationality and vulnerability" after the war; they drew on myth and the collective unconscious (Met).
- **Fit for Teodor's feelings:** gestural: rage, exhilaration, grief as struggle, desire, release, restlessness; colour field: awe, mourning, stillness, longing, the sacred, loneliness (**judgement**).
- Avoided: depicted objects and narrative, decorative prettiness, the controlled "beautiful" (Met).

## 3. Media, supports and grounds
- **Gestural:** oil and enamel/house paint on large canvas. Pollock poured and dripped "thinned paint onto raw canvas laid on the ground", without brush, stretcher or easel (Met); he "danced around it pouring paint from the can or trailing it from the brush or a stick" (Tate). An earlier mural was painted mostly upright with flicked paint and an enamel-like pink ([Getty, Pollock's *Mural*](https://getty.edu/conservation/our_projects/science/modpaints/modpaints_jp_mural.html)). De Kooning painted oil on canvas and on paper, often with charcoal, and pressed newspaper onto wet paint to lift and transfer images (Tate, *Women Singing II*).
- **Colour field:** thin paint on raw or barely sized cotton duck. Rothko's *Black on Maroon* (1958) ground is a hot animal-glue size tinted with ultramarine and lithol red, "like water", brushed with large decorators' brushes, saturating the canvas "as a translucent stain" ([Tate Papers 23, Conserving Rothko's *Black on Maroon*](https://www.tate.org.uk/research/tate-papers/23/conserving-mark-rothkos-black-on-maroon-1958-the-construction-of-a-representative-sample-and-the-removal-of-graffiti-ink)).
- **Teodor default:** oil on `canvas`. Gestural ground: raw cotton duck `#e4dccb` (**judgement**: the colour of unprimed duck); colour field ground: a tinted size stain chosen from the feeling, e.g. a dark maroon `#4a1f22` (**judgement**, after the Tate stratigraphy). Second medium: gouache or ink on paper for gestural works on paper (**judgement**).

## 4. Palette
- **Gestural principle:** either a reduced palette (black and white, or a few colours and earths) or an all-over interlace of several colours; colour serves energy, not description (**judgement**; the Met and Tate texts do not discuss colour). De Kooning mixed his colours from recipes based on things he saw, keeping "about eighty porcelain jars of colors he has pre-mixed himself" (Hess, quoted in Tate, *Women Singing II*).
- **Gestural colours (approximate, judgement):** black `#161514`, raw-canvas white `#ece6d8`, aluminium grey `#9c9ea0`, flesh pink `#e3a99b`, cadmium yellow `#e8b923`, ochre `#c08f3e`, cadmium red `#c33a2c`, teal-blue `#2f6f86`.
- **Colour field principle:** two or three close or deeply contrasting colours in large areas, built from thin layers so light comes through (Tate Papers 23: very thin layers, wet-on-wet, glazes of egg and dammar). Pigments documented in *Black on Maroon*: French ultramarine, cadmium red, lithol red, bone black, iron oxide and earths, kaolin, barytes (Tate Papers 23).
- **Colour field colours (approximate, judgement):** maroon `#5a1e24`, oxblood `#3d1418`, black `#1a1514`, burnt orange `#b8502a`, cadmium red `#b2302a`, deep plum `#4b2a3d`, ochre-yellow `#d3a540`, ultramarine `#2b3a7a`, cool grey `#6e7078`.
- **Avoid:** pastel prettiness in gestural mode; hard digital gradients in colour field mode; any colour wheel "harmony" chosen by rule (**judgement**).

## 5. Light and value
- No depicted light. In gestural mode value contrast is the energy (black against raw canvas); in colour field mode light comes from within the stacked thin layers and from the soft edges where colours meet (Met: "luminous"; Tate Papers 23: thin layers, glazes) (**judgement** on mechanism).
- Value key: gestural mixed, often high contrast; colour field low and close-valued for the contemplative works, or saturated mid-values (**judgement**).

## 6. Drawing and form
- **Gestural:** drawing happens in paint, at full arm; figures may emerge and dissolve (de Kooning's women, traced and transferred from vellum, Tate *Women Singing II*). No preparatory design required (Met: spontaneity, improvisation).
- **Colour field:** form is the rectangle or band: "simplified, large-format, color-dominated fields" (Met); soft-edged rectangles stacked vertically, or a field divided by a narrow vertical band (Met mentions Newman with Rothko; the band is **judgement** from his best-known format).

## 7. Composition
- **Gestural:** all-over: no single focus, marks spread to the edges, edges cropped (**judgement**; the term "all-over" was not in reachable sources); or a few big structural strokes in tension.
- **Colour field:** frontal, symmetrical, vertical; two or three rectangles floating in a field with a margin of field colour around them (**judgement** from the Tate stratigraphy of field + figure).
- **Scale:** big, meant to envelop (Met). Teodor's default 1200x1600 is fine for colour field; gestural mode may choose a wider canvas, e.g. 1800x1200 (**judgement**).

## 8. Marks and edges
- **Gestural:** large brushes, "sweeping gestural marks" (Tate); poured, trailed and dripped lines (Tate, Met); flicked paint (Getty); thin stain-like layers and thick puckered skins in the same work ([MoMA, *One: Number 31, 1950* conservation](https://www.moma.org/explore/inside_out/2013/01/08/momas-jackson-pollock-conservation-project-looking-closely-at-one-number-31-1950/)); scraping down (Tate, *Women Singing II*).
- **Colour field:** broad decorators' brushes, "very active and very graphic" movement even in a quiet field (Rice, quoted in Tate Papers 23); feathered, soft edges to the figure (Tate Papers 23: "feathered edges of the black form"; Met: "soft-edged rectangles"). Brushwork visible up close, invisible at distance (**judgement**).

## 9. Process (the order of work)
**Gestural** (Met, Tate, MoMA, Tate *Women Singing II*):
1. Raw canvas, on the floor or the wall.
2. First marks without a plan: pour, trail, sweep; thin paint.
3. Layering wet-into-wet: further colours "in quick succession" so they mingle (MoMA *One*).
4. Look, then respond: build up, scrape down, build up again, over days or months for de Kooning (Tate, *Women Singing II*: "built up, scraped down and built up again over a period of several months").
5. Stop when the field holds together as one event (**judgement**).
Teodor phases: no blockout; drawing phase is replaced by a felt "warm-up" of marks; lay-in = 2-3; development = 4 (with `look thumb` and `look mirror` between episodes); finishing = 5.

**Colour field** (Tate Papers 23, *Black on Maroon*):
1. A thin tinted glue size stained into the raw canvas.
2. The field colour in thin layers, wet on wet, with large brushes, about a day between layers.
3. A sealing glaze (dammar) over the field.
4. Feathered thin layers at the edges of the figure (an orange beneath the black).
5. The figure colour as the last paint layer.
6. Final glazes of whole egg and dammar, applied unevenly so the surface varies between matte and sheen.
Teodor phases: drawing = a simple schema of rectangles; lay-in = 1-2; development = 3-5; finishing = 6.

## 10. Finishing
- **Gestural:** no varnish, no unifying glaze; finished is a judgement that the event is complete (**judgement**). Overworking was part of the process for some (scraping), not a failure.
- **Colour field:** the last glazes of egg and dammar (Tate Papers 23); the surface left unvarnished in the conventional sense ("unprotected by glazing or a coherent varnish layer", same source). Teodor: one or two very low-opacity glazes over parts of the field, never the whole evenly (**judgement**).

## 11. Pitfalls for Teodor
- **Pastiche:** drip-splatter all over a canvas "like Pollock", or two red rectangles on maroon "like Rothko". Teodor builds his own field and gesture from the felt state and chooses colour from the feeling (**judgement**).
- **Random noise:** gestures without a felt arc look like a screensaver; the gestures must change with the feeling over the session (**judgement**; Rosenberg's "event").
- **Digital gradient:** a colour field made of perfect gradients reads as a graphic, not a painting (AGENTS.md "It must be a painting").
- **Nearest wrong feelings:** gestural drifts into chaos or anger by default even when the feeling is joy; colour field drifts into décor or calm "wellness" when it should be grave or overwhelming (**judgement**; Met's "sublime rather than beautiful").
- **Habits:** no figure from behind, no doorway; this current has no subject to hide in.

## 12. Engine mapping
- **Gestural, oil (exists, v8):** `new "#e4dccb" [1800 1200] --medium oil --support canvas`; no `scene` or `transfer`; `free` with `brush: "flat"` or `"round"` sizes 40-120, `length` long, `curve` 0.4-0.8, `load` and `impasto` varying with the episode, `direction` from the gesture; trailed lines with `strokes` using `liner` sizes 4-12 along long curved paths; `knife` for scraping and drags; `smudge` to push wet paint; layering with `dry` 0.2-0.4 between episodes so colours mingle wet-into-wet. Felt state runs high arousal and low care; let it change during the session.
- **Colour field, oil:** `new "#4a1f22"`; a schema of soft rectangles via `ref`; `match` or `free` with `flat` 80-160, `edge: "lost"`, `pressure` low, `load` low (thin paint), several passes with `dry` between; `blend` along the rectangle edges for feathering; `glaze` with very low opacity (0.04-0.1) in irregular regions for the egg/dammar sheen.
- **Felt bias:** gestural: arousal +0.4, tension +0.1, care -0.4; colour field: arousal -0.3, tension 0.0, care +0.2 (**judgement**; colour field movement is "very active" yet the result is contemplative). The JSON default is gestural; both are under `modes`.
- **Gaps:** no pour or drip tool (paint falling from a stick or can, pooling, puddle edges, thin lines from height); no flick/splatter; no flat-on-the-floor orientation (gravity flow); no raw-canvas absorption (stain bleeding into unprimed cotton, soak-stain); no matte/gloss variation in the render; no scraping tool that removes paint (knife only drags); no newspaper transfer; no house-paint/enamel behaviour (skins, puckering); gouache and ink are being added but not yet usable.
