## 1. In one paragraph
Icons are sacred images of Christ, the Virgin, saints and feast scenes, made in Byzantium and carried on in the Orthodox world (Greece, the Balkans, Romania, Russia) up to today. They were made in many media (mosaic, ivory, enamel, metal), but the word now mostly means a painted wooden panel; surviving panels run from the pre-iconoclast encaustic icons of Sinai through the tempera-on-gold panels of the Middle and Late Byzantine periods, with iconoclasm (726-87 and 815-43) as the break [1][2]. An icon is not a picture of a scene to look into: it is made for an encounter. The frontal figure gazes out at the worshipper, and contemplation of the icon was understood as direct communication with the holy person shown [1][2]. Its forms follow established conventions so that a saint stays recognisable across centuries [2].

## 2. How it carries feeling
- **Presence, stillness and address, not drama.** Frontality and the outward gaze make a face-to-face encounter [2]; the feeling is of being looked at and received. Gesture is restrained and conventional (the Hodegetria's hand pointing to the Child [1]).
- **Light as revelation, not illumination.** Gold is "painting with light itself" [3]; there are no cast shadows, so the image stays flat and two-dimensional, and light arrives as the last layer of highlights and gold, not from a lamp in the scene [3][4]. The final white rays (ozhivki) "emanate from the eyes and dance over the surface of the flesh and hair" [3].
- **Space that comes toward the viewer.** Inverse perspective puts the vanishing point with the viewer, who is "drawn into the icon" [4]. The gold ground removes horizon and sky [4].
- **Avoided:** momentary expression, theatrical light, atmospheric depth, visible personal brushwork, narrative clutter. The emotion of an icon is calm grief, tenderness or gravity held in a still face **[J]**, drawn from [2][4].
- Teodor's feeling enters through the choice of subject-type, the colour of the robes and the tenderness or austerity of the face's modelling, not through gesture. Emotion research: `docs/research/emotion.md` once written (for now `docs/research.md`).

## 3. Media, supports and grounds
- **Historical:** egg tempera (pigment in diluted egg yolk) on a wooden panel; early Sinai icons also in encaustic [2][3]. The panel was often carved with a shallow recess (kovcheg), covered with glued linen and many coats of chalk-and-glue gesso, 15-20 layers being "not unusual", polished smooth and absorbent [3][4]. Some early icons were painted directly on wood without a white ground [5].
- **Gold:** red bole under the gilded areas, gold leaf laid and burnished [3]; gold was sometimes replaced by yellow paint with orpiment and ochre [5].
- **Default for Teodor:** egg tempera on a gessoed panel; ground white gesso `#f4f0e6` **[J]** (a warm chalk white). Fallback until the tempera medium exists: oil on `panel` with thin, lean layers **[J]**.

## 4. Palette
- **Principle:** a small set of mineral and earth pigments laid in flat local zones and modelled from dark to light; symbolic, not observed, colour; gold as the field of light [3][4][6].
- **Pigments** (historical list from [6], flesh layering from [3][5]; hex values are approximations **[J]**):
  - gold leaf `#c9a43a`, red bole `#8e3b28`
  - sankir / brown-green flesh base `#5a5233` [3][5]; green earth `#7d8a5f`
  - yellow ochre `#c49a45`, red ochre `#9a4a2e`, cinnabar `#c0392b` (flesh pink, Virgin's folds [6]), minium `#d8572a`
  - hematite purple-brown `#5c2b2b` (the Virgin's maphorion **[J]** colour association)
  - azurite `#2f5e9a`, ultramarine `#26408b`, malachite `#3f8a5c`
  - lead white `#f1ebdd`, carbon black `#1d1b19`
- **Avoid:** atmospheric blue-grey distance, broken or optical colour, modern synthetic brights, cast-shadow greys **[J]** from [4].

## 5. Light and value
No light source in the scene and no cast shadows [4]. Value runs from the dark base up through successive lighter layers to the white highlights; the key is middle-to-dark in the flesh with a high-key gold field **[J]**. Modelling builds volume through many thin translucent applications, shadows laid first in green, then brown, pink and white [5].

## 6. Drawing and form
Form is contour first: the design is drawn or traced and the lines incised or painted so they stay visible, then restored at the end [3]. Figures follow conventional types and proportions (elongated bodies, large eyes, long thin nose, small mouth) **[J]** (standard description; not in a read source). Drapery is a system of linear folds and highlight "assists" rather than observed cloth **[J]**.

## 7. Composition
Central, frontal, symmetric or near-symmetric; the figure fills the panel against gold; inscriptions name the figures [2][4]. Bust-length or half-length single figures, or feast scenes with figures and buildings in inverse perspective [4]. Borders frame the image **[J]**.

## 8. Marks and edges
Small soft-hair brushes; tempera "must be applied thinly in short, hatched strokes" [7]. Hard, drawn contours; inner modelling smoothed by floats (transparent glazes) between highlight layers [3]. Brushwork is not meant to be seen as personal gesture **[J]**. Edge hierarchy: crisp outlines everywhere, softest transitions inside the face.

## 9. Process (the order of work)
1. Prepare the panel: recess, linen, gesso layers, polish [3][4].
2. Draw the design; incise or paint the lines [3].
3. Bole and gold leaf on the background and haloes, burnish [3].
4. Repaint the guide lines [3].
5. Roskrysh: flat planes of dark local colour over the whole design; sankir on flesh and hair [3].
6. Highlights in progressively lighter, smaller layers, each followed by a float (transparent glaze) [3][5].
7. Last, sparing highlights; a golden glaze unifies the face [3].
8. Restore the final lines [3].
9. Ozhivki: the last white rays on eyes, hair and flesh; gold assist lines on robes [3][4].
10. Inscriptions, then olifa once the tempera has cured [3].

Teodor's phases **[J]**: phase 1 (drawing) = steps 1-4 as a flat SVG schema and `transfer`; phase 2 (laying in) = step 5; phase 3 (form and light) = steps 6-10.

## 10. Finishing
Finished meant the full ladder of highlights and the ozhivki, the restored lines, the inscription and the olifa: linseed oil with resin that "encapsulates the pigments like amber" and gives a deep shine [3][4]. Olifa darkens with soot from candles and incense over time [4]: Teodor's finish is the fresh, warm, slightly yellowed olifa, never fake age **[J]**.

## 11. Pitfalls for Teodor
- **Pastiche:** a "gold background and a sad face" without the layered dark-to-light build; faux cracks, fake soot, fake age.
- **Nearest wrong feelings:** melodrama (tears, writhing gesture), sentimental devotional print sweetness, a Renaissance perspective box with gold behind it, and Teodor's own drift into a hushed bittersweet interior (`docs/habits.md`) **[J]**.
- **Subject respect:** icons are living religious objects. Teodor paints in the visual language (frontality, gold, layered light) and need not paint a liturgical subject; a secular subject in this language is a choice, and inscriptions must not imitate real liturgical texts carelessly **[J]**.
- **Engine:** gold leaf can't be laid as a material; the blockout renders linear perspective.

## 12. Engine mapping (studio v8)
- **Medium:** egg tempera (being added) on panel; today `oil --support panel` with lean, thin paint (low `impasto`, low `load`).
- **Tools:** `liner` for incised/painted lines, ozhivki and gold assists (hard edge); small `round` for hatched highlights; `glaze` intention for floats and the final golden glaze; `blend` only lightly inside faces.
- **Intentions:** `transfer` for the drawing; `strokes` for lines and hatching; `match` with small sizes and high `faithful` only for the roskrysh; `glaze` for floats.
- **Felt state:** low arousal, high care, moderate tension (firm, controlled line).
- **Views:** `look notan 3` to keep the flat zones legible; `look edges` to check the outlines stay crisp.
- **Gaps:** no gilding (gold leaf with bole, burnished, reflective) and no metallic rendering; no tempera behaviour yet (fast drying, no wet blending, hatching); no olifa finishing op (oil has no finishes yet); `scene` builds linear perspective only, so inverse perspective needs a hand-drawn SVG schema; no incising tool; no inscription lettering tool.
