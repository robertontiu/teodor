# Etching and engraving (intaglio printmaking: burin engraving, etching, drypoint)

Researched 2026-11. Every value names its source. Values marked **judgement** have no published source and are editorial choices for Teodor. Values marked **measured** come from The Met's public-domain prints (`studio/targets.parts/etching-engraving.json`, `studio/calibration_refs.parts/etching-engraving.json`, `docs/calibration/etching-engraving.md`). This is a family of techniques. Printmakers are named only as historical context in the reference records. No setting follows one person's manner (rule 3, `docs/orchestration.md`).

## 1. In one paragraph
Intaglio prints are printed from lines sunk into a metal plate. The plate is inked, wiped and pressed into dampened paper, which takes the ink out of the lines. In **engraving**, "a wedge-shaped metal tool known as a burin is used to gouge clear, sharp furrows in a metal plate" ([Met, The Printed Image in the West: Engraving](https://www.metmuseum.org/en/essays/the-printed-image-in-the-west-engraving)). The engraver cuts the line, and the lines swell "with the push" and taper "with the release" ([Getty, *Artists' Things*: Burin](https://www.getty.edu/publications/artists-things/things/burin/)). In **etching**, "a plate of metal is first covered with a layer of acid-resistant varnish or wax, called the 'ground'", the artist "scratches through the ground with an etching needle", and acid "eats away the lines where the metal has been exposed" ([Met, The Printed Image in the West: Etching](https://www.metmuseum.org/en/essays/the-printed-image-in-the-west-etching)). The acid makes the line, so the needle draws as freely as a pencil. In **drypoint**, "a sharp stylus or needle is used to scratch lines directly into the metal plate", and the burr thrown up beside the line prints "as a rich, velvety black" ([Met, Drypoint](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/the-printed-image-in-the-west-drypoint)). Engraving came from goldsmithing and etching from the armourer's craft (Met, Etching). Engraving reached its systematic height in the 16th-17th centuries, etching from the 17th century, and the etching revival ran c. 1850-1900 (Met essays; [Hammer Museum, *Revivals and Modernity*](https://hammer.ucla.edu/blog/2016/02/revivals-and-modernity-the-printed-image-in-nineteenth-century-france-part-2)).

## 2. How it carries feeling
- **Engraving: order, clarity, permanence.** Its tone is built from systems of lines: parallel hatching, cross-hatching, swelling lines, and dots placed in the lozenges between crossed lines. The result is sculptural, formal and lucid. Mantegna's "short lines of varying width" sought "the tone that would give his figures a three-dimensional presence" (Met, Engraving). The dotted-lozenge system covers "the extremes of tone from the darkest shadows to brilliant light" ([Prints and Principles, "Dotted Lozenge"](https://www.printsandprinciples.com/2012/11/goltzius-dotted-lozenge.html)). Fit: gravity, ceremony, the timeless, the uncanny precision of a dream, a portrait's dignity (**judgement**).
- **Etching: immediacy, atmosphere, intimacy.** Etching is "appealingly light and delicate", "intimate and luminous", "expressive and atmospheric" (Met, Etching). Lalanne opposes flat biting, "executed with pen and ink or with the pencil", to stopped-out biting meant "to convey the idea of a painting" ([Lalanne, *A Treatise on Etching*, trans. Koehler 1880](https://archive.org/details/treatiseonetchin00lalauoft), §67). Fit: weather, cities, a sketch from life, solitude, melancholy, nocturnes, intimacy (**judgement**).
- **Drypoint: velvet and fragility.** It makes rich, soft blacks, and only "a small number of good impressions", "no more than a dozen", can be taken (Met, Drypoint). Fit: tenderness, night, a lost or fleeting moment (**judgement**).
- **Avoided:** colour, flat grey fills, soft blending without lines. A tone has to be made of lines, dots, bite or plate tone.

## 3. Media, supports and grounds
- **Plate:** copper or zinc for engraving (Met, Materials and Techniques: Engraving). Iron, copper or zinc for etching ([Met, Materials and Techniques: Etching](https://www.metmuseum.org/en/perspectives/materials-and-techniques-printmaking-etching)). The earliest German etchers used iron, "stronger than copper yet susceptible to rust" (Met, Etching). The plate is polished so that "only the intentional lines will be printed" (Met, Engraving).
- **Burin:** a square shaft tapering to a lozenge-shaped point, set in a mushroom handle. Control comes from "the range of pressure and the manifold subtleties in manipulation of the angle of the point" (Getty, Burin). The plate rests on a sandbag "so the printmaker can turn it while cutting curved lines" (Met, Engraving). Engraved lines are "V-shaped and taper to a point" ([History of Science Museum, Oxford, Engraving](https://hsm.ox.ac.uk/engraving)).
- **Etching needle and ground:** "a blunt stylus called an etching needle" (Met, Etching techniques). Lalanne uses a sewing needle for the fine point and "a medium embroidery needle" for the coarse, "evenly rounded" so it has no cutting edge. "The pressure used in drawing must remain the same throughout" (Lalanne, §§ on points and flat biting). The ground is a wax and resin varnish laid with a roller or dabber (Lalanne).
- **Acid and biting:** nitric acid (Lalanne). "A longer exposure will cause deeper and wider recesses", which "hold more ink and will thus print darker lines on paper" (Met, Etching techniques). "The depth and the breadth of the lines depends upon the thickness of the points used, and upon the length of time allowed for biting" (Lalanne, Introduction). Stopping out: "lighter areas are protected from further acid bite" (Met). Lalanne's test plate was bitten 3, 6, 10, 20 and 30 minutes (Lalanne, Plates). First bites last "three minutes in warm, or ... five minutes in cold weather" (Lalanne).
- **Drypoint:** "The dry point is held in a perpendicular position, and is used on the bare copper". Its line "has less depth of color than the bitten line" in the furrow. The burr is scraped off when a clean line is wanted (Lalanne, Dry point).
- **Ink, wiping and printing:** ink is spread with a cloth ball or card and wiped with tarlatan, "then the printmaker's palms or the sides of the hands remove the last traces". A printmaker may choose to "leave a thin layer of ink on the plate to create tone". The plate is printed "on the bed of a rolling printing press", under "a dampened sheet of paper and then printing blankets", which leaves the plate mark (Met, Engraving and Etching techniques). Retroussage: a soft rag "draws the ink out of the lines, and spreads it along their edges, so that ... the space between the lines is filled up by a vigorous tint" (Lalanne, §89).
- **Ink colour:** black, or brown in the 19th-century revival. In The Met's records of 1,380 black-and-white etchings from 1850-1925, 77 are catalogued "brown ink" (or sepia or bistre) and 180 "black ink" (**measured**, record counts, `docs/calibration/etching-engraving.md`). Measured absorption: black `[0.973, 1.003, 1.015]` (17 prints), brown `[0.826, 0.961, 1.213]` (1 print) (**measured**).
- **Paper:** laid paper (laid and chain lines from the mould's wires) until wove paper arrived in the 1750s ([Princeton Graphic Arts, "Laid or Wove"](https://www.princeton.edu/~graphicarts/2008/03/laid_or_wove.html)). 19th-century etchings were also printed on chine and Japan paper. Where 1850-1925 etching records name a paper, the counts are laid 265, wove 67, Japan or chine 140 (**measured**, record counts).
- **Teodor default:** medium `ink`, support `plate_laid` (10 px/mm: a 1200 × 1600 canvas is a 12 × 16 cm plate with its sheet), ground `#e4d8c6` (the measured median paper as photographed). For burin work set `"px_per_mm": 16` in `<canvas>_meta.json`, since engraved lines are 0.23 mm wide at 0.3 mm spacing (**measured**).

## 4. Palette
- **Principle:** one ink, black or warm brown, on cream paper. The tone is the ink's density in lines plus the plate tone left by wiping.
- **Colours:** ink `#000000` with `ink: "intaglio_black"` or `"intaglio_brown"`, paper `#e4d8c6` (**measured** medians). Fresh paper `#f2ecdf` (**judgement**).
- **Avoid:** colour, grey ink, and wash tones without lines (aquatint and mezzotint are other techniques, not modelled).

## 5. Light and value
- **Engraving:** a consistent light, with form modelled by hatching that swells into shadow and crosses in the deepest darks. Schongauer used "crosshatching in the deepest shadows to model the forms" (Met, Engraving).
- **Etching:** value comes from line density, line width and bite. "The coarser [points] serve for the foreground ... the finer ... for the receding distances", and "the lines are drawn more closely together here, or kept farther apart there" (Lalanne §68). Stopped-out biting gives the depth: lights bitten briefly, darks longest (Lalanne §69).
- **Measured (eyes, 60 prints):** key L* 67 ± 10, spread 18.5 ± 3.8, darks 0.09 ± 0.09, lights 0.61 ± 0.20, hard-edge share 0.17 ± 0.06, spectral slope -0.82 ± 0.15. That is middle-light and finely textured (**measured**).
- **Lines per mm by value** (median per work): engraving 2.5 (L* 60-80), 3.5 (40-60), 4.4 (< 40). Etching 0.34 (≥ 80), 1.45, 2.3, 2.3. Cross-hatched share: engraving 0.49 / 0.44 / 0.75, etching 0.10 / 0.46 / 0.39 / 0.55 (**measured**). Engraving builds dark by doubling and tripling crossed systems. Etching builds it by bite and width at a nearly constant density.

## 6. Drawing and form
- **Engraving:** form by systems. Parallel curved lines follow the surface ("swinging parallel arcs, counter-curves and cross-hatching", Getty, Burin), cross at a constant angle (measured median 55°), and swell in shadow. Dots go in the lozenges and flecks stand for texture (Met, Engraving: "short flecks of the saint's coarsely woven robe"). Lines "never crossed" in the simplest manner, with values "obtained by swelling the lines" ([Béguin, printmaking dictionary, Engraving](https://polymetaal.nl/beguin/mape/engraving.htm)).
- **Etching:** a free, even line, "the work of the needle", hatching in several directions, an open outline, and changes of needle and bite for distance (Lalanne). Measured crossing angle median 60° (**measured**).
- **Drypoint:** a few lines with burr accents in the darkest places (**judgement**, Met, Drypoint).

## 7. Composition
- The plate's rectangle and its platemark frame the image. A plain margin of paper surrounds it (Met: the plate mark).
- Engraving: the figure or scene fills the plate, often with a lettered margin (verses under the image in several references) (**judgement**: Teodor adds no lettering or fake inscriptions).
- Etching: vignetting, with the work fading out toward the plate edges. A light sky left as paper. Views, street corners and interiors (**judgement**, from the reference set).
- The print reverses the drawing ("a reversed image", Met, Engraving). Teodor draws the image as it should appear and leaves the reversal to the press (**judgement**).

## 8. Marks and edges
- **Burin line:** median width 0.23 mm (works 0.18-0.29), widest tenth 2.2× the median, width CV along lines 0.35, hatch spacing 0.30 mm, crossing angle 55° (**measured**, 8 engravings at 10-34 px/mm). It enters as a point, swells and tapers (Getty).
- **Etched line:** median width 0.36 mm (0.30-0.41), widest tenth 1.9× the median (deeper bites), CV 0.33, spacing 0.43 mm, crossing angle 60°, line ends mostly blunt (end-ratio lower quartile 0.80 against 0.76 for engraving) (**measured**, 11 etchings).
- **Drypoint:** a soft, velvety edge on one side of the line (Met; **judgement** on the side).
- **Plate tone:** a thin even film between the lines, darker where retroussage pulls ink from them (Met; Lalanne). Its density in the engine (0.02-0.06) is **judgement**.
- **Platemark:** the bevelled edge pressed into the paper (Met). Its width of about 0.5 mm in the engine is **judgement**.

## 9. Process (the order of work)
Historical (Met essays; Lalanne):
- Engraving: polish the plate, transfer the design, cut the outlines with the burin, then systems of hatching from light to dark, then dots and flicks, scraping and burnishing corrections. Proof states, ink, wipe clean, print.
- Etching: ground the plate, draw with needles of several sizes, bite the whole plate (3-5 minutes), stop out the lightest passages, bite again, stop out, and so on (up to about 30 minutes for the darkest). Remove the ground, proof, then retouch with drypoint or rebite. Print with a chosen wiping (clean, plate tone, retroussage).

Teodor's phases (`ink` medium, `plate_laid` or `plate_wove`):
- **Drawing:** outlines with `strokes`: `burin` (engraving) or `needle` with `bite: 3` (etching).
- **Lay-in:** `hatch` with the same tool. Engraving: `spacing` ≈ 0.3 mm × px/mm, `cross: 55`, long `length`. Etching: `spacing` ≈ 0.45 mm × px/mm, `cross: 60`, `bite: 3-4`.
- **Development (etching):** biting in stages: `finish bite {"minutes": 6, "where_darker": 0.55}`, then `{"minutes": 8, "where_darker": 0.33}` (each stage stops out what is lighter). Engraving: `match` with the burin for accents, then `finish lozenge {"level": 0.5}` in the darks.
- **Finishing:** `drypoint` accents. `finish platemark` (margin), then `finish wipe` (`tone` 0.02 for a clean-wiped engraving, 0.04-0.06 for an etching with plate tone, `retroussage` 0.2-0.4 for a softer, richer impression). Sign.

## 10. Finishing
- Finished meant a good **impression**: the plate inked, wiped and printed, with the platemark showing. For etching, the choice of wiping was part of the artist's intent ("creatively inking and wiping his plates" made each impression unique, Hammer Museum). For engraving, a clean wipe and crisp lines (**judgement**).
- States: a plate was proofed and reworked, and the last state is the finished one (Met; record media "third state of five").

## 11. Pitfalls for Teodor
- **Digital line-art look:** perfectly even, uniform hatching with no swell, no bite variation and no plate tone. Vary bite and width, and leave plate tone.
- **Over-biting into black mud:** too many crossed layers bitten too long close the lines up (a measured risk in the test: darks share 0.31 against 0.09 ± 0.09). Keep the darkest bite to the smallest areas.
- **Pastiche** of famous plates (a self-portrait in a beret, a Venetian canal, a classical title cartouche). Teodor etches his own subject (**judgement**).
- **Nearest wrong feelings:** antiquarian quaintness, banknote stiffness (engraving), the "souvenir view" (**judgement**).
- **Engine limits:** the core `hatch` lays a draughtsman's hatching (±15% spacing, short pieces). An engraver's systematic parallel lines and curved, form-following systems need `strokes` or a regular hatch (requested in the calibration note). No aquatint or mezzotint. No reversal.
