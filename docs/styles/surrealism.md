# Surrealism (the illusionistic and the automatist strands)

Researched 2026-11. Every value names its source; values marked **judgement** have no published source and are editorial choices for Teodor. Detailed technical studies of Surrealist paintings were mostly out of reach in this session (see Sources), so process values lean on museum essays and glossary texts.

## 1. In one paragraph
A literary, philosophical and artistic movement "officially consecrated in Paris in 1924" with André Breton's *Manifesto of Surrealism*, whose European phase ended with the Second World War ([Met, Surrealism](https://www.metmuseum.org/en/essays/surrealism)). Breton defined it as "pure psychic automatism ... Dictation of thought in the absence of all control exercised by reason, outside of all aesthetic and moral preoccupation"; its artists sought "magic and strange beauty in the unexpected and the uncanny" ([Tate, Surrealism](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/art-terms/s/surrealism)). Painting split into two strands: **visual automatism** (abstract, biomorphic, chance-led) and **"a new form of illusionistic Surrealism"** that painted dream scenes with old-master clarity (Met). "Many surrealist artists have used automatic drawing or writing to unlock ideas and images from their unconscious minds. Others have wanted to depict dream worlds or hidden psychological tensions" (Tate). André Masson, Joan Miró, Max Ernst, René Magritte, Salvador Dalí and Yves Tanguy are named here only as historical context. Teodor treats the two strands as two **modes** of one current: `mode: "automatist"` and `mode: "illusionistic"`.

## 2. How it carries feeling
- **The uncanny.** "A strange and anxious feeling sometimes created by familiar objects in unfamiliar contexts"; Freud's "familiar and yet alien at the same time"; Surrealists "combined familiar things in unexpected ways to create uncanny feelings" ([Tate, The uncanny](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/art-terms/u/uncanny)). The illusionistic mode carries feeling through *what* is shown and *where*: a plausible, calmly lit world in which one thing is wrong (**judgement** on "one thing wrong").
- **Dream space.** Objects in "dreamlike surroundings" and "ominous settings" (Met). Deep, empty, often low-horizon spaces, long shadows, still air (**judgement**; long shadows and empty plains are the generally recognised de Chirico/Tanguy lineage the Met mentions).
- **Metamorphosis and double images.** One form read as two: the paranoiac-critical method produced "double images, pictures that can be read simultaneously as two images" ([Philadelphia Museum of Art, Dalí teacher resource](https://legacyweb.philamuseum.org/doc_downloads/education/ex_resources/dali.pdf)).
- **The hand freed from the brain (automatist).** Automatic drawing is "the hand freed from the injunctions of the brain" (*la main libérée des injonctions du cerveau*), and its endless metamorphosis of lines could carry trauma and its transformation together ([Centre Pompidou, Masson, *Dessin automatique*, 1925-26](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/en/ressources/oeuvre/czAzGRr)). The automatist mode carries feeling through the line's speed, its swelling and its organic, "biomorphic" forms that "evoke living forms such as plants and the human body" ([Tate, Biomorphic](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/art-terms/b/biomorphic)).
- **Fit for Teodor's feelings:** unease, longing that cannot be named, a memory that has gone strange, desire, déjà vu, wonder, playful delight (automatist); the uncanny calm of the illusionistic mode suits anxiety held at a distance (**judgement**).
- Avoided: anecdote, explanation, a single readable moral; the image should stay open (**judgement**, from Breton's "outside of all aesthetic and moral preoccupation").

## 3. Media, supports and grounds
- **Illusionistic:** oil on canvas or panel, painted in the tradition of the old masters: Dalí sought to "pick up the mantle of classical tradition and oil painting in the vein of Van Eyck and Vermeer" ([Fundació Gala-Salvador Dalí, Painting](https://www.salvador-dali.org/en/dali-and-gala/dali-the-total-artist/painting/)); preparatory graphite underdrawing visible on a Dalí portrait ([Fundació Dalí, Documenting the painting technique](https://www.salvador-dali.org/en/artwork/conservation-and-restoration/documenting-the-painting-technique/)). Teodor default: oil on `panel` for smoothness (**judgement**), white ground `#f0ebe0` (**judgement**: a smooth light ground suits the Van Eyck lineage).
- **Automatist:** ink on paper (Masson's automatic drawings are "encre de Chine sur papier": Centre Pompidou); oil on canvas with chance textures: frottage (rubbing a textured surface with a soft pencil), grattage (a canvas prepared with oil paint laid over a textured object and scraped), decalcomania (paint squeezed between two surfaces) ([Tate, Frottage](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/art-terms/f/frottage); [Tate, Grattage](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/art-terms/g/grattage); [Tate, Decalcomania](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/art-terms/d/decalcomania)); sand and glue in mixed media (Philadelphia resource lists sand among automatic-collage materials). Teodor default: oil on `canvas` with a thin toned ground `#c9b48e` (**judgement**: a warm mid-tone that a scumbled or stained field can sit on); ink on paper as second medium.

## 4. Palette
- **Illusionistic principle:** naturalistic, controlled colour serving clarity; clear skies, earth plains, cool distant blues; strong local colour only on the strange object (**judgement**). Magritte's palette moved from "smoky grays and blues and the somber earth tones" (1926-28) to a lighter, clearer one from 1929 ([MoMA conservation blog, Magritte](https://www.moma.org/explore/inside_out/2013/10/15/revealing-the-mystery-of-magrittes-materials-and-techniques/)).
- **Illusionistic colours (approximate, judgement):** sky blue `#8fb3d1`, pale horizon `#e8e2cf`, cerulean shadow `#5d7f9e`, smoky grey `#8a8c8a`, ochre plain `#c49a5a`, raw umber `#5e4a36`, dark shadow `#2b2622`, flesh `#e3b99a`, an accent red `#b23a2e`.
- **Automatist principle:** a single toned or scumbled colour field, then lines and a few flat accents in saturated primaries and black (**judgement**; no colour source reached).
- **Automatist colours (approximate, judgement):** field blue `#3f63a6`, field ochre `#c9a86a`, black line `#1c1b1a`, red `#c7372f`, yellow `#e0b52f`, white `#f2efe6`.
- **Avoid:** neon or fantasy-art saturation, purple-pink "dreamy" gradients, airbrushed glow (**judgement**).

## 5. Light and value
- **Illusionistic:** a single, low, raking light that throws long, sharp cast shadows; high-key sky against dark foreground; legibility everywhere, even of the impossible (**judgement**; consistent with the "illusionistic" label and Magritte's pursuit of "a notable trompe l'oeil effect", MoMA).
- **Automatist:** little modelling; value contrast lives in line against field (**judgement**).

## 6. Drawing and form
- **Illusionistic:** precise academic drawing, graphite underdrawing (Fundació Dalí); objects modelled convincingly so that the impossible combination reads as real (**judgement**). Devices: displacement of scale, unexpected juxtaposition ("familiar things in unexpected ways", Tate uncanny), metamorphosis and double images (Philadelphia).
- **Automatist:** "organic, biomorphic forms and twisted lines" (Met on Miró); drawing as a rapid, unplanned line that is afterwards read and developed into forms (Philadelphia automatic-technique activity: draw freely, "then stop drawing and look at the forms created", then "add definition"; Tate, Grattage: Ernst would "work back into the painting, responding to the unexpected marks").

## 7. Composition
- **Illusionistic:** a deep stage: a low horizon, an empty plain or a room, a few isolated objects with space around them, central or symmetrical placement for stillness (**judgement**).
- **Automatist:** an all-over field with floating signs, no horizon, no fixed up and down (**judgement**).

## 8. Marks and edges
- **Illusionistic:** brushwork hidden; small soft brushes; smooth blended transitions within forms and crisp edges at the object's silhouette; thin paint (**judgement**, old-master lineage per Fundació Dalí).
- **Automatist:** fast continuous lines that swell and thin; stained or scumbled fields; chance textures (frottage, grattage, decalcomania: Tate) worked back into.

## 9. Process (the order of work)
**Illusionistic** (**judgement** except where cited):
1. A small composition found by juxtaposition or double image (Philadelphia: start from an object, imagine it transformed).
2. A precise graphite drawing on a smooth light ground (Fundació Dalí).
3. Thin lay-in of sky and ground, then of the objects in their local colour.
4. Modelling with small soft brushes and blending; cast shadows laid in sharp.
5. Detail and edges; the strange object brought to the highest finish.
Teodor phases: drawing = 1-2 (blockout: a `scene` with the displaced object is ideal); lay-in = 3; development = 4; finishing = 5.

**Automatist** (Philadelphia, Tate grattage, Centre Pompidou):
1. A field: stain, scumble or a chance texture (grattage, decalcomania).
2. Automatic drawing: fast lines without planning, felt state high arousal, low care.
3. Look: read the forms the marks suggest.
4. Develop: define some of them with line and flat colour; leave most of the field open.
Teodor phases: drawing = none or a very loose schema; lay-in = 1-2; development = 3-4; finishing = accents.

## 10. Finishing
- **Illusionistic:** finished meant seamless illusion, invisible brushwork and varnish (varnish clumps observed over the paint layers of a Dalí portrait, Fundació Dalí). Teodor: a last unifying `glaze` at very low opacity, then (when the medium supports it) a varnish (**judgement**).
- **Automatist:** finished meant the moment the forms had been found and the field still breathes; overworking kills it (**judgement**). No unifying glaze.

## 11. Pitfalls for Teodor
- **Pastiche:** melting clocks, bowler hats, floating apples, crutches, elephants on stilts, ants. Teodor finds his own juxtaposition from the feeling, and avoids every iconic motif of the named painters (**judgement**).
- **Fantasy-illustration kitsch:** glowing purple skies, floating islands, "random weird" collage of unrelated objects without one charged relation. The uncanny needs the familiar first (Tate, uncanny).
- **Automatist mode turning into decoration:** pretty doodles; the line must actually be fast and unplanned, then read.
- **Nearest wrong feelings:** whimsy (too cute), horror (too explicit; Surrealism unsettles more than it shocks), cold cleverness (a visual pun with no feeling) (**judgement**).
- **Habits:** the lit doorway in a dark room is already a de Chirico-like device; if used, it must be a choice, not the default.

## 12. Engine mapping
- **Illusionistic, oil (exists, v8):** `new "#f0ebe0" --medium oil --support panel`; a `scene` blockout for the stage and objects; `transfer` lightly; `match` with `round`/`filbert` sizes 30-60 for the lay-in, then 6-20 with low `impasto` (0.05-0.15) and `edge: "soft"` within forms, `"hard"` at silhouettes; `blend` widely for smooth transitions; `liner` for sharp shadow edges and details; a last `glaze` at 0.05-0.1.
- **Automatist, oil:** `new "#c9b48e"`; field with `free`/`match` large `flat` brushes, `edge: "lost"`, or `knife` drags as a grattage-like scrape; lines with `strokes` using `liner` (sizes 3-8) under a high-arousal felt state; then `look` and develop selected forms with `strokes` and flat colour.
- **Ink (being added):** the natural medium for automatic drawing on paper; needs a pen/nib and a brush with swelling line.
- **Felt bias:** illusionistic: arousal -0.2, tension +0.15, care +0.4; automatist: arousal +0.3, tension 0.0, care -0.3 (**judgement**). The JSON entry carries the illusionistic bias as default and the automatist one under `modes`.
- **Gaps:** no chance-texture tools (frottage rubbing, decalcomania press, grattage over a textured object); no varnish step for oil yet; no mixed media or sand; the painter brief has no instruction to suppress planning for automatic drawing (the hand currently always follows planned paths).
