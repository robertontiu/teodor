# Woodblock (`woodblock`)

**What it is.** A Japanese multi-block colour woodblock print (ukiyo-e), and also the black-and-white woodcut of German Expressionism. It is not a brush medium: each stroke of a tool marks out a carved block, and that block is printed once, as a whole, when the stroke is over. Colours are transparent water-based films laid over the paper and earlier impressions, so overlapping blocks combine like overprinted colour (Kubelka-Munk layers). Nothing can be blended or smudged: `blend` intentions do nothing in this medium.

**Supports.**
- `washi` (default): hosho paper, soft kozo fibre. Long fibres and a cloudy formation show in the paper and in flat colour.
- `coarse`: a heavier, grainier paper for the woodcut. The ink thins out in the paper's hollows and the wood grain shows more strongly.

**Tools** (`"brush": ...` in `strokes`, `free` and `match`):
- `key`: the keyblock line: a brush drawing the carver cut and left standing. It prints dense and crisp, always in register. Usually black (`#1b1a1c`; indigo for a blanket or water). It is calligraphic, as ukiyo-e key lines are (one even wire round everything reads as a flat vector drawing): it swells with pressure and with a slow swell along the line, each stroke has its own weight (some pressed, some light; `"weight_spread"`, default 0.4, 0 for an even line), and its ends are blunt-rounded like a cut line rather than a brush's long point. **Vary it by part**, as the prints do (measured on Met prints, `docs/calibration/woodblock.md`): faces and hands finest (about 0.1-0.3 mm: the eyes, nose, mouth, fingers), drapery and outer contours heavier (up to 2-3× the median), landscape contours in between. `"width_mm"` gives the line's width in mm at the print's scale (`new --px-per-mm`; a 1200x1600 canvas is 30x40 cm at 4 px/mm, where 0.25 mm is one pixel: for a figure print with faces, work at 8 px/mm or crop closer). Otherwise `size` is in px (size 3 ≈ 0.45 mm at 4 px/mm). `liner` is accepted as another name for it.
  - **Hair** (kewari): `"lines": n` cuts each stroke as n fine parallel lines, `"spacing"` px apart (default 0.65 mm), that gather where the pressure is light and end one by one: the hairline and the strands of a coiffure. `size` is then each strand's width (0.1-0.2 mm). Draw hair as several such strokes following the strands' flow, from the hairline outward.
- `block`: a colour block. A block is one carved plank, printed once, as a flat, slightly uneven transparent film: wood grain, baren mottle, paper fibre, a slightly heavier edge where the pigment pools, and a registration slip of 0 to 2 px against the keyline. How its shape is decided:
  - With a schema (`match`), the stroke only says *which area*: the carver cuts the whole connected area of the schema around the stroke that has that colour (within `"tolerance"` delta-E, default 12), stopping at keylines already printed, and the colour runs into the middle of the keyline. So fields come out flat and bounded by their edges, not as overlapping stroke shapes. `"carve": "stroke"` turns this off; `"carve": "region"` turns it on in `strokes` and `free` too (needs a schema).
  - Without a schema (`strokes`, `free`), the stroke's own area is carved.
  - The stroke's seed: the carver looks at the schema under the stroke, off the keyline and inside an area (not on its anti-aliased edge), and takes the area of the stroke's own colour if the stroke touches it (match samples that colour where the stroke starts; in `strokes` give the area's colour), else the colour most of the stroke covers. So a small area (an ear, an eye) is found by a stroke bigger than it, as long as the stroke's colour is that area's. A seed that finds no area carves the stroke's own shape, and the episode reports which seeds did that and where.
  - One block per colour: a stroke whose colour is within 8 delta-E of an earlier block (or whose carved area that block already printed) is more of the same plank. It only adds the area the block has not printed yet, with the same film, registration and wood, so a field built from many strokes prints as one even impression.
  - `"reprint": true` prints the block once more (to deepen a colour): **one more impression per intention**, however many strokes or seeds mark its areas (each stroke adds only the area this impression has not printed yet). `"reprint": 2` is two more. To print a block a third time, use another intention. `"density"` sets that impression's film instead of the block's own.
  - The printer mixes the ink to give the colour you ask for over what is already there, as far as a transparent colour can (it can only darken). `"density"` prints a fixed film thickness instead (1.0 is roughly the named colour over white paper); `"opacity"` (default 0.3) sets how much the colour covers what is under it; `"grain"` the wood grain; `"registration": 0` removes the slip. This is the default tool, so `match` prints blocks.
- `bokashi`: a graded block, colour wiped into water. Full strength where the stroke starts, fading to the paper where it ends; the fade runs along the stroke's path (each point takes the distance along the path of its nearest path point), so a wide brush does not leave a round edge. `"fade": "end"` reverses it, `"both"` makes it strong in the middle. For a Hiroshige sky, draw one very wide stroke (size larger than the canvas width) from just above the top edge down to where the sky should go pale.
- `gouge`: the woodcut gouge. It cuts white out of a black block: print a black block first (a `glaze` with opacity 1, or a `block`), then cut. `"cut": "v"` gives V-gouge wedges (pointed at both ends), `"u"` gives U-gouge channels with rounded ends and a little ink caught on the floor of wide cuts. The default is V for sizes under 14, U otherwise. Edges splinter along the wood grain (horizontal). Cuts show the paper, not earlier colour blocks: to have colour in the cuts, print it after cutting.

**Intentions.**
- `glaze` prints one flat block over the region: its shape is the region (hard carved edge; `feather` only rounds it slightly), and `opacity` sets the film (0.2 is a light tint, 1.0 a full black block).
- `strokes` and `free`: each stroke is its own block, so many small strokes look like many small blocks. Large flat fields read best as a `glaze` region or a few broad `block` strokes.
- `match` carves and prints the schema's colour areas as flat blocks (see `block`): many strokes over one field print it once. Use it for the main fields, then add the keyline last (or first: blocks then stop at it). A schema with smooth gradients comes out as bands of flat colour, as a print would; use `bokashi` for graded skies.
- Felt state: tension and arousal make keylines and gouge marks jagged and broken (the Expressionist hand); care makes them clean.

**Order of work.** As a printer would: lightest colour blocks first, darker ones on top, bokashi on its own block, keyline last (or first). Woodcut: black block, then gouge.

**Drying and time.** None to speak of: each impression is printed onto damp paper and does not flow afterwards. `dry` does nothing.

**Finishing** (no varnish or fixative):
- `finish seal '{"at": "br", "size": 60, "color": "#b8322a"}'`: Teodor's own mark printed as a red seal block, TEO / DOR cut out in white (`text`: Latin letters only, lines split by `\n`). Imitation Japanese or Chinese characters are refused: a fake seal or a fake Japanese signature is what the brief forbids. `'{"mark": "signature"}'` cuts his handwritten signature in the keyblock instead. Either counts as the signature.
- `sign [auto|br|bl|tr|tl]`: the signature cut in the keyblock and printed in sumi (in ukiyo-e the signature is part of the blocks, not hand-written).
- `finish impression`: the final pressing; printed areas sink slightly into the paper (shown under raking light). With `'{"region": {...}, "height": 1.6}'` it does blind embossing (karazuri): the region is raised without ink.

**Finishing check** (`report finish`): signed (seal or signature); for washi, a key line printed and its width's widest tenth over its median (Met prints 2.6-2.7; flagged below 1.6, a judgement: an even wire).

**Calibration** (Nov 2026; report: `docs/calibration/woodblock.md`). Measured against 8 Met ukiyo-e prints (Hiroshige, Hokusai) at the canvas scale of 4 px/mm, the canvas taken as a print of about 30x40 cm.
- Set from data: the flat-field texture of a printed block (wood grain `GRAIN` 0.3, baren mottle `MOTTLE` 0.55, paper fibre in the film `FIBRE` 0.5) to the std L* of hand-chosen flat passages (2.9, IQR 2.6-3.4, n = 8; rendered 1.3 light, 2.6 mid, 3.7 dark field); the paper's own texture `PAPER_K` 0.22 to a bare margin (std L* 1.3, n = 1, weak); the keyline size recommendation to the measured keyline width (0.35 mm, IQR 0.32-0.43, n = 7, at the photographs' resolution); width variation along keylines (CV 0.32, IQR 0.28-0.35; rendered 0.26-0.33).
- Structural change: blocks are carved areas printed once (above), because the references are flat fields bounded by the keyline, not overlapping shapes. The statistics do not capture this on their own (in a scene test the large-scale unevenness of a field, `mottle`, came from overlapping stroke shapes before and from print texture after); it was judged by eye against the prints, side by side (`docs/gallery/woodblock_washi_scene.jpg`).
- Key line (Nov 26): measured on 10 crops of faces, hands, hair and contours from Met figure prints (Utamaro, Eishi; 10 px/mm) and the 7 landscape references: width CV along lines 0.35-0.40, widest tenth / median 2.0-2.7, free-end ratio 0.74-0.84. The tool (key film at 20 px/mm, one size, auto articulation): widest tenth / median 1.32 → 1.97, end ratio 0.47 → 0.72, CV 0.28 → 0.33. Set from those: `KEY_RHYTHM` 0.5, `KEY_WEIGHT` 0.4, the ordinary (not the liner's) landing and lift. Hair spacing 0.65 mm is weak data (three crops). The rest of the width contrast is the painter's choice of size by part.
- Still by eye: the swell's wavelength (10-18 sizes), the colour-identity and area tolerances (8 and 12 delta-E), the seed's flatness threshold (3 delta-E per px), edge pooling `POOL` 0.45, registration slip 0-2 px, the woodcut paper (`coarse`) and gouge, the seal, the pattern of the grain (more streaky figure than rings) and of the mottle (fine speckle).
- Colours: the pigments of ukiyo-e in a water binder, from `studio/pigments.json` (FORS masstones in gum arabic, the nearest binder measured; hue measured, value and chroma approximate): indigo `#1b1c23`, Prussian blue `#19172f`, red lead `#f7632f`, vermilion `#ac322d`, orpiment `#d7a73f`, lamp black (sumi) `#303030` (masstones: dilute them for prints). The seal's `#b8322a` and the key's `#1b1a1c` agree in hue with vermilion (33 deg) and lamp black (neutral).
- Not calibrated: the hard-edge share (0.28 in the references at this scale) depends on the composition (bokashi and painted gradients make soft edges); carved edges themselves fall in the hard mode (10-90% rise below 0.5 mm), as in the prints.

**Limits.** A block's colour follows the schema's area colour (plus any palette or `color_shift` you ask for), not each stroke's sampled colour. No mica, no metallic or opaque white body colour (gofun), no wiping of ink off a block after printing. Wood grain always runs horizontally. A gouge over bare paper changes nothing. A key line narrower than one pixel can't be printed: at 4 px/mm that is anything under 0.25 mm.
